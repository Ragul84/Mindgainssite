Grammar MCQs with answers

Use these 5 worked Grammar questions to revise the SSC English syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 1,679 questions.

1. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Neither of the two candidates (A)/ have been able to (B)/ secure the minimum qualifying marks (C)/ for the interview. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). 'Neither of' is followed by a plural noun but takes a singular verb. Therefore, 'have' should be replaced with 'has'.

2. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. If I ______ a bird, I would fly across the ocean.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. am
  4. had been

Answer: were

Explanation: This is a hypothetical conditional sentence (Type 2). In such sentences, 'were' is used for all subjects (including 'I', 'he', 'she', 'it') in the 'if' clause to express an unreal or improbable condition.

3. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. The manager, along with his team members, (A)/ are working diligently (B)/ to meet the project deadline (C)/ by next week. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). When two subjects are joined by 'along with', 'as well as', 'together with', etc., the verb agrees with the first subject. Here, 'The manager' is singular, so the verb should be 'is' instead of 'are'.

4. Choose the most appropriate option to complete the sentence. Hardly had he entered the room ______ the phone rang.

  1. than
  2. when
  3. then
  4. as soon as

Answer: when

Explanation: The correlative conjunction for 'Hardly' (or 'Scarcely') is 'when' (or 'before'). 'Than' is used with 'No sooner'.

5. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. She is one of the most brilliant students (A)/ that has ever (B)/ attended this university (C)/ in its history. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). When 'one of the' is followed by a plural noun and a relative pronoun (like 'that', 'who', 'which'), the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun (students), not 'one'. So, 'has' should be 'have'.
