International Trade MCQs with answers

Use these 5 worked International Trade questions to revise the TNPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. India's economic liberalisation was initiated in:

  1. 1947
  2. 1951
  3. 1991
  4. 2001

Answer: 1991

Explanation: India adopted LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation) reforms in 1991 under PM Narasimha Rao and FM Manmohan Singh, triggered by a balance of payments crisis.

2. The WTO (World Trade Organisation) replaced which organisation?

  1. IMF
  2. GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)
  3. World Bank
  4. UNCTAD

Answer: GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)

Explanation: WTO replaced GATT on 1 January 1995. It administers multilateral trade agreements and provides a forum for trade negotiations and dispute resolution.

3. The World Trade Organization (WTO) was established in:

  1. 1995 — replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1947); HQ in Geneva, Switzerland; 164 members; deals with international trade rules
  2. 1947
  3. 2000
  4. 1990

Answer: 1995 — replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1947); HQ in Geneva, Switzerland; 164 members; deals with international trade rules

Explanation: WTO (January 1, 1995): replaced GATT (1947) after Uruguay Round negotiations (1986-1994). HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. 164 member countries. Functions: sets and enforces global trade rules, resolves trade disputes, negotiates new trade agreements. India is a founding member. Doha Development Round (2001): stalled over agricultural subsidies.

4. The term 'dumping' in international trade means:

  1. Exporting goods to a foreign market at prices below production cost or below domestic price — considered unfair trade practice; subject to anti-dumping duties under WTO rules
  2. Disposing industrial waste
  3. Selling expired goods
  4. Selling goods at higher price abroad

Answer: Exporting goods to a foreign market at prices below production cost or below domestic price — considered unfair trade practice; subject to anti-dumping duties under WTO rules

Explanation: Dumping: selling exported goods below cost of production (or below home market price) to gain market share in foreign country. Damages domestic industries in the importing country. WTO's Anti-Dumping Agreement: allows importing country to impose anti-dumping duties to offset price difference. India frequently imposes anti-dumping duties on Chinese goods.

5. The term 'Current Account' in Balance of Payments includes:

  1. Trade in goods (merchandise trade balance), trade in services (services account), primary income (wages, dividends, interest received/paid), and secondary income (remittances, transfers)
  2. Only goods trade
  3. Only capital flows (FDI, FPI)
  4. Only loans and grants

Answer: Trade in goods (merchandise trade balance), trade in services (services account), primary income (wages, dividends, interest received/paid), and secondary income (remittances, transfers)

Explanation: Current Account (CA) = Merchandise trade (visible trade) + Services trade (invisible trade: IT, tourism, shipping) + Primary Income (factor income: wages, profits, dividends, interest) + Secondary Income (current transfers: remittances, government grants). India's CA deficit largely reflects merchandise trade deficit (imports > exports), partially offset by services surplus and remittances.
