1. The Second Five Year Plan (1956-61) was based on the model by:

  1. P.C. Mahalanobis — emphasized heavy industrialization (steel plants: Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela) and public sector growth
  2. Mahatma Gandhi
  3. Jawaharlal Nehru alone
  4. IMF recommendation

Answer: P.C. Mahalanobis — emphasized heavy industrialization (steel plants: Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela) and public sector growth

Explanation: Second FYP (Mahalanobis Plan, 1956-61): focused on heavy industry and capital goods (Feldman-Mahalanobis model — investment in capital goods to boost long-term growth). Steel plants: Bhilai (Soviet assistance), Durgapur (British), Rourkela (West German). Also established IITs and other institutions.

2. The Make in India initiative was launched by:

  1. PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol
  2. PM Manmohan Singh
  3. NITI Aayog independently
  4. Ministry of Commerce

Answer: PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol

Explanation: Make in India (September 25, 2014): India as global design and manufacturing hub. 25 focus sectors: automobiles, aviation, chemicals, IT, pharma, construction, food processing, textiles, etc. The iconic logo: walking lion made of cogwheels (industry symbol). Complemented by: Invest India, Startup India, Skill India, Digital India.

3. The Union Budget is prepared by which Ministry?

  1. Ministry of Finance — specifically the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) and Department of Revenue coordinate the budget; Finance Minister presents to Parliament
  2. Prime Minister's Office
  3. Ministry of Commerce
  4. Planning Commission (abolished)

Answer: Ministry of Finance — specifically the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) and Department of Revenue coordinate the budget; Finance Minister presents to Parliament

Explanation: Union Budget preparation: Ministry of Finance (DEA — Budget Division) issues budget circular to all ministries for expenditure estimates; Department of Revenue handles tax proposals (Direct Tax: CBDT, Indirect Tax: CBIC). Finance Minister consults stakeholders, states, industry. Finance Bill (tax changes) + Appropriation Bill (expenditure) presented together.

4. The concept of 'infant industry protection' argues:

  1. New/young industries in developing countries need temporary tariff protection to grow and become internationally competitive — used by USA (19th century), Germany (Bismarck era), Japan, South Korea, and now by developing economies including India
  2. All industries need permanent protection
  3. Free trade is never beneficial
  4. Only agricultural industries need protection

Answer: New/young industries in developing countries need temporary tariff protection to grow and become internationally competitive — used by USA (19th century), Germany (Bismarck era), Japan, South Korea, and now by developing economies including India

Explanation: Infant Industry Argument (Friedrich List, 1841 — 'National System of Political Economy'): nascent industries with potential scale economies need temporary protection (tariffs, subsidies) against established foreign competitors until they achieve global competitiveness. USA protected its manufacturing (1800s-1900s) with high tariffs. South Korea's POSCO steel. Counter: protection tends to become permanent, breeds inefficiency.

5. The 'marginal cost of funds based lending rate' (MCLR) is different from 'base rate' in that MCLR:

  1. Uses marginal cost of funds (current deposit rates at margin, not average cost) to set lending rate — more dynamic, passes monetary policy changes faster to borrowers than base rate (which used average cost)
  2. Is the same as base rate
  3. Is set by government not banks
  4. Is only for home loans

Answer: Uses marginal cost of funds (current deposit rates at margin, not average cost) to set lending rate — more dynamic, passes monetary policy changes faster to borrowers than base rate (which used average cost)

Explanation: MCLR (April 2016): replaces Base Rate (April 2010). Base Rate used average cost of funds (lagged, slow to change). MCLR uses: marginal cost of deposits (current rates, not historical average), CRR maintenance cost, operating cost, and tenor premium. Changed monthly (MCLR) vs quarterly (Base Rate). Since EBLR (2019): retail and MSME loans linked to external benchmark (repo rate) — fastest transmission.

6. The National Credit Guarantee Trust (NCGTC) oversees:

  1. Credit guarantees for MSME loans (without collateral) — provides guarantee cover to lending institutions so they lend to MSMEs; also operates PM Mudra Yojana and PM SVANidhi guarantees
  2. Housing loan guarantees
  3. Agricultural loan waivers
  4. Export credit guarantees (that's ECGC)

Answer: Credit guarantees for MSME loans (without collateral) — provides guarantee cover to lending institutions so they lend to MSMEs; also operates PM Mudra Yojana and PM SVANidhi guarantees

Explanation: NCGTC (National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company, 2014): provides credit guarantees for MSME loans through: Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE), Credit Guarantee Fund for Skill Development (CGFSD), Credit Guarantee Fund for Educational Loans (CGFEL), Credit Guarantee Scheme for Start-Ups. Borrowers don't need collateral; banks get guaranteed coverage.

7. Structural unemployment is caused by:

  1. Economic downturns reducing demand
  2. Seasonal variations in production
  3. Mismatch between workers' skills and available jobs due to technological change
  4. Workers voluntarily changing jobs

Answer: Mismatch between workers' skills and available jobs due to technological change

Explanation: Structural unemployment: skills mismatch due to technology changes, industry shifts, or globalization. Long-term; requires retraining. E.g., manufacturing workers displaced by automation.

8. The Bombay Plan (1944) was drafted by Indian industrialists including Tata and Birla, proposing:

  1. Free market economy with no government intervention
  2. Central planning with significant state investment in industry
  3. Complete nationalization of all industries
  4. Agricultural-led growth model

Answer: Central planning with significant state investment in industry

Explanation: Bombay Plan: 8 industrialists (JRD Tata, GD Birla, etc.) proposed a 15-year plan with heavy state investment in infrastructure and industry, import substitution, and mixed economy framework.

9. India's Wholesale Price Index (WPI) is published by:

  1. RBI
  2. CSO/NSO
  3. Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Office of the Economic Adviser)
  4. MOSPI

Answer: Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Office of the Economic Adviser)

Explanation: WPI: published monthly by Office of the Economic Adviser (OEA), Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry. Base year 2011-12, 697 commodities.

10. The MSME sector in India is defined under the MSME Development (Amendment) Act 2020 based on:

  1. Number of employees only
  2. Investment in plant & machinery/equipment AND annual turnover
  3. Annual turnover only
  4. Capital employed only

Answer: Investment in plant & machinery/equipment AND annual turnover

Explanation: MSME 2020 definition: Micro (Investment ≤Rs 1 cr, Turnover ≤Rs 5 cr), Small (Investment ≤Rs 10 cr, Turnover ≤Rs 50 cr), Medium (Investment ≤Rs 50 cr, Turnover ≤Rs 250 cr). Composite criteria (investment + turnover).

11. The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) is responsible for:

  1. Collecting import/export duties
  2. Formulating and implementing India's Foreign Trade Policy (FTP)
  3. Regulating foreign exchange transactions
  4. Negotiating trade agreements

Answer: Formulating and implementing India's Foreign Trade Policy (FTP)

Explanation: DGFT: under Ministry of Commerce and Industry. Implements FTP, issues Importer Exporter Code (IEC), administers export promotion schemes (EPCG, Advance Authorization, MEIS/RODTEP). Also issues export/import licenses.

12. The Index of Industrial Production (IIP) uses which base year and is published by:

  1. 2004-05, Ministry of Commerce
  2. 2011-12, National Statistical Office (NSO)
  3. 2011-12, Ministry of Commerce
  4. 2004-05, NSO

Answer: 2011-12, National Statistical Office (NSO)

Explanation: IIP: base year 2011-12 (revised from 2004-05 in 2017). Published monthly by NSO (National Statistical Office) under MoSPI. Measures growth in industry: Mining (14.4%), Manufacturing (77.6%), Electricity (8%).

13. India's Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 classified industries into schedules. Schedule A industries were exclusively reserved for:

  1. Private sector
  2. Public sector (State)
  3. Cooperative sector
  4. Joint sector

Answer: Public sector (State)

Explanation: IPR 1956: Schedule A (17 industries) — exclusive State domain (arms, atomic energy, railways, coal, steel). Schedule B — State to establish, private allowed. Schedule C — private sector.

14. Who is known as the 'Father of Indian Economic Planning'?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. P.C. Mahalanobis
  3. Manmohan Singh
  4. V.K.R.V. Rao

Answer: P.C. Mahalanobis

Explanation: P.C. Mahalanobis (1893-1972): statistician and planner, architect of India's second Five Year Plan (1956-61). Founder of ISI (Indian Statistical Institute). His 'two-sector' model emphasized heavy industry.

Explanation: NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015 to foster cooperative federalism.
