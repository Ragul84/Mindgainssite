Industry MCQs with answers

Use these 5 worked Industry questions to revise the TNPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 700 questions.

1. The Second Five Year Plan (1956-61) was based on the model by:

  1. P.C. Mahalanobis — emphasized heavy industrialization (steel plants: Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela) and public sector growth
  2. Mahatma Gandhi
  3. Jawaharlal Nehru alone
  4. IMF recommendation

Answer: P.C. Mahalanobis — emphasized heavy industrialization (steel plants: Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela) and public sector growth

Explanation: Second FYP (Mahalanobis Plan, 1956-61): focused on heavy industry and capital goods (Feldman-Mahalanobis model — investment in capital goods to boost long-term growth). Steel plants: Bhilai (Soviet assistance), Durgapur (British), Rourkela (West German). Also established IITs and other institutions.

2. The Make in India initiative was launched by:

  1. PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol
  2. PM Manmohan Singh
  3. NITI Aayog independently
  4. Ministry of Commerce

Answer: PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol

Explanation: Make in India (September 25, 2014): India as global design and manufacturing hub. 25 focus sectors: automobiles, aviation, chemicals, IT, pharma, construction, food processing, textiles, etc. The iconic logo: walking lion made of cogwheels (industry symbol). Complemented by: Invest India, Startup India, Skill India, Digital India.

3. The Union Budget is prepared by which Ministry?

  1. Ministry of Finance — specifically the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) and Department of Revenue coordinate the budget; Finance Minister presents to Parliament
  2. Prime Minister's Office
  3. Ministry of Commerce
  4. Planning Commission (abolished)

Answer: Ministry of Finance — specifically the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) and Department of Revenue coordinate the budget; Finance Minister presents to Parliament

Explanation: Union Budget preparation: Ministry of Finance (DEA — Budget Division) issues budget circular to all ministries for expenditure estimates; Department of Revenue handles tax proposals (Direct Tax: CBDT, Indirect Tax: CBIC). Finance Minister consults stakeholders, states, industry. Finance Bill (tax changes) + Appropriation Bill (expenditure) presented together.

4. The concept of 'infant industry protection' argues:

  1. New/young industries in developing countries need temporary tariff protection to grow and become internationally competitive — used by USA (19th century), Germany (Bismarck era), Japan, South Korea, and now by developing economies including India
  2. All industries need permanent protection
  3. Free trade is never beneficial
  4. Only agricultural industries need protection

Answer: New/young industries in developing countries need temporary tariff protection to grow and become internationally competitive — used by USA (19th century), Germany (Bismarck era), Japan, South Korea, and now by developing economies including India

Explanation: Infant Industry Argument (Friedrich List, 1841 — 'National System of Political Economy'): nascent industries with potential scale economies need temporary protection (tariffs, subsidies) against established foreign competitors until they achieve global competitiveness. USA protected its manufacturing (1800s-1900s) with high tariffs. South Korea's POSCO steel. Counter: protection tends to become permanent, breeds inefficiency.

5. The 'marginal cost of funds based lending rate' (MCLR) is different from 'base rate' in that MCLR:

  1. Uses marginal cost of funds (current deposit rates at margin, not average cost) to set lending rate — more dynamic, passes monetary policy changes faster to borrowers than base rate (which used average cost)
  2. Is the same as base rate
  3. Is set by government not banks
  4. Is only for home loans

Answer: Uses marginal cost of funds (current deposit rates at margin, not average cost) to set lending rate — more dynamic, passes monetary policy changes faster to borrowers than base rate (which used average cost)

Explanation: MCLR (April 2016): replaces Base Rate (April 2010). Base Rate used average cost of funds (lagged, slow to change). MCLR uses: marginal cost of deposits (current rates, not historical average), CRR maintenance cost, operating cost, and tenor premium. Changed monthly (MCLR) vs quarterly (Base Rate). Since EBLR (2019): retail and MSME loans linked to external benchmark (repo rate) — fastest transmission.
