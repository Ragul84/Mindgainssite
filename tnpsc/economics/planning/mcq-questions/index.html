26. The Bharat Net project aims to:

  1. Provide broadband connectivity to all Gram Panchayats (2.5 lakh panchayats) using OFC, radio, and satellite — run by BSNL/DOT; enable Digital India in rural areas
  2. Build highways
  3. Develop ports
  4. Electrify villages (that's Saubhagya scheme)

Answer: Provide broadband connectivity to all Gram Panchayats (2.5 lakh panchayats) using OFC, radio, and satellite — run by BSNL/DOT; enable Digital India in rural areas

Explanation: BharatNet (2011, earlier NOFN — National Optical Fibre Network): world's largest rural broadband connectivity project. Connects all 2.5 lakh gram panchayats with optical fiber (OFC). Implemented by BSNL through state governments. Enables: e-governance, telemedicine, e-education, e-commerce in rural India. Essential backbone for Digital India.

27. Long-run economic growth is determined by:

  1. Improvements in productivity (technology, education, health, institutions) and accumulation of physical capital (Solow growth model)
  2. Short-term demand fluctuations
  3. Monetary policy alone
  4. Exchange rate movements

Answer: Improvements in productivity (technology, education, health, institutions) and accumulation of physical capital (Solow growth model)

Explanation: Long-run growth determinants (Robert Solow's model): capital accumulation (investment) and technological progress (total factor productivity). Endogenous growth theory (Paul Romer): knowledge, human capital, and innovation drive sustained growth. Key: education quality, R&D spending, institutions, rule of law, and infrastructure.

28. Food Corporation of India (FCI) was established to:

  1. Procure food grains from farmers at MSP, store, and distribute them through PDS — established 1965; HQ: New Delhi; India's largest food storage network
  2. Export food grains
  3. Process food products
  4. Regulate food prices directly

Answer: Procure food grains from farmers at MSP, store, and distribute them through PDS — established 1965; HQ: New Delhi; India's largest food storage network

Explanation: FCI (1965, FCI Act 1964): functions: procurement of wheat and rice from farmers at MSP (mainly Punjab, Haryana, UP), building and managing strategic food reserves (buffer stocks), distributing to state governments for PDS at Central Issue Price (CIP). FCI's buffer stock of grain helps stabilize prices and ensure food security.

29. The Smart Cities Mission in India aims to:

  2. Build 100 new cities from scratch
  3. Demolish old cities

  1. 1954 — first recipients: C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji), C.V. Raman, and Dr. S. Radhakrishnan; maximum 3 per year; given for exceptional service to nation
  2. 1947
  3. 1950
  4. 1960

Answer: 1954 — first recipients: C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji), C.V. Raman, and Dr. S. Radhakrishnan; maximum 3 per year; given for exceptional service to nation

Explanation: Bharat Ratna (1954): India's highest civilian honor. First three recipients: C. Rajagopalachari (last Governor-General, Tamil Nadu), C.V. Raman (Nobel laureate physicist), and Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan (philosopher, later President). No monetary prize. Given for exceptional service in art, literature, science, and public service.

31. The term 'trade surplus' means:

  1. Exports > Imports (country earns more from exports than it spends on imports) — opposite of trade deficit
  2. Imports > Exports (trade deficit)
  3. Equal exports and imports
  4. High foreign exchange reserves

Answer: Exports > Imports (country earns more from exports than it spends on imports) — opposite of trade deficit

Explanation: Trade Balance = Exports - Imports. Trade Surplus: Exports > Imports (positive balance). Trade Deficit: Imports > Exports (negative balance). India consistently runs a trade deficit (imports more than exports). India's trade deficit ~$240 billion (2022-23). Offset partially by services surplus, remittances. China and Germany have large trade surpluses.

32. The term 'natural monopoly' refers to:

  1. Industry where one firm can produce output most efficiently (lowest cost) due to very high fixed costs — e.g., railways, electricity distribution, water supply, pipelines
  2. Monopoly given by nature/geography
  3. Monopoly on natural resources
  4. Government monopoly only

Answer: Industry where one firm can produce output most efficiently (lowest cost) due to very high fixed costs — e.g., railways, electricity distribution, water supply, pipelines

Explanation: Natural Monopoly: occurs when average cost declines continuously with scale (economies of scale so large that one firm serves the whole market more efficiently than multiple competitors). Examples: electricity grid, railway tracks, water pipes, telecom infrastructure. Typically regulated by government (or government-owned) to prevent monopoly pricing.

33. The National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF) was set up to:

  1. Fund long-term infrastructure projects (roads, ports, airports, power, railways) through equity and quasi-equity — India's first sovereign wealth-type fund; established 2015
  2. Provide home loans
  3. Fund defense purchases
  4. Regulate stock markets

Answer: Fund long-term infrastructure projects (roads, ports, airports, power, railways) through equity and quasi-equity — India's first sovereign wealth-type fund; established 2015

Explanation: NIIF (2015): India's quasi-sovereign wealth fund for infrastructure. 49% owned by Government of India; 51% by foreign/domestic institutional investors (Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, Singapore GIC, etc.). Three funds: Master Fund (greenfield/brownfield infra), Fund of Funds, Strategic Opportunities Fund. Total committed capital: ~$4.5 billion.

34. The phenomenon of 'Dutch Disease' refers to:

  1. Boom in one sector (e.g., natural resource discovery) causing currency appreciation and decline in other export sectors (especially manufacturing) — named after Netherlands' natural gas boom in 1960s
  2. A type of financial crisis
  3. An agricultural disease
  4. Import of cheap manufactured goods

Answer: Boom in one sector (e.g., natural resource discovery) causing currency appreciation and decline in other export sectors (especially manufacturing) — named after Netherlands' natural gas boom in 1960s

Explanation: Dutch Disease: named after Netherlands' North Sea gas discovery (1959). Large foreign currency inflows from resource boom → currency appreciates → manufacturing becomes uncompetitive → deindustrialization. Examples: Nigeria (oil), Venezuela (oil). Prevention: sovereign wealth funds (Norway), diversification strategies.

  4. National telecom policy

In 2023, government merged BBNL with BSNL to streamline the BharatNet project and leverage BSNL's existing infrastructure, workforce, and right-of-way permissions for faster rural broadband rollout.

  4. NABARD

42. The economic concept 'invisible hand' was given by:

  1. Adam Smith (in 'Wealth of Nations,' 1776) — the idea that individuals pursuing self-interest in free markets unintentionally promote public good through competition
  2. John Maynard Keynes
  3. Karl Marx
  4. Milton Friedman

Answer: Adam Smith (in 'Wealth of Nations,' 1776) — the idea that individuals pursuing self-interest in free markets unintentionally promote public good through competition

Explanation: Adam Smith's 'invisible hand': when individuals act in their own self-interest (profit motive) in competitive markets, they are guided as if by an 'invisible hand' to produce goods/services society wants at efficient prices. Basis of free-market capitalism. Wealth of Nations (1776) also argued for division of labor, free trade, and against mercantilism.

43. The 'demographic transition' model describes:

  1. Four stages of population change from high birth/death rates (pre-industrial) to low birth/death rates (post-industrial) — most developing countries are in stage 2-3 (high birth, declining death rates)
  2. Immigration and emigration patterns
  3. Urban-rural population movement
  4. Only mortality rate changes

Answer: Four stages of population change from high birth/death rates (pre-industrial) to low birth/death rates (post-industrial) — most developing countries are in stage 2-3 (high birth, declining death rates)

Explanation: Demographic Transition Model (Warren Thompson, 1929): Stage 1: high birth + high death rates (pre-industrial, stable population). Stage 2: high birth + declining death (rapid population growth — India in this stage since 1950s). Stage 3: declining birth + low death (slowing growth). Stage 4: low birth + low death (stable, developed countries).

44. The 'multiplier-accelerator interaction' in Keynesian economics explains:

  1. Business cycles: initial investment increases income (multiplier effect) → increased income raises demand for capital goods (accelerator effect) → further investment → cycles of expansion and contraction
  2. Only inflation
  3. Only unemployment
  4. Only exchange rate fluctuations

Answer: Business cycles: initial investment increases income (multiplier effect) → increased income raises demand for capital goods (accelerator effect) → further investment → cycles of expansion and contraction

Explanation: Keynesian business cycle theory: Multiplier (ΔY = k × ΔI) + Accelerator (ΔI = v × ΔY): initial autonomous investment → income multiplied by k → higher income accelerates further investment → boom (expansion). When income growth slows, acceleration reverses, investment falls, income multiplier works downward → recession. Paul Samuelson formalized this interaction.

45. The 'principal-agent problem' in economics occurs when:

  1. Agent (employee/manager) may not act in the best interests of principal (owner/employer) due to information asymmetry and different incentives — leads to moral hazard and adverse selection
  2. Government is the agent for citizens
  3. Central bank is agent for government
  4. Banks are agents for depositors only

Answer: Agent (employee/manager) may not act in the best interests of principal (owner/employer) due to information asymmetry and different incentives — leads to moral hazard and adverse selection

Explanation: Principal-Agent Problem: principal (owner, shareholder) hires agent (manager, employee) but cannot perfectly monitor agent's actions. Agent may shirk, take excessive risks, or pursue own interests at principal's expense. Solutions: incentive contracts (bonuses, stock options), monitoring, reputation effects. Related: corporate governance, insurance (insurer-insured), doctor-patient.

47. The 'Big Push' theory of economic development was proposed by:

  1. Paul Rosenstein-Rodan (1943) — argues developing countries need massive coordinated investment across sectors simultaneously to escape poverty trap
  2. W.W. Rostow
  3. Walt Whitman
  4. John Keynes

Answer: Paul Rosenstein-Rodan (1943) — argues developing countries need massive coordinated investment across sectors simultaneously to escape poverty trap

Explanation: Big Push Theory (Rosenstein-Rodan, 1943): developing countries face coordination failure — no single entrepreneur invests because market too small, but if all invest simultaneously, market becomes viable. Solution: coordinated, large-scale investment across sectors simultaneously. Influenced India's Five Year Plans (simultaneous industrialization across sectors).

48. India's Sovereign Credit Rating is assessed by:

  1. International credit rating agencies: Moody's, S&P (Standard & Poor's), Fitch — India's rating is 'Baa3' (Moody's, lowest investment grade) / 'BBB-' (S&P/Fitch)
  2. World Bank only
  3. RBI internally
  4. IMF only

Answer: International credit rating agencies: Moody's, S&P (Standard & Poor's), Fitch — India's rating is 'Baa3' (Moody's, lowest investment grade) / 'BBB-' (S&P/Fitch)

Explanation: Sovereign credit ratings measure a country's ability to repay debt. India: Moody's Baa3 (lowest investment grade — BBB equivalent); S&P BBB-; Fitch BBB- (all at lowest investment grade). Higher rating → lower borrowing cost for government and companies. India's stable outlook means no immediate upgrade/downgrade expected.

49. An 'inclusive growth' strategy targets:

  1. Economic growth that benefits all sections of society — particularly poor, marginalized, women, tribals, minorities — through social protection, education, health, employment generation
  2. Only GDP growth
  3. Only industrial growth
  4. Only agricultural growth

Answer: Economic growth that benefits all sections of society — particularly poor, marginalized, women, tribals, minorities — through social protection, education, health, employment generation

Explanation: Inclusive Growth: broad-based growth where benefits reach the bottom of the pyramid. India's Planning Commission and NITI Aayog emphasize inclusive growth. Indicators: HDI improvement, poverty reduction, gender parity, social mobility, access to basic services. Related: sustainable development goals (SDGs) — 'leaving no one behind.'

50. The 'Marshall Plan' was:

  1. US aid program to rebuild Western European economies after World War II (1948-1952) — $13 billion in grants/loans; rebuilt Germany, France, Italy etc.; named after Secretary of State George Marshall
  2. World Bank's first lending program
  3. IMF's first loan program
  4. GATT precursor agreement

Answer: US aid program to rebuild Western European economies after World War II (1948-1952) — $13 billion in grants/loans; rebuilt Germany, France, Italy etc.; named after Secretary of State George Marshall

Explanation: Marshall Plan (European Recovery Program, 1948-52): US provided $13 billion (equivalent to ~$160 billion today) in economic aid to rebuild Western Europe after WWII. Helped restore industrial production, trade, and prevent Soviet influence from spreading to economically devastated Europe. George Marshall (US Secretary of State) proposed it; won Nobel Peace Prize 1953.
