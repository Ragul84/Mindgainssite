Planning MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Planning questions to revise the TNPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The Planning Commission of India was replaced by:

  1. Finance Commission
  2. NITI Aayog
  3. Economic Advisory Council
  4. CAG

Answer: NITI Aayog

Explanation: NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission in January 2015. It serves as a policy think tank.

2. SEBI regulates which sector?

  1. Banking sector
  2. Insurance sector
  3. Securities and Capital Markets
  4. Agricultural markets

Answer: Securities and Capital Markets

Explanation: SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) is the regulatory body for securities markets in India, protecting investor interests.

3. FDI stands for:

  1. Foreign Direct Investment
  2. Federal Direct Income
  3. Financial Development Index
  4. Foreign Debt Instrument

Answer: Foreign Direct Investment

Explanation: FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) is investment made by a foreign entity in a business or production in another country, providing control or significant influence.

4. The 'Gini Coefficient' measures:

  1. National income
  2. Inequality of income distribution (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality)
  3. Standard of living
  4. Economic growth rate

Answer: Inequality of income distribution (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality)

Explanation: Gini Coefficient (0 to 1 or 0 to 100) measures income inequality. 0 = perfect equality (everyone has same income); 1 = perfect inequality (one person has all income). India's Gini is approximately 0.35-0.38.

5. The law of demand states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)
  2. When price rises, demand rises
  3. Demand is always constant
  4. Supply determines demand

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)

Explanation: Law of Demand (Alfred Marshall): price and quantity demanded have an inverse relationship — as price rises, quantity demanded falls (consumers buy less; some switch to substitutes). Exceptions (Giffen goods, Veblen goods, necessities, wartime hoarding). Demand curve slopes downward.

6. The law of supply states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)
  2. When price rises, supply falls
  3. Supply is constant regardless of price
  4. Demand determines supply

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)

Explanation: Law of Supply: price and quantity supplied have a direct relationship — as price rises, producers are willing to supply more (higher profit incentive). Supply curve slopes upward. Exceptions: agricultural products (time lag), antiques/art (fixed supply). Market equilibrium: demand = supply.

7. India's Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in:

  1. January 2015 — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) chaired by the Prime Minister, unlike Planning Commission
  2. 2010
  3. 2000
  4. 2020

Answer: January 2015 — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) chaired by the Prime Minister, unlike Planning Commission

Explanation: NITI Aayog: established January 1, 2015, replacing the Planning Commission (est. 1950). NITI Aayog is a policy think tank (not a fund-allocating body like Planning Commission). Chaired by PM. CEO runs daily operations. Prepares national strategies and provides technical advice. Planning Commission's 5-year plans replaced by 3-year, 7-year strategies.

8. India's Five Year Plans were a feature of which economic model?

  1. Socialist/Mixed economy planning — Soviet-influenced centralized planning to guide economic development (1951-2017)
  2. Pure capitalist economy
  3. Free market economy
  4. Laissez-faire economy

Answer: Socialist/Mixed economy planning — Soviet-influenced centralized planning to guide economic development (1951-2017)

Explanation: India's Five-Year Plans (1951-2017, 12 plans total) were inspired by Soviet Union's central planning model. First Plan (1951-56): agriculture focus (Bhakra dam, community development). Second Plan (Nehru-Mahalanobis): heavy industry. 12th Plan ended 2017. NITI Aayog replaced with strategy documents.

9. The White Revolution in India refers to:

  1. Operation Flood (1970-1996) — massive increase in milk production; led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India') through AMUL cooperative model
  2. Green Revolution
  3. Blue Revolution (fisheries)
  4. Pink Revolution (meat/poultry)

Answer: Operation Flood (1970-1996) — massive increase in milk production; led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India') through AMUL cooperative model

Explanation: White Revolution/Operation Flood: Dr. Verghese Kurien (NDDB — National Dairy Development Board) set up milk cooperatives (AMUL model) to eliminate middlemen, give fair price to farmers, and collect/process milk efficiently. India became world's largest milk producer. AMUL started in Anand, Gujarat, 1946.

10. Public Distribution System (PDS) in India is meant to:

  1. Distribute subsidized food grains (rice, wheat, sugar, kerosene) to Below Poverty Line (BPL) families through ration shops (Fair Price Shops)
  2. Distribute fertilizers
  3. Manage mid-day meals in schools
  4. Export food grains

Answer: Distribute subsidized food grains (rice, wheat, sugar, kerosene) to Below Poverty Line (BPL) families through ration shops (Fair Price Shops)

Explanation: PDS: network of about 5.3 lakh Fair Price Shops (ration shops) distributing subsidized food. Managed jointly by central (Food Corporation of India procures and allocates) and state governments (distributes). NFSA 2013 (National Food Security Act) extended PDS to ~81 crore people (75% rural, 50% urban population).

11. Sen's Capability Approach to development was proposed by:

  1. Amartya Sen — Nobel Prize in Economics 1998; argues development should expand people's capabilities and freedoms, not just income
  2. Paul Samuelson
  3. Milton Friedman
  4. John Maynard Keynes

Answer: Amartya Sen — Nobel Prize in Economics 1998; argues development should expand people's capabilities and freedoms, not just income

Explanation: Amartya Sen (Nobel 1998): capabilities approach — development as freedom/expansion of real opportunities. Focus on what people are actually able to do and be (capabilities), not just income. Influenced HDI design. Major works: 'Development as Freedom' (1999), 'The Idea of Justice'. Bengal famine research: famines caused by lack of entitlements, not just food scarcity.

12. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) is:

  1. Investment by a foreign entity in a business in another country with significant control (typically 10%+ ownership) — in plant, machinery, or management control
  2. Portfolio investment (FPI/FII — just buying shares)
  3. Loans from foreign banks
  4. Government-to-government aid

Answer: Investment by a foreign entity in a business in another country with significant control (typically 10%+ ownership) — in plant, machinery, or management control

Explanation: FDI: foreign entity invests in a domestic company (typically 10%+ equity = significant control). Brings: capital, technology, management expertise, global market access. FPI/FII: foreign portfolio investment (buying shares without control). India attracted $70 billion+ FDI in 2021-22. Top FDI sectors: services, computer, telecom, construction.

13. SENSEX is the benchmark stock market index of:

  1. Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) — comprises 30 largest, most actively traded companies; launched January 1, 1986 (base year 1978-79 = 100)
  2. National Stock Exchange (NSE — that's NIFTY 50)
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. Finance Ministry

Answer: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) — comprises 30 largest, most actively traded companies; launched January 1, 1986 (base year 1978-79 = 100)

Explanation: SENSEX (Sensitive Index): BSE's 30-stock index. NSE's benchmark: NIFTY 50 (50 stocks). BSE (1875): world's oldest stock exchange in Asia, at Dalal Street, Mumbai. NSE (1992): India's largest exchange by trading volume. SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India, 1992): regulator of securities market.

14. The Kuznets Curve (Environmental Kuznets Curve) suggests:

  1. Environmental degradation first increases then decreases as per capita income rises (inverted U-shape) — poor countries can't afford clean tech; rich countries can
  2. Income inequality always rises with growth
  3. Pollution is always increasing
  4. Environment improves immediately with development

Answer: Environmental degradation first increases then decreases as per capita income rises (inverted U-shape) — poor countries can't afford clean tech; rich countries can

Explanation: Environmental Kuznets Curve (EKC): as income rises, pollution/environmental degradation first increases (industrialization), reaches a peak, then decreases (clean technology, service economy, environmental regulations). Named after Simon Kuznets. The original Kuznets Curve deals with income inequality (inverted U) as economic development progresses.

15. India's first stock exchange was:

  1. Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, 1875) — established as 'The Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association' under a banyan tree on Dalal Street, Mumbai
  2. NSE (1992)
  3. Calcutta Stock Exchange (1908)
  4. Madras Stock Exchange (1937)

Answer: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, 1875) — established as 'The Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association' under a banyan tree on Dalal Street, Mumbai

Explanation: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, 1875): Asia's oldest stock exchange. Started informally in 1855 under a banyan tree, formalized as 'The Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association.' Moved to Dalal Street, Mumbai. India's second oldest exchange: Ahmedabad Stock Exchange (1894). Calcutta Stock Exchange: 1908. Madras Stock Exchange: 1937.

16. The term 'multiplier effect' in economics means:

  1. An initial change in spending leads to a larger final change in income — if multiplier = 2, then Rs 100 crore investment → Rs 200 crore income increase
  2. Prices multiplying during inflation
  3. Population growth multiplier
  4. Export multiplier only

Answer: An initial change in spending leads to a larger final change in income — if multiplier = 2, then Rs 100 crore investment → Rs 200 crore income increase

Explanation: Keynesian Multiplier: k = 1/(1-MPC) = 1/MPS. Where MPC = Marginal Propensity to Consume (fraction of extra income spent). If MPC = 0.8, k = 1/(1-0.8) = 5. An injection of Rs 100 crore in government spending creates Rs 500 crore in total income (as each round of spending becomes income for someone else).

17. India is classified as a:

  1. Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)
  2. Pure socialist economy
  3. Pure capitalist economy
  4. Closed economy

Answer: Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)

Explanation: India's mixed economy (as in the Constitution's Directive Principles): public sector controls strategic industries (defense, rail, atomic energy), while private sector operates in most areas. Since 1991 reforms, India has moved significantly toward market economy with reduced government control.

18. India's largest trading partner (exports + imports combined) in recent years is:

  1. USA (largest export destination), China (largest import source) — China tops as overall trading partner by value
  2. Pakistan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. UAE

Answer: USA (largest export destination), China (largest import source) — China tops as overall trading partner by value

Explanation: India's top trading partners: USA is the largest export destination (~18% of India's exports). China is the largest source of imports (~15% of India's imports). As a bilateral trading partner (exports + imports combined), China and USA alternately top the list. UAE and Saudi Arabia are also major partners.

19. Remittances from Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) contribute to India's:

  1. Current Account (in Balance of Payments) — India is the world's largest remittance recipient (~$100 billion annually)
  2. Capital Account only
  3. Trade Balance (goods exports)
  4. Fiscal deficit

Answer: Current Account (in Balance of Payments) — India is the world's largest remittance recipient (~$100 billion annually)

Explanation: NRI remittances: India consistently receives the highest remittances globally (~$100 billion in 2022-23). Recorded under 'transfers' in the Current Account of BoP. Major sources: USA, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UK. They help finance India's current account deficit and support rural household incomes.

20. The term 'Laissez-Faire' in economics means:

  1. Let it be/do — minimal government intervention; markets self-regulate through price mechanism (Adam Smith's 'invisible hand')
  2. Heavy government control
  3. Protectionist trade policy
  4. Mixed economy

Answer: Let it be/do — minimal government intervention; markets self-regulate through price mechanism (Adam Smith's 'invisible hand')

Explanation: Laissez-faire (French: 'let do/let go'): economic philosophy advocating minimal government intervention. Markets, through price mechanism ('invisible hand' — Adam Smith, 'Wealth of Nations,' 1776), efficiently allocate resources. Associated with classical economics. Opposite: interventionism/planned economy.

21. The Atal Innovation Mission (AIM) was established under:

  1. NITI Aayog — to promote innovation and entrepreneurship across India through Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) in schools and Atal Incubation Centres (AICs)
  2. Ministry of Finance
  3. CSIR
  4. RBI

Answer: NITI Aayog — to promote innovation and entrepreneurship across India through Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) in schools and Atal Incubation Centres (AICs)

Explanation: AIM (2016, under NITI Aayog): flagship initiative to promote innovation. Key programs: Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) — maker spaces in schools (Grades 6-12) with 3D printers, robotics kits, etc. Atal Incubation Centres (AICs): support startups. Atal Community Innovation Centres (ACICs): tier-2/3 cities and rural areas.

22. Digital India mission aims to:

  1. Transform India into a digitally empowered society — three pillars: digital infrastructure, digital services, digital literacy; launched 2015
  2. Replace paper currency
  3. Digitize only government records
  4. Promote IT exports only

Answer: Transform India into a digitally empowered society — three pillars: digital infrastructure, digital services, digital literacy; launched 2015

Explanation: Digital India (2015): three pillars: (1) Digital Infrastructure (broadband highways, mobile connectivity, public internet access, eGovernance) (2) Digital Services (DigiLocker, e-Hospitals, e-Education, BHIM-UPI payments, Aadhaar) (3) Digital Literacy (PMGDISHA). India now has 900+ million internet users.

23. The Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (Clean India Mission) was launched on:

  1. October 2, 2014 (Gandhi Jayanti) — to achieve Open Defecation Free (ODF) India; 9 crore+ toilets built; PM Modi started by personally sweeping Valmiki colony, New Delhi
  2. August 15, 2014
  3. January 26, 2015
  4. April 5, 2016

Answer: October 2, 2014 (Gandhi Jayanti) — to achieve Open Defecation Free (ODF) India; 9 crore+ toilets built; PM Modi started by personally sweeping Valmiki colony, New Delhi

Explanation: Swachh Bharat Mission (Oct 2, 2014, Gandhi's 145th birthday): two sub-missions — SBM-Grameen (rural, MoDRWS) and SBM-Urban (MoHUA). Phase 1 (2014-19): ODF (Open Defecation Free) goal — 60 crore people stopped open defecation. Phase 2 (2020-25): ODF+, solid/liquid waste management, plastic waste management.

24. The term 'circular economy' refers to:

  1. An economic system that eliminates waste by reusing, repairing, refurbishing, and recycling materials — contrasts with linear economy (take-make-dispose)
  2. Economic cycle of boom and bust
  3. Circular flow of income in national accounting
  4. Trade circle between countries

Answer: An economic system that eliminates waste by reusing, repairing, refurbishing, and recycling materials — contrasts with linear economy (take-make-dispose)

Explanation: Circular Economy: regenerative system that keeps resources in use for as long as possible. Principles: reduce consumption, reuse products, recycle materials, recover energy. Contrasts with linear (take-make-waste) economy. UN SDGs include circular economy elements. EU's Circular Economy Action Plan (2020). Key to achieving net-zero and sustainable development.

25. India's largest public sector undertaking (PSU) by revenue is:

  1. Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) — petroleum refining and marketing; consistently India's largest company by revenue; followed by ONGC, BPCL
  2. Life Insurance Corporation (LIC)
  3. State Bank of India
  4. NTPC

Answer: Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) — petroleum refining and marketing; consistently India's largest company by revenue; followed by ONGC, BPCL

Explanation: Indian Oil Corporation (IOC): India's largest company by revenue (~Rs 8 lakh crore). It is a Fortune 500 company (ranked ~94th globally). Refines and markets petroleum products through its extensive pipeline and retail network (~30,000 outlets). Other large PSUs: ONGC (oil exploration), BPCL, HPCL, Coal India, SBI, LIC, NTPC.
