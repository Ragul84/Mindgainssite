Poverty Welfare MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Poverty Welfare questions to revise the TNPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Poverty line in India is determined based on:

  1. Income only
  2. Calorie intake / consumption expenditure
  3. Asset ownership
  4. Employment status

Answer: Calorie intake / consumption expenditure

Explanation: India's poverty line is primarily based on minimum required calorie intake (2,400 kcal/day rural; 2,100 kcal/day urban) and the expenditure needed to achieve it. The Tendulkar Committee revised this methodology.

2. Economics is defined as the study of:

  1. How individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants
  2. Only money and banking
  3. Only government finance
  4. Only international trade

Answer: How individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants

Explanation: Economics (from Greek 'oikonomia' = household management): study of how scarce resources (land, labor, capital, enterprise) are allocated to satisfy unlimited human wants. Alfred Marshall: 'Science of material welfare.' Lionel Robbins: 'Science of choice under scarcity.'

3. The National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) guarantees:

  1. 100 days of unskilled manual work per year to any rural household that demands it — minimum wage paid, unemployed allowance if work not provided within 15 days
  2. 200 days of work
  3. Employment to urban workers
  4. Skilled employment only

Answer: 100 days of unskilled manual work per year to any rural household that demands it — minimum wage paid, unemployed allowance if work not provided within 15 days

Explanation: MGNREGA (2005, implemented 2006): provides legal right to 100 days of unskilled work per household per year at minimum wage. If work not provided within 15 days, unemployment allowance paid. Works include road construction, water conservation, land development. Among world's largest social protection programs. Renamed to include Gandhi: MGNREGS.

4. The Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY) aims to provide:

  1. Housing for All — affordable housing to urban and rural poor by 2022 (extended to 2024); includes PMAY-Urban and PMAY-Gramin
  2. Free food for all
  3. Employment guarantee
  4. Healthcare for all

Answer: Housing for All — affordable housing to urban and rural poor by 2022 (extended to 2024); includes PMAY-Urban and PMAY-Gramin

Explanation: PMAY (2015): targets housing for all urban slum dwellers and rural homeless. PMAY-Urban: credit-linked subsidy scheme for home loans (for EWS/LIG/MIG). PMAY-Gramin: provides Rs 1.2 lakh (plain areas) or Rs 1.3 lakh (NE, hilly) grant for rural house construction. Completed 3 crore+ houses.

5. The National Rural Health Mission (NHM) was launched in:

  1. 2005 — to improve healthcare in rural India; later merged with National Urban Health Mission to form NHM in 2013; ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) are key workers
  2. 2000
  3. 2010
  4. 1995

Answer: 2005 — to improve healthcare in rural India; later merged with National Urban Health Mission to form NHM in 2013; ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) are key workers

Explanation: NRHM (National Rural Health Mission, 2005): flagship health scheme for rural areas. Key feature: ASHA workers — trained female community health activists (one per 1000 population) who link communities with health facilities. NRHM + National Urban Health Mission merged into NHM (National Health Mission) in 2013.

6. The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme was introduced to:

  1. Incentivize companies to increase domestic manufacturing — financial incentives based on incremental sales; initially 13 sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles
  2. Replace GST
  3. Provide employment guarantee
  4. Support agriculture

Answer: Incentivize companies to increase domestic manufacturing — financial incentives based on incremental sales; initially 13 sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles

Explanation: PLI Scheme (2020-21): government gives manufacturers financial incentives (3-5% of incremental production over a base year) to boost domestic manufacturing. Initially 13 sectors: mobile phones (Apple, Samsung set up factories), pharmaceuticals (API), automobiles, textiles, food processing, etc. Aims to make India a global manufacturing hub.

7. The Atal Pension Yojana (APY) targets:

  1. Unorganized sector workers — provides guaranteed pension of Rs 1,000-5,000/month at age 60; administered by PFRDA; co-contribution by government for eligible subscribers
  2. Government employees
  3. Corporate sector workers
  4. Farmers only

Answer: Unorganized sector workers — provides guaranteed pension of Rs 1,000-5,000/month at age 60; administered by PFRDA; co-contribution by government for eligible subscribers

Explanation: APY (2015, replaced Swavalamban scheme): for unorganized sector workers. Subscribers aged 18-40 contribute monthly; at 60, receive guaranteed pension of Rs 1,000/2,000/3,000/4,000/5,000 per month. Government (co-contribution of 50% of contribution or Rs 1,000/year, whichever lower) for eligible subscribers for 5 years. Administered by PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority).

8. The UDAN (Ude Desh Ka Aam Nagrik) scheme is related to:

  1. Regional Air Connectivity Scheme — makes air travel affordable on unserved/underserved routes; caps airfare for 1-hour flight; launched 2016
  2. Road connectivity
  3. Rail connectivity
  4. Port development

Answer: Regional Air Connectivity Scheme — makes air travel affordable on unserved/underserved routes; caps airfare for 1-hour flight; launched 2016

Explanation: UDAN (Ude Desh Ka Aam Nagrik — Let the Common Citizen Fly) 2016: provides affordable air connectivity to Tier-2/Tier-3 cities. Viability Gap Funding (VGF) provided to airlines operating on unserved routes. Capped airfare: Rs 2,500 for 1-hour flight. Revived and developed many regional airports. Over 400 routes operationalized.

9. The AMRUT (Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation) scheme focuses on:

  1. Basic urban infrastructure (water supply, sewerage, storm water, parks, non-motorized transport) for cities with 1 lakh+ population; 500 cities; 2015-2022 (AMRUT 2.0: 2021-26)
  2. Rural roads
  3. Agricultural irrigation
  4. Housing for BPL only

Answer: Basic urban infrastructure (water supply, sewerage, storm water, parks, non-motorized transport) for cities with 1 lakh+ population; 500 cities; 2015-2022 (AMRUT 2.0: 2021-26)

Explanation: AMRUT (2015, replaced JNNURM): provides every household with a tap with assured supply of water and a sewerage connection; parks and public spaces. 500 cities. AMRUT 2.0 (2021-26): 100% coverage of urban water supply, 100% sewerage management, rejuvenation of water bodies, and operational & maintenance reforms.

10. The Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY scheme provides:

  1. Health insurance of Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalization to ~10 crore poor/vulnerable families (50 crore beneficiaries) — launched 2018
  2. Primary health care only
  3. Cash transfer to sick persons
  4. Free medicines only

Answer: Health insurance of Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalization to ~10 crore poor/vulnerable families (50 crore beneficiaries) — launched 2018

Explanation: Ayushman Bharat - Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY, 2018): world's largest health assurance scheme. Covers Rs 5 lakh per family per year for hospitalization. Target: 10.74 crore poor/vulnerable families (50 crore beneficiaries) — bottom 40% of population. Cashless treatment at empanelled private and public hospitals. SECC 2011 data used for beneficiary identification.

11. The One District One Product (ODOP) scheme promotes:

  1. Each district of India to develop and market one unique product — integrates local crafts, agriculture, and industry into value chain; 761 products from 761 districts identified
  2. One crop per district
  3. One factory per district
  4. One bank per district

Answer: Each district of India to develop and market one unique product — integrates local crafts, agriculture, and industry into value chain; 761 products from 761 districts identified

Explanation: ODOP (2018, DPIIT under Ministry of Commerce): identifies and promotes one distinctive product per district. Products range from handicrafts (Pashmina, Kanjeevaram silk) to food products (Rasgulla of Kolkata, Darjeeling tea) to agricultural products. Creates a brand identity, links artisans/farmers to markets, and promotes exports.

12. The Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA) was launched to:

  1. Universalize elementary education (Classes 1-8) — free and compulsory education for all children 6-14 years; merged into Samagra Shiksha in 2018
  2. Provide higher education
  3. Provide skill training
  4. Build universities

Answer: Universalize elementary education (Classes 1-8) — free and compulsory education for all children 6-14 years; merged into Samagra Shiksha in 2018

Explanation: SSA (2001): flagship program for universalizing elementary education (RTE Act 2009 gave it constitutional backing). Established/upgraded schools in unserved habitations, recruited teachers, built classrooms. Mid-Day Meal Scheme complemented SSA. In 2018, SSA, RMSA (secondary), and Teacher Education merged into Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan.

13. The National Pension System (NPS) replaced the Old Pension Scheme (OPS) for Central Government employees from:

  1. January 1, 2004 (for employees joining on/after that date) — contributory scheme where employee contributes 10% and government 14% of basic+DA; managed by PFRDA
  2. 2010
  3. 1999
  4. 2008

Answer: January 1, 2004 (for employees joining on/after that date) — contributory scheme where employee contributes 10% and government 14% of basic+DA; managed by PFRDA

Explanation: NPS (National Pension System, 2004): replaced defined benefit OPS for central government employees joining from January 1, 2004 onwards. Contributory (employee: 10%, employer: 14% for central govt). Market-linked returns. Managed by PFRDA. NPS extended to all citizens in 2009 (Swavalamban/NPS-Lite for unorganized sector).

14. The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) was launched to:

  1. Provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected habitations (500+ population in plain areas, 250+ in hilly/tribal areas) — launched 2000, PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee
  2. Build national highways
  3. Build expressways
  4. Develop urban roads only

Answer: Provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected habitations (500+ population in plain areas, 250+ in hilly/tribal areas) — launched 2000, PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee

Explanation: PMGSY (2000): flagship scheme to provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected rural habitations. Target: 500+ population habitations in plain areas, 250+ in NE/hilly/tribal. World's largest rural road program. PMGSY Phase III (2019): upgrade 1.25 lakh km of existing rural roads. Funded 60% Centre: 40% State (90:10 for NE/special category).

15. The 'poverty trap' is a situation where:

  1. Poor people cannot escape poverty because they lack resources to invest in health, education, or productive assets — low income → low savings → low investment → low income (vicious cycle)
  2. Only high taxation causes poverty
  3. Poverty is self-fulfilling prophecy due to geography
  4. Lack of democracy causes poverty

Answer: Poor people cannot escape poverty because they lack resources to invest in health, education, or productive assets — low income → low savings → low investment → low income (vicious cycle)

Explanation: Poverty Trap (Jeffrey Sachs): poor people/countries stuck in poverty because: too poor to save/invest → capital doesn't accumulate → productivity stays low → income stays low. Solutions: 'Big Push' (massive external aid to break the cycle), conditional cash transfers, microfinance. Counter-view (Daron Acemoglu, James Robinson): institutions matter more than external shocks.

16. The National Sample Survey (NSS) / PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey) provides data on:

  1. Employment, unemployment, wages, living standards, consumer expenditure in India — PLFS replaced NSS on employment from 2017; annual survey with quarterly urban data
  2. Industrial production
  3. GDP only
  4. Agricultural output

Answer: Employment, unemployment, wages, living standards, consumer expenditure in India — PLFS replaced NSS on employment from 2017; annual survey with quarterly urban data

Explanation: NSO conducts: PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey, 2017): quarterly urban data + annual rural+urban data on employment, wages, migration patterns. HCES (Household Consumer Expenditure Survey): spending patterns (last conducted 2022-23 after gap since 2011-12 — 2017-18 data was withheld due to controversy). These surveys inform poverty estimates and policy.

17. Which Five Year Plan was the most successful in India in terms of achieving its growth target?

  1. Sixth Five Year Plan (1980-85) — achieved 5.7% vs target of 5.2% growth; under PM Indira Gandhi; focused on poverty alleviation, self-reliance; Jawahar Rozgar Yojana launched
  2. First FYP
  3. Second FYP
  4. Eleventh FYP

Answer: Sixth Five Year Plan (1980-85) — achieved 5.7% vs target of 5.2% growth; under PM Indira Gandhi; focused on poverty alleviation, self-reliance; Jawahar Rozgar Yojana launched

Explanation: Sixth FYP (1980-85): India's most successful plan — exceeded its growth target. Focus: poverty alleviation, self-reliance, minimum needs program. Also notable: Third FYP (1961-66) was derailed by 1962 India-China war, 1965 India-Pakistan war, and drought → 'plan holiday' 1966-69. Eleventh FYP (2007-12) achieved 7.9% growth.

18. The concept of 'disguised unemployment' is different from 'seasonal unemployment' in that:

  1. Disguised unemployment: excess labor with zero marginal product (mainly in agriculture, year-round). Seasonal unemployment: workers unemployed during off-season (only part of year)
  2. They are the same
  3. Seasonal is worse than disguised
  4. Disguised unemployment only in cities

Answer: Disguised unemployment: excess labor with zero marginal product (mainly in agriculture, year-round). Seasonal unemployment: workers unemployed during off-season (only part of year)

Explanation: Disguised Unemployment (structural): workers appear employed but contribute zero output (farm has 5 workers when 3 suffice). Zero marginal product. Removing 2 workers doesn't reduce output. Seasonal Unemployment: workers unemployed part of year (farmers during off-season, tourism workers in lean season). Cyclic unemployment: due to recession. Frictional: between jobs.

19. The Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) scheme provides:

  1. Package of 6 services to children 0-6 years and pregnant/lactating mothers: supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-up, referral services, pre-school education, nutrition & health education
  2. Only food to children
  3. Only education
  4. Only vaccination

Answer: Package of 6 services to children 0-6 years and pregnant/lactating mothers: supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-up, referral services, pre-school education, nutrition & health education

Explanation: ICDS (1975): world's largest and most comprehensive early childhood development program. 6 services through Anganwadi Centres: (1) Supplementary nutrition, (2) Immunization, (3) Health check-up, (4) Referral services, (5) Pre-school non-formal education (3-6 years), (6) Nutrition & health education (for women). 1.4 million Anganwadis, 14 crore beneficiaries.

20. India's MUDRA loan scheme has three categories named after:

  1. Three stages of business growth: Shishu (baby — up to Rs 50,000 for new/small businesses), Kishore (adolescent — Rs 50,000-5 lakh, for established micro units), Tarun (young adult — Rs 5-10 lakh, for medium micro units)
  2. Three rivers
  3. Three goddesses
  4. Three states of India

Answer: Three stages of business growth: Shishu (baby — up to Rs 50,000 for new/small businesses), Kishore (adolescent — Rs 50,000-5 lakh, for established micro units), Tarun (young adult — Rs 5-10 lakh, for medium micro units)

Explanation: MUDRA (Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency) loan categories: Shishu (Rs 50,000): startups, very small; Kishore (Rs 50k-5 lakh): established small businesses; Tarun (Rs 5-10 lakh): medium micro units. Tarun Plus (2024): up to Rs 20 lakh for Tarun category with good track record. All collateral-free. Interest rates typically 10-12%.

21. The index 'Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)' measures poverty using:

  1. Three dimensions: health (nutrition, child mortality), education (years of schooling, school attendance), and living standards (cooking fuel, sanitation, water, electricity, housing, assets) — UNDP/OPHI
  2. Only income below $1.90/day
  3. Only calorie intake
  4. Only income inequality

Answer: Three dimensions: health (nutrition, child mortality), education (years of schooling, school attendance), and living standards (cooking fuel, sanitation, water, electricity, housing, assets) — UNDP/OPHI

Explanation: MPI (UNDP and OPHI — Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative): multidimensional poverty measure using 10 indicators across 3 dimensions. A household is 'deprived' in each indicator; 'multidimensionally poor' if deprived in >33% of weighted indicators. India's MPI: 415 million exited poverty 2005-06 to 2019-21 (massive reduction). India publishes National MPI annually.

22. The term 'terms of trade' in international economics refers to:

  1. Ratio of export price index to import price index — if terms of trade improve (exports become relatively more expensive), country can import more with same exports; important for commodity exporters
  2. Trade agreements
  3. Trade volumes
  4. Trade deficits

Answer: Ratio of export price index to import price index — if terms of trade improve (exports become relatively more expensive), country can import more with same exports; important for commodity exporters

Explanation: Terms of Trade (ToT) = (Export Price Index / Import Price Index) × 100. If ToT rises: country's exports buy more imports → welfare improves. If ToT falls: exports buy fewer imports → welfare falls. Important for developing countries exporting primary commodities (prices volatile, tend to fall relative to manufactured goods — Prebisch-Singer hypothesis). India: ToT typically negative due to oil imports.

23. India's 'One Nation One Ration Card' (ONORC) scheme allows:

  1. PDS (ration card) beneficiaries to claim their subsidized food entitlement from any Fair Price Shop in India — especially beneficial for migrant workers who move to different states for work
  2. Only local ration card use
  3. Multiple ration cards
  4. Free food without card

Answer: PDS (ration card) beneficiaries to claim their subsidized food entitlement from any Fair Price Shop in India — especially beneficial for migrant workers who move to different states for work

Explanation: ONORC (2019-2021, nationwide rollout): using Aadhaar-linked biometric authentication at FPS (Fair Price Shop), beneficiary can claim PDS quota from any FPS in India. Critical for: migrant workers (can claim entitlement at destination state), seasonal migrants. All 36 states/UTs joined ONORC by 2021. 80+ crore beneficiaries. Managed through Integrated Management of PDS (IM-PDS) portal.

24. The Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS) provides:

  1. Rs 945 crore seed funding to early-stage startups through incubators — for proof of concept, prototype development, trials, market entry; Max Rs 20 lakh grant or Rs 50 lakh soft loan per startup
  2. Venture capital for large startups
  3. Government equity in startups
  4. Export subsidies for startups

Answer: Rs 945 crore seed funding to early-stage startups through incubators — for proof of concept, prototype development, trials, market entry; Max Rs 20 lakh grant or Rs 50 lakh soft loan per startup

Explanation: SISFS (2021-25): Rs 945 crore over 4 years. Grants through selected incubators: Rs 20 lakh for proof of concept/validation (PoC, prototype, trials); Rs 50 lakh as soft loan for commercialization/market entry. Incubators apply to DPIIT; approved incubators select startups. Fills the gap between angel investment and formal VC: 'seed funding' stage.

25. Frictional unemployment occurs when:

  1. The economy is in recession
  2. Workers are between jobs or searching for better jobs
  3. Jobs exist but skills don't match
  4. Seasonal work ends

Answer: Workers are between jobs or searching for better jobs

Explanation: Frictional unemployment: temporary unemployment while workers transition between jobs or search for better ones. It's natural and exists even in a healthy economy (part of 'natural rate of unemployment').
