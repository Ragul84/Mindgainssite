26. Priority Sector Lending (PSL) requires Indian commercial banks to lend what percentage of ANBC to priority sectors?

  1. 25%
  2. 30%
  3. 40%
  4. 50%

Answer: 40%

Explanation: PSL target: 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) for domestic scheduled commercial banks. Priority sectors: Agriculture (18%), MSMEs, Education, Housing, Weaker Sections (10%).

27. Arthur Lewis's Dual Economy model describes development as movement of labour from:

  1. Urban to rural sector
  2. Traditional agricultural to modern industrial sector
  3. Informal to formal economy
  4. Agriculture to services

Answer: Traditional agricultural to modern industrial sector

Explanation: Lewis (1954): unlimited labour supply in subsistence agriculture moves to the industrial/modern sector at a subsistence wage, fueling industrial growth until wages equalize.

28. The Agriculture Produce Market Committee (APMC) Act was designed to:

  1. Export agricultural produce
  2. Protect farmers by regulating markets and preventing exploitation by middlemen
  3. Provide crop insurance
  4. Promote organic farming

Answer: Protect farmers by regulating markets and preventing exploitation by middlemen

Explanation: APMC Acts (state subject) created regulated mandis to ensure fair prices for farmers. Critics say they create cartels. The three Farm Acts 2020 (later repealed) aimed to allow sales outside APMCs.

29. E-NAM (electronic National Agriculture Market) is a pan-India online trading platform launched in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2016
  3. 2018
  4. 2020

Answer: 2016

Explanation: e-NAM launched April 14, 2016 by SFAC (Small Farmers Agribusiness Consortium) under Ministry of Agriculture. Connects APMC mandis online for transparent price discovery.

30. The Soil Health Card scheme launched in 2015 aims to:

  1. Subsidize fertilizers
  2. Provide farmers with soil nutrient status and fertilizer recommendations
  3. Promote organic certification
  4. Map agricultural land via satellite

Answer: Provide farmers with soil nutrient status and fertilizer recommendations

Explanation: Soil Health Card: issued every 2 years to farmers; provides information on 12 parameters (N, P, K, pH, micronutrients) with crop-wise recommendations to optimize fertilizer use.

31. Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme provides short-term credit to farmers for:

  1. Land purchase only
  2. Purchase of farm machinery only
  3. Crop cultivation, post-harvest expenses, maintenance, and allied activities
  4. Long-term investment in irrigation

Answer: Crop cultivation, post-harvest expenses, maintenance, and allied activities

Explanation: KCC (1998, NABARD): provides revolving credit for crop cultivation, post-harvest expenses, allied activities (fisheries, animal husbandry since 2018-19), and maintenance needs at subsidized rates.

32. India's first Five Year Plan (1951-56) primarily focused on:

  1. Heavy industrialization
  2. Agricultural development and rehabilitation of refugees
  3. Export promotion
  4. Social sector development

Answer: Agricultural development and rehabilitation of refugees

Explanation: First FYP (1951-56): Harrod-Domar model; focus on agriculture (after 1943 Bengal famine memories), power, transport, rehabilitation of 1947 partition refugees. Bhakra Nangal dam a key project. Exceeded growth targets.

33. The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) is a scheme for:

  1. Crop insurance — provides financial support to farmers suffering crop loss due to natural calamities, pests, or disease; launched 2016, low premium (2% for kharif, 1.5% rabi)
  2. Free seeds to farmers
  3. Minimum support price guarantee
  4. Agricultural credit

Answer: Crop insurance — provides financial support to farmers suffering crop loss due to natural calamities, pests, or disease; launched 2016, low premium (2% for kharif, 1.5% rabi)

Explanation: PMFBY (February 2016): replaced NAIS and MNAIS. Features: uniform premium of 2% for kharif crops, 1.5% for rabi crops, 5% for commercial/horticultural crops; remaining premium paid by Centre and State equally. Uses satellite, remote sensing, and smartphone apps for rapid loss assessment. Covers pre-sowing to post-harvest losses.

34. Disguised unemployment is most commonly found in:

  1. Agricultural sector — more workers than needed; marginal productivity of labor is zero; removing them does not reduce output
  2. Manufacturing sector
  3. Services sector
  4. Government sector

Answer: Agricultural sector — more workers than needed; marginal productivity of labor is zero; removing them does not reduce output

Explanation: Disguised (concealed) unemployment: more people employed than necessary; additional workers add nothing to output (zero marginal product). Common in Indian agriculture (joint farming by family members). Unlike open unemployment (person seeks work but can't find it), disguised unemployment involves apparently 'working' people.

35. India's agricultural marketing is regulated through:

  1. State-level APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) Acts — mandated that farmers sell through regulated mandis; APMC reforms (Model APMC Act 2003) and Farm Laws 2020 (repealed 2021) aimed to allow direct selling
  2. Central government alone
  3. RBI
  4. NABARD directly

Answer: State-level APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) Acts — mandated that farmers sell through regulated mandis; APMC reforms (Model APMC Act 2003) and Farm Laws 2020 (repealed 2021) aimed to allow direct selling

Explanation: APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) system: most states have APMCs that regulate agricultural markets (mandis). Farmers had to sell produce only through licensed commission agents in regulated markets. Critiqued for middleman exploitation. Model APMC Act 2003: allow private markets, direct farmer-buyer links. Farm Laws 2020 (allowing direct selling, contract farming) were repealed November 2021 after farmer protests.

36. The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) launched in 2016 replaced which earlier crop insurance schemes?

  1. RKVY and NAIS
  2. NAIS (National Agricultural Insurance Scheme) and MNAIS
  3. PM-KISAN and RKVY
  4. PMRY and NAIS

Answer: NAIS (National Agricultural Insurance Scheme) and MNAIS

Explanation: PMFBY (January 2016): replaced NAIS (1999) and MNAIS (2010). Premium rates: Kharif 2%, Rabi 1.5%, commercial/horticultural crops 5%. Actuarial premium difference borne by Centre and State.
