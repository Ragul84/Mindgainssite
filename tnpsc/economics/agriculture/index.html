Agriculture MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Agriculture questions to revise the TNPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 581 questions.

1. The 'Green Revolution' in India was associated with:

  1. Increase in forest cover
  2. High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds for food grains (wheat and rice)
  3. Development of renewable energy
  4. Growth of vegetable production

Answer: High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds for food grains (wheat and rice)

Explanation: The Green Revolution (1960s-70s) introduced HYV seeds (especially wheat by Norman Borlaug and M.S. Swaminathan), modern irrigation, and fertilisers, leading to a dramatic increase in food grain production.

2. NABARD stands for:

  1. National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development
  2. National Banking Authority for Regional Development
  3. New Agricultural Banking Reserve Department
  4. National Association for Banking and Rural Districts

Answer: National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development

Explanation: NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) was established in 1982. It provides credit for agricultural and rural development and supervises cooperative banks and RRBs.

3. The First Five Year Plan of India (1951-56) focused on:

  1. Agriculture, irrigation, and power (dams: Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Damodar Valley); based on Harrod-Domar growth model
  2. Heavy industrialization
  3. IT sector
  4. Infrastructure only

Answer: Agriculture, irrigation, and power (dams: Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Damodar Valley); based on Harrod-Domar growth model

Explanation: First FYP (1951-56): priority on agriculture, irrigation, and power generation to overcome food shortages and post-partition economic disruption. Planned outlay: Rs 2,069 crore. Major projects: Bhakra-Nangal (Punjab), Damodar Valley Corporation, Hirakud Dam. Also focused on land reforms.

4. The Minimum Support Price (MSP) is:

  1. Government-guaranteed price for agricultural produce — ensures farmers get fair price and are not exploited by market forces; recommended by CACP
  2. Market price of commodities
  3. Minimum wage for agricultural workers
  4. Tax on agricultural income

Answer: Government-guaranteed price for agricultural produce — ensures farmers get fair price and are not exploited by market forces; recommended by CACP

Explanation: MSP: announced by Central Government (based on CACP — Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices recommendations) before sowing season. Government agencies (FCI, NAFED) purchase crops at MSP if market price falls below it. MSP for 23 major crops including paddy, wheat, pulses, oilseeds, and cotton. Not legally binding on private buyers.

5. The NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) was established in:

  1. 1982 — apex bank for agricultural and rural credit; provides refinance to cooperative banks, RRBs, and commercial banks for agricultural loans
  2. 1935
  3. 1969
  4. 2000

Answer: 1982 — apex bank for agricultural and rural credit; provides refinance to cooperative banks, RRBs, and commercial banks for agricultural loans

Explanation: NABARD (est. July 12, 1982) based on B. Sivaraman Committee recommendations. HQ: Mumbai. Functions: refinancing apex cooperative banks, RRBs (Regional Rural Banks), and commercial banks for agriculture/rural development; rural infrastructure development (RIDF); watershed development; Self-Help Group (SHG) - Bank Linkage Programme.

6. Bank nationalization in India (major phase) occurred in:

  1. 1969 — PM Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 major private banks (those with deposits > Rs 50 crore); second phase 1980: 6 more banks nationalized
  2. 1947
  3. 1991
  4. 1935

Answer: 1969 — PM Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 major private banks (those with deposits > Rs 50 crore); second phase 1980: 6 more banks nationalized

Explanation: Bank Nationalization 1969: PM Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 private banks with deposits over Rs 50 crore (Central Bank, Bank of India, Canara Bank, etc.) to direct credit to priority sectors (agriculture, small industry). Second phase 1980: 6 more nationalized. SBI: nationalized 1955 (formerly Imperial Bank). RBI: 1949.

7. The Eleventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution lists subjects under:

  1. Panchayati Raj institutions (29 subjects including agriculture, land, roads, drinking water, education, poverty alleviation) after 73rd Amendment 1992
  2. State legislature subjects
  3. Union List subjects
  4. Concurrent List subjects

Answer: Panchayati Raj institutions (29 subjects including agriculture, land, roads, drinking water, education, poverty alleviation) after 73rd Amendment 1992

Explanation: 73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992): gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj (3-tier local self-government). 11th Schedule: 29 subjects devolved to panchayats including: agriculture, land reforms, rural electrification, water management, primary education, primary health, roads, poverty alleviation. States choose which subjects to actually transfer.

8. The Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) is a scheme for:

  1. Skill development — free short-term training to youth in industry-relevant skills; recognition of prior learning (RPL); National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) implements it
  2. Mid-day meals
  3. Employment guarantee
  4. Housing for youth

Answer: Skill development — free short-term training to youth in industry-relevant skills; recognition of prior learning (RPL); National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) implements it

Explanation: PMKVY (2015): flagship skill development scheme under Skill India Mission. Free short-term training across 300+ job roles in 38 sectors (construction, IT, healthcare, agriculture, etc.). On successful training + assessment: placement assistance and Rs 8,000 reward. PMKVY 4.0: focus on Industry 4.0 skills, digital and green jobs.

9. The Asian Development Bank (ADB) is headquartered in:

  1. Manila, Philippines — established 1966; provides loans/technical assistance to developing Asian countries; India is the largest borrower from ADB
  2. Tokyo
  3. Singapore
  4. Beijing

Answer: Manila, Philippines — established 1966; provides loans/technical assistance to developing Asian countries; India is the largest borrower from ADB

Explanation: ADB (Asian Development Bank, 1966): HQ in Manila, Philippines. 68 member countries (49 from Asia-Pacific). Provides: project loans, policy-based loans, technical assistance, equity investments. India is one of ADB's largest borrowers (infrastructure, urban development, agriculture). USA and Japan are largest shareholders.

10. NABARD's function of refinancing is:

  1. Providing funds to cooperative banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and commercial banks against their agriculture/rural lending portfolio — enabling them to extend more credit to farmers
  2. Providing loans directly to farmers only
  3. Setting agricultural prices
  4. Managing FCI stocks

Answer: Providing funds to cooperative banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and commercial banks against their agriculture/rural lending portfolio — enabling them to extend more credit to farmers

Explanation: NABARD refinancing: Short-term credit (seasonal agricultural operations: crop loans) and Long-term credit (land development, farm machinery, irrigation) to: District/State Cooperative Banks, Regional Rural Banks, Commercial Banks. NABARD also gives direct loans to state governments (RIDF — Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) for rural projects.

11. The Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) was launched to:

  1. Incentivize states to increase investment in agriculture — 11th Plan scheme with 100% Central funding for states that increased agriculture budget share; restructured in 2017 as RAFTAAR
  2. Provide crop insurance
  3. Fix MSP
  4. Export agricultural produce

Answer: Incentivize states to increase investment in agriculture — 11th Plan scheme with 100% Central funding for states that increased agriculture budget share; restructured in 2017 as RAFTAAR

Explanation: RKVY (Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana, 2007): launched under 11th Five Year Plan. States qualifying (those increasing agriculture budget share above 3-year moving average) got 100% central grants for agriculture projects. States had flexibility. Restructured in 2017 as RKVY-RAFTAAR (Remunerative Approaches for Agriculture and Allied sector Rejuvenation).

12. The Agri-Stack or Digital Agriculture Mission aims to:

  1. Create a digital public infrastructure (DPI) for agriculture — farmer registry, crop sown registry, farm boundaries using satellite/GIS to enable precision farming and targeted subsidies
  2. Replace all agricultural workers with robots
  3. Automate MSP procurement
  4. Create digital agricultural exports only

Answer: Create a digital public infrastructure (DPI) for agriculture — farmer registry, crop sown registry, farm boundaries using satellite/GIS to enable precision farming and targeted subsidies

Explanation: Digital Agriculture Mission / Agri-Stack (2021-2025): federated farmer database (Farmer ID linked to land records, Aadhaar), Crop Sown Registry (what crops are grown, digitized land records), Remote Sensing and GIS maps of farm plots. Enables: targeted input delivery (seeds, fertilizer), precision advisory (weather, crop insurance, agri-credit).

13. Priority Sector Lending (PSL) norms require banks to lend:

  1. 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors — agriculture (18%), MSMEs, education, housing, renewable energy, social infrastructure, weaker sections
  2. 50% to agriculture only
  3. No specific requirement
  4. Only to government projects

Answer: 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors — agriculture (18%), MSMEs, education, housing, renewable energy, social infrastructure, weaker sections

Explanation: PSL: RBI mandates Scheduled Commercial Banks lend 40% of ANBC to priority sectors. Subsectors: Agriculture (18% of ANBC, of which 8% for small/marginal farmers), MSMEs, Education, Housing (affordable), Renewable Energy, Social Infrastructure. Foreign banks with <20 branches: 40% (different sub-targets). Priority Sector Lending Certificates (PSLCs) allow trading PSL shortfalls/surplus among banks.

14. The Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme was launched to:

  1. Provide timely and adequate credit to farmers for agricultural needs (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, post-harvest) at subsidized interest rates (7% with 3% interest subvention for prompt repayment)
  2. Only for tractor purchase
  3. Personal loans for farmers
  4. Export finance for farmers

Answer: Provide timely and adequate credit to farmers for agricultural needs (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, post-harvest) at subsidized interest rates (7% with 3% interest subvention for prompt repayment)

Explanation: KCC (1998, based on RV Gupta Committee): short-term revolving credit facility for: crop cultivation expenses, post-harvest expenses, farm maintenance, allied activities, consumption needs. Interest: 7% p.a. (government subvention of 3% for prompt payment → effective 4%). Credit limit: crop loan + 10% for post-harvest + 20% for farm maintenance + 10% insurance. Now being made digital (Kisan Credit Card via mobile app).

15. India's 'Silent Revolution' in agriculture refers to:

  1. Second Green Revolution for eastern India (Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, UP) and diversification into horticulture, animal husbandry, and fisheries; also called 'Evergreen Revolution' by M.S. Swaminathan
  2. Original Green Revolution
  3. White Revolution
  4. Blue Revolution

Answer: Second Green Revolution for eastern India (Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, UP) and diversification into horticulture, animal husbandry, and fisheries; also called 'Evergreen Revolution' by M.S. Swaminathan

Explanation: Second Green Revolution/Evergreen Revolution (M.S. Swaminathan): need to extend agricultural productivity gains to eastern India and dryland farming regions while maintaining ecological sustainability. Also: shift from cereal monoculture to diversification (horticulture, dairying, poultry, fisheries) for nutritional security and farmer income.

16. India's 'Unified Portal for Agricultural Statistics' (UPAg) was launched to:

  1. Provide real-time agricultural data — crop sowing area, production estimates, MSP, market prices — to farmers, policymakers, and researchers via a single platform
  2. Replace FCI
  3. Automate MSP procurement
  4. Provide agri loans directly

Answer: Provide real-time agricultural data — crop sowing area, production estimates, MSP, market prices — to farmers, policymakers, and researchers via a single platform

Explanation: UPAg (2022, Ministry of Agriculture): first-of-its-kind single platform integrating agricultural statistics from multiple agencies. Data: crop sowing/area, production, trade, price (wholesale, retail, MSP), input costs, import/export. Makes data accessible to farmers via mobile app. Aims to improve crop planning and reduce information asymmetry.

17. The term 'crop diversification' in agriculture means:

  1. Shifting from monoculture to growing multiple crops including horticulture, spices, oilseeds, pulses — reduces risk, improves nutrition, and can increase farmer income compared to wheat/rice only
  2. Growing only wheat and rice
  3. Farming in multiple countries
  4. Diversification of farm ownership

Answer: Shifting from monoculture to growing multiple crops including horticulture, spices, oilseeds, pulses — reduces risk, improves nutrition, and can increase farmer income compared to wheat/rice only

Explanation: Crop Diversification: growing a variety of crops (different from traditional cereal monoculture). Benefits: reduces market and weather risk, improves soil health, nutrition security, higher value crops (horticulture → higher income than cereals). Government promotes with MSP for pulses and oilseeds, cluster development programs, and horticulture development programs.

18. The 'double cropping' system in agriculture refers to:

  1. Growing two crops per year on the same piece of land — kharif (June-November, monsoon crop: paddy, cotton, maize) + rabi (November-April, winter crop: wheat, mustard, chickpea)
  2. Growing crops in two countries
  3. Two varieties of same crop simultaneously
  4. Mixed cropping of two types

Answer: Growing two crops per year on the same piece of land — kharif (June-November, monsoon crop: paddy, cotton, maize) + rabi (November-April, winter crop: wheat, mustard, chickpea)

Explanation: Double Cropping: two crops sequentially in a year. India's cropping seasons: Kharif (sown with SW monsoon, harvested Oct-Nov): paddy, cotton, maize, soybean, groundnut. Rabi (sown Nov, harvested April): wheat, mustard, chickpea, barley. Zaid (short summer season, March-June): watermelon, cucumber, vegetables. Irrigation enables double/triple cropping (Punjab, Haryana, UP).

19. The 'production credit' versus 'investment credit' in agricultural finance:

  1. Production credit: short-term (crop loans for seeds, fertilizer, labour via KCC, 1 season); Investment credit: long-term (land development, irrigation, farm equipment, 3-7 years) — both needed for agriculture
  2. Both are same type
  3. Investment credit is not available in India
  4. Production credit only exists in India

Answer: Production credit: short-term (crop loans for seeds, fertilizer, labour via KCC, 1 season); Investment credit: long-term (land development, irrigation, farm equipment, 3-7 years) — both needed for agriculture

Explanation: Agricultural credit types: Short-term/production credit: crop loans for seasonal inputs (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, labour) — via KCC, cooperative banks, MFIs. Long-term/investment credit: land development, irrigation installation, tractors, farm machinery, orchards — 3-7 years. NABARD refinances both types through banks and cooperatives.

20. The Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM-AASHA) scheme comprises:

  1. Three sub-schemes: Price Support Scheme (PSS), Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS), and Private Procurement & Stockist Scheme (PPSS) — to ensure farmers get MSP even in market
  2. Only MSP announcement
  3. Only crop insurance
  4. Only direct income support

Answer: Three sub-schemes: Price Support Scheme (PSS), Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS), and Private Procurement & Stockist Scheme (PPSS) — to ensure farmers get MSP even in market

Explanation: PM-AASHA (2018): umbrella scheme for MSP implementation. PSS: government agencies physically procure crops when prices fall below MSP (for oilseeds, pulses, copra). PDPS: farmers sell in market, government pays difference between MSP and market price directly (for oilseeds). PPSS: private traders can also procure at MSP after paying security deposit (pilot for oilseeds).

21. India's Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchai Yojana (PMKSY) aims to:

  1. Achieve 'Har Khet Ko Paani' (water to every field) and 'More Crop Per Drop' — through irrigation expansion, micro-irrigation (drip, sprinkler), and watershed development
  2. Only for surface water irrigation
  3. Only canal irrigation
  4. Flood control

Answer: Achieve 'Har Khet Ko Paani' (water to every field) and 'More Crop Per Drop' — through irrigation expansion, micro-irrigation (drip, sprinkler), and watershed development

Explanation: PMKSY (2015): convergence of water management programs. Components: AIBP (Accelerated Irrigation Benefits Programme — complete ongoing projects), Har Khet Ko Pani (new irrigation coverage), Per Drop More Crop (micro-irrigation: drip/sprinkler for water efficiency), Watershed Development, PM Krishi Sinchai Yojana-PDMC. India: 52% of net sown area is rain-fed — increasing irrigation coverage is critical.

22. India's Agriculture Export Policy 2018 targets:

  1. $60 billion agricultural exports by 2022 (now extended to 2025) from ~$30 billion baseline — through cluster-based approach, value addition, brand building (GI tags), and export infrastructure
  2. Only rice exports
  3. Only organic farming exports
  4. Import substitution

Answer: $60 billion agricultural exports by 2022 (now extended to 2025) from ~$30 billion baseline — through cluster-based approach, value addition, brand building (GI tags), and export infrastructure

Explanation: Agriculture Export Policy 2018 (Ministry of Commerce): targets doubling agri exports. Key features: Agri Export Zones (AEZ) concept, cluster-based approach (integrated 12 clusters initially), GI (Geographical Indication) products promotion (Darjeeling tea, Basmati rice, Alphonso mango), value addition promotion, infrastructure (cold chain, testing labs), e-commerce for agri exports.

23. The 'Unified Lending Interface' (ULI) is:

  1. RBIH's digital public infrastructure for credit — connects lenders with data sources (land records, crop data, GST, account aggregator) for consent-based credit assessment; especially for MSME/agri loans
  2. Unified payment interface (that's UPI)
  3. Platform for student loans only
  4. Government housing loan portal

Answer: RBIH's digital public infrastructure for credit — connects lenders with data sources (land records, crop data, GST, account aggregator) for consent-based credit assessment; especially for MSME/agri loans

Explanation: ULI (Unified Lending Interface): announced by RBI Governor (2023), developed by RBIH. Digital infrastructure connecting banks/NBFCs/lenders with multiple data sources (state land records, satellite crop data, milk pouring data, GSTN, Account Aggregator Framework) for seamless credit assessment. Particularly useful for MSME and agriculture loan underwriting (where formal income proofs are unavailable).

24. India's 'Agri-SURE' (Agriculture Startup Fund) was announced in:

  1. 2023 Union Budget — Rs 2,200 crore fund under NABARD for supporting agricultural startups, rural enterprises, and agri-tech companies in underserved areas
  2. 2015
  3. 2018
  4. 2010

Answer: 2023 Union Budget — Rs 2,200 crore fund under NABARD for supporting agricultural startups, rural enterprises, and agri-tech companies in underserved areas

Explanation: Agri SURE (Agriculture Scale-Up through Rural Enterprises) Fund: Rs 2,200 crore fund announced in Union Budget 2023-24. Managed by NABARD. Provides financial support to agricultural startups and rural enterprises particularly in farm equipment, precision agriculture, post-harvest management, and agri-tech. Complementary to Startup India and PMKSY.

25. The Green Revolution (1960s-70s) was associated with:

  1. Norman Borlaug (Nobel Peace Prize 1970) — developed semi-dwarf HYV wheat varieties; worked with CIMMYT; brought to India by M.S. Swaminathan and promoted by PM Indira Gandhi's government
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru only
  3. Planning Commission alone
  4. NABARD's initiative

Answer: Norman Borlaug (Nobel Peace Prize 1970) — developed semi-dwarf HYV wheat varieties; worked with CIMMYT; brought to India by M.S. Swaminathan and promoted by PM Indira Gandhi's government

Explanation: Green Revolution: Norman Borlaug (USA, Nobel 1970) developed semi-dwarf wheat varieties (higher yield, resistant to lodging — bending with wind). Introduced to India by M.S. Swaminathan (who worked with IARI) under Agriculture Minister C. Subramaniam and PM Indira Gandhi's government. Punjab/Haryana became wheat-surplus states. Pakistan, Mexico also benefited. Also: IR-8 'miracle rice' (IRRI, Philippines).
