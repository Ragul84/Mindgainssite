26. The telephone was invented by:

  1. Alexander Graham Bell (1876)
  2. Thomas Edison
  3. Guglielmo Marconi
  4. Nikola Tesla

Answer: Alexander Graham Bell (1876)

Explanation: Alexander Graham Bell patented the telephone on March 7, 1876. He made the first telephone call on March 10, 1876. However, Antonio Meucci had developed a similar device earlier (officially recognized by US Congress in 2002).

27. Bleaching powder is:

  1. Calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)Cl) — a mixture of CaOCl₂
  2. Calcium carbonate
  3. Sodium hypochlorite
  4. Chlorine gas

Answer: Calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)Cl) — a mixture of CaOCl₂

Explanation: Bleaching powder (Chloride of lime) is Ca(OCl)Cl — made by passing chlorine over slaked lime. It releases chlorine on contact with water/acids, which bleaches cotton fabrics and disinfects water. Used in swimming pools.

28. Plaster of Paris is:

  1. Calcium sulphate hemihydrate (CaSO₄·½H₂O)
  2. Calcium carbonate
  3. Calcium chloride
  4. Calcium hydroxide

Answer: Calcium sulphate hemihydrate (CaSO₄·½H₂O)

Explanation: Plaster of Paris (POP) is calcium sulphate hemihydrate (CaSO₄·½H₂O). Made by heating gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) at ~120°C. On adding water, it sets into hard gypsum — used in sculpting, casting, and medical plaster.

29. Deficiency of Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) causes:

  1. Beriberi (affects nervous system and heart)
  2. Scurvy
  3. Rickets
  4. Night blindness

Answer: Beriberi (affects nervous system and heart)

Explanation: Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) deficiency causes Beriberi — 'Wet Beriberi' (affects cardiovascular system, oedema) and 'Dry Beriberi' (peripheral neuropathy). Common in populations whose diet is mainly polished white rice.

30. Deficiency of Vitamin B3 (Niacin) causes:

  1. Pellagra (4Ds: Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia, Death)
  2. Beriberi
  3. Rickets
  4. Anaemia

Answer: Pellagra (4Ds: Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia, Death)

Explanation: Vitamin B3 (Niacin) deficiency causes Pellagra — characterized by the '4Ds': Dermatitis (skin rash), Diarrhoea, Dementia, and Death if untreated. Common in maize-based diets (maize lacks available niacin).

31. Vitamin B12 deficiency causes:

  1. Pernicious anaemia (megaloblastic anaemia) and nerve damage
  2. Beriberi
  3. Scurvy
  4. Rickets

Answer: Pernicious anaemia (megaloblastic anaemia) and nerve damage

Explanation: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) deficiency causes pernicious anaemia (megaloblastic anaemia — large, abnormal red blood cells) and peripheral neuropathy. Found only in animal products — vegans are at risk.

32. Iodine deficiency causes:

  1. Goitre (enlarged thyroid gland) and cretinism (in newborns)
  2. Anaemia
  3. Night blindness
  4. Rickets

Answer: Goitre (enlarged thyroid gland) and cretinism (in newborns)

Explanation: Iodine is needed to make thyroid hormones (T3, T4). Deficiency causes Goitre (enlarged thyroid). In pregnant women, iodine deficiency causes cretinism (intellectual disability) in newborns. Iodised salt prevents deficiency.

33. Mitosis results in:

  1. Two identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as parent (2n → 2n)
  2. Four cells with half chromosomes
  3. Gametes
  4. Genetically varied cells

Answer: Two identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as parent (2n → 2n)

Explanation: Mitosis: cell division producing 2 identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number (2n). Used for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. PMAT phases: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.

34. Meiosis results in:

  1. Four genetically diverse haploid cells (2n → 4 cells of n)
  2. Two identical diploid cells
  3. Two haploid cells
  4. Four identical cells

Answer: Four genetically diverse haploid cells (2n → 4 cells of n)

Explanation: Meiosis: two successive cell divisions producing 4 haploid (n) cells (gametes) from one diploid (2n) parent cell. Genetic variation through crossing-over (prophase I) and independent assortment. Meiosis I and Meiosis II.

35. X-rays were discovered by:

  1. Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen (1895)
  2. Marie Curie
  3. Ernest Rutherford
  4. Henri Becquerel

Answer: Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen (1895)

Explanation: X-rays were discovered by Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen on 8 November 1895. He won the first Nobel Prize in Physics (1901) for this discovery. X-rays penetrate soft tissue but are absorbed by dense materials like bone.

36. CT scan (Computed Tomography) uses:

  1. X-rays from multiple angles to create 3D images of internal body structures
  2. Magnetic fields
  3. Ultrasound
  4. Radioactive isotopes

Answer: X-rays from multiple angles to create 3D images of internal body structures

Explanation: CT (Computed Tomography / CAT scan): X-ray beams rotate around the body from multiple angles. Computer compiles cross-sectional 2D images to create detailed 3D images of organs, tissues, and bones.

37. PET scan (Positron Emission Tomography) uses:

  1. Radioactive tracers (usually FDG — fluorodeoxyglucose) to image metabolic activity
  2. X-rays
  3. Ultrasound
  4. Magnetic fields only

Answer: Radioactive tracers (usually FDG — fluorodeoxyglucose) to image metabolic activity

Explanation: PET scan uses radioactive tracers (e.g., FDG — a radioactive glucose). Active cells (especially cancer cells) take up more glucose. Positrons emitted by the tracer produce gamma rays detected to create functional images.

38. The 10% rule in ecology states:

  1. Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next
  2. 10% of food is wasted
  3. 10% of species survive
  4. Energy is transferred 100% but 10% is heat

Answer: Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next

Explanation: 10% Rule (Lindemann, 1942): only about 10% of energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next level (90% is lost as heat, excretion, respiration). This limits food chain length to about 4-5 levels.

39. The planet closest to the Sun is:

  1. Mercury
  2. Venus
  3. Earth
  4. Mars

Answer: Mercury

Explanation: Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun (average 57.9 million km). It is also the smallest planet. Despite being closest, Venus is hotter due to its thick CO₂ atmosphere (greenhouse effect) — Mercury has no atmosphere.

40. Infrared radiation is used in:

  1. TV remote controls, night-vision cameras, thermal imaging, and heat lamps
  2. X-ray machines
  3. Gamma ray therapy
  4. UV sterilization

Answer: TV remote controls, night-vision cameras, thermal imaging, and heat lamps

Explanation: Infrared (IR) radiation is used in TV/AC remotes (near-IR), night-vision equipment (thermal cameras detect body heat), physiotherapy heat lamps, and food warmers. IR thermometers measure temperature without contact.

41. An exothermic reaction is one that:

  1. Releases heat energy to surroundings (ΔH < 0)
  2. Absorbs heat from surroundings
  3. Requires light energy
  4. Proceeds only at high temperature

Answer: Releases heat energy to surroundings (ΔH < 0)

Explanation: Exothermic reactions release heat (negative enthalpy change, ΔH < 0). Examples: combustion of fuels, respiration, neutralization (acid + base), rusting of iron, formation of water. The surroundings feel warmer.

42. The chemical formula of common table salt is:

  1. NaCl
  2. NaHCO₃
  3. Na₂CO₃
  4. NaOH

Answer: NaCl

Explanation: Table salt is sodium chloride (NaCl) — ionic compound formed from Na⁺ and Cl⁻. It is produced by evaporation of seawater or mining rock salt. Tamil Nadu (Tuticorin) is a major salt producer.

43. Bakelite (a thermosetting plastic) is used for:

  1. Electrical switch boards, handles, and early telephone casings — it does not melt on reheating
  2. Food containers
  3. Clothing
  4. Window panes

Answer: Electrical switch boards, handles, and early telephone casings — it does not melt on reheating

Explanation: Bakelite is the first synthetic polymer (Leo Baekeland, 1907) — phenol-formaldehyde resin. Being thermosetting, it does not soften on heating, making it ideal for electrical switches, handles, and insulating parts.

44. Antibodies are produced by:

  1. B-lymphocytes (B-cells) in response to antigens
  2. T-lymphocytes
  3. Red blood cells
  4. Platelets

Answer: B-lymphocytes (B-cells) in response to antigens

Explanation: B-lymphocytes (B-cells) produce antibodies (immunoglobulins) when stimulated by antigens. Antibodies are Y-shaped proteins that specifically bind to antigens and neutralize pathogens. T-cells coordinate the immune response.

45. The human body's first line of defense against pathogens includes:

  1. Skin, mucus membranes, tears (lysozyme), stomach acid (HCl), nasal hairs
  2. White blood cells
  3. Antibodies
  4. Lymph nodes

Answer: Skin, mucus membranes, tears (lysozyme), stomach acid (HCl), nasal hairs

Explanation: First line of defense (non-specific): intact skin (physical barrier), mucus traps pathogens, lysozyme in tears/saliva kills bacteria, HCl in stomach destroys pathogens, nasal cilia filter particles. Second line: phagocytes, fever, inflammation.

46. HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks which cells?

  1. CD4+ T-helper cells (T-lymphocytes) — destroying the immune system's coordination
  2. Red blood cells
  3. Platelets
  4. Liver cells

Answer: CD4+ T-helper cells (T-lymphocytes) — destroying the immune system's coordination

Explanation: HIV specifically targets CD4+ T-helper lymphocytes (T-cells), which coordinate immune responses. As T-cell count drops, the immune system weakens, leading to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) — susceptibility to opportunistic infections.

47. The enzyme pepsin digests proteins in the:

  1. Stomach (pepsin works in acidic conditions, pH 1.5-2, produced as inactive pepsinogen)
  2. Mouth
  3. Small intestine
  4. Large intestine

Answer: Stomach (pepsin works in acidic conditions, pH 1.5-2, produced as inactive pepsinogen)

Explanation: Pepsin is a protease enzyme that breaks proteins into peptides in the stomach. It is secreted as inactive pepsinogen by chief cells of the stomach; hydrochloric acid (from parietal cells) converts it to active pepsin.

48. Xylem and phloem are plant tissues that transport:

  1. Xylem: water and minerals (upward from roots); Phloem: sugars/food (bidirectionally)
  2. Xylem: food; Phloem: water
  3. Only water
  4. Only food

Answer: Xylem: water and minerals (upward from roots); Phloem: sugars/food (bidirectionally)

Explanation: Xylem (dead tissue) transports water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves (upward). Phloem (living tissue) transports photosynthetic products (mainly sucrose) from leaves to other parts — up or down (bidirectional transport).

49. The primary cause of acid rain is:

  1. Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from burning fossil fuels, reacting with water to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Methane
  4. Ammonia

Answer: Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from burning fossil fuels, reacting with water to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃

Explanation: Acid rain is caused by SO₂ and NOₓ emissions (from power plants, vehicles, industries) reacting with atmospheric water to form sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃). Normal rain pH ≈ 5.6; acid rain pH < 5.6. Damages buildings, forests, lakes.

50. Biomagnification refers to:

  1. Increasing concentration of toxic substances (DDT, mercury) at each higher trophic level in a food chain
  2. Reduction in biodiversity
  3. Increase in plant growth
  4. Decomposition process

Answer: Increasing concentration of toxic substances (DDT, mercury) at each higher trophic level in a food chain

Explanation: Biomagnification (bioaccumulation): non-biodegradable toxins (DDT, PCBs, mercury, lead) accumulate in fatty tissues and become more concentrated at each trophic level. Top predators (eagles, polar bears, humans) have the highest concentrations.
