Chemistry Acids MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Chemistry Acids questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 109 questions.

1. A solution with pH less than 7 is:

  1. Neutral
  2. Basic
  3. Acidic
  4. Salt

Answer: Acidic

Explanation: pH < 7 is acidic (e.g., vinegar, lemon juice). pH = 7 is neutral (water). pH > 7 is basic/alkaline (e.g., soap, NaOH).

2. Which gas is most abundant in Earth's atmosphere?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Argon
  4. Nitrogen

Answer: Nitrogen

Explanation: Nitrogen (N2) makes up about 78% of Earth's atmosphere, followed by oxygen (21%), and argon (0.9%).

3. Malaria is caused by:

  1. Bacteria
  2. Virus
  3. Protozoan (Plasmodium)
  4. Fungus

Answer: Protozoan (Plasmodium)

Explanation: Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites, which are protozoa. The parasite is transmitted to humans through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

4. Photosynthesis takes place in which organelle?

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Nucleus
  3. Chloroplast
  4. Ribosome

Answer: Chloroplast

Explanation: Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Only plant cells and some protists have chloroplasts.

5. Acids turn blue litmus paper:

  1. Green
  2. Red
  3. Yellow
  4. Colourless

Answer: Red

Explanation: Acids turn blue litmus paper red (and keep red litmus red). Bases turn red litmus blue.

6. Which scientist discovered the structure of DNA (Double Helix)?

  1. Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch
  2. Watson and Crick (with Franklin's X-ray data)
  3. Mendel and Darwin
  4. Hershey and Chase

Answer: Watson and Crick (with Franklin's X-ray data)

Explanation: James Watson and Francis Crick discovered the double helix structure of DNA in 1953, using X-ray crystallography data from Rosalind Franklin. They received the Nobel Prize in 1962.

7. The unit of pressure is:

  1. Pascal (Pa)
  2. Newton (N)
  3. Joule (J)
  4. Watt (W)

Answer: Pascal (Pa)

Explanation: The SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa). 1 Pa = 1 N/m². Atmospheric pressure at sea level is about 101,325 Pa (1 atm). Named after Blaise Pascal.

8. PH less than 7 indicates:

  1. Acidic solution
  2. Basic (alkaline) solution
  3. Neutral solution
  4. Saturated solution

Answer: Acidic solution

Explanation: pH scale: 0-7 = acidic (H⁺ > OH⁻), pH 7 = neutral (pure water), pH 7-14 = basic/alkaline (OH⁻ > H⁺). Strong acids have pH near 0; strong bases near 14. Lemon juice pH~2; blood pH~7.4.

9. The chemical formula of common salt (Sodium Chloride) is:

  1. NaCl
  2. NaOH
  3. Na₂CO₃
  4. HCl

Answer: NaCl

Explanation: Common salt is Sodium Chloride, formula NaCl. NaOH is Sodium Hydroxide (caustic soda). Na₂CO₃ is Sodium Carbonate (washing soda). HCl is Hydrochloric acid.

10. Baking soda (Sodium Bicarbonate) has the formula:

  1. NaHCO₃
  2. Na₂CO₃
  3. NaOH
  4. NaCl

Answer: NaHCO₃

Explanation: Baking soda is Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃). It is used in cooking (raising agent), fire extinguishers, and antacids. Na₂CO₃ is Washing soda (Sodium Carbonate). NaOH is Caustic soda.

11. The chemical name of vinegar is:

  1. Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) — dilute solution (5%)
  2. Citric acid
  3. Lactic acid
  4. Formic acid

Answer: Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) — dilute solution (5%)

Explanation: Vinegar is a dilute solution (5%) of acetic acid (CH₃COOH / ethanoic acid). It is produced by fermentation of ethanol. Used as a food preservative and condiment.

12. The acid present in the human stomach is:

  1. Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
  2. Sulfuric acid
  3. Nitric acid
  4. Acetic acid

Answer: Hydrochloric acid (HCl)

Explanation: The stomach produces Hydrochloric acid (HCl) to aid digestion. The pH of stomach acid is 1.5-3.5. The stomach lining is protected by mucus. Excess acid causes acidity/heartburn.

13. The most abundant gas in the Earth's atmosphere is:

  1. Nitrogen (about 78%)
  2. Oxygen (21%)
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Argon

Answer: Nitrogen (about 78%)

Explanation: Earth's atmosphere composition: Nitrogen (N₂) ~78%, Oxygen (O₂) ~21%, Argon ~0.9%, Carbon dioxide ~0.04%, and trace amounts of other gases. The 1% (besides N₂ and O₂) is mainly Argon.

14. DNA is found mainly in:

  1. Nucleus (also in mitochondria and chloroplasts)
  2. Ribosome
  3. Cell membrane
  4. Cytoplasm (all of it)

Answer: Nucleus (also in mitochondria and chloroplasts)

Explanation: DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) is mainly in the nucleus (chromosomes). Small amounts are also found in mitochondria (mtDNA) and chloroplasts (cpDNA). DNA carries the genetic information of an organism.

15. Radioactivity was discovered by:

  1. Henri Becquerel (1896)
  2. Marie Curie
  3. Ernest Rutherford
  4. Albert Einstein

Answer: Henri Becquerel (1896)

Explanation: Radioactivity was discovered by Henri Becquerel in 1896 when he found that uranium salts emitted penetrating radiation. Marie Curie and Pierre Curie subsequently discovered polonium and radium (1898).

16. Alpha particles are:

  1. Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2)
  2. Electrons
  3. Electromagnetic radiation
  4. Neutrons

Answer: Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2)

Explanation: Alpha particles (α) are helium-4 nuclei with 2 protons and 2 neutrons (charge +2). They have the greatest ionizing power but least penetrating power — stopped by a sheet of paper or a few cm of air.

17. A lever is a simple machine based on the principle of:

  1. Moments (torque) — force × distance from fulcrum
  2. Friction
  3. Gravity
  4. Pressure

Answer: Moments (torque) — force × distance from fulcrum

Explanation: A lever consists of a rigid bar pivoted at a fulcrum. Mechanical advantage = effort arm / load arm. Three classes of levers based on position of fulcrum, effort, and load. Examples: seesaw (1st), wheelbarrow (2nd), tweezers (3rd).

18. The process of absorption of water and mineral salts by roots from soil is called:

  1. Absorption (root uptake)
  2. Transpiration
  3. Osmosis only
  4. Diffusion only

Answer: Absorption (root uptake)

Explanation: Roots absorb water by osmosis through root hair cells (thin-walled extensions of root epidermal cells). Mineral salts are absorbed by active transport (against concentration gradient, using energy/ATP).

19. The process by which nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert atmospheric N₂ into ammonia (NH₃) is:

  1. Nitrogen fixation
  2. Nitrification
  3. Denitrification
  4. Ammonification

Answer: Nitrogen fixation

Explanation: Nitrogen fixation: conversion of atmospheric N₂ to NH₃ (ammonia) by nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium in legume root nodules; Azotobacter free-living). Plants then absorb nitrates (NO₃⁻) for protein synthesis.

20. Leguminous plants (beans, peas, groundnuts) improve soil fertility because:

  1. Rhizobium bacteria in their root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen
  2. They have deep roots
  3. They produce more organic matter
  4. They absorb fewer nutrients

Answer: Rhizobium bacteria in their root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen

Explanation: Rhizobium bacteria live symbiotically in root nodules of leguminous plants. They fix atmospheric N₂ into ammonia, which becomes available to plants as nitrates. After harvest, fixed nitrogen remains in the soil.

21. The study of insects is called:

  1. Entomology
  2. Ornithology
  3. Ichthyology
  4. Herpetology

Answer: Entomology

Explanation: Entomology is the study of insects. Ornithology = birds. Ichthyology = fish. Herpetology = reptiles and amphibians. Mammology = mammals. Malacology = mollusks.

22. Animals that maintain constant body temperature regardless of environment are called:

  1. Homeotherms (warm-blooded) — birds and mammals
  2. Poikilotherms (cold-blooded)
  3. Ectotherms
  4. Heterotherms

Answer: Homeotherms (warm-blooded) — birds and mammals

Explanation: Homeotherms (warm-blooded): birds and mammals maintain constant body temperature regardless of environment (using internal heat generation). Poikilotherms (cold-blooded): reptiles, fish, amphibians — body temp varies with environment.

23. The process of digestion begins in:

  1. Mouth (mechanical and chemical digestion begin here)
  2. Stomach
  3. Small intestine
  4. Esophagus

Answer: Mouth (mechanical and chemical digestion begin here)

Explanation: Digestion begins in the mouth: teeth mechanically break food, saliva (amylase enzyme) starts chemical digestion of starch. Food travels: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine.

24. The main function of the kidney is:

  1. Filtration of blood and excretion of urine (removing metabolic waste)
  2. Producing blood cells
  3. Digesting fats
  4. Producing hormones only

Answer: Filtration of blood and excretion of urine (removing metabolic waste)

Explanation: Kidneys filter blood, removing waste products (urea, uric acid, creatinine) and excess water/salts to form urine. Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons. They also regulate blood pressure (renin) and pH.

25. Which gland is called the 'master gland'?

  1. Pituitary gland
  2. Thyroid gland
  3. Adrenal gland
  4. Pancreas

Answer: Pituitary gland

Explanation: Pituitary gland (at the base of the brain) is the 'master gland' — it secretes hormones that control other endocrine glands (thyroid, adrenal, reproductive glands). Controlled by the hypothalamus.
