Physics Motion MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Physics Motion questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 70 questions.

1. What force keeps us on the ground?

  1. Magnetic force
  2. Friction
  3. Gravitational force
  4. Electrical force

Answer: Gravitational force

Explanation: Gravity (gravitational force) is the force of attraction between the Earth and all objects on it. It keeps us grounded.

2. Newton's Second Law of Motion states that Force = :

  1. Mass × Velocity
  2. Mass × Acceleration
  3. Velocity / Time
  4. Mass × Distance

Answer: Mass × Acceleration

Explanation: Newton's Second Law: F = ma (Force = Mass × Acceleration). Greater force = greater acceleration for the same mass.

3. The 'Law of Gravitation' was proposed by:

  1. Albert Einstein
  2. Galileo Galilei
  3. Isaac Newton
  4. Nicolaus Copernicus

Answer: Isaac Newton

Explanation: Sir Isaac Newton formulated the Universal Law of Gravitation (1687): F = Gm1m2/r², stating that every object attracts every other object with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

4. The SI unit of pressure is:

  1. Newton
  2. Pascal
  3. Joule
  4. Bar

Answer: Pascal

Explanation: The SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa), named after Blaise Pascal. 1 Pa = 1 Newton per square metre (N/m²).

5. Newton's First Law of Motion states that:

  1. A body at rest or uniform motion continues unless acted upon by an external force
  2. Force equals mass times acceleration
  3. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction
  4. Momentum is always conserved

Answer: A body at rest or uniform motion continues unless acted upon by an external force

Explanation: Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia): A body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force. This is why we feel a jerk when a bus suddenly brakes.

6. Newton's Second Law of Motion (F = ma) means:

  1. Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration
  2. Force is proportional to velocity
  3. Force is inversely proportional to mass
  4. All bodies accelerate at the same rate

Answer: Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration

Explanation: F = ma — the net force acting on a body equals the product of its mass and acceleration. A larger force produces greater acceleration; a larger mass requires more force to achieve the same acceleration.

7. Newton's Third Law of Motion states:

  1. For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction
  2. Objects fall at 9.8 m/s²
  3. F = ma
  4. Momentum is conserved

Answer: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction

Explanation: Newton's Third Law: For every action (force), there is an equal and opposite reaction. Example: rocket engines push exhaust gas backward (action), gas pushes rocket forward (reaction).

8. The SI unit of force is:

  1. Newton (N)
  2. Dyne
  3. Joule
  4. Pascal

Answer: Newton (N)

Explanation: The SI unit of force is Newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². 1 Newton is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². Named after Sir Isaac Newton.

9. The acceleration due to gravity on Earth is approximately:

  1. 9.8 m/s²
  2. 9.8 m/s
  3. 10 m/s²
  4. 9 m/s²

Answer: 9.8 m/s²

Explanation: The acceleration due to gravity (g) on Earth's surface is approximately 9.8 m/s² (often approximated as 10 m/s²). It varies slightly with latitude (less at equator, more at poles) and altitude (decreases with height).

10. Which law states that the total momentum of a closed system remains constant?

  1. Law of Conservation of Momentum
  2. Newton's Second Law
  3. Law of Conservation of Energy
  4. Archimedes' Principle

Answer: Law of Conservation of Momentum

Explanation: Law of Conservation of Momentum: The total linear momentum of a closed system (no external forces) remains constant. Total momentum before collision = Total momentum after collision.

11. Archimedes' Principle states that:

  1. A body immersed in fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of displaced fluid
  2. Pressure increases with depth
  3. Fluids flow from high to low pressure
  4. All bodies sink in water

Answer: A body immersed in fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of displaced fluid

Explanation: Archimedes' Principle: When a body is fully or partially submerged in a fluid, it experiences a buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced. This explains why ships float and hot air balloons rise.

12. Bernoulli's Principle states that:

  1. In a fluid, increased velocity causes decreased pressure
  2. Pressure increases with depth
  3. Buoyancy equals weight of displaced fluid
  4. Gases expand when heated

Answer: In a fluid, increased velocity causes decreased pressure

Explanation: Bernoulli's Principle: In a streamlined fluid flow, as velocity increases, pressure decreases. This explains lift in airplane wings, the Venturi effect, and why shower curtains are pulled inward.

13. Work done is measured in:

  1. Joules (J)
  2. Watts (W)
  3. Newton (N)
  4. Pascal (Pa)

Answer: Joules (J)

Explanation: Work = Force × Distance. The SI unit of work is Joule (J). 1 J = 1 N × 1 m. Work is done only when a force displaces an object in the direction of the force.

14. Black holes are formed when:

  1. Massive stars collapse after exhausting their nuclear fuel (core collapse → extreme gravity from which light cannot escape)
  2. Stars explode
  3. Two galaxies merge
  4. Planets are destroyed

Answer: Massive stars collapse after exhausting their nuclear fuel (core collapse → extreme gravity from which light cannot escape)

Explanation: Black holes form when massive stars (>20 solar masses) exhaust nuclear fuel and their cores collapse under gravity. The gravity is so strong that even light cannot escape (escape velocity > c). Detected through gravitational effects.

15. The pressure at which blood is pumped out of the heart is called:

  1. Blood pressure — systolic (contraction) over diastolic (relaxation), normal 120/80 mmHg
  2. Pulse rate
  3. Cardiac output
  4. Heart rate

Answer: Blood pressure — systolic (contraction) over diastolic (relaxation), normal 120/80 mmHg

Explanation: Blood pressure is the force exerted by blood on artery walls. Systolic pressure (top number) = when heart contracts (~120 mmHg). Diastolic pressure (bottom number) = when heart relaxes (~80 mmHg). High BP (hypertension) > 140/90 mmHg.

16. A lactometer is used to measure:

  1. The purity/density of milk (to detect adulteration with water)
  2. Temperature of milk
  3. Acidity of milk
  4. Fat content directly

Answer: The purity/density of milk (to detect adulteration with water)

Explanation: A lactometer measures the density (specific gravity) of milk. Pure milk has specific gravity ~1.028-1.033. When water is added, density decreases. It is based on Archimedes' principle — the lactometer floats at different levels depending on milk density.

17. A body in uniform circular motion has:

  1. Constant speed but changing velocity (centripetal acceleration directed toward center)
  2. Constant velocity
  3. Zero acceleration
  4. Increasing speed

Answer: Constant speed but changing velocity (centripetal acceleration directed toward center)

Explanation: In uniform circular motion, speed (magnitude of velocity) is constant but velocity direction continuously changes. This requires centripetal acceleration = v²/r directed toward the center. The centripetal force provides this acceleration (tension, gravity, friction, etc.).

18. The escape velocity from Earth's surface is approximately:

  1. 11.2 km/s (about 40,000 km/h) — minimum speed to escape Earth's gravitational field
  2. 7.9 km/s
  3. 3 km/s
  4. 30 km/s

Answer: 11.2 km/s (about 40,000 km/h) — minimum speed to escape Earth's gravitational field

Explanation: Escape velocity from Earth = √(2gR) ≈ 11.2 km/s. This is the minimum speed needed for an object to escape Earth's gravity without further propulsion. Orbital velocity (first cosmic velocity) ≈ 7.9 km/s for low Earth orbit.

19. Newton's Law of Gravitation states that gravitational force is:

  1. Directly proportional to product of masses and inversely proportional to square of distance (F = Gm₁m₂/r²)
  2. Equal to mass times acceleration
  3. Directly proportional to distance
  4. Inversely proportional to mass

Answer: Directly proportional to product of masses and inversely proportional to square of distance (F = Gm₁m₂/r²)

Explanation: Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation: F = Gm₁m₂/r², where G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² is the gravitational constant. The force is attractive, acts between all masses, and decreases with square of distance.

20. When a ball is thrown upward, at the highest point:

  1. Velocity = 0 but acceleration = g (9.8 m/s²) downward (still under gravity)
  2. Both velocity and acceleration = 0
  3. Only acceleration = 0
  4. Velocity is maximum

Answer: Velocity = 0 but acceleration = g (9.8 m/s²) downward (still under gravity)

Explanation: At the highest point of projectile motion, the vertical velocity component = 0 (ball momentarily stops going up). However, gravitational acceleration (g = 9.8 m/s² downward) acts at all times — it is g that decelerates the ball going up and accelerates it coming down.

21. A pulley is used to:

  1. Change the direction of force and/or reduce the effort needed to lift a load (mechanical advantage)
  2. Increase speed only
  3. Store energy
  4. Convert heat to work

Answer: Change the direction of force and/or reduce the effort needed to lift a load (mechanical advantage)

Explanation: A single fixed pulley changes the direction of force (effort direction changes, but magnitude stays same — no mechanical advantage). A movable pulley halves the effort needed (MA=2). Combination block-and-tackle systems provide higher mechanical advantages for cranes and elevators.

22. The theory of relativity (Special and General) was developed by:

  1. Albert Einstein — Special Relativity (1905, E=mc²) and General Relativity (1915, gravity as curvature of spacetime)
  2. Isaac Newton
  3. Max Planck
  4. Niels Bohr

Answer: Albert Einstein — Special Relativity (1905, E=mc²) and General Relativity (1915, gravity as curvature of spacetime)

Explanation: Einstein: Special Relativity (1905) — at high speeds, time dilates, length contracts, mass increases; E=mc² (mass-energy equivalence). General Relativity (1915) — gravity is curvature of spacetime by mass/energy. Predicted: gravitational waves, black holes, gravitational lensing.

23. Artificial Intelligence (AI) is primarily based on:

  1. Machine learning — algorithms that improve with data; deep learning uses neural networks mimicking the brain
  2. Physical robots only
  3. Simple rule-based programs only
  4. Quantum computers only

Answer: Machine learning — algorithms that improve with data; deep learning uses neural networks mimicking the brain

Explanation: Modern AI: Machine Learning (ML) — algorithms learn patterns from data. Deep Learning (DL): multi-layer artificial neural networks (ANNs) — basis for image recognition, language models (GPT), voice assistants. Natural Language Processing (NLP), Computer Vision, and Reinforcement Learning are sub-fields.

24. The phenomenon of 'capillary action' is due to:

  1. Adhesion (water attracted to glass/plant cell walls) and cohesion (water-water attraction via H bonds) — water rises in narrow tubes against gravity
  2. Gravity alone
  3. Atmospheric pressure alone
  4. Osmosis

Answer: Adhesion (water attracted to glass/plant cell walls) and cohesion (water-water attraction via H bonds) — water rises in narrow tubes against gravity

Explanation: Capillary action: water rises in narrow tubes (capillaries) against gravity. Due to: adhesion (water attracted to tube walls) > gravity on that water column + cohesion (water molecules attract each other). Occurs in plant xylem vessels (helps water ascent), paper (chromatography), and wicks (candles, lamps).

25. Phototropism in plants means:

  1. Growth of plant parts in response to light — shoots grow toward light (positive phototropism); roots grow away from light (negative phototropism)
  2. Response to gravity
  3. Response to touch
  4. Response to water

Answer: Growth of plant parts in response to light — shoots grow toward light (positive phototropism); roots grow away from light (negative phototropism)

Explanation: Tropism: directed growth response to a stimulus. Phototropism: light. Geotropism (gravitropism): gravity (roots down, shoots up). Hydrotropism: water (roots toward water). Thigmotropism: touch (tendrils wrap around supports). Chemotropism: chemicals (pollen tube grows toward ovule). Controlled by auxin redistribution.
