26. Peristalsis is the movement that:

  1. Propels food through the digestive tract — wave-like contraction and relaxation of smooth muscles in esophagus, stomach, and intestines
  2. Pumps blood through the heart
  3. Moves air through lungs
  4. Transports urine through ureters

Answer: Propels food through the digestive tract — wave-like contraction and relaxation of smooth muscles in esophagus, stomach, and intestines

Explanation: Peristalsis: wave-like contractions of circular and longitudinal smooth muscles in the digestive tract. Circular muscles contract behind a food bolus; longitudinal muscles relax → bolus moves forward. Occurs in esophagus (pushes food to stomach even against gravity), stomach (churning), small/large intestines. Also in ureters (urine to bladder).

27. The phenomenon of 'surface tension' in water explains:

  1. Why water strider insects can walk on water, why water forms droplets, and why a needle can float on still water surface
  2. Why water conducts electricity
  3. Why water is transparent
  4. Why water has high boiling point

Answer: Why water strider insects can walk on water, why water forms droplets, and why a needle can float on still water surface

Explanation: Surface tension: cohesive forces between water molecules at the surface create a 'skin' that resists deformation. Surface molecules have fewer neighbors → net inward pull. Water has high surface tension (72.8 mN/m at 25°C) due to H-bonding. Soap lowers surface tension → bubbles, better wetting.

28. The study of the human mind and behavior is:

  1. Psychology (psyche = mind/soul; logos = study) — scientific study of mental processes and behavior
  2. Psychiatry (medical treatment of mental disorders)
  3. Sociology (study of society)
  4. Neuroscience (study of nervous system)

Answer: Psychology (psyche = mind/soul; logos = study) — scientific study of mental processes and behavior

Explanation: Psychology: scientific study of behavior and mental processes (perception, cognition, emotion, personality, behavior). Wilhelm Wundt (1879) opened first experimental psychology lab (Leipzig). Major approaches: Freudian/psychoanalytic, behavioral, cognitive, humanistic, and biological. Psychiatry: medical specialty treating mental disorders (uses medications).

29. Pascal's Law states that:

  1. Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions (basis of hydraulic systems)
  2. Pressure increases with velocity in flowing fluids
  3. Buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid
  4. Gases expand to fill their container

Answer: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions (basis of hydraulic systems)

Explanation: Pascal's Law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and without change throughout the fluid (in all directions). Applications: hydraulic press, hydraulic brakes, hydraulic jacks, dentist's chairs. A small force can lift huge weights.

30. The principle behind an aircraft wing generating lift is:

  1. Bernoulli's Principle — air moves faster over curved upper surface, creating lower pressure on top than below, generating lift
  2. Newton's 3rd Law alone
  3. Gravity reversal
  4. Magnetic levitation

Answer: Bernoulli's Principle — air moves faster over curved upper surface, creating lower pressure on top than below, generating lift

Explanation: Aircraft wings (airfoils) use Bernoulli's Principle: air over the curved upper surface travels faster than air below, creating lower pressure above the wing. The pressure difference generates an upward lift force. Newton's 3rd Law also contributes (wing deflects air downward).

31. Isaac Newton's contribution to mathematics includes:

  1. Development of calculus (simultaneously and independently with Leibniz) — also discovered binomial theorem and made contributions to infinite series
  2. Logarithms (Napier)
  3. Coordinate geometry (Descartes)
  4. Complex numbers (Euler)

Answer: Development of calculus (simultaneously and independently with Leibniz) — also discovered binomial theorem and made contributions to infinite series

Explanation: Isaac Newton (1642-1727): Principia Mathematica (1687) — laws of motion and gravitation. Mathematics: co-invented calculus (he called it 'method of fluxions'; Leibniz's notation dx/dy is used today), generalized binomial theorem, developed infinite series. Physics: optics (white light decomposition), reflecting telescope.

32. A car accelerates uniformly from rest to a speed of 20 m/s in 10 seconds. What is its acceleration?

  1. 2 m/s²
  2. 0.5 m/s²
  3. 10 m/s²
  4. 200 m/s²

Answer: 2 m/s²

Explanation: Acceleration = (final velocity - initial velocity) / time = (20 - 0) / 10 = 2 m/s².

33. Which of the following is true for a body moving with uniform circular motion?

  1. Its velocity is constant
  2. Its speed is constant
  3. Its acceleration is zero
  4. It experiences no force

Answer: Its speed is constant

Explanation: In uniform circular motion, speed remains constant but velocity changes due to direction change, resulting in centripetal acceleration and force.

34. If a ball is thrown vertically upward with a velocity of 30 m/s, what is the maximum height reached? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

  1. 45 m
  2. 90 m
  3. 30 m
  4. 60 m

Answer: 45 m

Explanation: Using v² = u² - 2gh, at maximum height v = 0, so h = u²/(2g) = 900 / 20 = 45 m.

35. The slope of a velocity-time graph gives:

  1. Displacement
  2. Acceleration
  3. Speed
  4. Force

Answer: Acceleration

Explanation: Slope of velocity-time graph is change in velocity per unit time, which is acceleration.

36. Newton's first law of motion is also known as:

  1. Law of gravitation
  2. Law of inertia
  3. Law of action-reaction
  4. Law of conservation of momentum

Answer: Law of inertia

Explanation: Newton's first law states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force, which is the principle of inertia.

37. A train moving at 72 km/h applies brakes and comes to a stop in 10 seconds. The retardation is:

  1. 2 m/s²
  2. 7.2 m/s²
  3. 1 m/s²
  4. 0.5 m/s²

Answer: 2 m/s²

Explanation: 72 km/h = 20 m/s, retardation = (0 - 20) / 10 = -2 m/s², magnitude is 2 m/s².

38. For a freely falling body, the distance covered in the nth second is proportional to:

  1. n
  2. 2n - 1
  3. n²
  4. n(n + 1)

Answer: 2n - 1

Explanation: Distance in nth second = u + (g/2)(2n - 1). For free fall u = 0, so it is proportional to (2n - 1).

39. Which of the following pairs represents the correct SI units of velocity and acceleration?

  1. m/s², m/s
  2. m/s, m/s²
  3. m/s, m
  4. m/s², m/s²

Answer: m/s, m/s²

Explanation: SI unit of velocity is m/s and of acceleration is m/s².

40. A body of mass 2 kg moving with a velocity of 10 m/s is brought to rest in 2 seconds by a constant force. The magnitude of the force is:

  1. 10 N
  2. 20 N
  3. 5 N
  4. 40 N

Answer: 10 N

Explanation: Acceleration = (0 - 10) / 2 = -5 m/s², so force = mass × acceleration = 2 × 5 = 10 N.

41. The unit of momentum is:

  1. kg m/s
  2. N s
  3. Both A and B
  4. kg m/s²

Answer: Both A and B

Explanation: Momentum = mass × velocity (kg m/s) is equivalent to Newton-second (N s) because force = dp/dt.

42. A car accelerates uniformly from rest to a velocity of 20 m/s in 10 seconds. What is the distance covered by the car during this time?

  1. 100 m
  2. 200 m
  3. 50 m
  4. 400 m

Answer: 100 m

Explanation: Using s = ut + ½at², with u=0, a=2 m/s² (from v=u+at), t=10 s gives s = 0 + ½×2×100 = 100 m.

43. Which of the following physical quantities is a vector?

  1. Speed
  2. Distance
  3. Velocity
  4. Mass

Answer: Velocity

Explanation: Velocity has both magnitude and direction, making it a vector, while speed, distance, and mass are scalars.

  1. 45 m

45. Newton's first law of motion is also known as the law of

  1. Inertia
  2. Acceleration
  3. Action and reaction
  4. Gravitation

Answer: Inertia

Explanation: Newton's first law states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force, hence called the law of inertia.

46. A force of 20 N acts on a body of mass 5 kg for 4 seconds. The change in momentum of the body is

  1. 80 kg m/s
  2. 100 kg m/s
  3. 20 kg m/s
  4. 40 kg m/s

Answer: 80 kg m/s

Explanation: Change in momentum = Force × time = 20 N × 4 s = 80 kg m/s.

47. A ball is dropped from a height of 80 m. How long does it take to reach the ground? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

  1. 4 s
  2. 8 s
  3. 2 s
  4. 6 s

Answer: 4 s

Explanation: Using s = ½gt², t = √(2s/g) = √(160/10) = √16 = 4 s.

48. When a moving bus suddenly stops, passengers are thrown forward due to

  1. Friction
  2. Inertia of motion
  3. Gravity
  4. Centripetal force

Answer: Inertia of motion

Explanation: Passengers continue moving forward due to inertia of motion when the bus stops suddenly, as per Newton's first law.

49. A ball is thrown vertically upward with an initial velocity of 20 m/s. Neglecting air resistance, what is the maximum height reached by the ball? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

  1. 10 m
  2. 20 m
  3. 30 m
  4. 40 m

Answer: 20 m

Explanation: Using v² = u² - 2gh, at maximum height v=0, so h = u²/(2g) = (20²)/(2*10) = 20 m.

50. Consider the following statements about a body under uniform acceleration from rest: 1. The velocity is directly proportional to time. 2. The distance covered is directly proportional to the square of time. Which of the above statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: For uniform acceleration from rest, v = at (proportional to time) and s = 1/2 at² (proportional to t²); both statements are correct.
