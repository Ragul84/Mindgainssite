Biology Plants MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Biology Plants questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 60 questions.

1. The process by which plants lose water through leaves is:

  1. Respiration
  2. Transpiration
  3. Evaporation
  4. Osmosis

Answer: Transpiration

Explanation: Transpiration is the process of water movement through a plant and its evaporation from aerial parts, mainly leaves, through stomata.

2. The process by which plants lose water through leaves is called:

  1. Transpiration
  2. Evaporation
  3. Respiration
  4. Photosynthesis

Answer: Transpiration

Explanation: Transpiration is the process by which plants lose water vapor through stomata (pores) in leaves. It creates a 'pull' that helps water move up from roots to leaves (transpiration pull). About 97-99% of absorbed water is transpired.

3. A food chain always starts with:

  1. Producers (autotrophs — plants that photosynthesize)
  2. Herbivores
  3. Carnivores
  4. Decomposers

Answer: Producers (autotrophs — plants that photosynthesize)

Explanation: Food chain: Producers (plants) → Primary consumers (herbivores) → Secondary consumers (carnivores) → Tertiary consumers → Decomposers. Energy flows from producers upward; each level has only ~10% efficiency.

4. A symbiotic relationship where both organisms benefit is called:

  1. Mutualism (e.g., bees and flowers, Rhizobium and legumes)
  2. Parasitism
  3. Commensalism
  4. Competition

Answer: Mutualism (e.g., bees and flowers, Rhizobium and legumes)

Explanation: Mutualism: both organisms benefit. Examples: Bee-flower (bee gets nectar, flower gets pollinated); Rhizobium-legume (bacteria get nutrients, plant gets fixed nitrogen); Mycorrhizal fungi-plant roots.

5. The term 'biodiversity hotspot' was coined by:

  1. Norman Myers (1988) — areas with high species richness and endemism under significant threat of habitat loss
  2. Charles Darwin
  3. E.O. Wilson
  4. Humboldt

Answer: Norman Myers (1988) — areas with high species richness and endemism under significant threat of habitat loss

Explanation: Norman Myers coined 'biodiversity hotspot' (1988). Criteria: ≥1,500 endemic vascular plant species AND ≥70% of original habitat already destroyed. There are 36 global hotspots. India has 4: Western Ghats-Sri Lanka, Himalaya, Indo-Burma, and Sundaland (Andaman-Nicobar).

6. The water cycle (hydrological cycle) involves:

  1. Evaporation (water→vapor), condensation (vapor→clouds), precipitation (rain/snow), surface runoff, and infiltration → continuous recycling
  2. Only evaporation
  3. Only precipitation
  4. Only river flow

Answer: Evaporation (water→vapor), condensation (vapor→clouds), precipitation (rain/snow), surface runoff, and infiltration → continuous recycling

Explanation: The water cycle: solar energy evaporates water from oceans/land → water vapor rises and condenses into clouds → precipitation (rain, snow) → some runs off (rivers), some infiltrates (groundwater), some transpires through plants → evaporates again. Continuous cycle driven by solar energy.

7. The study of heredity and variation is called:

  1. Genetics (founded by Gregor Mendel's pea plant experiments, 1865; term coined by William Bateson, 1905)
  2. Ecology
  3. Cytology
  4. Taxonomy

Answer: Genetics (founded by Gregor Mendel's pea plant experiments, 1865; term coined by William Bateson, 1905)

Explanation: Genetics: science of heredity and variation. Gregor Mendel (1822-1884, Austrian monk) did pea plant hybridization experiments establishing laws of inheritance (1865). Term 'genetics' coined by William Bateson (1905). 'Gene' coined by Wilhelm Johannsen (1909). Chromosome theory: Morgan, Sutton, Boveri.

8. The father of genetics is:

  1. Gregor Mendel
  2. Charles Darwin
  3. Thomas Morgan
  4. Francis Crick

Answer: Gregor Mendel

Explanation: Gregor Mendel (1822-1884) is the 'Father of Genetics'. Through his pea plant experiments, he discovered the laws of inheritance: Law of Segregation and Law of Independent Assortment.

9. Which of the following plant hormones is primarily responsible for promoting cell elongation?

  1. Auxin
  2. Gibberellin
  3. Cytokinin
  4. Abscisic acid

Answer: Auxin

Explanation: Auxin is the primary hormone responsible for cell elongation in stems and coleoptiles.

10. The process by which plants lose water vapor through stomata is called?

  1. Transpiration
  2. Guttation
  3. Photosynthesis
  4. Respiration

Answer: Transpiration

Explanation: Transpiration is the loss of water vapor from plant leaves mainly through stomata.

11. In which part of a plant does photosynthesis primarily occur?

  1. Stem
  2. Root
  3. Leaf
  4. Flower

Answer: Leaf

Explanation: Photosynthesis primarily occurs in the leaves, which contain chloroplasts.

12. Which of the following is a parasitic plant that lacks chlorophyll?

  1. Cuscuta
  2. Mango
  3. Neem
  4. Banyan

Answer: Cuscuta

Explanation: Cuscuta (dodder) is a parasitic plant that lacks chlorophyll and derives nutrition from its host.

13. The tissue responsible for the transport of water and minerals in plants is?

  1. Xylem
  2. Phloem
  3. Epidermis
  4. Cortex

Answer: Xylem

Explanation: Xylem is the vascular tissue that transports water and dissolved minerals from roots to other parts.

14. Which pigment gives green color to plants and is essential for photosynthesis?

  1. Carotene
  2. Xanthophyll
  3. Chlorophyll
  4. Anthocyanin

Answer: Chlorophyll

Explanation: Chlorophyll is the green pigment that captures light energy for photosynthesis.

15. Which plant hormone is primarily responsible for promoting cell elongation and stem growth?

  1. Auxin
  2. Gibberellin
  3. Cytokinin
  4. Abscisic acid

Answer: Auxin

Explanation: Auxin promotes cell elongation in stems, while gibberellin stimulates stem elongation and seed germination.

16. In which part of the chloroplast does the light-dependent reaction of photosynthesis occur?

  1. Stroma
  2. Thylakoid membrane
  3. Granum
  4. Inner membrane

Answer: Thylakoid membrane

Explanation: Light-dependent reactions occur on the thylakoid membrane, where chlorophyll absorbs light to produce ATP and NADPH.

17. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of dicotyledonous plants?

  1. Parallel venation
  2. Fibrous root system
  3. Two cotyledons
  4. Scattered vascular bundles

Answer: Two cotyledons

Explanation: Dicots have two cotyledons, reticulate venation, and a taproot system, unlike monocots.

18. The process of transpiration in plants primarily occurs through which structure?

  1. Lenticels
  2. Stomata
  3. Cuticle
  4. Root hairs

Answer: Stomata

Explanation: Stomata, mainly on leaves, are the primary sites for transpiration and gas exchange.

19. Which pigment is essential for the absorption of light energy in photosynthesis?

  1. Carotene
  2. Xanthophyll
  3. Chlorophyll a
  4. Phytochrome

Answer: Chlorophyll a

Explanation: Chlorophyll a is the primary photosynthetic pigment; others are accessory pigments.

20. The Casparian strip in plant roots is found in which tissue?

  1. Epidermis
  2. Cortex
  3. Endodermis
  4. Pericycle

Answer: Endodermis

Explanation: The Casparian strip, a suberin band in the endodermis, regulates water and ion movement into the stele.

21. Which of the following plants is an example of a gymnosperm?

  1. Mango
  2. Pinus
  3. Fern
  4. Moss

Answer: Pinus

Explanation: Pinus is a gymnosperm with naked seeds; mango is an angiosperm, fern is a pteridophyte, moss is a bryophyte.

22. The movement of water from roots to leaves is mainly explained by which theory?

  1. Root pressure theory
  2. Cohesion-tension theory
  3. Capillary theory
  4. Osmotic theory

Answer: Cohesion-tension theory

Explanation: The cohesion-tension theory describes water movement via transpiration pull and water cohesion in xylem.

23. Which plant tissue is responsible for the secondary growth in dicot stems?

  1. Apical meristem
  2. Intercalary meristem
  3. Lateral meristem
  4. Parenchyma

Answer: Lateral meristem

Explanation: Lateral meristem (cambium) causes secondary growth in thickness, unlike apical meristem which increases length.

24. The nitrogen-fixing bacteria Rhizobium is associated with the roots of which plant family?

  1. Solanaceae
  2. Fabaceae
  3. Poaceae
  4. Asteraceae

Answer: Fabaceae

Explanation: Rhizobium forms symbiotic nodules on roots of Fabaceae (legume) plants, fixing atmospheric nitrogen.

25. The primary pigment responsible for capturing light energy in photosynthesis is

  1. Chlorophyll a
  2. Chlorophyll b
  3. Carotenoids
  4. Phycoerythrin

Answer: Chlorophyll a

Explanation: Chlorophyll a is the main photosynthetic pigment that directly converts light energy into chemical energy.
