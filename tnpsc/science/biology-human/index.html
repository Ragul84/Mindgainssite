Biology Human MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Biology Human questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 72 questions.

1. Which is the largest organ in the human body?

  1. Heart
  2. Liver
  3. Skin
  4. Brain

Answer: Skin

Explanation: The skin is the largest organ of the human body. It protects the body, regulates temperature, and provides sensation.

2. How many bones does an adult human body have?

  1. 104
  2. 186
  3. 206
  4. 312

Answer: 206

Explanation: An adult human skeleton has 206 bones. Babies are born with about 270-300 bones; many fuse together as we grow.

3. Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination?

  1. Cerebrum
  2. Medulla Oblongata
  3. Cerebellum
  4. Thalamus

Answer: Cerebellum

Explanation: The cerebellum is responsible for coordination of movement, balance, and fine motor skills.

4. Which enzyme in the saliva begins the digestion of starch?

  1. Pepsin
  2. Trypsin
  3. Amylase (Ptyalin)
  4. Lipase

Answer: Amylase (Ptyalin)

Explanation: Salivary amylase (ptyalin) begins the chemical digestion of starch in the mouth, breaking it into maltose.

5. The largest animal on Earth is:

  1. Blue Whale (Balaenoptera musculus)
  2. African Elephant
  3. Sperm Whale
  4. Whale Shark

Answer: Blue Whale (Balaenoptera musculus)

Explanation: Blue Whale is the largest animal on Earth — up to 33 m long and 180 tonnes. Its heart is the size of a small car. Despite its size, it feeds on tiny krill (shrimp-like crustaceans).

6. A relationship where one organism benefits and the other is harmed is:

  1. Parasitism (e.g., tapeworm in humans, mistletoe on trees)
  2. Mutualism
  3. Commensalism
  4. Predation

Answer: Parasitism (e.g., tapeworm in humans, mistletoe on trees)

Explanation: Parasitism: parasite benefits (nutrition, shelter), host is harmed (reduced fitness). Examples: tapeworm (host: human), Plasmodium (host: human + mosquito), mistletoe on trees, head lice.

7. The part of the brain that controls balance and coordination of movement is:

  1. Cerebellum (the 'little brain' at the back)
  2. Cerebrum
  3. Medulla oblongata
  4. Hypothalamus

Answer: Cerebellum (the 'little brain' at the back)

Explanation: Cerebellum ('little brain') controls balance, coordination, and fine motor movement. Cerebrum handles higher functions (thought, memory, sensation). Medulla oblongata controls breathing, heart rate. Hypothalamus regulates body temperature, hunger, thirst.

8. The thyroid gland is located in the:

  1. Neck, in front of the trachea — produces thyroxine which regulates metabolism
  2. Abdomen
  3. Brain
  4. Chest

Answer: Neck, in front of the trachea — produces thyroxine which regulates metabolism

Explanation: The thyroid gland (butterfly-shaped) is located in the neck, in front of the larynx/trachea. It produces thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), which regulate metabolic rate, growth, and development. Iodine is essential for thyroid hormone production.

9. The human heart has how many chambers?

  1. Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle
  2. Two chambers
  3. Three chambers
  4. Five chambers

Answer: Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle

Explanation: The human heart has 4 chambers: right atrium and right ventricle (receive deoxygenated blood from body, pump to lungs) and left atrium and left ventricle (receive oxygenated blood from lungs, pump to body). Left ventricle has the thickest wall.

10. The blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart is:

  1. Pulmonary vein (the only vein that carries oxygenated blood)
  2. Pulmonary artery
  3. Aorta
  4. Vena cava

Answer: Pulmonary vein (the only vein that carries oxygenated blood)

Explanation: Pulmonary veins (4 of them, 2 from each lung) carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. Note: arteries generally carry oxygenated blood EXCEPT pulmonary artery. Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs.

11. The site of fertilization in the human female reproductive system is:

  1. Fallopian tube (oviduct) — where sperm meets the egg after ovulation
  2. Uterus
  3. Ovary
  4. Cervix

Answer: Fallopian tube (oviduct) — where sperm meets the egg after ovulation

Explanation: Fertilization normally occurs in the fallopian tube (usually the ampulla region). The fertilized egg (zygote) travels to the uterus and implants in the endometrium. Fertilization outside the fallopian tube is called ectopic pregnancy.

12. The process by which a fertilized egg develops into an organism is called:

  1. Embryogenesis — cell division (cleavage), differentiation, and organogenesis
  2. Meiosis
  3. Mitosis
  4. Metamorphosis

Answer: Embryogenesis — cell division (cleavage), differentiation, and organogenesis

Explanation: Embryogenesis: after fertilization, the zygote undergoes cleavage (rapid mitotic divisions) → blastula → gastrulation → organogenesis. In humans, this process takes about 9 months. The embryo becomes a fetus after 8 weeks.

13. Sex of a human child is determined by:

  1. The father's sperm — X-bearing sperm gives girl (XX), Y-bearing sperm gives boy (XY)
  2. The mother only
  3. Both equally
  4. Temperature during pregnancy

Answer: The father's sperm — X-bearing sperm gives girl (XX), Y-bearing sperm gives boy (XY)

Explanation: Human sex determination: females are XX, males are XY. All eggs carry X chromosome; sperm carry either X or Y. If Y-bearing sperm fertilizes egg → XY (boy). If X-bearing sperm → XX (girl). So the father's sperm determines the child's sex, not the mother.

14. Binomial nomenclature (scientific naming of organisms) uses:

  1. Genus name (capitalized) + species name (lowercase) in Latin/Greek, italicized — e.g., Homo sapiens, Panthera tigris
  2. Common name only
  3. Only one Latin name
  4. Kingdom and phylum names

Answer: Genus name (capitalized) + species name (lowercase) in Latin/Greek, italicized — e.g., Homo sapiens, Panthera tigris

Explanation: Carl Linnaeus (1758) established binomial nomenclature: each organism gets a two-part Latin name — genus (capitalized) + specific epithet (lowercase). Example: Homo sapiens (humans), Panthera tigris (tiger), Oryza sativa (rice), Mangifera indica (mango). Universally accepted system.

15. The first human to walk on the Moon was:

  1. Neil Armstrong (20 July 1969, Apollo 11) — 'One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind'
  2. Buzz Aldrin
  3. Yuri Gagarin (first human in space, 1961)
  4. John Glenn

Answer: Neil Armstrong (20 July 1969, Apollo 11) — 'One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind'

Explanation: Neil Armstrong was the first human on the Moon (July 20, 1969, Apollo 11 mission). Buzz Aldrin was the second. Michael Collins orbited the Moon in the command module. Yuri Gagarin (USSR) was the first human in space (April 12, 1961, Vostok 1).

16. The specific heat capacity of water (4186 J/kg/K) is important because:

  1. Water can absorb/release large amounts of heat with minimal temperature change — moderates Earth's climate and makes water ideal as a coolant
  2. Water is a good conductor of electricity
  3. Water has high density
  4. Water is transparent

Answer: Water can absorb/release large amounts of heat with minimal temperature change — moderates Earth's climate and makes water ideal as a coolant

Explanation: Water's high specific heat (4186 J/kg/K — among highest of all common substances): coastal areas have milder climate than inland (ocean as heat reservoir), used as coolant in car radiators and nuclear reactors, and is why the human body (mostly water) maintains stable temperature.

17. The normal human body temperature (oral) is:

  1. 37°C (98.6°F) — fever: >37.5°C (99.5°F); hypothermia: <35°C (95°F)
  2. 36°C
  3. 38°C
  4. 40°C

Answer: 37°C (98.6°F) — fever: >37.5°C (99.5°F); hypothermia: <35°C (95°F)

Explanation: Normal body temperature: ~37°C (98.6°F) orally. Regulated by the hypothalamus. Rectal temperature is ~0.5°C higher; axillary (armpit) is ~0.5°C lower. Fever (pyrexia): >37.5°C — sign of infection/inflammation. Heat stroke: >40°C (life-threatening). Hypothermia: <35°C.

18. Insulin was first isolated in:

  1. 1921 by Frederick Banting and Charles Best (University of Toronto) — used to treat diabetes; Nobel Prize 1923 to Banting and Macleod
  2. 1900
  3. 1945
  4. 1960

Answer: 1921 by Frederick Banting and Charles Best (University of Toronto) — used to treat diabetes; Nobel Prize 1923 to Banting and Macleod

Explanation: Frederick Banting and Charles Best isolated insulin from dog pancreas in 1921, supervised by John Macleod at University of Toronto. First human treated: Leonard Thompson (diabetic boy, 1922). Nobel Prize 1923: Banting and Macleod. Banting shared with Best; Macleod shared with James Collip who purified it.

19. The largest organ of the human body is:

  1. Skin — surface area ~1.5-2 m², weighs ~3-5 kg; functions: protection, temperature regulation, sensation, Vitamin D synthesis
  2. Liver (largest internal organ)
  3. Lungs
  4. Brain

Answer: Skin — surface area ~1.5-2 m², weighs ~3-5 kg; functions: protection, temperature regulation, sensation, Vitamin D synthesis

Explanation: Skin is the largest organ: 1.5-2 m² surface area, ~3-5 kg. Three layers: epidermis (outer, waterproof), dermis (collagen, blood vessels, hair follicles), hypodermis (fat, insulation). Functions: barrier against pathogens/UV, temperature regulation (sweat, vasodilation/constriction), sensory (touch, pain, temperature), Vitamin D synthesis. Liver is the largest internal organ.

20. Which branch of biology deals with the study of heredity and variation?

  1. Genetics — founded by Gregor Mendel's pea plant experiments (1856-1863); Watson & Crick discovered DNA structure in 1953
  2. Ecology
  3. Taxonomy
  4. Physiology

Answer: Genetics — founded by Gregor Mendel's pea plant experiments (1856-1863); Watson & Crick discovered DNA structure in 1953

Explanation: Genetics studies how traits are inherited (heredity) and how variations arise in populations. Mendel discovered laws of inheritance using pea plants. Modern genetics: DNA structure (Watson, Crick, Franklin, 1953), Human Genome Project (2003), CRISPR gene editing (2012). 'Gene' coined by Wilhelm Johannsen (1909).

21. The largest internal organ of the human body is:

  1. Liver
  2. Kidney
  3. Lung
  4. Heart

Answer: Liver

Explanation: The liver is the largest internal organ (about 1.5 kg). Functions: bile production (for fat digestion), detoxification, glycogen storage, synthesis of plasma proteins, blood clotting factors, and cholesterol regulation.

22. The human heart beats approximately how many times per minute (at rest)?

  1. 72 times (60-100 bpm range)
  2. 100 times
  3. 50 times
  4. 120 times

Answer: 72 times (60-100 bpm range)

Explanation: Normal resting heart rate: 60-100 bpm (beats per minute), average ~72 bpm. Athletes often have lower rates (40-60 bpm). The heart pumps about 5 liters of blood per minute at rest.

23. Plastic is problematic for the environment because:

  1. It is non-biodegradable (takes hundreds of years to decompose, breaks into microplastics)
  2. It is too heavy
  3. It floats on water only
  4. It is toxic when manufactured

Answer: It is non-biodegradable (takes hundreds of years to decompose, breaks into microplastics)

Explanation: Plastics are synthetic polymers that are non-biodegradable — they persist in the environment for hundreds of years. They break down into microplastics (< 5mm) that enter food chains, harming marine life and humans.

24. Which plant hormone is responsible for fruit ripening?

  1. Ethylene (a gaseous plant hormone — used commercially to ripen bananas, avocados; causes yellowing of leaves)
  2. Auxin (promotes stem elongation)
  3. Gibberellin (promotes germination, stem elongation)
  4. Cytokinin (promotes cell division)

Answer: Ethylene (a gaseous plant hormone — used commercially to ripen bananas, avocados; causes yellowing of leaves)

Explanation: Ethylene (C₂H₄) is the plant hormone responsible for fruit ripening, senescence (leaf falling), and responses to stress. It is used commercially to ripen bananas, mangoes, and tomatoes after transport. Calcium carbide (generates acetylene, similar effect) is used but is harmful — illegal in food.

25. 'Dialysis' is necessary when:

  1. Kidneys fail to filter waste from blood (renal failure) — dialysis artificially filters blood using a semipermeable membrane
  2. Liver fails
  3. Heart fails
  4. Lungs fail (requires ventilator)

Answer: Kidneys fail to filter waste from blood (renal failure) — dialysis artificially filters blood using a semipermeable membrane

Explanation: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD): kidneys lose ability to filter blood, causing buildup of urea, creatinine, potassium, and fluid. Treatment: hemodialysis (blood filtered through dialyzer machine 3×/week) or peritoneal dialysis (dialysate fluid in abdomen, peritoneum acts as filter). Kidney transplant is the definitive treatment.
