Chemistry Matter MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Chemistry Matter questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 78 questions.

1. What is the chemical symbol for water?

  1. WA
  2. H2O
  3. HO2
  4. OH

Answer: H2O

Explanation: Water is H2O — two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

2. Which is the hardest natural substance on Earth?

  1. Gold
  2. Iron
  3. Diamond
  4. Granite

Answer: Diamond

Explanation: Diamond is the hardest natural substance with a Mohs hardness of 10. It is a form of carbon with atoms arranged in a crystal lattice.

3. What does DNA stand for?

  1. Deoxyribonucleic Acid
  2. Dynamic Nuclear Acid
  3. Direct Nucleic Amplifier
  4. Dense Natural Amino acid

Answer: Deoxyribonucleic Acid

Explanation: DNA stands for Deoxyribonucleic Acid. It is the molecule that carries genetic information in all living organisms.

4. The chemical formula of sodium chloride (common salt) is:

  1. NaCl
  2. Na2Cl
  3. NaCl2
  4. Na2Cl2

Answer: NaCl

Explanation: Sodium chloride (common salt) has the formula NaCl — one sodium (Na) and one chlorine (Cl) atom.

5. The splitting of an atom is called:

  1. Nuclear Fusion
  2. Nuclear Fission
  3. Radioactive Decay
  4. Ionization

Answer: Nuclear Fission

Explanation: Nuclear Fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus (like U-235) into smaller nuclei, releasing large amounts of energy. This is the principle used in nuclear reactors and atomic bombs.

6. The atomic number of an element is:

  1. The number of protons in the nucleus
  2. The number of neutrons
  3. The total number of protons and neutrons
  4. The number of electrons in the outermost shell

Answer: The number of protons in the nucleus

Explanation: Atomic number (Z) = number of protons in the nucleus. It uniquely identifies an element. For a neutral atom, atomic number = number of electrons. Mass number = protons + neutrons.

7. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with:

  1. Same atomic number but different mass numbers (different neutrons)
  2. Same mass number but different atomic number
  3. Same protons and neutrons but different electrons
  4. Different atomic numbers

Answer: Same atomic number but different mass numbers (different neutrons)

Explanation: Isotopes have the same number of protons (same element) but different numbers of neutrons, hence different mass numbers. Example: Carbon-12, Carbon-13, Carbon-14 all have 6 protons but 6, 7, 8 neutrons respectively.

8. The modern periodic table arranges elements in order of:

  1. Increasing atomic number (number of protons)
  2. Increasing atomic mass
  3. Increasing number of electrons
  4. Alphabetical order

Answer: Increasing atomic number (number of protons)

Explanation: The modern periodic table (Moseley's arrangement, 1913) arranges elements in increasing order of atomic number (not atomic mass as Mendeleev did). Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties.

9. The valence electrons are electrons in:

  1. The outermost shell (determines chemical reactivity and bonding)
  2. The innermost shell
  3. The second shell
  4. The nucleus

Answer: The outermost shell (determines chemical reactivity and bonding)

Explanation: Valence electrons are in the outermost electron shell (valence shell) of an atom. They determine an element's chemical properties and how it forms bonds. Elements with 8 valence electrons (noble gases) are highly stable.

10. Chemical bonds form because:

  1. Atoms share or transfer electrons to achieve stable electron configuration (usually 8 valence electrons — octet rule)
  2. Atoms lose neutrons
  3. Protons attract protons
  4. Electrons repel electrons

Answer: Atoms share or transfer electrons to achieve stable electron configuration (usually 8 valence electrons — octet rule)

Explanation: Atoms form chemical bonds (covalent, ionic, metallic) to achieve stable electron configurations. The octet rule says most atoms are stable with 8 valence electrons (like noble gases). Hydrogen needs 2 (duet rule).

11. Ionic bonds form between:

  1. Metals and non-metals (transfer of electrons)
  2. Two non-metals
  3. Two metals
  4. Non-metals and noble gases

Answer: Metals and non-metals (transfer of electrons)

Explanation: Ionic bonds form when one atom (usually metal) transfers electrons to another (usually non-metal), forming positive (cation) and negative (anion) ions that attract each other. Example: NaCl (table salt) — Na⁺ + Cl⁻.

12. Covalent bonds form between:

  1. Non-metals sharing electrons
  2. Metal and non-metal
  3. Two metals
  4. Ions

Answer: Non-metals sharing electrons

Explanation: Covalent bonds form when two non-metal atoms share pairs of electrons. Example: H₂O (water) — oxygen shares electrons with two hydrogens. Many organic compounds have covalent bonds.

13. The most abundant element in the Earth's crust is:

  1. Oxygen (about 46%)
  2. Silicon
  3. Aluminum
  4. Iron

Answer: Oxygen (about 46%)

Explanation: Oxygen is the most abundant element in Earth's crust by mass (~46%), followed by Silicon (~28%), Aluminum (~8%), and Iron (~5%). In Earth's atmosphere, Nitrogen (78%) is most abundant.

14. The hardest natural substance is:

  1. Diamond (pure carbon in cubic structure)
  2. Corundum (Sapphire/Ruby)
  3. Topaz
  4. Quartz

Answer: Diamond (pure carbon in cubic structure)

Explanation: Diamond (10 on Mohs scale) is the hardest natural substance — carbon atoms arranged in a strong 3D covalent lattice (tetrahedral arrangement). Graphite is also pure carbon but arranged in layers (soft, used as lubricant).

15. The chemical formula of water is:

  1. H₂O
  2. HO₂
  3. H₂O₂
  4. H₃O

Answer: H₂O

Explanation: Water is H₂O — two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom by covalent bonds. H₂O₂ is Hydrogen Peroxide (a bleaching agent). Water has a bent/V-shaped molecular geometry.

16. Nuclear fission is the process of:

  1. Splitting a heavy nucleus into smaller fragments, releasing energy
  2. Combining light nuclei to form heavier ones
  3. Emission of alpha particles
  4. Beta decay

Answer: Splitting a heavy nucleus into smaller fragments, releasing energy

Explanation: Nuclear fission: a heavy nucleus (e.g., U-235) splits into smaller fragments when bombarded with neutrons, releasing enormous energy and more neutrons (chain reaction). Basis for nuclear power plants and atomic bombs.

17. Polymers are large molecules made from:

  1. Repeating small units called monomers
  2. Single large atoms
  3. Two or three molecules
  4. Metal atoms

Answer: Repeating small units called monomers

Explanation: Polymers are large molecules (macromolecules) made by linking many small repeating units called monomers. Natural polymers: rubber, cellulose, starch, proteins, DNA. Synthetic polymers: plastics (polyethylene, nylon, PVC).

18. Osmosis is the movement of water:

  1. From high water concentration to low water concentration through a semi-permeable membrane
  2. From low to high concentration (active transport)
  3. Of solute particles only
  4. Through open pores in any direction

Answer: From high water concentration to low water concentration through a semi-permeable membrane

Explanation: Osmosis: movement of water molecules from a region of higher water concentration (dilute solution/pure water) to lower water concentration (concentrated solution) through a selectively permeable membrane.

19. Which element is most abundant in the human body (by mass)?

  1. Oxygen (~65%)
  2. Carbon (~18%)
  3. Hydrogen (~10%)
  4. Nitrogen (~3%)

Answer: Oxygen (~65%)

Explanation: Human body composition (by mass): Oxygen ~65%, Carbon ~18%, Hydrogen ~10%, Nitrogen ~3%, Calcium ~1.5%, Phosphorus ~1%. Oxygen is mainly in water (H₂O) and organic molecules.

20. The greenhouse gas with the highest global warming potential (per molecule) over 100 years is:

  1. SF₆ (sulfur hexafluoride) — ~23,500 times CO₂; or among common gases: N₂O (~265×), CH₄ (~28×), CO₂ (1×)
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Water vapor
  4. Ozone

Answer: SF₆ (sulfur hexafluoride) — ~23,500 times CO₂; or among common gases: N₂O (~265×), CH₄ (~28×), CO₂ (1×)

Explanation: Among common greenhouse gases: CO₂ (baseline 1×), CH₄ (methane, ~28× CO₂), N₂O (nitrous oxide, ~265×), HFCs/CFCs (hundreds to thousands×). SF₆ has the highest GWP (~23,500×). CO₂ is most abundant though.

21. Rutherford's gold foil experiment (1909) proved:

  1. The atom has a tiny, dense, positively-charged nucleus with electrons orbiting around it (disproving Thomson's plum-pudding model)
  2. Electrons are in fixed orbits
  3. Atoms are indivisible
  4. Neutrons exist in the nucleus

Answer: The atom has a tiny, dense, positively-charged nucleus with electrons orbiting around it (disproving Thomson's plum-pudding model)

Explanation: Geiger-Marsden experiment (Rutherford): alpha particles fired at gold foil mostly passed through, but some deflected at large angles. This proved: atom is mostly empty space, with a tiny, dense, positively-charged nucleus. Rutherford's model (1911).

22. The atomic number of an element represents:

  1. Number of protons in the nucleus (also equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom)
  2. Mass of the atom
  3. Number of neutrons
  4. Number of electrons only

Answer: Number of protons in the nucleus (also equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom)

Explanation: Atomic number (Z) = number of protons in the nucleus. It uniquely identifies an element. For a neutral atom, protons = electrons. Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons. Neutrons = A - Z.

23. Water (H₂O) is a polar molecule because:

  1. Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen, creating partial negative charge on O and partial positive charge on H (bent shape, 104.5°)
  2. Hydrogen atoms are heavier
  3. It has ionic bonds
  4. It has 3 atoms

Answer: Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen, creating partial negative charge on O and partial positive charge on H (bent shape, 104.5°)

Explanation: Water is polar due to: (1) O is more electronegative than H, pulling electron density toward O, and (2) the molecule's bent geometry (104.5°) means the dipoles don't cancel. This polarity gives water its unique properties: high surface tension, capillary action, and ability to dissolve ionic compounds.

24. The role of decomposers in an ecosystem is:

  1. Break down dead organic matter into simpler inorganic compounds, returning nutrients to the soil (nutrient cycling)
  2. Fix nitrogen from atmosphere
  3. Produce food by photosynthesis
  4. Consume plants

Answer: Break down dead organic matter into simpler inorganic compounds, returning nutrients to the soil (nutrient cycling)

Explanation: Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down dead organisms and waste into simpler inorganic materials (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus) that are returned to soil and atmosphere. This is essential for nutrient cycling — without decomposers, nutrients would be locked in dead matter.

25. The element with the highest electronegativity is:

  1. Fluorine (F) — most electronegative element (Pauling scale: 3.98); top of halogen group
  2. Oxygen
  3. Chlorine
  4. Nitrogen

Answer: Fluorine (F) — most electronegative element (Pauling scale: 3.98); top of halogen group

Explanation: Electronegativity increases across a period and up a group. Fluorine (Group 17, Period 2) has the highest electronegativity (3.98 on Pauling scale). It attracts electrons most strongly, explaining why HF is a weak acid despite F being in Group 17 (strong H-F bond).
