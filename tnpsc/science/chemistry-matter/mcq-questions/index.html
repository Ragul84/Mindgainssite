26. The Periodic Table was arranged by Mendeleev based on:

  1. Atomic mass and periodic repetition of properties (1869) — gaps left for undiscovered elements predicted correctly
  2. Atomic number (modern periodic table by Moseley, 1913)
  3. Electron configuration
  4. Number of neutrons

Answer: Atomic mass and periodic repetition of properties (1869) — gaps left for undiscovered elements predicted correctly

Explanation: Mendeleev (1869) arranged elements by atomic mass, noticing properties repeat periodically. He left gaps for undiscovered elements (gallium, germanium) and correctly predicted their properties. The modern periodic table (Moseley, 1913) arranges by atomic number (protons).

27. Renewable energy sources include:

  1. Solar, wind, hydroelectric, tidal, geothermal, and biomass energy — replenished naturally and do not deplete over time
  2. Coal, oil, and natural gas (non-renewable — fossil fuels)
  3. Nuclear energy (uranium is finite — debated)
  4. All forms of energy

Answer: Solar, wind, hydroelectric, tidal, geothermal, and biomass energy — replenished naturally and do not deplete over time

Explanation: Renewable energy: derived from natural processes replenished on human timescales. Solar (photovoltaic, solar thermal), wind (turbines), hydroelectric (dam turbines), tidal (tidal barrages), geothermal (Earth's internal heat), biomass (organic matter). India's renewable energy target: 500 GW by 2030.

28. Hemoglobin is important because it:

  1. Carries oxygen from lungs to tissues (each Hb molecule carries 4 O₂ molecules; iron gives blood its red color)
  2. Clots blood
  3. Fights infection
  4. Produces antibodies

Answer: Carries oxygen from lungs to tissues (each Hb molecule carries 4 O₂ molecules; iron gives blood its red color)

Explanation: Hemoglobin (Hb) is a protein in red blood cells (RBCs) containing 4 heme groups, each with an iron (Fe²⁺) atom that binds one O₂. In lungs: O₂ + Hb → oxyhemoglobin (bright red). In tissues: O₂ released → deoxyhemoglobin (dark red). Also carries CO₂ (as carbaminohemoglobin, ~20%).

29. The Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC) is located at:

  1. Trombay, Mumbai (Maharashtra) — India's premier nuclear research centre, established 1954
  2. Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu
  3. Hyderabad
  4. Bengaluru

Answer: Trombay, Mumbai (Maharashtra) — India's premier nuclear research centre, established 1954

Explanation: BARC (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, est. 1954 by Dr. Homi Bhabha as Atomic Energy Establishment Trombay/AEET, renamed 1967): India's flagship nuclear research centre. Developed Dhruva research reactor, nuclear weapons program, and peaceful nuclear applications. HQ at Trombay, Mumbai.

30. The chemical formula of ozone is:

  1. O₃ (three oxygen atoms) — allotrope of oxygen found in stratosphere; also a pollutant at ground level
  2. O₂
  3. O
  4. O₄

Answer: O₃ (three oxygen atoms) — allotrope of oxygen found in stratosphere; also a pollutant at ground level

Explanation: Ozone (O₃) is an allotrope of oxygen — same element, different molecular form. In stratosphere (15-35 km): absorbs UV radiation. At ground level: pollutant (component of smog, harmful to lungs). O₂ is regular atmospheric oxygen. Diamond and graphite are allotropes of carbon.

31. The process by which a liquid turns into vapor at any temperature is called:

  1. Evaporation (below boiling point, from surface only) — distinct from boiling (throughout the liquid at boiling point)
  2. Condensation
  3. Sublimation
  4. Freezing

Answer: Evaporation (below boiling point, from surface only) — distinct from boiling (throughout the liquid at boiling point)

Explanation: Evaporation: surface molecules with enough kinetic energy escape into vapor phase, at any temperature. Rate increases with: higher temperature, lower humidity, more surface area, wind. Evaporation causes cooling (evaporative cooling — basis for desert coolers, sweating). Boiling: vaporization throughout the liquid.

32. Catalysts affect chemical reactions by:

  1. Providing an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy — increasing reaction rate without being consumed
  2. Providing heat to the reaction
  3. Increasing temperature
  4. Acting as reactants

Answer: Providing an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy — increasing reaction rate without being consumed

Explanation: Catalysts lower activation energy (Ea) → more molecules have enough energy to react → faster reaction rate. Catalysts are not consumed (regenerated at end). Examples: enzymes (biological catalysts, highly specific), platinum in car catalytic converters, iron in Haber process (N₂ + H₂ → NH₃).

33. The unit of radioactivity is:

  1. Becquerel (Bq) — 1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second; older unit: Curie (1 Ci = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq)
  2. Gray
  3. Sievert
  4. Roentgen

Answer: Becquerel (Bq) — 1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second; older unit: Curie (1 Ci = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq)

Explanation: Radioactivity units: Becquerel (Bq) = 1 nuclear decay/second (SI unit). Curie (Ci) = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq (older unit). Gray (Gy) = absorbed dose of radiation (1 J/kg). Sievert (Sv) = effective dose (accounts for radiation type's biological harm). Dose in Sieverts matters for radiation protection.

34. The universal solvent is:

  1. Water — due to polarity, it can dissolve more substances than any other liquid; ionic and polar compounds dissolve well
  2. Alcohol
  3. Benzene
  4. Acetone

Answer: Water — due to polarity, it can dissolve more substances than any other liquid; ionic and polar compounds dissolve well

Explanation: Water is called the 'universal solvent' because its polar nature allows it to dissolve most ionic compounds (NaCl, KCl) and polar molecules. 'Like dissolves like': polar solvents dissolve polar/ionic solutes; non-polar solvents (benzene, hexane) dissolve non-polar solutes (fats, oils).

35. The study of the structure of the human body is called:

  1. Anatomy (from Greek 'ana' = up, 'temnein' = to cut) — historically studied by dissection; physiology studies function
  2. Physiology (function)
  3. Histology (tissue under microscope)
  4. Cytology (cells)

Answer: Anatomy (from Greek 'ana' = up, 'temnein' = to cut) — historically studied by dissection; physiology studies function

Explanation: Anatomy: study of body structure. Human anatomy studied from ancient times; modern anatomy pioneered by Andreas Vesalius (1543, 'De Humani Corporis Fabrica'). Gross anatomy: visible to naked eye. Microscopic anatomy: histology (tissues), cytology (cells). Physiology: study of how body functions.

36. The element essential for the functioning of thyroid gland is:

  1. Iodine (I) — needed to produce thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3); deficiency causes goiter and cretinism
  2. Calcium
  3. Iron
  4. Phosphorus

Answer: Iodine (I) — needed to produce thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3); deficiency causes goiter and cretinism

Explanation: Iodine is essential for thyroid hormone synthesis: T4 (thyroxine, 4 iodine atoms) and T3 (triiodothyronine, 3 iodine atoms). Deficiency → goiter (enlarged thyroid), hypothyroidism, and in infants: cretinism (severe mental and physical retardation). Iodized salt (NaI or KI added) prevents deficiency.

37. The element phosphorus is essential for living organisms because:

  1. It is a component of DNA, RNA, ATP (energy currency), phospholipid membranes, and bones/teeth (calcium phosphate)
  2. It is needed for photosynthesis directly
  3. It is part of hemoglobin
  4. It fixes nitrogen

Answer: It is a component of DNA, RNA, ATP (energy currency), phospholipid membranes, and bones/teeth (calcium phosphate)

Explanation: Phosphorus (P) is vital in: DNA/RNA backbone (phosphate groups), ATP/ADP (energy currency), phospholipids (cell membrane bilayer), and hydroxyapatite (Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂ — main mineral in bones and teeth). A macronutrient for all living things. Plants absorb as HPO₄²⁻ from soil.

38. The element Helium was first discovered:

  1. In the Sun's spectrum before being found on Earth (1868 by Janssen and Lockyer from solar eclipse observation; 1895 by Ramsay on Earth)
  2. In air first
  3. In water
  4. In rocks first

Answer: In the Sun's spectrum before being found on Earth (1868 by Janssen and Lockyer from solar eclipse observation; 1895 by Ramsay on Earth)

Explanation: Helium (He, atomic number 2): named after Helios (Greek sun god). First detected spectroscopically in the Sun's corona during the 1868 solar eclipse by Pierre Janssen and Norman Lockyer — before it was found on Earth. William Ramsay discovered it on Earth in 1895 from uranium mineral clevite. Second most abundant element in the universe.

39. The branch of chemistry that deals with carbon compounds is:

  1. Organic chemistry — carbon forms ~10 million known compounds due to tetravalency and catenation; all life is carbon-based
  2. Inorganic chemistry (non-carbon compounds)
  3. Physical chemistry (principles/theories)
  4. Analytical chemistry (identification/quantification)

Answer: Organic chemistry — carbon forms ~10 million known compounds due to tetravalency and catenation; all life is carbon-based

Explanation: Organic chemistry: study of carbon compounds (except CO₂, CO, carbonates, cyanides which are inorganic). ~10 million known organic compounds (far more than inorganic). All biomolecules (proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, DNA) are organic. Friedrich Wöhler (1828) disproved vitalism by synthesizing urea (organic) from inorganic ammonium cyanate.

40. The Chandrayaan-1 mission's major discovery was:

  1. Confirmed presence of water molecules on the Moon's surface (2008) — using Moon Impact Probe and M³ spectrometer from NASA
  2. First Moon landing by India
  3. Discovered lunar helium-3
  4. Found oxygen in lunar soil

Answer: Confirmed presence of water molecules on the Moon's surface (2008) — using Moon Impact Probe and M³ spectrometer from NASA

Explanation: Chandrayaan-1 (October 2008): India's first lunar mission. Key discovery: NASA's Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³) instrument on board confirmed the presence of water/hydroxyl (OH) molecules on the lunar surface (announced 2009). Also discovered magnesium, aluminum, silicon in lunar soil. Moon Impact Probe (MIP) crash-landed on Moon.

41. The 'Bohr Model' of the atom proposed that electrons:

  1. Move randomly around the nucleus
  2. Orbit the nucleus in specific fixed energy levels (shells)
  3. Are embedded in a positive sphere
  4. Exist as probability clouds

Answer: Orbit the nucleus in specific fixed energy levels (shells)

Explanation: Niels Bohr (1913) proposed that electrons orbit the nucleus in specific discrete energy levels (shells). Electrons absorb or emit energy when jumping between levels.

42. DNA stands for:

  1. Deoxyribonucleic Acid
  2. Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid
  3. Diribonucleic Acid
  4. Double Nucleic Acid

Answer: Deoxyribonucleic Acid

Explanation: DNA = Deoxyribonucleic Acid. It is a double-helix molecule made of nucleotides. Each nucleotide has a deoxyribose sugar, phosphate group, and one of four bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C).

43. ISRO's Chandrayaan-1 mission was significant because:

  1. It confirmed the presence of water molecules on the Moon (2008)
  2. It was India's first Mars mission
  3. It landed a rover on the Moon
  4. It was India's first satellite

Answer: It confirmed the presence of water molecules on the Moon (2008)

Explanation: Chandrayaan-1 (October 2008 launch) discovered evidence of water molecules on the Moon — its Moon Impact Probe (MIP) and NASA's M3 instrument confirmed water/hydroxyl. India's first lunar mission.

  1. Father of India's Nuclear Programme
  2. Father of India's Space Programme
  3. Father of India's Green Revolution
  4. Father of India's Missile Programme

Answer: Father of India's Nuclear Programme

Explanation: Dr. Homi J. Bhabha (1909-1966) is the Father of India's Nuclear Programme. He founded the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) and Atomic Energy Establishment (now BARC). First Chairman of India's Atomic Energy Commission.

45. Graphene is:

  1. A single layer of carbon atoms in a hexagonal lattice — strongest known material, excellent conductor
  2. A form of diamond
  3. A plastic material
  4. A glass-like substance

Answer: A single layer of carbon atoms in a hexagonal lattice — strongest known material, excellent conductor

Explanation: Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice. It is the world's strongest material (200× stronger than steel), excellent electrical and thermal conductor. Isolated by Geim and Novoselov (Nobel 2010).

46. Soap works by:

  1. Having a hydrophilic head (water-loving, ionic) and hydrophobic tail (water-repelling, fat-loving) — surrounds grease in micelles that wash away
  2. Being acidic
  3. Being a strong base that dissolves everything
  4. Changing the pH of water to neutral

Answer: Having a hydrophilic head (water-loving, ionic) and hydrophobic tail (water-repelling, fat-loving) — surrounds grease in micelles that wash away

Explanation: Soap molecules are sodium/potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids. The hydrophobic tail dissolves into grease/oil; the hydrophilic head stays in water. Multiple soap molecules surround a grease droplet to form a micelle, which can be washed away with water.

47. The gas used as a refrigerant that is being phased out due to ozone depletion is:

  1. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — e.g., Freon R-12 — replaced by HFCs (R-134a) under Montreal Protocol (1987)
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Oxygen
  4. Nitrogen

Answer: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — e.g., Freon R-12 — replaced by HFCs (R-134a) under Montreal Protocol (1987)

Explanation: CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons, Freons) were used as refrigerants and aerosol propellants. UV radiation in stratosphere breaks them down, releasing Cl radicals that destroy ozone (one Cl can destroy 100,000 ozone molecules). Replaced by HFCs under Montreal Protocol (1987).

48. Biogas (gobar gas) is mainly composed of:

  1. Methane (~50-75%) and CO₂ (~25-45%) — produced by anaerobic digestion of organic waste (animal dung, food waste)
  2. Propane and butane
  3. Hydrogen and oxygen
  4. Carbon monoxide

Answer: Methane (~50-75%) and CO₂ (~25-45%) — produced by anaerobic digestion of organic waste (animal dung, food waste)

Explanation: Biogas is produced by anaerobic decomposition of organic matter by methanogens (archaea). Composition: ~60% CH₄, ~35% CO₂, small amounts of H₂S and other gases. Used as cooking fuel. Biogas plants reduce waste, provide renewable energy, and the slurry is a fertilizer.

49. The term 'absolute zero' refers to:

  1. 0 Kelvin (-273.15°C) — the lowest theoretically possible temperature where all thermal motion stops
  2. 0°C
  3. 0°F
  4. The freezing point of water

Answer: 0 Kelvin (-273.15°C) — the lowest theoretically possible temperature where all thermal motion stops

Explanation: Absolute zero (0 K = -273.15°C = -459.67°F) is the theoretical lowest possible temperature. At this temperature, atoms have minimum quantum mechanical energy. Nothing can be cooled below absolute zero. Kelvin scale starts at absolute zero (0 K), so no negative temperatures in Kelvin.

50. The Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR) is located at:

  1. Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu — India's second nuclear research centre after BARC Mumbai
  2. Chennai
  3. Coimbatore
  4. Madurai

Answer: Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu — India's second nuclear research centre after BARC Mumbai

Explanation: IGCAR (Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research) is at Kalpakkam, near Chennai, Tamil Nadu. It operates the Fast Breeder Test Reactor (FBTR) and is building the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR). Kalpakkam also has the Madras Atomic Power Station (MAPS).
