Physics Optics MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Physics Optics questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 92 questions.

1. Which gas do plants take in to make food during photosynthesis?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Carbon Dioxide
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Carbon Dioxide

Explanation: Plants use carbon dioxide (CO2) from air, water from soil, and sunlight to make food (glucose) through photosynthesis.

2. What is the speed of light?

  1. 3 × 10^6 m/s
  2. 3 × 10^8 m/s
  3. 3 × 10^10 m/s
  4. 3 × 10^4 m/s

Answer: 3 × 10^8 m/s

Explanation: The speed of light in vacuum is approximately 3 × 10^8 m/s (300,000 km/s).

3. Which part of the plant makes food using sunlight?

  1. Roots
  2. Stem
  3. Leaf
  4. Flower

Answer: Leaf

Explanation: Leaves contain chlorophyll (green pigment) and perform photosynthesis to make food using sunlight, CO2, and water.

4. What type of energy does the Sun give to Earth?

  1. Mechanical energy
  2. Chemical energy
  3. Nuclear energy
  4. Solar (radiant) energy

Answer: Solar (radiant) energy

Explanation: The Sun releases nuclear energy through fusion reactions, which reaches Earth as solar/radiant energy (light and heat).

5. Which vitamin is produced in the body by exposure to sunlight?

  1. Vitamin A
  2. Vitamin B12
  3. Vitamin C
  4. Vitamin D

Answer: Vitamin D

Explanation: Vitamin D is synthesized in the skin when exposed to ultraviolet B (UVB) radiation from sunlight. It is essential for bone health.

6. Sound cannot travel through:

  1. Water
  2. Air
  3. Steel
  4. Vacuum

Answer: Vacuum

Explanation: Sound requires a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel. It cannot travel through a vacuum because there are no particles to transmit the vibrations.

7. The phenomenon of light bending when it passes from one medium to another is:

  1. Reflection
  2. Diffraction
  3. Refraction
  4. Dispersion

Answer: Refraction

Explanation: Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another with different optical density (e.g., from air to glass).

8. Which gas is produced during photosynthesis and released into the atmosphere?

  1. Carbon Dioxide
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Oxygen
  4. Methane

Answer: Oxygen

Explanation: Oxygen is released as a byproduct of photosynthesis when water molecules are split (photolysis) to provide electrons for the light reactions.

9. The 'Photoelectric Effect' was explained by:

  1. Isaac Newton
  2. Max Planck
  3. Albert Einstein
  4. Niels Bohr

Answer: Albert Einstein

Explanation: Albert Einstein explained the photoelectric effect in 1905 using the concept of light as photons (quanta of energy). This work won him the Nobel Prize in Physics (1921).

10. Which is the lightest element in the periodic table?

  1. Helium
  2. Hydrogen
  3. Lithium
  4. Carbon

Answer: Hydrogen

Explanation: Hydrogen (H) is the lightest element with atomic number 1 and atomic mass ~1. It is also the most abundant element in the universe.

11. The speed of light in vacuum is:

  1. 3 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately 300,000 km/s)
  2. 3 × 10⁶ m/s
  3. 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s
  4. 3 × 10⁵ m/s

Answer: 3 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately 300,000 km/s)

Explanation: The speed of light in vacuum (c) is exactly 299,792,458 m/s ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s ≈ 300,000 km/s. Nothing can travel faster than the speed of light (Einstein's special relativity).

12. Which phenomenon causes the rainbow to form?

  1. Dispersion and total internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets
  2. Reflection of sunlight only
  3. Refraction of light only
  4. Interference of light

Answer: Dispersion and total internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets

Explanation: Rainbow forms because sunlight entering a water droplet is refracted, internally reflected, and dispersed (separated into colours). The 7 colours (VIBGYOR — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) are separated.

13. Total Internal Reflection occurs when:

  1. Light travels from denser to rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle
  2. Light hits a smooth surface
  3. Light passes through a prism
  4. Light bends around obstacles

Answer: Light travels from denser to rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle

Explanation: TIR occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle. TIR is the principle behind optical fibers and diamond's brilliance.

14. A convex lens is used in:

  1. Cameras, magnifying glasses, and correcting farsightedness (hypermetropia)
  2. Correcting nearsightedness
  3. Rearview mirrors
  4. Periscopes

Answer: Cameras, magnifying glasses, and correcting farsightedness (hypermetropia)

Explanation: Convex (converging) lenses are thicker in the middle. They converge parallel light rays to a focal point. Used in cameras, magnifying glasses, telescopes (objective lens), and to correct farsightedness.

15. A concave lens is used to correct:

  1. Myopia (nearsightedness/shortsightedness)
  2. Hypermetropia (farsightedness)
  3. Astigmatism
  4. Presbyopia

Answer: Myopia (nearsightedness/shortsightedness)

Explanation: Concave (diverging) lenses are thinner in the middle and diverge light rays. They are used to correct myopia (nearsightedness) — where distant objects appear blurry.

16. Which colour of light has the shortest wavelength?

  1. Violet
  2. Red
  3. Yellow
  4. Blue

Answer: Violet

Explanation: Violet light has the shortest wavelength (~380-450 nm) and highest frequency in the visible spectrum. Red has the longest wavelength (~620-750 nm) and lowest frequency. The visible spectrum is VIBGYOR.

17. The primary colours of light (additive colour mixing) are:

  1. Red, Green, Blue (RGB)
  2. Red, Yellow, Blue
  3. Red, Green, Yellow
  4. Cyan, Magenta, Yellow

Answer: Red, Green, Blue (RGB)

Explanation: Primary colours of light: Red, Green, Blue (RGB — additive mixing). Combining all three gives white light. Computer/TV screens use RGB. Pigment primary colours (subtractive) are Cyan, Magenta, Yellow (CMY).

18. Sound travels fastest in:

  1. Solids (e.g., steel ~5,960 m/s)
  2. Liquids
  3. Gases
  4. Vacuum

Answer: Solids (e.g., steel ~5,960 m/s)

Explanation: Sound is a mechanical wave (needs a medium). It travels fastest in solids (5,960 m/s in steel), slower in liquids (1,497 m/s in water), and slowest in gases (343 m/s in air at 20°C). It cannot travel in vacuum.

19. The speed of sound in air at 20°C is approximately:

  1. 343 m/s
  2. 300 m/s
  3. 1,497 m/s
  4. 1,000 m/s

Answer: 343 m/s

Explanation: Speed of sound in air at 20°C is about 343 m/s (approximately 1,235 km/h). Mach 1 = speed of sound. Aircraft flying faster than Mach 1 are supersonic.

20. The human ear can hear sounds in the frequency range:

  1. 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)
  2. 2 Hz to 200 Hz
  3. 200 Hz to 200,000 Hz
  4. 0-20 Hz

Answer: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)

Explanation: The human audible range is 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Sounds below 20 Hz are infrasound (heard by elephants and whales). Sounds above 20,000 Hz are ultrasound (heard by bats and dogs). Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz).

21. SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging) works on the principle of:

  1. Reflection of ultrasound waves
  2. Interference of sound waves
  3. Diffraction of sound
  4. Resonance of sound

Answer: Reflection of ultrasound waves

Explanation: SONAR uses ultrasound waves emitted into water; they reflect off objects (submarines, fish, seabed) and return to the source. By measuring the time taken, distance is calculated. Used in submarines and fish finding.

22. The 'kitchen of the cell' (food factory) is:

  1. Chloroplast
  2. Mitochondria
  3. Ribosome
  4. Golgi apparatus

Answer: Chloroplast

Explanation: Chloroplasts are called the 'kitchen of the cell' because they carry out photosynthesis — using sunlight, CO₂, and water to produce glucose (food) and oxygen. Found only in plant cells. Contain chlorophyll.

23. Nuclear fusion is the process of:

  1. Combining light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy
  2. Splitting heavy nuclei
  3. Beta decay
  4. Alpha emission

Answer: Combining light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy

Explanation: Nuclear fusion: light nuclei (H-2 deuterium + H-3 tritium) fuse at extremely high temperatures to form heavier nuclei (He-4), releasing enormous energy. Occurs in stars (the sun). Future clean energy source (ITER project).

24. Einstein's famous equation E = mc² means:

  1. Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared — mass and energy are equivalent
  2. Energy = mass × charge
  3. E = momentum × velocity
  4. E = force × time

Answer: Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared — mass and energy are equivalent

Explanation: E = mc² (Einstein, 1905): energy (E) = mass (m) × speed of light squared (c²). This shows mass and energy are equivalent. Small mass converts to enormous energy (nuclear reactions). Basis for nuclear technology.

25. Lasers work on the principle of:

  1. Stimulated emission of radiation — coherent, monochromatic, collimated light
  2. Spontaneous emission
  3. Fluorescence
  4. Bioluminescence

Answer: Stimulated emission of radiation — coherent, monochromatic, collimated light

Explanation: LASER (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation): photons stimulate excited atoms to emit identical photons — creating coherent (same phase), monochromatic (single wavelength), collimated (parallel) light. Used in medicine, cutting, communications.
