Chemistry Reactions MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Chemistry Reactions questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 60 questions.

1. Rusting of iron is a chemical process called:

  1. Sublimation
  2. Corrosion (oxidation)
  3. Reduction
  4. Decomposition

Answer: Corrosion (oxidation)

Explanation: Rusting is the corrosion of iron. It is an oxidation reaction: 4Fe + 3O2 + 6H2O → 4Fe(OH)3 (hydrated iron oxide). Moisture and oxygen are needed.

2. Rusting of iron is an example of:

  1. Oxidation (iron reacts with oxygen and water to form iron oxide)
  2. Reduction
  3. Neutralization
  4. Fermentation

Answer: Oxidation (iron reacts with oxygen and water to form iron oxide)

Explanation: Rusting is oxidation: 4Fe + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃ → 2Fe₂O₃·3H₂O (hydrated iron oxide). Prevention: painting, galvanizing (zinc coating), oiling, stainless steel (iron + chromium alloy).

3. Transition metals are characterized by:

  1. Partially filled d-orbitals — multiple oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalytic activity
  2. Only one oxidation state
  3. Being non-metals
  4. Having no metallic properties

Answer: Partially filled d-orbitals — multiple oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalytic activity

Explanation: Transition metals (Groups 3-12) have partially filled d-orbitals. Properties: multiple oxidation states (Fe can be +2 or +3), form coloured compounds (Cu is blue, Cr is green), good catalysts (Fe in Haber process, Pt in catalytic converters).

4. Oxidation is:

  1. Loss of electrons (or gain of oxygen, or loss of hydrogen)
  2. Gain of electrons
  3. Loss of protons
  4. Gain of neutrons

Answer: Loss of electrons (or gain of oxygen, or loss of hydrogen)

Explanation: Oxidation: loss of electrons (or gain of oxygen, or loss of hydrogen). Reduction: gain of electrons (or loss of oxygen, or gain of hydrogen). Redox reactions involve simultaneous oxidation and reduction. OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

5. A catalyst is a substance that:

  1. Increases reaction rate without being consumed (remains unchanged after reaction)
  2. Is consumed in the reaction
  3. Decreases temperature
  4. Increases the amount of product

Answer: Increases reaction rate without being consumed (remains unchanged after reaction)

Explanation: A catalyst speeds up a chemical reaction by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy, without being consumed in the process. Biological catalysts are called enzymes.

6. In a chemical reaction, the law of conservation of mass states:

  1. Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products (Lavoisier, 1774)
  2. Mass increases in exothermic reactions
  3. Mass decreases in endothermic reactions
  4. Mass is not conserved in reactions

Answer: Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products (Lavoisier, 1774)

Explanation: Antoine Lavoisier (1774) established: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products. This is why chemical equations must be balanced.

7. The process of nitrogen fixation is carried out by:

  1. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium in legume root nodules, Azotobacter free-living in soil, Cyanobacteria in water/soil) — convert N₂ gas to NH₃
  2. Plants directly
  3. Lightning (some, but minor)
  4. Only Haber Process industrially

Answer: Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium in legume root nodules, Azotobacter free-living in soil, Cyanobacteria in water/soil) — convert N₂ gas to NH₃

Explanation: Biological nitrogen fixation: bacteria reduce N₂ → NH₃ using enzyme nitrogenase. Rhizobium (symbiotic with legumes: peas, beans, soy, peanuts). Azotobacter (free-living aerobic). Cyanobacteria (e.g., Anabaena). Haber-Bosch process (industrial, 400-500°C, 150-200 atm, Fe catalyst): produces artificial fertilizers.

8. Which of the following reactions involves the combination of two or more substances to form a single product?

  1. Decomposition reaction
  2. Combination reaction
  3. Displacement reaction
  4. Double displacement reaction

Answer: Combination reaction

Explanation: A combination reaction is defined as a reaction where two or more reactants combine to form a single product.

9. The reaction between an acid and a base to form salt and water is known as:

  1. Oxidation
  2. Reduction
  3. Neutralization
  4. Hydrolysis

Answer: Neutralization

Explanation: Neutralization is the chemical reaction between an acid and a base that produces a salt and water.

10. In the reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, the substance that is oxidized is:

  1. Oxygen
  2. Hydrogen
  3. Water
  4. Both hydrogen and oxygen

Answer: Hydrogen

Explanation: Hydrogen gains oxygen to form water, so it is oxidized; oxygen is reduced.

11. Which type of reaction is represented by the equation: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃?

  1. Combination
  2. Decomposition
  3. Single displacement
  4. Double displacement

Answer: Double displacement

Explanation: This is a double displacement reaction where ions exchange between two compounds to form new compounds.

12. The process of gaining oxygen or losing hydrogen during a chemical reaction is called:

  1. Reduction
  2. Oxidation
  3. Catalysis
  4. Electrolysis

Answer: Oxidation

Explanation: Oxidation is defined as the gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen by a substance.

13. Which of the following is an example of a decomposition reaction?

  1. Burning of coal
  2. Photosynthesis
  3. Electrolysis of water
  4. Rusting of iron

Answer: Electrolysis of water

Explanation: Electrolysis of water breaks down water into hydrogen and oxygen gases, which is a decomposition reaction.

14. When zinc metal is added to copper sulphate solution, the blue colour fades. This is an example of:

  1. Combination reaction
  2. Decomposition reaction
  3. Displacement reaction
  4. Double displacement reaction

Answer: Displacement reaction

Explanation: Zinc displaces copper from copper sulphate, forming zinc sulphate and copper metal, a single displacement reaction.

15. The chemical reaction in which heat is absorbed is called:

  1. Exothermic reaction
  2. Endothermic reaction
  3. Redox reaction
  4. Catalytic reaction

Answer: Endothermic reaction

Explanation: An endothermic reaction absorbs heat energy from the surroundings.

16. Rancidity in food is caused by the reaction of fats and oils with:

  1. Nitrogen
  2. Oxygen
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Water vapor

Answer: Oxygen

Explanation: Rancidity is the oxidation of fats and oils when exposed to oxygen in the air.

17. Which of the following is an example of a combination reaction?

  1. 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
  2. CaCO3 → CaO + CO2
  3. Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu
  4. AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO3

Answer: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O

Explanation: A combination reaction involves two or more reactants combining to form a single product.

18. Which type of reaction is represented by the electrolysis of water?

  1. Combination
  2. Decomposition
  3. Displacement
  4. Double displacement

Answer: Decomposition

Explanation: Electrolysis of water breaks water into hydrogen and oxygen, which is a decomposition reaction.

19. A solution with pH 3 is how many times more acidic than a solution with pH 5?

  1. 2
  2. 10
  3. 100
  4. 1000

Answer: 100

Explanation: The pH scale is logarithmic; each unit change represents a tenfold change in H+ concentration, so pH 3 is 100 times more acidic than pH 5.

20. The reaction between an acid and a base to form salt and water is called?

  1. Hydrolysis
  2. Neutralization
  3. Saponification
  4. Esterification

Answer: Neutralization

Explanation: Neutralization is the reaction of an acid with a base to produce salt and water.

21. In the reaction Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu, which element is oxidized?

  1. Zn
  2. Cu
  3. S
  4. O

Answer: Zn

Explanation: Zinc loses electrons (oxidation number increases from 0 to +2), so it is oxidized.

22. Which of the following reactions is endothermic?

  1. Burning of methane
  2. Decomposition of calcium carbonate
  3. Neutralization of HCl and NaOH
  4. Respiration

Answer: Decomposition of calcium carbonate

Explanation: Decomposition of calcium carbonate requires heat input, making it an endothermic reaction.

23. Which metal can displace hydrogen from dilute acid?

  1. Copper
  2. Silver
  3. Zinc
  4. Gold

Answer: Zinc

Explanation: Zinc is more reactive than hydrogen and can displace it from dilute acids.

24. The reaction between silver nitrate and sodium chloride forms a white precipitate. The precipitate is?

  1. AgCl
  2. NaNO3
  3. Ag2O
  4. NaCl

Answer: AgCl

Explanation: AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl (white precipitate) + NaNO3, so the precipitate is silver chloride.

25. Which substance is used as a catalyst in the hydrogenation of vegetable oils?

  1. Nickel
  2. Iron
  3. Platinum
  4. Vanadium pentoxide

Answer: Nickel

Explanation: Nickel is commonly used as a catalyst in the hydrogenation of vegetable oils to produce margarine.
