26. The main site of absorption of nutrients is:

  1. Small intestine (especially the jejunum)
  2. Stomach
  3. Large intestine
  4. Liver

Answer: Small intestine (especially the jejunum)

Explanation: The small intestine (particularly the jejunum) is the main site of nutrient absorption. Villi and microvilli (brush border) greatly increase surface area. Large intestine absorbs water and forms feces.

27. The World Wide Web (WWW) was invented by:

  1. Tim Berners-Lee (1989-91)
  2. Bill Gates
  3. Steve Jobs
  4. Al Gore

Answer: Tim Berners-Lee (1989-91)

Explanation: Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web in 1989 (proposal) and 1991 (public launch) at CERN, Switzerland. He created HTML, URL, and HTTP. He deliberately made it free and open, not proprietary.

28. The first artificial satellite was:

  1. Sputnik 1 (USSR, 4 October 1957)
  2. Explorer 1 (USA)
  3. Aryabhata (India)
  4. Vanguard (USA)

Answer: Sputnik 1 (USSR, 4 October 1957)

Explanation: Sputnik 1 (Soviet Union, 4 October 1957) was the world's first artificial satellite. This launched the Space Age and the Space Race between USA and USSR. India's first satellite: Aryabhata (1975).

29. The alkali metals (Group 1) are highly reactive because:

  1. They have only 1 valence electron (easily lost to form +1 ions)
  2. They are radioactive
  3. They have many electrons
  4. They are non-metals

Answer: They have only 1 valence electron (easily lost to form +1 ions)

Explanation: Alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr) each have 1 valence electron that is easily lost. They react vigorously with water (sodium + water → NaOH + H₂ gas). Reactivity increases down the group.

30. Halogens (Group 17) are reactive non-metals because:

  1. They have 7 valence electrons and need just 1 more to complete their octet
  2. They have 8 valence electrons
  3. They are always liquid
  4. They have no neutrons

Answer: They have 7 valence electrons and need just 1 more to complete their octet

Explanation: Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At) have 7 valence electrons — one short of a full octet. They readily gain one electron (forming -1 ions in ionic bonds) or share electrons (covalent bonds). Fluorine is the most reactive non-metal.

31. Washing soda (Sodium carbonate) formula is:

  1. Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
  2. NaHCO₃
  3. NaOH
  4. Na₂SO₄

Answer: Na₂CO₃·10H₂O

Explanation: Washing soda is hydrated sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O). Used in glass manufacturing, paper industry, laundry, and water softening. Baking soda is NaHCO₃. Caustic soda is NaOH.

32. Galvanizing is coating iron with:

  1. Zinc (to prevent corrosion/rusting)
  2. Tin
  3. Chromium
  4. Paint only

Answer: Zinc (to prevent corrosion/rusting)

Explanation: Galvanizing is coating iron/steel with zinc to prevent rusting. Even if the zinc coating is scratched, zinc (more reactive) corrodes preferentially (sacrificial protection), protecting iron. Used in buckets, roofing sheets, pipes.

33. Iron deficiency causes:

  1. Anaemia (reduced haemoglobin — reduced oxygen-carrying capacity)
  2. Goitre
  3. Rickets
  4. Night blindness

Answer: Anaemia (reduced haemoglobin — reduced oxygen-carrying capacity)

Explanation: Iron deficiency anaemia is the most common nutritional deficiency worldwide. Iron is needed for haemoglobin synthesis. Symptoms: fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath. Sources: red meat, leafy greens, lentils.

34. Fat-soluble vitamins are:

  1. Vitamins A, D, E, and K (stored in fatty tissue and liver)
  2. Vitamins B and C
  3. Vitamins A, B, C, and D
  4. Vitamins C and K

Answer: Vitamins A, D, E, and K (stored in fatty tissue and liver)

Explanation: Fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E, K — absorbed with dietary fat and stored in body fat/liver (toxicity possible from excess). Water-soluble vitamins: B complex and C — not stored in body (excess excreted in urine, toxicity rare).

35. Decomposers in an ecosystem are:

  1. Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Secondary consumers
  4. Producers

Answer: Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients

Explanation: Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead organisms and waste products into simpler inorganic substances (minerals, CO₂, water), recycling nutrients back into the soil/ecosystem. Essential for nutrient cycling.

36. The chemical used to test for the presence of starch is:

  1. Iodine solution (turns blue-black in presence of starch)
  2. Benedict's solution
  3. Biuret reagent
  4. Fehling's solution

Answer: Iodine solution (turns blue-black in presence of starch)

Explanation: Iodine solution turns from brown/yellow to blue-black in the presence of starch. Benedict's solution tests for reducing sugars (glucose, fructose). Biuret reagent tests for proteins.

37. Electrolysis of water produces:

  1. Hydrogen at cathode, Oxygen at anode (ratio 2:1 by volume)
  2. Oxygen at cathode, Hydrogen at anode
  3. Only hydrogen
  4. Only oxygen

Answer: Hydrogen at cathode, Oxygen at anode (ratio 2:1 by volume)

Explanation: In electrolysis of water: cathode (negative electrode) produces H₂, anode (positive electrode) produces O₂. Ratio H₂:O₂ = 2:1 by volume. Electrolyte (like H₂SO₄ or NaOH) is added to increase conductivity.

38. Which polymer is used to make non-stick cookware?

  1. Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE/Teflon)
  2. Polythene (LDPE)
  3. Nylon
  4. PVC

Answer: Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE/Teflon)

Explanation: Teflon (PTFE — polytetrafluoroethylene) is used for non-stick coatings. Its C-F bonds are very strong, making it chemically inert, heat-resistant, and giving it extremely low friction (non-stick property).

39. Nylon was the world's first synthetic fiber, made from:

  1. Polyamide (adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine) — invented by DuPont, 1935
  2. Cellulose
  3. Polyester
  4. Petroleum wax

Answer: Polyamide (adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine) — invented by DuPont, 1935

Explanation: Nylon (polyamide) was invented by Wallace Carothers at DuPont (1935). It was first used for stockings (1940). Strong, elastic, and resistant to wear — used in ropes, toothbrushes, parachutes, and clothing.

40. Bile is produced by the liver and stored in:

  1. Gallbladder — released into small intestine to emulsify fats
  2. Pancreas
  3. Stomach
  4. Large intestine

Answer: Gallbladder — released into small intestine to emulsify fats

Explanation: Bile is produced by the liver, stored and concentrated in the gallbladder, and released into the duodenum (small intestine). Bile emulsifies fats (breaks large fat droplets into smaller ones), increasing surface area for lipase enzymes to digest them.

41. Absorption of most digested nutrients occurs in:

  1. Small intestine (jejunum and ileum) — finger-like villi increase surface area
  2. Stomach
  3. Large intestine
  4. Mouth

Answer: Small intestine (jejunum and ileum) — finger-like villi increase surface area

Explanation: The small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) is the main site of nutrient absorption. Villi and microvilli (brush border) greatly increase the surface area. The large intestine mainly absorbs water and electrolytes.

42. The male reproductive organ in flowers is the:

  1. Stamen (consists of anther and filament — anther produces pollen grains)
  2. Pistil
  3. Sepal
  4. Petal

Answer: Stamen (consists of anther and filament — anther produces pollen grains)

Explanation: Stamen is the male reproductive organ. It has: anther (produces and releases pollen/male gametes) and filament (supports the anther). The pistil (carpel) is the female organ: stigma + style + ovary.

43. The unit of work and energy in the SI system is:

  1. Joule (J) — 1 Joule = 1 Newton × 1 meter
  2. Watt
  3. Pascal
  4. Newton

Answer: Joule (J) — 1 Joule = 1 Newton × 1 meter

Explanation: Joule is the SI unit of work, energy, and heat. 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s². Power (rate of doing work) is measured in Watts (1 W = 1 J/s). 1 kWh (kilowatt-hour) = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

44. The energy stored in a stretched spring is:

  1. Elastic potential energy (EPE = ½kx² where k is spring constant and x is extension)
  2. Kinetic energy
  3. Chemical energy
  4. Nuclear energy

Answer: Elastic potential energy (EPE = ½kx² where k is spring constant and x is extension)

Explanation: Elastic potential energy is stored in a deformed spring: EPE = ½kx² (Hooke's Law: F = kx). When released, EPE converts to kinetic energy. Applications: watch springs, spring scales, shock absorbers, athletic pole vault.

45. Carbon can form so many compounds because:

  1. It has 4 valence electrons and can form 4 covalent bonds, chain (catenation), and ring structures with itself and many elements
  2. It is the most abundant element
  3. It is a metal
  4. It has 12 atomic mass

Answer: It has 4 valence electrons and can form 4 covalent bonds, chain (catenation), and ring structures with itself and many elements

Explanation: Carbon's unique properties enabling millions of organic compounds: (1) tetravalency — 4 covalent bonds possible, (2) catenation — forms long chains/rings of C-C bonds, (3) forms bonds with H, O, N, S, halogens. Branch of chemistry studying carbon compounds is organic chemistry.

46. The process of burning fuels in limited oxygen to produce carbon monoxide (CO) instead of CO₂ is:

  1. Incomplete combustion — CO is a colorless, odorless, toxic gas (binds hemoglobin 200× more than O₂)
  2. Complete combustion
  3. Oxidation
  4. Rusting

Answer: Incomplete combustion — CO is a colorless, odorless, toxic gas (binds hemoglobin 200× more than O₂)

Explanation: Incomplete combustion (limited O₂): C + O₂ → CO (instead of CO₂). CO is extremely toxic — binds hemoglobin 200 times more tightly than O₂, preventing O₂ transport. Vehicle exhaust and faulty gas heaters are sources. Symptoms: headache, dizziness, death.

47. Which chemical is used in fire extinguishers to put out class B (liquid/oil) fires?

  1. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — displaces oxygen, smothering the fire without damaging electrical equipment
  2. Water (damages electronics, spreads oil fires)
  3. Sodium bicarbonate foam (class B)
  4. Dry sand

Answer: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — displaces oxygen, smothering the fire without damaging electrical equipment

Explanation: CO₂ extinguishers: CO₂ gas displaces oxygen, extinguishing the fire. Safe for electrical fires (non-conductive). Class A (solid): water or foam. Class B (flammable liquids): CO₂, dry chemical, foam. Class C (electrical): CO₂, dry chemical (not water).

48. GPS (Global Positioning System) works by:

  1. Receiving signals from at least 4 satellites and calculating position by trilateration (timing of signals)
  2. Mobile phone towers
  3. Ground-based radar
  4. Magnetic compass

Answer: Receiving signals from at least 4 satellites and calculating position by trilateration (timing of signals)

Explanation: GPS uses a constellation of 24+ satellites. The receiver measures the time delay of signals from multiple satellites (minimum 4) and calculates its 3D position by trilateration. Accuracy: ~5 meters for civilian use. Developed by US DoD; India's equivalent: NavIC (IRNSS).

49. The orbital period of Earth around the Sun is:

  1. 365.25 days (365 days 5 hours 48 minutes 46 seconds) — which is why we add a leap year every 4 years
  2. 24 hours
  3. 30 days
  4. 100 days

Answer: 365.25 days (365 days 5 hours 48 minutes 46 seconds) — which is why we add a leap year every 4 years

Explanation: Earth takes 365.25 days to orbit the Sun (one solar year). To account for the extra 0.25 days, we add a leap day (Feb 29) every 4 years. Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours (one day). The Moon orbits Earth in ~27.3 days.

50. Dengue fever is caused by:

  1. DENV virus (4 serotypes) transmitted by Aedes aegypti mosquito (also Aedes albopictus) — bites during daytime
  2. Plasmodium parasite
  3. Bacteria
  4. Fungi

Answer: DENV virus (4 serotypes) transmitted by Aedes aegypti mosquito (also Aedes albopictus) — bites during daytime

Explanation: Dengue virus (DENV, 4 serotypes: DENV-1 to 4) is transmitted by the Aedes aegypti mosquito (and A. albopictus). Unlike malaria mosquitoes (which bite at night), Aedes bites during daytime. Dengue causes high fever, severe body ache, rash, and can cause hemorrhagic fever (severe dengue).
