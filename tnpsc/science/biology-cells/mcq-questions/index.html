26. AIDS is caused by:

  1. HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)
  2. Human Papillomavirus (HPV)
  3. Herpes virus
  4. Bacteria

Answer: HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)

Explanation: AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) is caused by HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus). HIV attacks CD4⁺ T cells (helper T cells), weakening the immune system. Transmitted through blood, sexual contact, or mother to child.

27. Vaccination works by:

  1. Stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies without causing disease
  2. Killing bacteria directly
  3. Providing immediate immunity with antibodies
  4. Boosting white blood cell count only

Answer: Stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies without causing disease

Explanation: Vaccines contain weakened/killed pathogens or their antigens. They stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies and memory cells. If the real pathogen attacks later, memory cells recognize it and mount a quick, strong response.

28. The Centre for Cellular and Molecular Biology (CCMB) is located in:

  1. Hyderabad (Telangana) — premier biological research institution under CSIR
  2. Bengaluru
  3. Pune
  4. Chennai

Answer: Hyderabad (Telangana) — premier biological research institution under CSIR

Explanation: CCMB (Council of Scientific and Industrial Research - Centre for Cellular and Molecular Biology) is in Hyderabad. It conducted pioneering work on genome sequencing of Indian populations, ancient DNA, and COVID-19 research during the pandemic.

29. Liquid nitrogen is used to:

  1. Store biological samples, freeze food (cryogenic freezing), cryotherapy, and semiconductor manufacturing — boils at -196°C
  2. Produce oxygen
  3. As a fuel
  4. Sterilize water

Answer: Store biological samples, freeze food (cryogenic freezing), cryotherapy, and semiconductor manufacturing — boils at -196°C

Explanation: Liquid nitrogen (LN₂, boiling point -196°C = 77 K) is used for: cryogenic preservation (sperm, eggs, stem cells), food industry (quick-freezing maintains quality), dermatology (cryotherapy for warts), and cooling electronics. It is produced by fractional distillation of liquid air.

30. The fat-soluble vitamins are:

  1. Vitamins A, D, E, and K — stored in liver and fatty tissues (excess accumulates and can be toxic)
  2. Vitamins B-complex and C (water-soluble — excreted in urine, not stored)
  3. All vitamins
  4. Only Vitamin D

Answer: Vitamins A, D, E, and K — stored in liver and fatty tissues (excess accumulates and can be toxic)

Explanation: Fat-soluble vitamins (ADEK) are absorbed with dietary fat and stored in liver/adipose tissue. Excess can build up to toxic levels. Water-soluble vitamins (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7, B9, B12, and C) are not stored — excess excreted in urine, so daily intake is more important.

31. Scurvy is caused by deficiency of:

  1. Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) — needed for collagen synthesis; symptoms: bleeding gums, loose teeth, slow wound healing
  2. Vitamin D
  3. Vitamin A
  4. Vitamin B1

Answer: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) — needed for collagen synthesis; symptoms: bleeding gums, loose teeth, slow wound healing

Explanation: Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency): Vitamin C is essential for collagen synthesis (connective tissue protein). Symptoms: bleeding gums, loose teeth, skin spots, fatigue, delayed wound healing. Common in sailors on long voyages without fresh produce. James Lind (1747) showed citrus fruits prevent scurvy.

32. Carbohydrates provide how much energy per gram?

  1. 4 kcal/g (same as proteins); fats provide 9 kcal/g; alcohol provides 7 kcal/g
  2. 9 kcal/g
  3. 2 kcal/g
  4. 7 kcal/g

Answer: 4 kcal/g (same as proteins); fats provide 9 kcal/g; alcohol provides 7 kcal/g

Explanation: Energy content: carbohydrates = 4 kcal/g (16.7 kJ/g), proteins = 4 kcal/g, fats = 9 kcal/g (37.7 kJ/g, most energy-dense), alcohol = 7 kcal/g. Carbs are the preferred energy source (converted to glucose for cellular respiration). Daily carb requirement: ~45-65% of total calories.

33. Which of the following is the primary function of the Golgi apparatus in a eukaryotic cell?

  1. Protein synthesis
  2. Lipid synthesis
  3. Modification and packaging of proteins
  4. ATP production

Answer: Modification and packaging of proteins

Explanation: The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts, and packages proteins for transport to other parts of the cell or secretion.

34. In which phase of the cell cycle does DNA replication occur?

  1. G1 phase
  2. S phase
  3. G2 phase
  4. M phase

Answer: S phase

Explanation: DNA replication takes place during the S (synthesis) phase of interphase.

35. Which organelle is responsible for the detoxification of drugs and poisons in liver cells?

  1. Lysosome
  2. Peroxisome
  3. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum
  4. Rough endoplasmic reticulum

Answer: Smooth endoplasmic reticulum

Explanation: Smooth endoplasmic reticulum contains enzymes that detoxify harmful substances and also synthesizes lipids.

36. What is the main difference between a prokaryotic cell and a eukaryotic cell?

  1. Presence of ribosomes
  2. Presence of a nucleus
  3. Presence of cytoplasm
  4. Presence of cell membrane

Answer: Presence of a nucleus

Explanation: Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus, while eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane.

37. Which cell organelle is known as the 'powerhouse of the cell'?

  1. Nucleus
  2. Ribosome
  3. Mitochondrion
  4. Chloroplast

Answer: Mitochondrion

Explanation: Mitochondria generate ATP through oxidative phosphorylation, providing energy for cellular activities.

38. In plant cells, which structure provides rigidity and maintains cell shape?

  1. Cell membrane
  2. Cell wall
  3. Vacuole
  4. Plasmodesmata

Answer: Cell wall

Explanation: The cell wall, composed mainly of cellulose, provides structural support and maintains the shape of plant cells.

39. Which of the following is NOT a component of the endomembrane system?

  1. Endoplasmic reticulum
  2. Golgi apparatus
  3. Lysosomes
  4. Mitochondria

Answer: Mitochondria

Explanation: Mitochondria are not part of the endomembrane system; they are semi-autonomous organelles with their own DNA.

40. The process by which a cell engulfs large particles or microorganisms is called:

  1. Pinocytosis
  2. Phagocytosis
  3. Exocytosis
  4. Osmosis

Answer: Phagocytosis

Explanation: Phagocytosis is the engulfment of large solid particles by the cell membrane, forming a phagosome.

41. Which of the following statements about lysosomes is correct?

  1. They synthesize proteins
  2. They contain hydrolytic enzymes for digestion
  3. They produce ATP
  4. They store genetic material

Answer: They contain hydrolytic enzymes for digestion

Explanation: Lysosomes are membrane-bound vesicles containing digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and foreign substances.

42. During mitosis, the stage in which sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles is:

  1. Prophase
  2. Metaphase
  3. Anaphase
  4. Telophase

Answer: Anaphase

Explanation: In anaphase, the centromeres split and sister chromatids are pulled apart toward opposite spindle poles.

43. Which organelle is responsible for ATP production in eukaryotic cells?

  1. Ribosome
  2. Mitochondria
  3. Chloroplast
  4. Golgi apparatus

Answer: Mitochondria

Explanation: Mitochondria are known as the powerhouses of the cell, generating ATP through cellular respiration.

44. The cell wall of plant cells is primarily composed of which substance?

  1. Cellulose
  2. Chitin
  3. Peptidoglycan
  4. Lignin

Answer: Cellulose

Explanation: Plant cell walls are mainly made of cellulose, a polysaccharide providing structural support.

45. What is the primary function of ribosomes in a cell?

  1. Lipid synthesis
  2. Protein synthesis
  3. DNA replication
  4. Energy production

Answer: Protein synthesis

Explanation: Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis, translating mRNA into polypeptide chains.

46. Which of the following is a difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?

  1. Presence of nucleus
  2. Presence of cell membrane
  3. Presence of cytoplasm
  4. Presence of ribosomes

Answer: Presence of nucleus

Explanation: Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus, while eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus.

47. In plant cells, which organelle contains its own DNA and is involved in photosynthesis?

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Chloroplast
  3. Vacuole
  4. Nucleus

Answer: Chloroplast

Explanation: Chloroplasts have their own DNA and carry out photosynthesis, converting light energy to chemical energy.

48. During which phase of mitosis do chromosomes line up at the equatorial plate?

  1. Prophase
  2. Metaphase
  3. Anaphase
  4. Telophase

Answer: Metaphase

Explanation: In metaphase, chromosomes align along the metaphase plate (equatorial plate) before separation.

49. The fluid matrix inside the cell that surrounds organelles is called?

  1. Cytosol
  2. Nucleoplasm
  3. Cytoplasm
  4. Stroma

Answer: Cytosol

Explanation: Cytosol is the semi-fluid substance filling the cell, excluding the organelles.

50. Which cell organelle is involved in the modification, packaging, and sorting of proteins?

  1. Endoplasmic reticulum
  2. Golgi apparatus
  3. Lysosome
  4. Peroxisome

Answer: Golgi apparatus

Explanation: The Golgi apparatus modifies, packages, and sorts proteins for transport to their destinations.
