Environment MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Environment questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 80 questions.

1. The study of living organisms and their interactions with the environment is called:

  1. Ecology
  2. Biology
  3. Zoology
  4. Entomology

Answer: Ecology

Explanation: Ecology studies the interactions between organisms and their environment (biotic and abiotic factors). Ernst Haeckel coined the term 'Ecology' in 1866. Ecosystem = community of organisms + their environment.

2. The term 'ecology' was coined by:

  1. Ernst Haeckel (1866) — from Greek 'oikos' (household) + 'logos' (study) — study of interactions between organisms and their environment
  2. Charles Darwin
  3. Gregor Mendel
  4. Aristotle

Answer: Ernst Haeckel (1866) — from Greek 'oikos' (household) + 'logos' (study) — study of interactions between organisms and their environment

Explanation: Ernst Haeckel (1866) coined 'ecology' (Ökologie in German) from Greek oikos (house/habitat) + logos (study). Ecology studies interactions between organisms and their abiotic environment. Key concepts: ecosystem, food web, energy flow, nutrient cycles, population dynamics, and biodiversity.

3. India's Samudrayaan project aims to:

  1. Send Indian astronauts (manned submersible 'Matsya 6000') to 6,000 m ocean depth to explore deep-sea resources
  2. Send astronauts to the Moon
  3. Develop ocean-based solar power
  4. Map ocean currents

Answer: Send Indian astronauts (manned submersible 'Matsya 6000') to 6,000 m ocean depth to explore deep-sea resources

Explanation: Samudrayaan (2021) is India's first manned deep-ocean mission: the 'Matsya 6000' submersible will carry 3 people to 6,000 m depth in the Indian Ocean to study polymetallic nodules (manganese, nickel, copper, cobalt) and deep-sea biodiversity. Operated by NIOT, Chennai.

4. Which greenhouse gas is primarily responsible for global warming?

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Oxygen
  3. Nitrogen
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Carbon dioxide

Explanation: Carbon dioxide is the most abundant anthropogenic greenhouse gas, trapping heat in the atmosphere.

5. The pH of acid rain is typically less than:

  1. 5.6
  2. 7.0
  3. 6.5
  4. 8.0

Answer: 5.6

Explanation: Normal rainwater has a pH of about 5.6 due to dissolved CO2; values below 5.6 indicate acid rain.

6. Which layer of the atmosphere contains the ozone layer?

  1. Stratosphere
  2. Troposphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Thermosphere

Answer: Stratosphere

Explanation: The ozone layer is located in the stratosphere, about 15-30 km above Earth's surface.

7. What is the major cause of the depletion of the ozone layer?

  1. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Sulfur dioxide
  4. Nitrogen oxides

Answer: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

Explanation: CFCs release chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy ozone molecules in the stratosphere.

8. Which of the following is a renewable source of energy?

  1. Solar power
  2. Natural gas
  3. Coal
  4. Petroleum

Answer: Solar power

Explanation: Solar power is derived from sunlight, which is naturally replenished, unlike fossil fuels.

9. BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) is a measure of:

  1. Organic pollution in water
  2. Dissolved oxygen in air
  3. Soil fertility
  4. Noise level

Answer: Organic pollution in water

Explanation: BOD indicates the amount of oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water.

10. Which of the following is a primary pollutant?

  1. Carbon monoxide
  2. Ozone
  3. Peroxyacetyl nitrate
  4. Sulfuric acid

Answer: Carbon monoxide

Explanation: Carbon monoxide is emitted directly from sources like vehicles, while the others are secondary pollutants formed in the atmosphere.

11. The term 'eutrophication' refers to:

  1. Nutrient enrichment of water bodies
  2. Soil erosion
  3. Desertification
  4. Air pollution

Answer: Nutrient enrichment of water bodies

Explanation: Eutrophication is the process where excessive nutrients (e.g., nitrates, phosphates) cause algal blooms and oxygen depletion.

12. Which of the following is a non-biodegradable waste?

  1. Plastic bottles
  2. Paper wastes
  3. Vegetable peels
  4. Cotton cloth

Answer: Plastic bottles

Explanation: Plastic bottles are synthetic polymers that do not decompose naturally, unlike organic materials.

13. The 'Chipko Movement' in India was primarily associated with:

  1. Forest conservation
  2. River pollution control
  3. Wildlife protection
  4. Soil conservation

Answer: Forest conservation

Explanation: The Chipko Movement involved villagers hugging trees to prevent deforestation, highlighting forest conservation.

14. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Oxygen
  3. Nitrogen
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Carbon dioxide

Explanation: Carbon dioxide traps heat in the atmosphere, making it a major greenhouse gas; the others are not significant greenhouse gases.

15. The term 'Biodiversity Hotspot' was coined by:

  1. Norman Myers
  2. E.O. Wilson
  3. Rachel Carson
  4. Charles Darwin

Answer: Norman Myers

Explanation: Norman Myers introduced the concept of biodiversity hotspots in 1988 to identify priority conservation areas.

16. What is the primary cause of acid rain?

  1. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Chlorofluorocarbons
  4. Ozone

Answer: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides

Explanation: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides released from burning fossil fuels form acids when mixed with atmospheric water vapor, causing acid rain.

17. Which international agreement aims to reduce greenhouse gas emissions?

  1. Montreal Protocol
  2. Kyoto Protocol
  3. Ramsar Convention
  4. CITES

Answer: Kyoto Protocol

Explanation: The Kyoto Protocol is an international treaty that commits developed countries to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

18. Which of the following is a primary greenhouse gas released from landfills due to the anaerobic decomposition of organic waste?

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Methane
  4. Oxygen

Answer: Methane

Explanation: Methane is produced by anaerobic decomposition of organic matter in landfills and is a potent greenhouse gas.

19. The term 'Biosphere' refers to which of the following?

  1. The solid outer layer of the Earth
  2. The zone where life exists on Earth
  3. The layer of gases surrounding the Earth
  4. The water bodies on Earth

Answer: The zone where life exists on Earth

Explanation: Biosphere is the global sum of all ecosystems, comprising the zone of life on Earth, including land, water, and atmosphere.

20. Which of the following is a primary cause of acid rain?

  1. Excess carbon dioxide in the atmosphere
  2. Release of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides
  3. Depletion of the ozone layer
  4. Increase in greenhouse gases

Answer: Release of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides

Explanation: Acid rain is mainly caused by emissions of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) reacting with water vapor in the atmosphere.

21. Eutrophication of water bodies is mainly caused by:

  1. Excessive algal growth
  2. Increased oxygen levels
  3. Excess of nutrients like phosphates and nitrates
  4. Acidification from industrial waste

Answer: Excess of nutrients like phosphates and nitrates

Explanation: Eutrophication occurs due to excessive nutrients (phosphates and nitrates) from fertilizers and sewage, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion.

22. Which of the following is a non-biodegradable pollutant?

  1. Cow dung
  2. Vegetable peels
  3. Plastic bags
  4. Cotton cloth

Answer: Plastic bags

Explanation: Non-biodegradable pollutants are not decomposed naturally; plastic bags remain in the environment for hundreds of years.

23. The 'Greenhouse Effect' is primarily caused by:

  1. The Earth's rotation
  2. Trapping of solar radiation by certain gases
  3. Reflection of sunlight by clouds
  4. Volcanic eruptions releasing ash

Answer: Trapping of solar radiation by certain gases

Explanation: Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, water vapor) trap infrared radiation, warming the Earth's surface; this is the greenhouse effect.

24. Which of the following is an indicator species for assessing environmental health?

  1. Cockroach
  2. Lichen
  3. Housefly
  4. Rat

Answer: Lichen

Explanation: Lichens are sensitive to air pollution, especially sulfur dioxide, and are used as bioindicators to assess air quality.

25. The term 'Biodiversity hotspot' refers to an area that is:

  1. With high species richness and high threat
  2. With low species diversity
  3. Completely protected from human interference
  4. An artificial ecosystem

Answer: With high species richness and high threat

Explanation: Biodiversity hotspots are regions with high endemism and significant habitat loss, requiring conservation priority.
