26. Which electromagnetic wave has the highest penetrating power?

  1. Gamma rays
  2. X-rays
  3. Ultraviolet rays
  4. Radio waves

Answer: Gamma rays

Explanation: Gamma rays have the highest energy and penetrating power in the EM spectrum. They can penetrate dense materials like lead (though thick lead shields them). Used in cancer treatment (radiotherapy) and sterilizing medical equipment.

27. The hormone insulin is produced by the pancreas and its function is:

  1. Lower blood glucose levels by facilitating glucose uptake into cells (deficiency → Type 1 diabetes)
  2. Increase blood glucose levels
  3. Regulate calcium levels
  4. Control growth

Answer: Lower blood glucose levels by facilitating glucose uptake into cells (deficiency → Type 1 diabetes)

Explanation: Insulin (produced by beta cells of islets of Langerhans in pancreas) lowers blood glucose by helping cells absorb glucose. Deficiency of insulin or insulin resistance leads to diabetes mellitus. Glucagon (alpha cells) raises blood glucose.

28. An ammeter measures:

  1. Electric current in amperes — connected in series with the circuit
  2. Voltage (EMF) — connected in parallel
  3. Resistance — connected in parallel
  4. Power consumption

Answer: Electric current in amperes — connected in series with the circuit

Explanation: An ammeter measures electric current (in amperes). It must be connected in series with the circuit (so all current flows through it). Voltmeter measures voltage (EMF) and is connected in parallel. Ohmmeter measures resistance.

29. Nuclear energy in power plants is generated by:

  1. Controlled nuclear fission of U-235 or Pu-239 in reactors (chain reaction releases heat to produce steam)
  2. Nuclear fusion
  3. Radioactive decay heat only
  4. Chemical combustion

Answer: Controlled nuclear fission of U-235 or Pu-239 in reactors (chain reaction releases heat to produce steam)

Explanation: Nuclear power plants use controlled fission: neutrons split U-235 (or Pu-239) atoms, releasing energy and 2-3 more neutrons (chain reaction). Heat is used to boil water → steam → turbine → electricity. India's reactors: Tarapur, Kalpakkam, Kaiga, Kudankulam, etc.

30. An ionic bond is formed by:

  1. Transfer of electrons from a metal (loses electrons, becomes cation) to a non-metal (gains electrons, becomes anion)
  2. Sharing of electrons
  3. Sharing of protons
  4. Attraction between same charges

Answer: Transfer of electrons from a metal (loses electrons, becomes cation) to a non-metal (gains electrons, becomes anion)

Explanation: Ionic bonds form when electronegativity difference is large (>1.7): metal loses electron(s) → cation; non-metal gains electron(s) → anion. Oppositely charged ions attract. Examples: NaCl, MgO, CaCl₂. Ionic compounds have high melting points, conduct electricity when dissolved.

31. The age of the universe according to current scientific consensus is approximately:

  1. 13.8 billion years (based on CMB observations by WMAP and Planck missions)
  2. 4.6 billion years (Earth's age)
  3. 6,000 years
  4. 100 billion years

Answer: 13.8 billion years (based on CMB observations by WMAP and Planck missions)

Explanation: The universe is approximately 13.8 billion years old, determined from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation and observations of the universe's expansion rate (Hubble constant). Earth is ~4.6 billion years old. The sun has ~5 billion years left.

32. Type 2 diabetes is characterized by:

  1. Insulin resistance — cells do not respond adequately to insulin (often linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and poor diet) — most common type (90% of cases)
  2. Complete absence of insulin-producing cells (autoimmune) — that is Type 1
  3. Bacterial infection of pancreas
  4. Genetic disorder only

Answer: Insulin resistance — cells do not respond adequately to insulin (often linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and poor diet) — most common type (90% of cases)

Explanation: Type 2 diabetes: cells develop insulin resistance (don't respond to insulin), so glucose cannot enter cells efficiently. Initially pancreas compensates by making more insulin; eventually it can't keep up. Risk factors: obesity, physical inactivity, poor diet, family history, age. Managed by diet, exercise, oral medications, sometimes insulin.

33. Which device is used to protect electrical circuits from excess current?

  1. Fuse (thin wire of low melting point alloy that melts and breaks the circuit when current exceeds rated value)
  2. Switch
  3. Resistor
  4. Capacitor

Answer: Fuse (thin wire of low melting point alloy that melts and breaks the circuit when current exceeds rated value)

Explanation: A fuse is a safety device — a thin wire (alloy of lead and tin) with a low melting point. Excess current heats and melts it, breaking the circuit and protecting appliances. MCB (Miniature Circuit Breaker) is a modern alternative that can be reset after tripping.

34. In a parallel circuit, the voltage across each branch is:

  1. Equal to the source voltage (same for all branches — unlike series where voltage divides)
  2. Divided equally
  3. Zero
  4. Double the source voltage

Answer: Equal to the source voltage (same for all branches — unlike series where voltage divides)

Explanation: In parallel circuits: voltage across each branch = supply voltage. Current divides among branches. Total resistance is LESS than the smallest individual resistance. Household electrical connections are parallel — all appliances get the same voltage (230V in India).

35. The rate at which electrical energy is consumed is called:

  1. Power (P = VI = I²R = V²/R), measured in Watts — 1 Watt = 1 Joule per second
  2. Energy
  3. Resistance
  4. Current

Answer: Power (P = VI = I²R = V²/R), measured in Watts — 1 Watt = 1 Joule per second

Explanation: Electrical power P = VI (Voltage × Current) = I²R = V²/R. Unit: Watt (W) or kilowatt (kW). Electrical energy consumed = P × t. 1 unit (kWh) = 1000 W × 1 hour = 3.6×10⁶ J. Electricity bills are based on units consumed.

36. A transformer works on the principle of:

  1. Mutual electromagnetic induction (Faraday's Law) — AC in primary coil induces changing magnetic field → EMF in secondary coil
  2. Direct current conversion
  3. Resistance change
  4. Capacitance

Answer: Mutual electromagnetic induction (Faraday's Law) — AC in primary coil induces changing magnetic field → EMF in secondary coil

Explanation: Transformer: AC in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic flux in the iron core, which induces EMF in the secondary coil. Ratio: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np. Step-up transformer increases voltage (for long-distance transmission to reduce current losses). Step-down reduces voltage for homes.

37. The frequency of AC (Alternating Current) supply in India is:

  1. 50 Hz — current changes direction 50 times per second (cycles per second)
  2. 60 Hz (USA/Canada)
  3. 100 Hz
  4. 25 Hz

Answer: 50 Hz — current changes direction 50 times per second (cycles per second)

Explanation: India uses 50 Hz AC supply at 230V (single phase). USA uses 60 Hz at 120V. In 50 Hz AC, the current direction reverses 100 times per second (50 complete cycles). AC is preferred for long-distance transmission because voltage can be stepped up/down using transformers.

38. The SI unit of electric charge is:

  1. Coulomb (C) — 1 Coulomb = charge of 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons; 1 Ampere = 1 Coulomb per second
  2. Volt
  3. Ampere
  4. Farad

Answer: Coulomb (C) — 1 Coulomb = charge of 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons; 1 Ampere = 1 Coulomb per second

Explanation: Coulomb (C) is the SI unit of electric charge. 1 C = charge transferred by 1 ampere current in 1 second. The charge of one electron = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C. Capacitance is measured in Farads. Voltage in Volts. Current in Amperes (= Coulombs/second).

39. The three modes of heat transfer are:

  1. Conduction (through solids, particle contact), Convection (through fluids, bulk movement), Radiation (through vacuum/medium as EM waves)
  2. Only conduction and convection
  3. Only radiation
  4. Conduction, radiation, and evaporation

Answer: Conduction (through solids, particle contact), Convection (through fluids, bulk movement), Radiation (through vacuum/medium as EM waves)

Explanation: Conduction: heat transfers through solid material by particle-to-particle collision (metals are best conductors). Convection: heat carried by moving fluid (hot fluid rises, cool sinks — basis for sea breeze, oven heating). Radiation: electromagnetic waves (infrared) transfer heat without medium (Sun→Earth).

40. The process of electroplating uses:

  1. Electrolysis — object to be plated is cathode, metal to be deposited is anode, salt solution of the metal is electrolyte
  2. Chemical deposition
  3. Heating
  4. Vacuum coating only

Answer: Electrolysis — object to be plated is cathode, metal to be deposited is anode, salt solution of the metal is electrolyte

Explanation: Electroplating: DC current through electrolyte solution. Object to be plated (cathode) gets metal ions deposited; anode (pure metal) dissolves to replenish ions. Examples: chrome plating car parts, gold plating jewelry, silver plating cutlery, zinc plating steel (galvanizing).

41. The outermost layer of Earth is called the:

  1. Crust — oceanic crust (5-10 km thick, basalt) and continental crust (30-70 km thick, granite); below is the mantle
  2. Mantle (below crust)
  3. Outer core (liquid iron/nickel)
  4. Inner core (solid iron)

Answer: Crust — oceanic crust (5-10 km thick, basalt) and continental crust (30-70 km thick, granite); below is the mantle

Explanation: Earth's internal structure from outside to inside: Crust (0-70 km) → Mantle (70-2890 km, solid rock but flows slowly) → Outer Core (2890-5150 km, liquid iron+nickel, generates magnetic field) → Inner Core (5150-6371 km, solid iron+nickel, extremely high pressure). Moho discontinuity: crust-mantle boundary.

42. The SI unit of electric resistance is:

  1. Ohm (Ω) — defined as 1 Volt per Ampere; named after Georg Simon Ohm who formulated Ohm's Law (V=IR)
  2. Ampere
  3. Watt
  4. Farad

Answer: Ohm (Ω) — defined as 1 Volt per Ampere; named after Georg Simon Ohm who formulated Ohm's Law (V=IR)

Explanation: Ohm (Ω): SI unit of electrical resistance, named after Georg Simon Ohm (Ohm's Law, 1827: V = IR). 1 Ω = 1 V/A. Resistors oppose current flow. In series: R_total = R₁+R₂+... In parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁+1/R₂+... Conductance (1/R) measured in Siemens (S).

43. Insulin is produced by:

  1. Beta cells of islets of Langerhans in the pancreas
  2. Liver
  3. Thyroid gland
  4. Adrenal gland

Answer: Beta cells of islets of Langerhans in the pancreas

Explanation: Insulin is produced by beta cells in the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas. It regulates blood glucose by enabling cells to absorb glucose. Deficiency causes diabetes mellitus (Type 1 = no insulin; Type 2 = insulin resistance).

44. A galvanometer is used to:

  1. Detect and measure small electric currents (deflects when current flows)
  2. Measure atmospheric pressure
  3. Measure temperature
  4. Measure light intensity

Answer: Detect and measure small electric currents (deflects when current flows)

Explanation: A galvanometer detects and measures small electric currents by the deflection of a coil in a magnetic field. It is the basis for ammeters (shunt added for high currents) and voltmeters (series resistor added).

45. Superconductivity occurs when:

  1. Certain materials (at very low temperatures) offer zero electrical resistance and expel magnetic fields (Meissner effect) — discovered 1911 by Kamerlingh Onnes
  2. Resistance decreases linearly with temperature
  3. Materials become semiconductors
  4. Temperature increases above room temperature

Answer: Certain materials (at very low temperatures) offer zero electrical resistance and expel magnetic fields (Meissner effect) — discovered 1911 by Kamerlingh Onnes

Explanation: Superconductivity: at very low critical temperatures (near absolute zero for conventional superconductors), resistance drops to exactly zero. Current flows indefinitely without loss. Used in MRI magnets, particle accelerators (LHC), maglev trains. High-temperature superconductors work at -135°C (still very cold).

46. The metal titanium is valued in aerospace and medical implants because:

  1. High strength-to-weight ratio, excellent corrosion resistance, biocompatibility (body doesn't reject it), and high melting point (1668°C)
  2. It is cheapest metal
  3. It is magnetic
  4. It has lowest density

Answer: High strength-to-weight ratio, excellent corrosion resistance, biocompatibility (body doesn't reject it), and high melting point (1668°C)

Explanation: Titanium (Ti): 4th most abundant metal in Earth's crust. Density: 4.5 g/cm³ (lighter than steel at 7.8 g/cm³, but stronger). Biocompatible (doesn't corrode in body fluids). Used in: aircraft frames, jet engines (high temp resistance), hip/knee implants, dental implants, spacecraft. Alloys with Al and V (Ti-6Al-4V): most common titanium alloy.

47. Which law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends?

  1. Joule's law
  2. Faraday's law
  3. Ohm's law
  4. Kirchhoff's law

Answer: Ohm's law

Explanation: Ohm's law gives the relationship V = IR, where current is proportional to voltage.

48. The resistance of a conductor is inversely proportional to its?

  1. Length
  2. Temperature
  3. Cross-sectional area
  4. Resistivity

Answer: Cross-sectional area

Explanation: Resistance R = ρL/A, so it is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area.

49. The unit of electric power is?

  1. Joule
  2. Watt
  3. Volt
  4. Ohm

Answer: Watt

Explanation: Electric power is measured in watts (W), where 1 W = 1 V·A.

50. A fuse wire is made of a material with?

  1. High melting point
  2. Low melting point
  3. High resistivity
  4. Both low melting point and high resistivity

Answer: Both low melting point and high resistivity

Explanation: Fuse wire has low melting point and high resistivity to melt quickly under overcurrent.
