Physics Electricity MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Physics Electricity questions to revise the TNPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 78 questions.

1. The 'Ohm's Law' states that:

  1. V = IR (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
  2. P = VI (Power = Voltage × Current)
  3. F = ma (Force = mass × acceleration)
  4. E = mc² (Energy = mass × speed of light squared)

Answer: V = IR (Voltage = Current × Resistance)

Explanation: Ohm's Law: V = IR. Voltage (V) across a conductor is directly proportional to the Current (I) through it, with Resistance (R) as the proportionality constant.

2. The SI unit of electric current is:

  1. Volt
  2. Ohm
  3. Ampere
  4. Watt

Answer: Ampere

Explanation: The SI unit of electric current is the Ampere (A), named after French physicist André-Marie Ampère.

3. Superconductivity is the phenomenon where:

  1. Electrical resistance becomes infinite at low temperatures
  2. Electrical resistance becomes zero below a critical temperature
  3. Materials become magnetic at high temperatures
  4. Electrons flow at the speed of light

Answer: Electrical resistance becomes zero below a critical temperature

Explanation: Superconductivity occurs when certain materials are cooled below their critical temperature, causing electrical resistance to drop to exactly zero, allowing current to flow without energy loss.

4. Ohm's Law states that:

  1. Current (I) = Voltage (V) / Resistance (R)
  2. Voltage = Current × Power
  3. Resistance = Current × Voltage
  4. Power = Resistance × Current

Answer: Current (I) = Voltage (V) / Resistance (R)

Explanation: Ohm's Law: V = I × R (Voltage = Current × Resistance). At constant temperature, current is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance. Named after Georg Simon Ohm.

5. Electric power is given by:

  1. P = V × I (Voltage × Current)
  2. P = V / I
  3. P = I / V
  4. P = R × I

Answer: P = V × I (Voltage × Current)

Explanation: Electric Power (P) = Voltage (V) × Current (I) = I²R = V²/R. The SI unit of power is Watt (W). 1 Watt = 1 Joule per second. A 60W bulb consumes 60 Joules per second.

6. A fuse wire in an electrical circuit is made of:

  1. Alloy with low melting point (tin-lead alloy)
  2. Copper
  3. Aluminium
  4. Iron

Answer: Alloy with low melting point (tin-lead alloy)

Explanation: Fuse wire is made of an alloy with a low melting point (typically tin-lead alloy). When current exceeds safe limits, the fuse wire melts (fuses), breaking the circuit and preventing damage. It is the weakest link in the circuit.

7. Electromagnets are used in:

  1. Electric bells, cranes, MRI machines, electric motors
  2. Only loudspeakers
  3. Only motors
  4. Only generators

Answer: Electric bells, cranes, MRI machines, electric motors

Explanation: Electromagnets (coil of wire wound on iron core carrying current) are used in: electric bells, cranes for lifting scrap metal, MRI machines, electric motors, generators, relays, loudspeakers, and maglev trains.

8. Heat transfer in fluids (liquids and gases) by bulk movement is called:

  1. Convection
  2. Conduction
  3. Radiation
  4. Diffusion

Answer: Convection

Explanation: Convection is heat transfer in fluids (liquids/gases) through the bulk movement of the fluid itself. Hot fluid (less dense) rises, cool fluid (denser) sinks, creating convection currents. Ocean currents and atmospheric circulation are examples.

9. Heat transfer through electromagnetic waves (without a medium) is:

  1. Radiation
  2. Conduction
  3. Convection
  4. Induction

Answer: Radiation

Explanation: Radiation is heat transfer through electromagnetic waves (infrared radiation). It does not need a medium — heat from the sun reaches Earth through vacuum. All objects above 0 K emit thermal radiation.

10. Gamma rays are:

  1. High-energy electromagnetic radiation — most penetrating type of nuclear radiation
  2. Alpha particles
  3. Beta particles
  4. X-rays from electrons

Answer: High-energy electromagnetic radiation — most penetrating type of nuclear radiation

Explanation: Gamma rays (γ) are high-energy electromagnetic radiation emitted from the nucleus during radioactive decay. They have the least ionizing power but greatest penetrating power — need several cm of lead or concrete to stop.

11. A transformer is used to:

  1. Change (increase or decrease) alternating current (AC) voltage
  2. Convert AC to DC
  3. Convert DC to AC
  4. Store electrical energy

Answer: Change (increase or decrease) alternating current (AC) voltage

Explanation: A transformer changes AC voltage using electromagnetic induction. Step-up transformers increase voltage (reduce current); step-down transformers decrease voltage (increase current). V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ (turns ratio).

12. A generator converts:

  1. Mechanical energy into electrical energy
  2. Electrical energy into mechanical energy
  3. Chemical energy into electrical energy
  4. Heat into electricity

Answer: Mechanical energy into electrical energy

Explanation: A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy (using electromagnetic induction — Faraday's law). A motor does the reverse (electrical → mechanical). Power plants use generators driven by steam turbines or water turbines.

13. Which principle is used in MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)?

  1. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) of hydrogen atoms in body tissue
  2. X-ray absorption
  3. Ultrasound reflection
  4. Radioactive isotope emission

Answer: Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) of hydrogen atoms in body tissue

Explanation: MRI uses Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) — hydrogen nuclei (protons) in body tissues align in a strong magnetic field. Radio waves flip them; as they return to alignment, they emit signals detected to create images.

14. Alternating current (AC) is preferred over DC for long-distance transmission because:

  1. Voltage can be stepped up (increasing voltage, reducing current) to reduce power loss in transmission lines
  2. AC is faster than DC
  3. DC cannot travel long distances
  4. AC generators are simpler

Answer: Voltage can be stepped up (increasing voltage, reducing current) to reduce power loss in transmission lines

Explanation: P_loss = I²R in transmission lines. By stepping up voltage (using transformers — only work with AC), current is reduced enormously, minimizing resistive heat loss. DC cannot be transformed easily.

15. The household electricity supply in India is:

  1. 230 V AC at 50 Hz
  2. 110 V AC at 60 Hz
  3. 220 V DC
  4. 415 V AC at 50 Hz

Answer: 230 V AC at 50 Hz

Explanation: India uses 230 V AC at 50 Hz for household supply. USA uses 110-120 V at 60 Hz. Three-phase supply for industries is 415 V. The frequency (50 Hz) means current changes direction 50 times per second.

16. In a series circuit, the total resistance is:

  1. Sum of individual resistances (R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃...)
  2. Less than the smallest resistance
  3. Reciprocal of sum of reciprocals
  4. Equal to the largest resistance

Answer: Sum of individual resistances (R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃...)

Explanation: In series circuits: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃... (total resistance is sum of all). Same current flows through all. Voltage divides. In parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂... (total resistance less than smallest).

17. The unit of electrical energy consumed is:

  1. Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — also called a 'unit'
  2. Joule only
  3. Watt
  4. Volt

Answer: Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — also called a 'unit'

Explanation: Electrical energy consumed = Power (kW) × Time (hours). 1 kWh = 1 'unit' of electricity on the electricity bill. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules. A 1000 W (1 kW) appliance running for 1 hour uses 1 unit.

18. Superconductors are materials that:

  1. Have zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature
  2. Have infinite resistance
  3. Conduct electricity only in one direction
  4. Only conduct heat, not electricity

Answer: Have zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature

Explanation: Superconductors exhibit zero electrical resistance below their critical temperature (Tc). Discovered by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes (1911) in mercury at 4.2 K. Used in MRI magnets, particle accelerators, maglev trains.

19. The largest planet in the Solar System is:

  1. Jupiter
  2. Saturn
  3. Uranus
  4. Neptune

Answer: Jupiter

Explanation: Jupiter is the largest planet — 1,321 Earths could fit inside it. It has a Great Red Spot (giant storm), 95 known moons (including Ganymede — the largest moon in the solar system), and a strong magnetic field.

20. The SI unit of magnetic flux is:

  1. Weber (Wb)
  2. Tesla
  3. Gauss
  4. Henry

Answer: Weber (Wb)

Explanation: Weber (Wb) is the SI unit of magnetic flux. 1 Weber = 1 Volt·second. Tesla (T) is the SI unit of magnetic flux density (magnetic field strength). 1 Tesla = 1 Weber per square meter.

21. A compass needle points towards the geographic North Pole because:

  1. Earth's geographic North Pole is magnetically a south pole (attracts the compass's north-seeking pole)
  2. Earth's core is iron
  3. Magnetic monopoles exist
  4. Earth rotates

Answer: Earth's geographic North Pole is magnetically a south pole (attracts the compass's north-seeking pole)

Explanation: Earth's geographic North Pole is actually the magnetic south pole — that's why the north-seeking end of a compass needle (which is a magnetic north pole) points toward it. Earth's magnetic axis is tilted about 11° from its rotational axis.

22. The phenomenon of electromagnetic induction was discovered by:

  1. Michael Faraday (1831)
  2. James Watt
  3. Nikola Tesla
  4. André-Marie Ampère

Answer: Michael Faraday (1831)

Explanation: Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction in 1831 — a changing magnetic field induces an EMF in a conductor. This principle is the basis for electric generators, transformers, and induction motors.

23. Permanent magnets are made of:

  1. Hard magnetic materials like alnico, ferrite, neodymium
  2. Soft iron
  3. Copper
  4. Aluminum

Answer: Hard magnetic materials like alnico, ferrite, neodymium

Explanation: Permanent magnets use hard magnetic materials (high coercivity) — alnico (Al+Ni+Co), ceramic/ferrite (iron oxide+barium/strontium), neodymium (Nd-Fe-B, strongest). Soft iron is used for temporary magnets/electromagnet cores.

24. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is based on:

  1. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) — hydrogen atoms align in magnetic field and emit radio waves when disturbed
  2. X-ray absorption
  3. Sound waves
  4. Radioactive tracers

Answer: Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) — hydrogen atoms align in magnetic field and emit radio waves when disturbed

Explanation: MRI uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves. Hydrogen protons in the body align with the magnetic field; when radio pulses disturb them, they emit signals that are used to create detailed images of soft tissues.

25. The correct order of electromagnetic spectrum from lowest to highest frequency is:

  1. Radio waves → Microwaves → IR → Visible → UV → X-rays → Gamma rays
  2. Gamma → X-ray → UV → Visible → IR → Microwave → Radio
  3. X-ray → UV → Visible → IR → Radio
  4. Radio → Visible → UV → X-ray

Answer: Radio waves → Microwaves → IR → Visible → UV → X-rays → Gamma rays

Explanation: Electromagnetic spectrum by increasing frequency (and energy): Radio < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible light < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Gamma rays. All travel at speed of light (3×10⁸ m/s) in vacuum.
