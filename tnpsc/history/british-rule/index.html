British Rule MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked British Rule questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between:

  1. British and Marathas
  2. British East India Company and Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal)
  3. British and Tipu Sultan
  4. British and Mughal Emperor

Answer: British East India Company and Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal)

Explanation: The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between the British East India Company and Siraj-ud-Daulah, Nawab of Bengal. British victory laid the foundation of British rule in India.

2. The 'Doctrine of Lapse' was used by which Governor-General to annex Indian states?

  1. Lord Dalhousie
  2. Lord Wellesley
  3. Lord Cornwallis
  4. Lord Curzon

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) used the Doctrine of Lapse to annex states without a natural male heir, including Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, and Awadh.

3. The 'Permanent Settlement' of 1793 was introduced by:

  1. Lord Wellesley
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Dalhousie
  4. Lord Clive

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement of Bengal in 1793. It fixed the land revenue to be paid to the British permanently by zamindars.

4. The Poligar Wars in Tamil Nadu were fought against:

  1. British East India Company
  2. Tipu Sultan
  3. Mughals
  4. Marathas

Answer: British East India Company

Explanation: Poligar Wars (Palayakkarar Wars, 1799-1805) — South Tamil Nadu's local chieftains (Poligars/Palayakkarars) fought the British East India Company for their independence.

5. The Doctrine of Lapse was introduced by:

  1. Dalhousie
  2. Hastings
  3. Wellesley
  4. Cornwallis

Answer: Dalhousie

Explanation: Doctrine of Lapse (Governor-General Lord Dalhousie, 1848-1856): states without natural heirs would be annexed by the British. Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, etc. were annexed.

6. After the 1857 revolt, India came under:

  1. Crown Rule
  2. Company Rule
  3. Mughal Rule
  4. Parliament Rule

Answer: Crown Rule

Explanation: After the 1857 revolt, the Government of India Act 1858 transferred power from the East India Company to the British Crown. Queen Victoria was proclaimed Empress of India in 1876.

7. Permanent Settlement was introduced by:

  1. Lord Cornwallis
  2. Lord Dalhousie
  3. Lord Hastings
  4. Lord Wellesley

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: Permanent Settlement of Bengal (1793) by Lord Cornwallis fixed land revenue permanently with zamindars, who became permanent owners of land if they paid fixed revenue.

8. 'Sati' was abolished by law under:

  1. Lord Bentinck
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Dalhousie
  4. Lord Hastings

Answer: Lord Bentinck

Explanation: Lord William Bentinck abolished the practice of Sati (widow self-immolation) by Regulation XVII in 1829, largely influenced by Raja Ram Mohan Roy's campaign.

9. Who was known as 'Maker of Modern India'?

  1. Lord Dalhousie
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Bentinck
  4. Lord Ripon

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: Lord Dalhousie (1848-1856) is called 'Maker of Modern India' for introducing railways, telegraph, postal reforms, engineering colleges, while also annexing many territories.

10. The British East India Company was established in:

  1. 1498
  2. 1600
  3. 1757
  4. 1800

Answer: 1600

Explanation: The British East India Company (EIC) was established on 31 December 1600 by a Royal Charter of Queen Elizabeth I to trade with the East Indies. It began as a trading company and gradually became a ruling power.

11. The Battle of Plassey (1757) gave the British control over:

  1. Madras
  2. Bengal
  3. Bombay
  4. Delhi

Answer: Bengal

Explanation: The Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah (Bengal Nawab) with the help of traitor Mir Jafar. This gave the EIC control over Bengal — beginning British colonial rule in India.

12. The Doctrine of Lapse (by Lord Dalhousie) stated:

  1. States with public debt would be taken over
  2. If an Indian ruler died without a natural heir, the state would be annexed by the British (no adoption allowed)
  3. All states must pay tribute
  4. Any state that refused alliance would be conquered

Answer: If an Indian ruler died without a natural heir, the state would be annexed by the British (no adoption allowed)

Explanation: Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie, 1848–1856): Princely states without natural heirs could not adopt successors — they would lapse to the Company. States annexed: Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854), Awadh (1856, on different grounds).

13. Sati was abolished in British India in:

  1. 1829 (Regulation XVII)
  2. 1820
  3. 1835
  4. 1856

Answer: 1829 (Regulation XVII)

Explanation: Sati (widow self-immolation) was abolished by Lord William Bentinck through Regulation XVII of 1829, following campaigns by Raja Ram Mohan Roy and other reformers. Earlier, efforts by William Carey and other missionaries had also influenced this decision.

14. The Hindu Widow Remarriage Act (1856) was championed by:

  1. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  2. Raja Ram Mohan Roy
  3. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  4. Dayananda Saraswati

Answer: Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar

Explanation: Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–1891) campaigned vigorously for widow remarriage. The Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act of 1856 (passed under Lord Dalhousie) legalised widow remarriage — a major social reform.

15. Who was the first Governor-General of India under British rule?

  1. Warren Hastings
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Wellesley
  4. Lord William Bentinck

Answer: Warren Hastings

Explanation: Warren Hastings was the first Governor-General of India, serving from 1773 to 1785. He played a key role in establishing British rule in India.

16. The British East India Company established its first factory in India at

  1. Surat
  2. Madras
  3. Bombay
  4. Calcutta

Answer: Surat

Explanation: The British East India Company established its first factory in India at Surat in 1612. This marked the beginning of British trade and influence in India.

17. The Regulating Act of 1773 was passed to

  1. Establish British rule in India
  2. Regulate the British East India Company
  3. Promote trade with India
  4. End the British East India Company's monopoly

Answer: Regulate the British East India Company

Explanation: The Regulating Act of 1773 was passed to regulate the British East India Company's affairs in India. It marked a significant step towards British government control over India.

18. The Indian Rebellion of 1857 was led by

  1. Mangal Pandey
  2. Bahadur Shah II
  3. Rani Lakshmibai
  4. Tantia Tope

Answer: Bahadur Shah II

Explanation: The Indian Rebellion of 1857 was led by various Indian leaders, including Bahadur Shah II. The rebellion was a major uprising against British rule in India.

19. The British government passed the Government of India Act in

  1. 1858
  2. 1909
  3. 1919
  4. 1935

Answer: 1858

Explanation: The British government passed the Government of India Act in 1858. This act marked the end of the British East India Company's rule in India and established direct British government control.

20. The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched by

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Subhas Chandra Bose
  4. B.R. Ambedkar

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920. The movement was a major step towards Indian independence from British rule.

21. The Quit India Movement was launched in

  1. 1940
  2. 1942
  3. 1945
  4. 1947

Answer: 1942

Explanation: The Quit India Movement was launched in 1942. The movement was a major uprising against British rule in India, demanding immediate independence.

22. The British East India Company's monopoly over Indian trade was ended by the

  1. Charter Act of 1813
  2. Charter Act of 1833
  3. Government of India Act 1858
  4. Indian Independence Act 1947

Answer: Charter Act of 1813

Explanation: The British East India Company's monopoly over Indian trade was ended by the Charter Act of 1813. This act opened up Indian trade to other British companies.

23. The Indian National Congress was founded in

  1. 1884
  2. 1885
  3. 1886
  4. 1887

Answer: 1885

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. The Congress was a major nationalist movement that played a significant role in India's struggle for independence from British rule.

24. Who was the first Governor-General of India during the British rule?

  1. Warren Hastings
  2. William Bentinck
  3. Charles Cornwallis
  4. Lord Curzon

Answer: Warren Hastings

Explanation: Warren Hastings was the first Governor-General of India, serving from 1773 to 1785. He played a key role in establishing British rule in India.

25. The British government passed the Regulating Act in:

  1. 1773
  2. 1784
  3. 1793
  4. 1813

Answer: 1773

Explanation: The British government passed the Regulating Act in 1773. This act aimed to regulate the affairs of the British East India Company in India.
