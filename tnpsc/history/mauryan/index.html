Mauryan MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Mauryan questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 307 questions.

1. Who was the first ruler of the Maurya Empire?

  1. Ashoka
  2. Chandragupta Maurya
  3. Bindusara
  4. Bimbisara

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire around 321 BCE with the help of Chanakya (Kautilya).

2. Who wrote 'Arthashastra', a treatise on statecraft and economic policy?

  1. Kalidasa
  2. Chanakya (Kautilya)
  3. Patanjali
  4. Vatsyayana

Answer: Chanakya (Kautilya)

Explanation: Arthashastra was written by Chanakya (Kautilya), the chief minister of Chandragupta Maurya. It is an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy.

3. The Buddhist councils were held to:

  1. Elect new kings
  2. Compile, recite, and standardise Buddhist scriptures
  3. Discuss political matters
  4. Spread Buddhism outside India

Answer: Compile, recite, and standardise Buddhist scriptures

Explanation: Four major Buddhist Councils: 1st at Rajgriha (483 BCE), 2nd at Vaishali (383 BCE), 3rd at Pataliputra (250 BCE, Ashoka's reign), 4th at Kundalvana, Kashmir (1st century CE, Kanishka's reign).

4. Which Buddhist council was held during the reign of Ashoka?

  1. 1st Council (Rajgriha)
  2. 2nd Council (Vaishali)
  3. 3rd Council (Pataliputra)
  4. 4th Council (Kashmir)

Answer: 3rd Council (Pataliputra)

Explanation: The 3rd Buddhist Council was held at Pataliputra (c. 250 BCE) under the patronage of Emperor Ashoka, presided over by Moggaliputta Tissa. The Pali Canon (Tipitaka) was finalised here.

5. Chandragupta Maurya established the Mauryan Empire in:

  1. 321 BCE
  2. 305 BCE
  3. 273 BCE
  4. 232 BCE

Answer: 321 BCE

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire in 321 BCE after defeating the last Nanda king (Dhana Nanda) with the help of Chanakya. He made Pataliputra (modern Patna) his capital.

6. Chanakya (Kautilya) authored:

  1. Arthashastra
  2. Indica
  3. Mudrarakshasa
  4. Rajatarangini

Answer: Arthashastra

Explanation: Chanakya (also Kautilya or Vishnugupta) wrote the Arthashastra — a comprehensive treatise on statecraft, economics, military strategy, and diplomacy. It is sometimes called 'the world's first political science text'.

7. The Greek ambassador Megasthenes visited the court of:

  1. Ashoka
  2. Bindusara
  3. Chandragupta Maurya
  4. Chandragupta II

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Megasthenes (c. 350–290 BCE) was the Greek ambassador sent by Seleucus I Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya. His account 'Indica' (mostly lost, known through fragments) described Mauryan India.

8. Ashoka's policy of Dhamma was inspired by:

  1. Jainism only
  2. Buddhism (after the Kalinga War in 261 BCE)
  3. Vedic religion
  4. Ajivika sect

Answer: Buddhism (after the Kalinga War in 261 BCE)

Explanation: The Kalinga War (261 BCE, 100,000 killed, 150,000 displaced) deeply affected Ashoka. He embraced Buddhism, renounced war, and adopted a policy of Dhamma — moral governance, non-violence, tolerance, and welfare.

9. Ashoka's edicts are found in which scripts?

  1. Only in Sanskrit
  2. Brahmi (majority), Kharosthi (northwest), Aramaic and Greek (west)
  3. Only in Pali
  4. Only in Tamil Brahmi

Answer: Brahmi (majority), Kharosthi (northwest), Aramaic and Greek (west)

Explanation: Ashoka's edicts are inscribed in: Brahmi script (most of India), Kharosthi (northwest — modern Pakistan/Afghanistan), Aramaic (Afghanistan), and Greek (Kandahar). Content is in Pali, Prakrit, Aramaic, and Greek.

10. The Mauryan Empire had its capital at:

  1. Taxila
  2. Ujjain
  3. Pataliputra (modern Patna)
  4. Vaishali

Answer: Pataliputra (modern Patna)

Explanation: Pataliputra (modern Patna, Bihar) was the capital of the Mauryan Empire. Megasthenes described it as a magnificent city surrounded by a wooden wall with 64 gates and 570 towers.

11. The last Mauryan emperor was:

  1. Ashoka
  2. Dasharatha
  3. Brihadratha
  4. Samprati

Answer: Brihadratha

Explanation: Brihadratha was the last Mauryan emperor. He was assassinated by his commander-in-chief Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BCE, ending the Mauryan dynasty and starting the Shunga dynasty.

12. The famous rock-cut architecture of the Mauryan period includes:

  1. Ajanta Caves
  2. Ellora Caves
  3. Barabar Caves (Bihar) — earliest rock-cut caves in India
  4. Elephanta Caves

Answer: Barabar Caves (Bihar) — earliest rock-cut caves in India

Explanation: The Barabar Caves (Bihar) are the earliest surviving rock-cut caves in India, built during Ashoka's reign (c. 3rd century BCE) for the Ajivika sect. They have highly polished interiors (Mauryan polish).

13. The Shunga dynasty was founded by:

  1. Vasudeva Shunga
  2. Pushyamitra Shunga
  3. Agnimitra
  4. Devabhuti

Answer: Pushyamitra Shunga

Explanation: Pushyamitra Shunga (185–149 BCE) founded the Shunga dynasty after assassinating Brihadratha, the last Mauryan emperor. He was a Brahmin and reversed some of Ashoka's Buddhist policies.

14. Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nanda dynasty with the help of:

  1. Chanakya
  2. Seleucus
  3. Bindusara
  4. Ashoka

Answer: Chanakya

Explanation: Chanakya (Kautilya), the brilliant political strategist and author of Arthashastra, helped Chandragupta Maurya overthrow the last Nanda king (Dhana Nanda) around 321 BCE.

15. The Arthashastra deals with:

  1. State administration and economics
  2. Astronomy
  3. Medicine
  4. Grammar

Answer: State administration and economics

Explanation: Arthashastra (Science of Polity) by Kautilya covers statecraft, economic policy, military strategy, welfare state, espionage, law, and diplomacy.

16. Kalinga was captured by Ashoka in:

  1. 261 BCE
  2. 273 BCE
  3. 232 BCE
  4. 269 BCE

Answer: 261 BCE

Explanation: Ashoka conquered Kalinga (modern Odisha) in 261 BCE (8th year of his reign), 13 years after becoming king. The massive bloodshed transformed him.

17. The term 'Dhamma' used by Ashoka was derived from:

  1. Pali
  2. Sanskrit
  3. Prakrit
  4. Tamil

Answer: Pali

Explanation: Dhamma is the Pali equivalent of Sanskrit 'Dharma'. Ashoka used Pali and Prakrit languages in his edicts to make them accessible to common people.

18. The Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador at the court of:

  1. Chandragupta Maurya
  2. Ashoka
  3. Bindusara
  4. Kanishka

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Megasthenes was sent as ambassador of Seleucus I Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya. His book 'Indica' gives valuable information about Mauryan India.

19. The Mauryan Empire declined after Ashoka mainly because of:

  1. Weak successors and economic strain
  2. Foreign invasions
  3. Natural disasters
  4. Religious conflicts

Answer: Weak successors and economic strain

Explanation: After Ashoka's death (232 BCE), weak successors, heavy expenditure on Buddhist welfare, and weakening of military led to rapid decline. Pusyamitra Sunga killed last Mauryan king in 185 BCE.

20. Ashokan pillars are made of:

  1. Polished sandstone
  2. Marble
  3. Granite
  4. Limestone

Answer: Polished sandstone

Explanation: Ashokan pillars are made of a single piece of highly polished Chunar sandstone. The polish is so fine that they look like metal — technique still not fully understood.

21. The Constituent Assembly adopted the National Flag on:

  1. 22 July 1947
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 26 November 1949

Answer: 22 July 1947

Explanation: The National Flag of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 July 1947, three weeks before independence. The horizontal tricolor with Ashoka Chakra replaced the INC flag.

22. Ashoka is referred to by name in which edict?

  1. Maski and Gujarra edicts
  2. Rock Edict XIII
  3. All pillar edicts
  4. Bhabru edict

Answer: Maski and Gujarra edicts

Explanation: The name 'Asoka' appears explicitly in the Maski (Raichur, Karnataka) and Gujarra (MP) minor rock edicts. In most edicts, he refers to himself as 'Devanampiya Piyadassi' (beloved of gods, Piyadassi).

23. Which Pandya king is mentioned in the Ashokan edicts as a southern border king?

  1. The Pandya king (unnamed in edicts)
  2. Nedunjeliyan
  3. Mudukudumi Peruvaludhi
  4. Talaiyalanganam's victor

Answer: The Pandya king (unnamed in edicts)

Explanation: Ashoka's Rock Edict II mentions 'the Pandyas' as southern neighbors where Ashoka's dharma principles were proclaimed. The Pandya king is not named but shows Mauryan awareness of southern kingdoms.

24. The Pandya kingdom's capital was:

  1. Madurai
  2. Puhar
  3. Vanji
  4. Kanchi

Answer: Madurai

Explanation: The Pandya kingdom's capital was Madurai (also called 'Koodal'). Pandyas ruled southern Tamil Nadu from Madurai. Megasthenes mentions Pandyas ruled by a woman (queen). They traded with Greeks and Romans.

25. The Kalinga State (modern Odisha) is associated with which ancient text?

  1. Hathigumpha inscription of Kharavela
  2. Arthashastra
  3. Mudrarakshasa
  4. Raghuvamsa

Answer: Hathigumpha inscription of Kharavela

Explanation: The Hathigumpha (Elephant Cave) inscription at Udayagiri, Odisha records the deeds of King Kharavela of Kalinga (1st century BCE), the greatest ruler of ancient Kalinga.
