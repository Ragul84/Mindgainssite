Mughal MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Mughal questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 311 questions.

1. The Taj Mahal was built by which Mughal emperor?

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Shah Jahan

Explanation: Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra (1632–1653) in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

2. Which Mughal emperor is known as 'Akbar the Great'?

  1. Humayun
  2. Babur
  3. Akbar
  4. Jahangir

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar (1556–1605) is regarded as one of the greatest Mughal emperors known for religious tolerance, administrative reforms, and expanding the empire.

3. Which was the first capital of the Mughal Empire in India?

  1. Agra
  2. Delhi
  3. Lahore
  4. Kabul

Answer: Agra

Explanation: Agra was the first capital of the Mughal Empire in India. Babur established it after the First Battle of Panipat (1526).

4. Sher Shah Suri introduced the currency called:

  1. Rupee
  2. Tanka
  3. Dam
  4. Mohur

Answer: Rupee

Explanation: Sher Shah Suri (1540-1545) introduced the silver rupee (rupiya) weighing 178 grains. He also introduced copper paisa and gold mohur — currency reforms that continued under Mughals.

5. Who was the author of 'Baburnama'?

  1. Babur
  2. Akbar
  3. Abul Fazl
  4. Gulbadan Begum

Answer: Babur

Explanation: Baburnama (Tuzuk-i-Baburi) was written by Babur himself in Chagatai Turkish — it is the first autobiography in Islamic literary tradition, describing his life, campaigns, and observations of India.

6. Which city was called 'City of Victory' after the Battle of Panipat 1526?

  1. None — it was already Agra
  2. Panipat
  3. Lahore
  4. Delhi (called Shahjahanabad later)

Answer: None — it was already Agra

Explanation: After the First Battle of Panipat, Babur captured Agra and made it his capital. Delhi was also used. The battle didn't create a new city; Babur ruled from existing cities.

7. Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) was established by:

  1. Akbar
  2. Babur
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar established Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri in 1575 CE for religious debates among scholars of different faiths — precursor to his Din-i-Ilahi.

8. The Battle of Khanwa (1527) was between:

  1. Babur and Rana Sanga
  2. Babur and Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Humayun and Sher Shah
  4. Akbar and Hemu

Answer: Babur and Rana Sanga

Explanation: Battle of Khanwa (17 March 1527): Babur defeated Rana Sanga (Rana Sangram Singh) of Mewar, who had formed a coalition of Rajput chiefs to challenge Babur.

9. Akbar defeated Hemu in:

  1. Second Battle of Panipat (1556)
  2. First Battle of Panipat (1526)
  3. Battle of Khanwa (1527)
  4. Battle of Haldighati (1576)

Answer: Second Battle of Panipat (1556)

Explanation: Second Battle of Panipat (5 November 1556): Akbar's general Bairam Khan defeated and killed Hemu (the last Hindu emperor to sit on Delhi's throne briefly), securing Mughal rule.

10. The Mughal emperor who was also called 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint):

  1. Aurangzeb
  2. Akbar
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Aurangzeb

Explanation: Aurangzeb was called 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint) for his piety — he fasted regularly, never drank alcohol, lived simply, and supplemented his income by copying Qurans and making prayer caps.

11. Akbar's 'Sulh-i-Kul' policy means:

  1. Universal peace and tolerance
  2. Military alliance
  3. Royal decree
  4. Religious conversion

Answer: Universal peace and tolerance

Explanation: Sulh-i-Kul (Peace with all/Absolute peace) was Akbar's policy of universal tolerance — treating all religions equally, respecting all faiths without discrimination.

12. Which Mughal emperor translated the Ramayana and Mahabharata into Persian?

  1. Akbar
  2. Shah Jahan
  3. Humayun
  4. Babur

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar had the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and other Sanskrit texts translated into Persian, making them accessible to Persian-speaking scholars and promoting cultural synthesis.

13. Babur founded the Mughal Empire after winning:

  1. Battle of Khanwa
  2. Battle of Ghaghra
  3. First Battle of Panipat (1526)
  4. Battle of Chausa

Answer: First Battle of Panipat (1526)

Explanation: Babur (Zahir-ud-din Muhammad) defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526) using gunpowder and artillery (tulugma tactics). This established the Mughal Empire. He then defeated Rana Sanga at Khanwa (1527).

14. Sher Shah Suri is famous for:

  1. Building the Taj Mahal
  2. Introducing the Grand Trunk Road, administrative reforms, land revenue system (zabt), and silver rupee
  3. Defeating Akbar
  4. Commissioning Akbarnama

Answer: Introducing the Grand Trunk Road, administrative reforms, land revenue system (zabt), and silver rupee

Explanation: Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545) defeated Humayun, reorganised the empire: Grand Trunk Road (Peshawar to Bengal), silver rupee (precursor to modern rupee), land revenue based on measurement (zabt), relay postal system, and built Sher Shah mosque.

15. Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat in:

  1. 1526
  2. 1556
  3. 1761
  4. 1527

Answer: 1526

Explanation: First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526): Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi using the Tulughma strategy and artillery, establishing the Mughal Empire in India.

16. The Mughal emperor famous for building Taj Mahal:

  1. Shah Jahan
  2. Akbar
  3. Jahangir
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Shah Jahan

Explanation: Shah Jahan (1628-1658) built the Taj Mahal at Agra (1632-1643) as a mausoleum for his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

17. Aurangzeb reimposed the 'Jizya' tax which had been abolished by:

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Babur
  4. Shah Jahan

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar abolished the discriminatory Jizya (poll tax on non-Muslims) in 1564 CE as part of his policy of religious tolerance. Aurangzeb reimposed it in 1679 CE.

18. Todar Mal was the Finance Minister of:

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Jahangir

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Raja Todar Mal was Akbar's revenue minister who introduced the Zabti (measurement) system of land revenue — measuring land and fixing revenue based on soil type and cultivation.

19. The 'Mansabdari system' was a Mughal system of:

  1. Military ranking and revenue
  2. Religious administration
  3. Land grants
  4. Educational hierarchy

Answer: Military ranking and revenue

Explanation: Mansabdari system (introduced by Akbar) graded military and civil officials (mansabdars) by numerical ranks (mansabs) determining salary and cavalry obligation.

20. The Battle of Haldighati (1576) was fought between:

  1. Maharana Pratap and Akbar's army
  2. Prithviraj and Ghori
  3. Shivaji and Aurangzeb
  4. Rana Sanga and Babur

Answer: Maharana Pratap and Akbar's army

Explanation: Battle of Haldighati (18 June 1576) was fought between Maharana Pratap of Mewar and the Mughal forces led by Man Singh I on Akbar's behalf. Pratap was defeated but never surrendered.

21. The Battle of Buxar (1764) was significant because:

  1. British defeated combined armies of three powers
  2. First British victory in India
  3. British acquired Bengal
  4. First use of firearms in India

Answer: British defeated combined armies of three powers

Explanation: Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764): British under Hector Munro defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula (Awadh), and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II — establishing British dominance.

22. The 'Ain-i-Akbari' was written by:

  1. Abul Fazl
  2. Birbal
  3. Todar Mal
  4. Tansen

Answer: Abul Fazl

Explanation: Ain-i-Akbari (Institutes of Akbar) was written by Abul Fazl, Akbar's court historian. It is the third volume of Akbarnama, providing detailed accounts of Akbar's administration, policies, and the Mughal court.

23. Akbar's 'Ain-i-Akbari' and 'Akbarnama' were written by:

  1. Birbal
  2. Abul Fazl
  3. Faizi
  4. Tansen

Answer: Abul Fazl

Explanation: Abul Fazl (1551–1602), a minister and historian, wrote Akbarnama (in 3 volumes, including the Ain-i-Akbari). The Ain-i-Akbari is a detailed account of Akbar's administration, court, culture, and statistics of the empire.

24. The Mughal land revenue system during Akbar's reign was called:

  1. Iqta
  2. Zamindari
  3. Dahsala or Zabti system (introduced by Todar Mal)
  4. Mahalwari

Answer: Dahsala or Zabti system (introduced by Todar Mal)

Explanation: Raja Todar Mal introduced the Dahsala (ten-year average) system: land was measured (zabt), soil quality classified into 4 types, and revenue fixed for 10 years based on average produce. It replaced earlier arbitrary systems.

25. Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in memory of:

  1. His mother
  2. His daughter
  3. His wife Mumtaz Mahal
  4. His sister

Answer: His wife Mumtaz Mahal

Explanation: Shah Jahan (1628–1658) built the Taj Mahal at Agra (1631–1648) in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal (Arjumand Banu Begum) who died during childbirth. It was built by 20,000 workers using white Makrana marble.
