26. The Nataraja (dancing Shiva) bronze is a Chola masterpiece. What does the cosmic dance (Ananda Tandava) symbolise?

  1. Shiva's anger
  2. The five cosmic activities: creation, preservation, destruction, concealment, grace
  3. Only war and destruction
  4. Agricultural prosperity

Answer: The five cosmic activities: creation, preservation, destruction, concealment, grace

Explanation: Nataraja's cosmic dance symbolises Panchakriya: Srishti (creation — right hand's raised drum), Sthiti (preservation — right hand's abhaya pose), Samhara (destruction — left hand with fire), Tirobhava (concealment — foot on dwarf Apasmara/ignorance), Anugraha (liberation — raised left foot).

27. The invasion of Timur (Tamerlane) devastated Delhi in:

  1. 1398 CE
  2. 1336 CE
  3. 1451 CE
  4. 1526 CE

Answer: 1398 CE

Explanation: Timur (Tamerlane) of Samarkand invaded India and sacked Delhi in 1398 CE, massacring 100,000+ prisoners and looting enormous wealth. This destroyed the Tughlaq dynasty and led to the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties.

28. Raja Ram Mohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj in:

  1. 1828
  2. 1818
  3. 1848
  4. 1838

Answer: 1828

Explanation: Brahmo Samaj was founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy on 20 August 1828 in Calcutta. It promoted monotheism, rationalism, and social reforms like abolition of sati.

29. The Arya Samaj was founded by:

  1. Dayananda Saraswati
  2. Ramakrishna
  3. Vivekananda
  4. Ram Mohan Roy

Answer: Dayananda Saraswati

Explanation: Swami Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875 at Bombay. Its motto: 'Go Back to the Vedas'. It opposed idol worship, casteism, and promoted education.

30. Jyotirao Phule founded:

  1. Satyashodhak Samaj
  2. Arya Samaj
  3. Brahmo Samaj
  4. Prarthana Samaj

Answer: Satyashodhak Samaj

Explanation: Jyotirao Phule founded Satyashodhak Samaj (Truth-Seekers' Society) in 1873 in Pune to fight caste discrimination and promote social equality for lower castes and women.

31. The Gulf War (1991) was triggered by:

  1. Iraq's invasion of Kuwait
  2. Iran's nuclear program
  3. Israel-Palestine conflict
  4. Afghanistan civil war

Answer: Iraq's invasion of Kuwait

Explanation: The First Gulf War (1990-91) was triggered by Iraq's invasion and annexation of Kuwait (2 August 1990). A US-led coalition liberated Kuwait in Operation Desert Storm.

32. The Permanent Settlement (1793) of land revenue was introduced by:

  1. Robert Clive
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Wellesley
  4. Lord Dalhousie

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement in Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa (1793). It fixed land revenue permanently with zamindars who became hereditary owners. Peasants (ryots) had no land rights — caused widespread impoverishment.

33. The 'Vaikom Satyagraha' (1924-25) was about:

  1. Temple entry for Dalits
  2. Right of untouchables to walk on public roads near Vaikom temple (Travancore/Kerala)
  3. Land rights for peasants
  4. Women's right to vote

Answer: Right of untouchables to walk on public roads near Vaikom temple (Travancore/Kerala)

Explanation: The Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–25) demanded the right of lower-caste people (Ezhavas, Pulayas) to walk on public roads near the Vaikom Mahadeva Temple in Travancore (Kerala). Periyar was a key participant. The roads were eventually opened.

34. Mahatma Jyotirao Phule founded which organisation for lower-caste upliftment?

  1. Brahmo Samaj
  2. Prarthana Samaj
  3. Satya Shodhak Samaj (1873)
  4. Arya Samaj

Answer: Satya Shodhak Samaj (1873)

Explanation: Jyotirao Phule (1827–1890) founded the Satya Shodhak Samaj (Truth-Seeking Society) in 1873 in Pune. He fought against Brahminical dominance, opened the first school for girls in India (with his wife Savitribai), and wrote Gulamgiri (Slavery).

35. The Arya Samaj was founded by Dayananda Saraswati in:

  1. 1850
  2. 1865
  3. 1875
  4. 1885

Answer: 1875

Explanation: Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in Bombay (1875). It advocated return to Vedic religion, rejected idol worship, caste by birth, and child marriage. It also started the Shuddhi (reconversion) movement.

36. The book 'Satyarth Prakash' was written by:

  1. Swami Vivekananda
  2. Dayananda Saraswati
  3. Ram Mohan Roy
  4. Keshab Chandra Sen

Answer: Dayananda Saraswati

Explanation: Satyarth Prakash ('Light of Truth', 1875) was written by Dayananda Saraswati. It outlined the principles of the Arya Samaj — monotheism, Vedic authority, rejection of idol worship, child marriage, and untouchability.

37. The INA (Indian National Army) was originally organised by:

  1. Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose
  2. Mohan Singh (1942) and later reorganised by Subhas Chandra Bose (1943)
  3. Bhagat Singh
  4. Lala Lajpat Rai

Answer: Mohan Singh (1942) and later reorganised by Subhas Chandra Bose (1943)

Explanation: INA was first organised by Captain Mohan Singh from Indian POWs captured by Japan in Malaya (1942). Subhas Chandra Bose took over leadership in 1943, reorganised it, declared Provisional Government of Free India, and launched the Imphal-Kohima offensive.

38. Subramania Bharati (Mahakavi Bharati) was a famous Tamil:

  1. Military leader
  2. Freedom fighter and poet who wrote revolutionary Tamil poems and songs
  3. Politician only
  4. Social reformer only

Answer: Freedom fighter and poet who wrote revolutionary Tamil poems and songs

Explanation: Subramania Bharati (1882–1921) was a Tamil poet and freedom fighter. His songs ('Vande Mataram' translation, 'Kuyil Pattu', 'Kannan Pattu') inspired the freedom movement. He advocated women's liberation, Dalit rights, and national unity.

39. Bal Gangadhar Tilak's famous saying was:

  1. 'Give me blood and I will give you freedom'
  2. 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it'
  3. 'Do or Die'
  4. 'Jai Hind'

Answer: 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it'

Explanation: Bal Gangadhar Tilak's famous declaration 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it' inspired the extremist nationalism. He used festivals (Ganesh Chaturthi, Shivaji Jayanti) to spread the freedom movement.

40. The Vellore Mutiny (1806) was the first major armed uprising against the British in South India. It was triggered by:

  1. Land revenue policies
  2. New military regulations (turbans, earrings, tilaks banned) — seen as attempts to Christianise soldiers
  3. Salt tax
  4. Forced labour

Answer: New military regulations (turbans, earrings, tilaks banned) — seen as attempts to Christianise soldiers

Explanation: The Vellore Mutiny (10 July 1806) was triggered by new uniform regulations that banned turbans, facial marks, and earrings for sepoys (soldiers), which they saw as forced Christianisation. The mutiny was suppressed; over 100 sepoys were executed.

41. The Vaikam Satyagraha (1924-25) was led by:

  1. T.K. Madhavan
  2. Periyar E.V. Ramasamy
  3. Gandhi
  4. Ambedkar

Answer: T.K. Madhavan

Explanation: Vaikam Satyagraha was launched in 1924 to win the right of lower castes to walk on roads near the Vaikam temple in Kerala. T.K. Madhavan initiated it; Periyar played key role.

42. The Tuticorin (Thoothukudi) Mass Agitation of 1908 was led by:

  1. V.O. Chidambaram Pillai
  2. Subramania Bharati
  3. C. Rajagopalachari
  4. Periyar

Answer: V.O. Chidambaram Pillai

Explanation: V.O. Chidambaram Pillai and Subramania Siva led the Tuticorin mass agitation in 1908, connected to the Swadeshi movement. VOC was sentenced to 6 years rigorous imprisonment.

43. The First Five-Year Plan of India focused on:

  1. Agriculture and infrastructure
  2. Industrialization
  3. Defense
  4. Education

Answer: Agriculture and infrastructure

Explanation: India's First Five-Year Plan (1951-56) was based on the Harrod-Domar model and focused on agriculture, irrigation, power, and rehabilitation after partition.

44. World War I was triggered by:

  1. Germany's invasion of Poland
  2. Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary at Sarajevo (28 June 1914)
  3. The sinking of the Lusitania
  4. Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor

Answer: Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary at Sarajevo (28 June 1914)

Explanation: WWI (1914–1918) was triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (heir to Austria-Hungary) by Gavrilo Princip (Serbian nationalist) in Sarajevo, Bosnia. This set off the alliance system and led to a global conflict.

45. Which of the following rivers is associated with the Indus Valley Civilization?

  1. Ganges
  2. Indus
  3. Brahmaputra
  4. Krishna

Answer: Indus

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilization is associated with the Indus river. It was a major river of the civilization.

46. What was the primary occupation of people during the Vedic age?

  1. Agriculture
  2. Trade
  3. Pastoralism
  4. Hunting

Answer: Pastoralism

Explanation: Pastoralism was the primary occupation of people during the Vedic age. They were mainly cattle rearers.

47. Who was the founder of the Mauryan dynasty?

  1. Chandragupta Maurya
  2. Ashoka
  3. Samudragupta
  4. Bimbisara

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya was the founder of the Mauryan dynasty. He founded the dynasty in 322 BCE.

48. Which of the following South Indian kingdoms built the Brihadeeswara temple?

  1. Cholas
  2. Pandyas
  3. Pallavas
  4. Cheras

Answer: Cholas

Explanation: The Cholas built the Brihadeeswara temple. It was built during the reign of Rajaraja Chola.

49. Who was the founder of the Vijayanagara empire?

  1. Harihara
  2. Bukka
  3. Krishnadeva Raya
  4. Ashoka

Answer: Harihara

Explanation: Harihara was the founder of the Vijayanagara empire. He founded the empire in 1336 CE.

50. Which of the following was a major consequence of the British conquest of India?

  1. Economic growth
  2. Cultural exchange
  3. Exploitation
  4. Industrialization

Answer: Exploitation

Explanation: Exploitation was a major consequence of the British conquest of India. The British exploited India's resources.
