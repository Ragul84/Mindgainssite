Vedic Age MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Vedic Age questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on:

  1. 13 April 1918
  2. 13 April 1919
  3. 13 April 1920
  4. 15 August 1919

Answer: 13 April 1919

Explanation: On 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi Day), British Brigadier General Reginald Dyer ordered troops to fire on a peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. Hundreds were killed.

2. The concept of 'Ahimsa' (non-violence) as a political tool was first applied by Gandhi in:

  1. South Africa (Satyagraha, 1906–1914)
  2. India, Champaran Satyagraha (1917)
  3. India, Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)
  4. India, Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)

Answer: South Africa (Satyagraha, 1906–1914)

Explanation: Gandhi first applied non-violent resistance (Satyagraha) in South Africa (1906–1914) while fighting for the rights of Indian immigrants. This experience shaped his philosophy for India's independence struggle.

3. The Aryans are believed to have migrated into India through the:

  1. Himalayas from Tibet
  2. Khyber and Bolan passes from Central Asia
  3. Arabian Sea from the Middle East
  4. Deccan from Africa

Answer: Khyber and Bolan passes from Central Asia

Explanation: The Indo-Aryans are believed to have entered India through the northwestern mountain passes (Khyber, Bolan) from Central Asia around 1500 BCE, though the exact nature of migration is debated.

4. The oldest of the four Vedas is:

  1. Samaveda
  2. Yajurveda
  3. Rigveda
  4. Atharvaveda

Answer: Rigveda

Explanation: The Rigveda (c. 1500–1200 BCE) is the oldest of the four Vedas, containing 1028 hymns (suktas) arranged in 10 mandalas. It is one of the oldest religious texts in the world.

5. The 'Gayatri Mantra' is from:

  1. Samaveda
  2. Yajurveda
  3. Rigveda (3rd mandala)
  4. Atharvaveda

Answer: Rigveda (3rd mandala)

Explanation: The Gayatri Mantra (Savitri Mantra) is from the Rigveda (3rd mandala, hymn 62, verse 10). It invokes the solar deity Savitr. It is considered the most sacred mantra in Hinduism.

6. The Later Vedic period saw the shift of Aryan culture from Punjab to:

  1. Deccan plateau
  2. Gangetic plain (Doab region)
  3. Bengal
  4. South India

Answer: Gangetic plain (Doab region)

Explanation: In the Later Vedic period (c. 1000–600 BCE), the centre of Aryan culture shifted from the Punjab to the Gangetic plain (between Ganga and Yamuna), known as the Doab or 'Madhyadesha'.

7. The Varna system in early Vedic society was based on:

  1. Birth
  2. Occupation (karma) — it was not hereditary initially
  3. Colour of skin
  4. Wealth

Answer: Occupation (karma) — it was not hereditary initially

Explanation: In the Rigvedic period, the varna system was based on occupation, not birth. The four varnas were Brahmin (priest), Kshatriya (warrior), Vaishya (trader), Shudra (servant). Hereditary rigidity came in the Later Vedic period.

8. The Purusha Sukta, which describes the creation of the four varnas, is in:

  1. Atharvaveda
  2. Rigveda (10th mandala)
  3. Upanishads
  4. Manusmriti

Answer: Rigveda (10th mandala)

Explanation: The Purusha Sukta (Rigveda, 10th Mandala, Hymn 90) describes the cosmic being Purusha's sacrifice: Brahmins from his mouth, Kshatriyas from arms, Vaishyas from thighs, Shudras from feet.

9. The Upanishads deal with:

  1. Rituals and sacrifices
  2. Philosophical and metaphysical ideas (Brahman, Atman, Maya, Karma)
  3. Military strategy
  4. Agricultural practices

Answer: Philosophical and metaphysical ideas (Brahman, Atman, Maya, Karma)

Explanation: The Upanishads (c. 800–200 BCE) are philosophical texts exploring the nature of ultimate reality (Brahman), individual soul (Atman), and their identity. They form the basis of Vedanta philosophy.

10. The epic 'Mahabharata' was originally composed by:

  1. Valmiki
  2. Vyasa (Krishna Dvaipayana)
  3. Tulsidas
  4. Kalidasa

Answer: Vyasa (Krishna Dvaipayana)

Explanation: The Mahabharata was composed by Vyasa (also called Krishna Dvaipayana or Veda Vyasa). The original was called 'Jaya' (8,800 verses), expanded to 'Bharata' (24,000), then 'Mahabharata' (100,000 verses).

11. The five vows (Pancha Mahavrata) of Jainism include all EXCEPT:

  1. Ahimsa (non-violence)
  2. Satya (truth)
  3. Asteya (non-stealing)
  4. Ahara (food consumption)

Answer: Ahara (food consumption)

Explanation: The five Mahavrata of Jainism: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya (non-theft), Brahmacharya (celibacy), and Aparigraha (non-possession). 'Ahara' (food) is not one of them. Brahmacharya was added by Mahavira to Parshvanatha's four vows.

12. Gayatri Mantra is found in:

  1. Rigveda
  2. Samaveda
  3. Yajurveda
  4. Atharvaveda

Answer: Rigveda

Explanation: Gayatri Mantra (dedicated to solar deity Savitri) is found in the Rigveda (3.62.10). It is the most sacred mantra in Hinduism.

13. Sabha and Samiti in Vedic period were:

  1. Tribal assemblies
  2. Religious centers
  3. Military units
  4. Tax collection bodies

Answer: Tribal assemblies

Explanation: Sabha and Samiti were tribal assemblies in the Vedic period. Sabha was a smaller, exclusive council of elders; Samiti was a larger, more democratic gathering.

14. Iron was first used in India during:

  1. Later Vedic period
  2. Mauryan period
  3. Gupta period
  4. Early Vedic period

Answer: Later Vedic period

Explanation: Iron came into widespread use in India during the Later Vedic period (1000-600 BCE), transforming agriculture, warfare, and craftsmanship.

15. Purushmedha refers to:

  1. Human sacrifice in Vedic rituals
  2. A Vedic prayer
  3. Marriage ceremony
  4. Battle formation

Answer: Human sacrifice in Vedic rituals

Explanation: Purushmedha was a ritual human sacrifice described in Vedic texts, though scholars debate whether it was actually practiced or purely symbolic.

16. Triratna of Jainism refers to:

  1. Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct
  2. Three Tirthankaras
  3. Three sacred texts
  4. Three monasteries

Answer: Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct

Explanation: Jain Triratna (Three Jewels): Right Faith (Samyak Darshan), Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana), Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra) — the path to liberation.

17. Theravada Buddhism is also called:

  1. Hinayana
  2. Mahayana
  3. Vajrayana
  4. Tantrayana

Answer: Hinayana

Explanation: Theravada (Way of the Elders) Buddhism is also called Hinayana (Lesser Vehicle) by Mahayana Buddhists. It emphasizes personal salvation and the original Pali Canon.

18. Aryabhata wrote the famous work:

  1. Aryabhatiya
  2. Arthashastra
  3. Ashtadhyayi
  4. Amarakosa

Answer: Aryabhatiya

Explanation: Aryabhata (476-550 CE) wrote Aryabhatiya — covering arithmetic, algebra, plane geometry, spherical trigonometry. He correctly calculated value of π and explained solar/lunar eclipses.

19. Bal Gangadhar Tilak gave the slogan:

  1. Swarajya is my birthright
  2. Jai Hind
  3. Vande Mataram
  4. Dilli Chalo

Answer: Swarajya is my birthright

Explanation: 'Swarajya is my birthright and I shall have it' was the famous slogan of Bal Gangadhar Tilak, the fiery nationalist who also started the Ganapati and Shivaji festivals for national awakening.

20. Who wrote 'Mudrarakshasa'?

  1. Vishakhadatta
  2. Kalidasa
  3. Bana
  4. Harsha

Answer: Vishakhadatta

Explanation: Mudrarakshasa by Vishakhadatta is a Sanskrit play depicting the political intrigue after Chandragupta Maurya's rise to power, with Chanakya as the main character.

21. The Nagara style of temple architecture is mainly found in:

  1. North India
  2. South India
  3. Both equally
  4. East India only

Answer: North India

Explanation: Nagara style (North Indian/Indo-Aryan) has a curvilinear tower (shikhara) over the sanctum. Dravidian style (South Indian) has a pyramidal tower (vimana). Vesara is a mix of both.

22. The Vellore Mutiny of 1806 was the first:

  1. Sepoy revolt against British in India
  2. Civil uprising
  3. Naval mutiny
  4. Police revolt

Answer: Sepoy revolt against British in India

Explanation: Vellore Mutiny (10 July 1806) was the first major sepoy mutiny against the British, 51 years before the 1857 revolt, triggered by new dress regulations seen as an attack on religion.

23. 'Janpad' in ancient India referred to:

  1. Territorial states in Vedic period
  2. Village assembly
  3. Tax collection unit
  4. Military camp

Answer: Territorial states in Vedic period

Explanation: Janapada (jana=tribe/people, pada=foot/territory) refers to the territorial states that developed in the Later Vedic period. 16 great Mahajanapadas existed around 600 BCE.

24. The 'Grand Trunk Road' was built by:

  1. Sher Shah Suri
  2. Akbar
  3. Ashoka
  4. British

Answer: Sher Shah Suri

Explanation: Grand Trunk Road from Chittagong to Kabul was built/renovated by Sher Shah Suri (16th century). Originally an ancient road, Sher Shah restored and extended it, planting trees and building sarais.

25. The Dravidian Movement in Tamil Nadu was influenced significantly by which organization?

  1. Justice Party (South Indian Liberal Federation)
  2. Indian National Congress
  3. Muslim League
  4. Arya Samaj

Answer: Justice Party (South Indian Liberal Federation)

Explanation: The Justice Party (South Indian Liberal Federation, 1916) was the forerunner of the Dravidian movement. It championed non-Brahmin rights and eventually evolved into the Dravidian parties.
