Tamil History MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Tamil History questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 322 questions.

26. The Three Sangams (literary academies) were presided over by the kings of:

  1. Pandyas
  2. Cholas
  3. Cheras
  4. Pallavas

Answer: Pandyas

Explanation: According to later Tamil tradition, the three legendary Sangams were patronized by Pandya kings at Madurai (1st & 3rd) and Kapatapuram (2nd).

27. 'Tolkappiyam' is a grammatical treatise on:

  1. Tamil language
  2. Sanskrit
  3. Pali
  4. Telugu

Answer: Tamil language

Explanation: Tolkappiyam by Tolkappiyar is the oldest surviving work in Tamil literature — a comprehensive grammar covering phonology, word formation, and poetics. Part of early Sangam literature.

28. 'Ettuthokai' means:

  1. Eight Anthologies
  2. Eight Kings
  3. Eight Poets
  4. Eight Epics

Answer: Eight Anthologies

Explanation: Ettuthokai (Eight Anthologies) is a collection of 8 Sangam-age anthologies of Tamil poetry: Ainkurunuru, Narrinai, Akananuru, Purananuru, Kalittokai, Kuruntokai, Patirruppattu, and Paripatal.

29. 'Pattupattu' refers to:

  1. Ten Idylls (long poems)
  2. Ten Kings
  3. Ten Battles
  4. Ten Temples

Answer: Ten Idylls (long poems)

Explanation: Pattupattu (Ten Idylls) are 10 longer Sangam poems, distinct from the Eight Anthologies, covering topics from love to war to nature.

30. The Chera king famous in Sangam literature for feeding both sides in the Kurukshetra war is:

  1. Udiyanjeral
  2. Senguttuvan
  3. Nedum Cheralathan
  4. Anthuvan Cheral

Answer: Udiyanjeral

Explanation: Udiyanjeral (Perum Cheral Irumporai) is the Chera king praised in Sangam literature (Purananuru) for feeding both the armies of the Mahabharata war.

31. The theme of 'Manimekalai' is:

  1. Buddhism and compassion
  2. Jainism and non-violence
  3. Shaivism
  4. Vaishnavism

Answer: Buddhism and compassion

Explanation: Manimekalai by Sittalai Sattanar propagates Buddhist philosophy. The heroine Manimekalai becomes a Buddhist nun and practices compassion and charity.

32. The Tirukkural is divided into three sections called:

  1. Aram, Porul, Inbam
  2. Akam, Puram, Tinai
  3. Anbu, Vinai, Veedu
  4. Kama, Artha, Dharma

Answer: Aram, Porul, Inbam

Explanation: Tirukkural by Tiruvalluvar is divided into: Aram (Virtue/Dharma), Porul (Wealth/Polity), Inbam (Love/Pleasure) — 133 chapters, 1330 couplets.

33. Which Sangam text is known as 'Tamil Bible'?

  1. Tirukkural
  2. Silappadikaram
  3. Manimekalai
  4. Tolkappiyam

Answer: Tirukkural

Explanation: Tirukkural by Tiruvalluvar is often called 'Tamil Bible' or 'Universal Veda' for its universal wisdom on virtue, wealth, and love applicable to all humanity.

34. Who authored 'Ashtadhyayi'?

  1. Panini
  2. Patanjali
  3. Bhartrhari
  4. Kalidasa

Answer: Panini

Explanation: Panini's Ashtadhyayi (4000 sutras) is the foundational text of Sanskrit grammar, one of the greatest intellectual achievements of ancient civilization.

35. The founder of the Pala dynasty of Bengal was:

  1. Gopala
  2. Dharmapala
  3. Devapala
  4. Mahipala

Answer: Gopala

Explanation: Gopala (750-770 CE) founded the Pala dynasty in Bengal. The Palas were great patrons of Buddhism and built the famous Vikramashila University.

36. Charaka Samhita deals with:

  1. Medicine (Ayurveda)
  2. Astronomy
  3. Grammar
  4. Music

Answer: Medicine (Ayurveda)

Explanation: Charaka Samhita by Charaka is one of the foundational texts of Ayurveda (Indian medicine) covering internal medicine, surgery, pharmacology, and medical ethics.

37. The Ajanta Caves are famous for:

  1. Buddhist paintings and sculptures
  2. Jain temples
  3. Hindu rock sculptures
  4. Cave dwellings

Answer: Buddhist paintings and sculptures

Explanation: Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra, 2nd century BCE – 7th century CE) are UNESCO World Heritage Sites famous for their magnificent Buddhist cave paintings and sculptures depicting Jataka stories.

38. The famous astronomer who stated Earth rotates on its axis was:

  1. Aryabhata
  2. Brahmagupta
  3. Varahamihira
  4. Bhaskara

Answer: Aryabhata

Explanation: Aryabhata (476-550 CE) stated that the Earth rotates on its own axis, causing apparent movement of stars — a revolutionary concept 1000 years before Copernicus.

39. The 'Periplus of the Erythraean Sea' was written by:

  1. An anonymous Greek merchant
  2. Megasthenes
  3. Fa-Hien
  4. Ptolemy

Answer: An anonymous Greek merchant

Explanation: 'Periplus of the Erythraean Sea' (1st century CE) written by an anonymous Greek merchant describes ancient trade routes and ports along the Indian Ocean, including Indian ports.

40. The Satavahana dynasty ruled mainly over:

  1. Deccan
  2. North India
  3. South India
  4. Bengal

Answer: Deccan

Explanation: Satavahanas (1st century BCE – 3rd century CE) were the first major dynasty of the Deccan, ruling from Pratishthana (Paithan). They were great patrons of Buddhism and trade.

41. Sangam Age Tamil literature mentions foreign trade with:

  1. Romans and Greeks
  2. Chinese
  3. Arabs only
  4. Persians only

Answer: Romans and Greeks

Explanation: Sangam literature mentions extensive trade with Rome and Greece — export of spices, pearls, cotton; import of wine, gold, horses. Roman coins found at Arikamedu and other Tamil sites.

42. The Rashtrakutas were the successors of:

  1. Chalukyas of Badami
  2. Pallavas
  3. Gangas
  4. Pandyas

Answer: Chalukyas of Badami

Explanation: Dantidurga overthrew the Chalukya ruler Kirtivarman II and established the Rashtrakuta dynasty in 753 CE, making them the major power in the Deccan.

43. 'Kamban Ramayanam' (Tamil Ramayana) was written during the reign of:

  1. Medieval Chola period
  2. Pallava period
  3. Pandya period
  4. Vijayanagara period

Answer: Medieval Chola period

Explanation: Kambar wrote the Tamil Ramayana (Iramavataram) around 12th century CE during the later Chola period, supported by a local chieftain Sadaiyappa Vallal.

44. The Pandya dynasty's capital was:

  1. Madurai
  2. Uraiyur
  3. Kaveripattanam
  4. Kanchipuram

Answer: Madurai

Explanation: Madurai was the capital of the Pandya dynasty throughout history — from Sangam Age through medieval period. Ancient Madurai is described in detail in Sangam literature (Maturaikkanci).

45. The Meenakshi Amman Temple at Madurai was mainly built by:

  1. Tirumalai Nayak
  2. Vijayanagara rulers
  3. Pandya rulers
  4. British

Answer: Tirumalai Nayak

Explanation: The Meenakshi Amman Temple in its present form was largely built during Tirumalai Nayak's rule (1623-1659 CE), though the temple itself dates back to much earlier Pandya times.

46. The Chera ports mentioned in Sangam literature include:

  1. Musiri and Thondi
  2. Poompuhar and Uraiyur
  3. Korkai and Kulachai
  4. Mamallapuram

Answer: Musiri and Thondi

Explanation: Musiri (Muziris) and Thondi were important Chera ports mentioned in Sangam literature and Periplus, known for trade in spices, particularly black pepper with Rome.

47. The 'Darasuram Temple' (Airavatesvara) was built by:

  1. Rajaraja II (Chola)
  2. Rajaraja I
  3. Rajendra I
  4. Kulottunga I

Answer: Rajaraja II (Chola)

Explanation: Airavatesvara Temple at Darasuram (near Kumbakonam) was built by Rajaraja II in the 12th century CE. Along with Thanjavur and Gangaikonda Cholapuram, it's a UNESCO heritage site (Great Living Chola Temples).

48. Guru Nanak Dev founded:

  1. Sikhism
  2. Brahmo Samaj
  3. Arya Samaj
  4. Prarthana Samaj

Answer: Sikhism

Explanation: Guru Nanak Dev (1469-1539 CE) founded Sikhism, emphasizing one God, equality of all humans, truthful living, service to humanity, and opposition to caste discrimination.

49. The Chishti Silsila of Sufi order in India was founded by:

  1. Moinuddin Chishti
  2. Nizamuddin Auliya
  3. Khwaja Bakhtiyar Kaki
  4. Qutb-ud-din Bakhtiyar Kaki

Answer: Moinuddin Chishti

Explanation: Moinuddin Chishti (1142-1236 CE) brought the Chishti order to India, establishing his khanqah at Ajmer. He and his followers emphasized love of God and service.

50. Ramanujacharya's Vishishtadvaita philosophy is the basis of:

  1. Sri Vaishnavism
  2. Shaivism
  3. Shaktism
  4. Advaita Vedanta

Answer: Sri Vaishnavism

Explanation: Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita is the philosophical basis of Sri Vaishnavism, which combines the philosophical tradition with the devotional tradition of the Alvars.
