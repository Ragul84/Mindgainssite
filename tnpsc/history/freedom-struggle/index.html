Freedom Struggle MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Freedom Struggle questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. India gained independence from British rule on:

  1. 26 January 1950
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 26 November 1949
  4. 30 January 1948

Answer: 15 August 1947

Explanation: India gained independence on 15 August 1947. Jawaharlal Nehru became the first Prime Minister.

2. Who is called the 'Father of the Nation' of India?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Subhas Chandra Bose
  3. Mahatma Gandhi
  4. Sardar Patel

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi (Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi) is called the Father of the Nation. He led India's non-violent independence movement.

3. The 'Salt March' (Dandi March) was led by:

  1. Subhas Chandra Bose
  2. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  3. Mahatma Gandhi
  4. Jawaharlal Nehru

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi led the Salt March (Dandi March) from 12 March to 6 April 1930, marching 241 miles to Dandi to make salt from sea water to protest the British salt tax.

4. The non-cooperation movement was launched in which year?

  1. 1905
  2. 1915
  3. 1920
  4. 1930

Answer: 1920

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920. It called for boycott of British goods, institutions, and courts.

5. Who founded the Indian National Congress in 1885?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  3. A.O. Hume
  4. Gopal Krishna Gokhale

Answer: A.O. Hume

Explanation: Allan Octavian Hume, a retired British civil servant, founded the Indian National Congress in 1885 in Bombay.

6. The 'Quit India Movement' was launched on:

  1. 8 August 1940
  2. 8 August 1942
  3. 15 August 1942
  4. 26 January 1942

Answer: 8 August 1942

Explanation: The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi on 8 August 1942 at the Bombay session of the All India Congress Committee with the slogan 'Do or Die'.

7. Who was the last Viceroy of British India?

  1. Lord Wavell
  2. Lord Curzon
  3. Lord Mountbatten
  4. Lord Irwin

Answer: Lord Mountbatten

Explanation: Lord Louis Mountbatten was the last Viceroy of British India (1947). He oversaw the partition of India and transfer of power on 15 August 1947.

8. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was annulled in:

  1. 1907
  2. 1910
  3. 1911
  4. 1919

Answer: 1911

Explanation: The Partition of Bengal (1905) by Lord Curzon was annulled in 1911 under Lord Hardinge following massive protests. The capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

9. Who wrote the book 'Discovery of India'?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Rabindranath Tagore
  4. Dr. Ambedkar

Answer: Jawaharlal Nehru

Explanation: Jawaharlal Nehru wrote 'Discovery of India' while imprisoned in Ahmednagar Fort (1944). It traces India's history from ancient times to the independence movement.

10. The 'Dandi March' protested against:

  1. Partition of Bengal
  2. Rowlatt Act
  3. Salt tax imposed by British
  4. Jalianwala Bagh massacre

Answer: Salt tax imposed by British

Explanation: The Dandi March (1930) protested against the British salt monopoly and the heavy salt tax. Gandhi walked 241 miles to Dandi to make salt from seawater — a symbol of civil disobedience.

11. The famous 'Chauri Chaura' incident of 1922 led to:

  1. Launch of Non-Cooperation Movement
  2. Suspension of Non-Cooperation Movement
  3. Launch of Civil Disobedience Movement
  4. Launch of Quit India Movement

Answer: Suspension of Non-Cooperation Movement

Explanation: On 5 February 1922, a mob attacked a police station at Chauri Chaura (UP), killing 22 policemen. Gandhi called off the Non-Cooperation Movement as a result.

12. The 'Lucknow Pact' (1916) was an agreement between:

  1. Congress and Muslim League
  2. Congress and British Government
  3. Muslim League and British Government
  4. Congress and Maratha leaders

Answer: Congress and Muslim League

Explanation: The Lucknow Pact (1916) was an agreement between the Indian National Congress (led by Tilak) and the All India Muslim League (led by Jinnah) for joint action against British rule and for separate electorates.

13. The 'Indian Independence Act 1947' was passed by the British Parliament on:

  1. 3 June 1947
  2. 4 July 1947
  3. 18 July 1947
  4. 15 August 1947

Answer: 18 July 1947

Explanation: The Indian Independence Act 1947 received Royal Assent on 18 July 1947. It provided for the partition of British India into two independent dominions — India and Pakistan.

14. The 'Rowlatt Act' (1919) was officially known as the:

  1. Indian Sedition Act
  2. The Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act
  3. Defence of India Act
  4. Press Restriction Act

Answer: The Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act (officially: Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, 1919) allowed the government to imprison people without trial. It was strongly opposed by Gandhi and led to nationwide protests.

15. The 'Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms' of 1919 introduced which concept in Indian administration?

  1. Provincial Autonomy
  2. Dyarchy (dual government) in provinces
  3. Complete responsible government
  4. Federal system

Answer: Dyarchy (dual government) in provinces

Explanation: The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act 1919) introduced 'Dyarchy' in provinces — dividing subjects into 'transferred' (under Indian ministers) and 'reserved' (under the Governor) categories.

16. The 'Poona Pact' (1932) was signed between Ambedkar and Gandhi on the issue of:

  1. Partition of India
  2. Separate electorates for Dalits (Depressed Classes)
  3. Provincial autonomy
  4. Temple entry for untouchables

Answer: Separate electorates for Dalits (Depressed Classes)

Explanation: The Poona Pact (September 1932) between B.R. Ambedkar and Gandhi provided reserved seats for Depressed Classes (Dalits/SCs) in the general electorate — instead of separate electorates (as Ambedkar had demanded). Gandhi fasted to oppose separate electorates.

17. The 'Champaran Satyagraha' (1917) was related to:

  1. Salt tax
  2. Indigo cultivation under Tinkathia system
  3. Land revenue policy
  4. Partition of Bengal

Answer: Indigo cultivation under Tinkathia system

Explanation: The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) was Gandhi's first Satyagraha in India. It was against the Tinkathia system — forcing peasants to cultivate indigo on 3/20th of their land and sell it to British planters at fixed prices.

18. Gopal Krishna Gokhale founded the:

  1. Servants of India Society
  2. Indian National Congress
  3. Hindu Mahasabha
  4. Brahmo Samaj

Answer: Servants of India Society

Explanation: Gopal Krishna Gokhale founded the Servants of India Society (1905) to train people for selfless public service. He was Gandhi's political mentor.

19. The Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) introduced:

  1. Separate electorates for Muslims
  2. Dyarchy
  3. Provincial autonomy
  4. Universal adult franchise

Answer: Separate electorates for Muslims

Explanation: Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909) introduced separate electorates for Muslims — Muslims would vote only for Muslim candidates — a communal step that sowed seeds of partition.

20. Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) proposed:

  1. Union of India with provinces and princely states
  2. Direct independence
  3. Partition
  4. Federal structure only for provinces

Answer: Union of India with provinces and princely states

Explanation: Cabinet Mission Plan (1946): proposed a three-tier federal structure — Union, provincial groups, and provinces — to keep India united. Congress accepted it but Muslim League initially rejected.

21. The Indian Independence Act was passed on:

  1. 18 July 1947
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 26 January 1947
  4. 14 August 1947

Answer: 18 July 1947

Explanation: The Indian Independence Act was passed by British Parliament on 18 July 1947, partitioning British India into two dominions — India and Pakistan — effective 15 August 1947.

22. Lord Mountbatten announced the date of partition as:

  1. 3 June 1947 plan
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 14 August 1947
  4. 18 July 1947

Answer: 3 June 1947 plan

Explanation: The Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan) of 3 June 1947 announced the partition of British India into India and Pakistan. 15 August was set as the transfer of power date.

23. The Poona Pact (1932) was signed between:

  1. Gandhi and Ambedkar
  2. Gandhi and Jinnah
  3. Gandhi and Churchill
  4. Congress and Muslim League

Answer: Gandhi and Ambedkar

Explanation: Poona Pact (September 1932): Gandhi fasted unto death against Communal Award's separate electorate for untouchables. Ambedkar and Gandhi signed the pact — reserved seats within joint electorates instead.

24. The first session of Indian National Congress was presided over by:

  1. W.C. Bonnerjee
  2. Dadabhai Naoroji
  3. A.O. Hume
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: W.C. Bonnerjee

Explanation: Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee) presided over the first session of the Indian National Congress in Bombay (December 1885).

25. 'Swaraj Party' was formed by:

  1. C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru
  2. Gandhi and Tilak
  3. Congress and Muslim League
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru

Explanation: Swaraj Party was formed in 1923 by C.R. Das (Deshbandhu) and Motilal Nehru, after disagreements within Congress on entering legislatures to 'wreck' the government from within.
