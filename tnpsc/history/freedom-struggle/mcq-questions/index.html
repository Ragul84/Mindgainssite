Freedom Struggle MCQs with answers

26. Emergency in India was declared in:

  1. 1975
  2. 1971
  3. 1965
  4. 1977

Answer: 1975

Explanation: Prime Minister Indira Gandhi declared the Emergency on 25 June 1975 (citing internal disturbance), lasting until 21 March 1977. Civil liberties were suspended and press censored.

27. Operation Blue Star (1984) was conducted at:

  1. Golden Temple, Amritsar
  2. Lal Masjid
  3. Hazratbal shrine
  4. Kedarnath

Answer: Golden Temple, Amritsar

Explanation: Operation Blue Star (June 1984): Indian Army stormed the Golden Temple in Amritsar to remove Sikh separatist militants led by Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale. Led to Indira Gandhi's assassination.

28. The Viceroy during the Quit India Movement was:

  1. Lord Linlithgow
  2. Lord Mountbatten
  3. Lord Wavell
  4. Lord Irwin

Answer: Lord Linlithgow

Explanation: Lord Linlithgow was Viceroy (1936-1943) during the Quit India Movement (August 1942). He immediately arrested all Congress leaders and suppressed the movement harshly.

29. The Cripps Mission (1942) was sent to India to:

  1. Offer dominion status after WWII for Indian cooperation
  2. Grant immediate independence
  3. Partition India
  4. Negotiate with INA

Answer: Offer dominion status after WWII for Indian cooperation

Explanation: Stafford Cripps Mission (March 1942) offered India dominion status after WWII in exchange for Indian support in the war. Congress rejected it as a 'Post-dated cheque on a crashing bank' (Gandhi).

30. The 'Dandi March' is also called:

  1. Salt Satyagraha
  2. Civil Disobedience Movement
  3. Non-Cooperation Movement
  4. Khilafat Movement

Answer: Salt Satyagraha

Explanation: Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930) launched the Civil Disobedience Movement and is also called the Salt Satyagraha. Gandhi's march to make salt symbolized civil disobedience against unjust British laws.

31. Who presided over the Lahore session of INC (1929) that declared Purna Swaraj?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Gandhi
  3. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  4. Subhas Bose

Answer: Jawaharlal Nehru

Explanation: Jawaharlal Nehru presided over the Lahore session of INC (December 1929) where the resolution for Purna Swaraj (complete independence) was adopted, leading to the Civil Disobedience Movement.

32. 'Swadeshi' movement was mainly a response to:

  1. Partition of Bengal 1905
  2. Rowlatt Act
  3. Jallianwala Bagh
  4. Simon Commission

Answer: Partition of Bengal 1905

Explanation: The Swadeshi movement (1905-1908) was launched in response to the partition of Bengal by Lord Curzon. It promoted use of Indian goods and boycott of British products.

33. Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal primarily:

  1. To weaken the nationalist movement
  2. For administrative efficiency only
  3. Due to religious reasons
  4. To promote regional identity

Answer: To weaken the nationalist movement

Explanation: Though Lord Curzon cited administrative efficiency, the partition of Bengal (1905) was primarily designed to break the unity of nationalist movements by creating Hindu and Muslim-majority provinces.

34. The Nehru Report (1928) was drafted by:

  1. Motilal Nehru committee
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru alone
  3. Gandhi and Nehru
  4. Simon Commission

Answer: Motilal Nehru committee

Explanation: Nehru Report (1928) was prepared by a committee under Motilal Nehru (father), responding to the Simon Commission's challenge to Indians to draft their own constitution.

35. The Treaty of Versailles (1919) formally ended:

  1. World War I
  2. World War II
  3. Franco-Prussian War
  4. Crimean War

Answer: World War I

Explanation: Treaty of Versailles (28 June 1919) formally ended WWI, imposing harsh reparations on Germany ($33 billion), limiting its military, and assigning war guilt — conditions that helped cause WWII.

36. The Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) was founded at:

  1. Belgrade Conference, 1961
  2. Bandung Conference, 1955
  3. Cairo Conference, 1964
  4. New Delhi, 1961

Answer: Belgrade Conference, 1961

Explanation: NAM was formally established at the 1st Non-Aligned Conference in Belgrade (1961), led by Nehru (India), Nasser (Egypt), Tito (Yugoslavia), Nkrumah (Ghana), and Sukarno (Indonesia).

37. Which Act introduced dyarchy in provinces?

  1. Government of India Act 1919
  2. Government of India Act 1935
  3. Indian Councils Act 1909
  4. Indian Independence Act 1947

Answer: Government of India Act 1919

Explanation: Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms) introduced dyarchy (dual government) in provinces — some subjects given to elected Indian ministers (transferred), others kept by British (reserved).

38. The 'Champaran Satyagraha' (1917) was Gandhi's first major satyagraha in India against:

  1. Indigo plantation system
  2. Salt law
  3. Partition of Bengal
  4. Rowlatt Act

Answer: Indigo plantation system

Explanation: Champaran Satyagraha (1917) in Bihar was Gandhi's first major satyagraha in India — against the tinkathia system forcing indigo cultivation on peasants. Gandhi's first major success in India.

39. The Kheda Satyagraha (1918) was about:

  1. Remission of land tax during famine
  2. Salt tax
  3. Indigo cultivation
  4. Sepoy grievances

Answer: Remission of land tax during famine

Explanation: Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat): Gandhi supported peasants' demand for remission of land revenue during a crop failure. It was his second major satyagraha in India after Champaran.

40. The partition of Bengal (1905) was done by:

  1. Lord Curzon
  2. Lord Minto
  3. Lord Morley
  4. Lord Mountbatten

Answer: Lord Curzon

Explanation: Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal on 16 October 1905 (dividing along communal lines: West Bengal + Bihar + Orissa vs. East Bengal + Assam). This sparked massive nationalist protests and the Swadeshi Movement. Partition was revoked in 1911.

41. The Indian National Congress was founded in:

  1. 1857
  2. 1875
  3. 1885
  4. 1905

Answer: 1885

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was founded on 28 December 1885 at Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College) by A.O. Hume, a retired British civil servant. Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee was its first president.

42. Mahatma Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in:

  1. 1919
  2. 1920
  3. 1930
  4. 1942

Answer: 1920

Explanation: Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement on 1 August 1920 (following the Khilafat grievances and Jallianwala Bagh massacre). Indians were asked to boycott British courts, schools, goods, and legislature. Suspended after Chauri Chaura violence (February 1922).

43. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919) occurred because:

  1. Gandhi gave orders to fire
  2. British General R.E.H. Dyer ordered troops to fire on a peaceful unarmed crowd gathered in the walled garden in Amritsar on 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi day)
  3. It was a planned revolt by revolutionaries
  4. It was a Hindu-Muslim riot

Answer: British General R.E.H. Dyer ordered troops to fire on a peaceful unarmed crowd gathered in the walled garden in Amritsar on 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi day)

Explanation: On 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi), thousands gathered at Jallianwala Bagh (walled garden), Amritsar, to protest the Rowlatt Act. General Dyer ordered troops to fire without warning — killing ~379 (official) to 1,000+ (unofficial). This caused worldwide outrage.

44. The Dandi March (Salt March) of 1930 was:

  1. A march to protest partition
  2. Gandhi's 241-mile march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi (Gujarat coast) to defy British salt laws by making salt — launching Civil Disobedience Movement
  3. A march against the Rowlatt Act
  4. A march to demand complete independence

Answer: Gandhi's 241-mile march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi (Gujarat coast) to defy British salt laws by making salt — launching Civil Disobedience Movement

Explanation: The Salt March (12 March – 6 April 1930): Gandhi led 78 followers on a 241-mile (385 km) march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi to make salt from seawater, defying the Salt Laws. This launched the Civil Disobedience Movement — thousands were arrested.

45. The Quit India Movement (1942) resolution was passed at:

  1. Bombay Session of Congress (AICC), August 1942
  2. Lahore Session
  3. Calcutta Session
  4. Wardha Session

Answer: Bombay Session of Congress (AICC), August 1942

Explanation: The Quit India Resolution was passed at the Bombay session of the AICC (7 August 1942) at Gowalia Tank Maidan. Gandhi gave the slogan 'Do or Die'. On 9 August, all Congress leaders were arrested; the movement turned violent.

46. India gained independence on:

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 January 1950
  3. 15 August 1950
  4. 26 January 1947

Answer: 15 August 1947

Explanation: India became independent on 15 August 1947 under the Indian Independence Act, 1947. Lord Mountbatten was the first Governor-General of India. Jawaharlal Nehru became the first Prime Minister. India became a Republic on 26 January 1950.

47. The 'Vedaranyam March' (1930) in Tamil Nadu was led by:

  1. Subramania Bharati
  2. C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji)
  3. Periyar
  4. T. Prakasam

Answer: C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji)

Explanation: C. Rajagopalachari led the Vedaranyam Salt Satyagraha (30 April 1930) in Tamil Nadu, paralleling Gandhi's Dandi March. He marched from Trichinopoly to Vedaranyam (240 km) to violate the salt law.

48. The partition of Bengal was annulled in:

  1. 1911
  2. 1905
  3. 1919
  4. 1916

Answer: 1911

Explanation: Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905. Due to massive protests (Swadeshi Movement), the partition was annulled in 1911 when the capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

49. The Rowlatt Act (1919) allowed:

  1. Detention without trial
  2. Separate electorates
  3. Dyarchy
  4. Self-government

Answer: Detention without trial

Explanation: Rowlatt Act (March 1919) allowed the British to imprison anyone suspected of sedition without trial. Gandhi called for its withdrawal and launched a hartal (nationwide strike).

50. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurred on:

  1. 13 April 1919
  2. 13 April 1918
  3. 13 March 1919
  4. 13 May 1919

Answer: 13 April 1919

Explanation: On 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi), General Reginald Dyer ordered troops to fire on a large peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. Hundreds were killed.
