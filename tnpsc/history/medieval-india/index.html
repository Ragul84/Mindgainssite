Medieval India MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Medieval India questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 314 questions.

1. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between:

  1. Akbar and Hemu
  2. Babur and Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Humayun and Sher Shah Suri
  4. Baber and Rana Sanga

Answer: Babur and Ibrahim Lodi

Explanation: The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur (founder of Mughal Empire) and Ibrahim Lodi (last ruler of Delhi Sultanate). Babur's victory established Mughal rule in India.

2. Tipu Sultan was the ruler of which kingdom?

  1. Hyderabad
  2. Mysore
  3. Maratha Empire
  4. Vijayanagara Empire

Answer: Mysore

Explanation: Tipu Sultan (1750–1799) was the Sultan of Mysore. He was called the 'Tiger of Mysore' for his fierce resistance against British expansion. He died in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War.

3. Which battle in 1565 CE decisively ended the Vijayanagara Empire?

  1. Battle of Talikota
  2. Battle of Panipat (Third)
  3. Battle of Tarain
  4. Battle of Haldighat

Answer: Battle of Talikota

Explanation: The Battle of Talikota (1565) was fought between the Vijayanagara Empire and the Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Bidar, Ahmadnagar, Golconda). The decisive defeat led to the sack of the Vijayanagara capital.

4. The first battle between Indian rulers and Mahmud of Ghazni was at:

  1. Battle of Waihind (1001)
  2. Battle of Tarain
  3. Battle of Panipat
  4. Battle of Somnath

Answer: Battle of Waihind (1001)

Explanation: Mahmud of Ghazni's first major battle in India was the Battle of Waihind (near Peshawar) in 1001 CE against Raja Jaipal of Punjab, whom Mahmud defeated.

5. The Tabaqat-i-Nasiri was written by:

  1. Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani
  2. Alberuni
  3. Ibn Battuta
  4. Amir Khusrau

Answer: Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani

Explanation: Tabaqat-i-Nasiri by Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani (1260 CE) is a valuable source of history of early Delhi Sultans, covering rulers up to Balban's period.

6. The treaty that ended the Third Anglo-Mysore War was:

  1. Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
  2. Treaty of Mangalore (1784)
  3. Treaty of Srirangapatna (1799)
  4. Treaty of Mysore (1799)

Answer: Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)

Explanation: Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) ended the Third Anglo-Mysore War — Tipu Sultan surrendered half his territory and paid large indemnity. The Fourth War (1799) ended with Tipu's death.

7. Tipu Sultan died defending:

  1. Srirangapatna Fort
  2. Mysore Palace
  3. Bangalore Fort
  4. Chitradurga

Answer: Srirangapatna Fort

Explanation: Tipu Sultan (Sultan of Mysore) died on 4 May 1799 defending his capital Srirangapatna against British forces. His death ended the kingdom of Mysore.

8. In Indian history, 'Second Battle of Tarain (1192)' established:

  1. Permanent Muslim rule in North India
  2. Maratha dominance
  3. Rajput supremacy
  4. British East India Company dominance

Answer: Permanent Muslim rule in North India

Explanation: Second Battle of Tarain (1192) — Muhammad Ghori's defeat of Prithviraj Chauhan — established permanent Muslim rule in North India, beginning the Delhi Sultanate period.

9. The Bahmani Kingdom was founded by:

  1. Alauddin Bahman Shah
  2. Mahmud Gawan
  3. Firuz Shah Bahmani
  4. Ibrahim Adil Shah

Answer: Alauddin Bahman Shah

Explanation: Alauddin Bahman Shah (Hassan Gangu, 1347-1358) founded the Bahmani Kingdom after revolting against Muhammad bin Tughluq of Delhi. It split into 5 Deccan Sultanates after 1518.

10. The first Sultan of Delhi was:

  1. Iltutmish
  2. Muhammad Ghori
  3. Qutb-ud-din Aibak
  4. Balban

Answer: Qutb-ud-din Aibak

Explanation: Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206–1210 CE) was the first Sultan of Delhi and founder of the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty. He was a slave of Muhammad Ghori. He started the Qutb Minar and the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque.

11. The five dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate in order were:

  1. Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi
  2. Khalji, Slave, Tughlaq, Lodi, Sayyid
  3. Lodi, Tughlaq, Slave, Khalji, Sayyid
  4. Slave, Lodi, Khalji, Sayyid, Tughlaq

Answer: Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi

Explanation: Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526): 1) Mamluk/Slave dynasty (1206–1290), 2) Khalji dynasty (1290–1320), 3) Tughlaq dynasty (1320–1414), 4) Sayyid dynasty (1414–1451), 5) Lodi dynasty (1451–1526).

12. Iltutmish is considered the true founder of the Delhi Sultanate because:

  1. He was the first Sultan
  2. He consolidated the Sultanate, got recognition from Caliph of Baghdad, completed Qutb Minar, and introduced iqta system
  3. He defeated Prithviraj Chauhan
  4. He invaded South India

Answer: He consolidated the Sultanate, got recognition from Caliph of Baghdad, completed Qutb Minar, and introduced iqta system

Explanation: Iltutmish (1210–1236) got recognition from the Abbasid Caliph (Islamic legitimacy), introduced the iqta system (land revenue assignments for military service), completed the Qutb Minar (started by Aibak), and introduced silver tanka and copper jital coins.

13. The policy of 'Blood and Iron' (despotism) was followed by:

  1. Iltutmish
  2. Alauddin Khalji
  3. Balban
  4. Muhammad bin Tughlaq

Answer: Balban

Explanation: Balban (1266–1287) followed the policy of 'Blood and Iron' (loh-o-khalid) — ruthless suppression of rebellion, strict court discipline, Persian concept of kingship (Zil-i-illahi = Shadow of God), and separation of military from revenue departments.

14. Alauddin Khalji introduced market reforms to:

  1. Increase state revenue
  2. Control prices of essential commodities to maintain a large army cheaply
  3. Benefit Muslim traders
  4. Punish Hindu merchants

Answer: Control prices of essential commodities to maintain a large army cheaply

Explanation: Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316) introduced price control markets (Sarai Adl) to keep commodity prices fixed, enabling him to maintain a large army (3,00,000 cavalry) at low cost. Four regulated markets: grain, cattle, cloth, miscellaneous.

15. The last Sultan of Delhi was:

  1. Sikandar Lodi
  2. Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Bahlul Lodi
  4. Daulat Khan Lodi

Answer: Ibrahim Lodi

Explanation: Ibrahim Lodi (1517–1526) was the last Sultan of Delhi. He was defeated and killed at the First Battle of Panipat (1526) by Babur, ending the Delhi Sultanate and beginning the Mughal Empire.

16. The Battle of Talikota (1565) resulted in:

  1. Vijayanagara victory over the Deccan Sultanates
  2. Destruction of the Vijayanagara Empire — the capital Hampi was sacked and ruined
  3. Mughal invasion of South India
  4. Alliance between Vijayanagara and Bijapur

Answer: Destruction of the Vijayanagara Empire — the capital Hampi was sacked and ruined

Explanation: The Battle of Talikota (Rakshasatangadi, 1565) was fought between Vijayanagara (Aliya Rama Raya) and the allied Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Bidar, Golconda). Rama Raya was killed, Hampi was devastated and never recovered.

17. The first battle of Tarain (1191) was fought between:

  1. Prithviraj III and Muhammad Ghori
  2. Prithviraj III and Mahmud of Ghazni
  3. Iltutmish and Prithviraj
  4. Ghori and Ghazni

Answer: Prithviraj III and Muhammad Ghori

Explanation: First Battle of Tarain (1191): Prithviraj Chahamana (Chauhan) defeated Muhammad Ghori. Second Battle (1192): Ghori defeated and killed Prithviraj, opening the way for Muslim rule in India.

18. The founder of the Slave Dynasty was:

  1. Qutb-ud-din Aibak
  2. Iltutmish
  3. Balban
  4. Razia Sultana

Answer: Qutb-ud-din Aibak

Explanation: Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206-1210 CE) founded the Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty — the first Muslim dynasty in India. He was a slave of Muhammad Ghori who rose to become a sultan.

19. The first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate was:

  1. Razia Sultana
  2. Chand Bibi
  3. Nur Jahan
  4. Jahanara

Answer: Razia Sultana

Explanation: Razia Sultana (1236-1240 CE), daughter of Iltutmish, was the first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate. She discarded purdah and held court openly.

20. Alauddin Khilji introduced 'Market Reforms' to:

  1. Control prices for military efficiency
  2. Encourage trade
  3. Increase tax revenue
  4. Promote agriculture

Answer: Control prices for military efficiency

Explanation: Alauddin Khilji's market reforms (Price Regulation) fixed prices of commodities — to maintain a large army cheaply and efficiently without increasing taxes.

21. The 'iqta' system in Delhi Sultanate was similar to:

  1. Feudal land grants for military service
  2. Tax farming
  3. Permanent settlement
  4. Zamindari system

Answer: Feudal land grants for military service

Explanation: Iqta was a grant of land revenue (not land itself) to military officers (iqtadars) in lieu of salary, in return for maintaining troops and providing military service.

22. Which dynasty built the Khajuraho temples?

  1. Gupta
  2. Maurya
  3. Pallava
  4. Chola

Answer: Gupta

Explanation: The Khajuraho temples were built during the Gupta dynasty. They are famous for their intricate carvings and sculptures.

23. Who founded the Vijayanagara empire?

  1. Harihara
  2. Bukka
  3. Krishna Deva
  4. Ashoka

Answer: Harihara

Explanation: Harihara and Bukka founded the Vijayanagara empire. They were brothers and local chieftains.

24. What was the capital of the Pallava kingdom?

  1. Kanchipuram
  2. Madurai
  3. Thanjavur
  4. Tirunelveli

Answer: Kanchipuram

Explanation: Kanchipuram was the capital of the Pallava kingdom. It was an important city for trade and culture.

25. Which South Indian kingdom built the Brihadeeswara temple?

  1. Chola
  2. Pallava
  3. Pandya
  4. Chera

Answer: Chola

Explanation: The Brihadeeswara temple was built by the Chola kingdom. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
