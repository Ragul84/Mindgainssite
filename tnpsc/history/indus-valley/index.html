Indus Valley MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Indus Valley questions to revise the TNPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The ancient city of Mohenjo-daro is located in present-day:

  1. India
  2. Pakistan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. Nepal

Answer: Pakistan

Explanation: Mohenjo-daro is located in Sindh province of present-day Pakistan. It was one of the major centres of the Indus Valley Civilization.

2. The Indus Valley Civilization is also known as:

  1. Vedic Civilization
  2. Harappan Civilization
  3. Aryan Civilization
  4. Dravidian Civilization

Answer: Harappan Civilization

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilization is also called Harappan Civilization, named after Harappa — one of its major sites (in present-day Pakistan), excavated in 1921.

3. The 'Wavell Plan' (1945) was presented at which conference?

  1. Round Table Conference
  2. Shimla Conference
  3. Lahore Conference
  4. London Conference

Answer: Shimla Conference

Explanation: The Wavell Plan (1945) was presented at the Shimla Conference. It proposed that the Executive Council of the Viceroy would have equal representation of Hindus and Muslims.

4. The Indus Valley Civilisation is also called:

  1. Vedic Civilisation
  2. Harappan Civilisation
  3. Dravidian Civilisation
  4. Gangetic Civilisation

Answer: Harappan Civilisation

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilisation is also called the Harappan Civilisation, named after Harappa — the first site to be excavated (1921). It was one of the four ancient river valley civilisations.

5. Harappa was excavated for the first time by:

  1. John Marshall
  2. R.D. Banerji
  3. Daya Ram Sahni
  4. Mortimer Wheeler

Answer: Daya Ram Sahni

Explanation: Harappa was first excavated by Daya Ram Sahni in 1921. Mohenjo-daro was excavated by R.D. Banerji in 1922. Sir John Marshall was the Director General of ASI who coordinated the excavations.

6. Mohenjo-daro is located in present-day:

  1. India (Gujarat)
  2. India (Rajasthan)
  3. Pakistan (Sindh)
  4. Pakistan (Punjab)

Answer: Pakistan (Sindh)

Explanation: Mohenjo-daro (meaning 'Mound of the Dead') is located in Sindh province, Pakistan, on the right bank of the Indus River. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

7. The 'Great Bath' found at Mohenjo-daro was:

  1. A royal bathing pool
  2. A large public water tank, possibly used for ritual bathing
  3. A reservoir for drinking water
  4. A swimming pool for children

Answer: A large public water tank, possibly used for ritual bathing

Explanation: The Great Bath (11.88m × 7m, 2.43m deep) was a large public bath, possibly used for ritual purification. It was made watertight with burnt bricks and bitumen. One of the most famous structures of the IVC.

8. Which Indus Valley site is famous for a dockyard?

  1. Harappa
  2. Mohenjo-daro
  3. Lothal
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Lothal

Explanation: Lothal (Gujarat) had a unique dockyard (214m × 36m) — the earliest known in the world. It connected to the Sabarmati river, suggesting advanced maritime trade. Also famous for bead-making factories.

9. The Indus Valley people worshipped:

  1. Only male gods
  2. Mother goddess, Pashupati (proto-Shiva), and animals like bull and unicorn
  3. The sun and moon exclusively
  4. No deities were found

Answer: Mother goddess, Pashupati (proto-Shiva), and animals like bull and unicorn

Explanation: IVC people worshipped a Mother Goddess (fertility), Pashupati (a seated figure surrounded by animals — proto-Shiva), trees (peepal), and animals. Figurines and seals show these religious elements.

10. The script of the Indus Valley Civilisation is:

  1. Brahmi
  2. Kharosthi
  3. Undeciphered pictographic script
  4. Devanagari

Answer: Undeciphered pictographic script

Explanation: The Indus script is a pictographic/logo-syllabic script that has not been deciphered. Over 400 signs have been identified. Without a bilingual inscription like the Rosetta Stone, scholars cannot read it.

11. The standard unit of weight used by the Harappans was:

  1. Based on the decimal system
  2. Based on the binary system (doubling: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64) and then decimal
  3. Identical to Mesopotamian weights
  4. There was no standard weight

Answer: Based on the binary system (doubling: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64) and then decimal

Explanation: Harappan weights followed a binary system up to 64, then decimal: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64,160,200,320,640. Cuboid chert weights were found across sites, suggesting standardised trade.

12. Kalibangan, an Indus Valley site, is located in:

  1. Gujarat
  2. Punjab
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Haryana

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: Kalibangan is located in Hanumangarh district, Rajasthan, on the Ghaggar (ancient Saraswati) river. Famous for fire altars, ploughed fields (earliest evidence of ploughing), and pre-Harappan settlement.

13. The decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation (c. 1900 BCE) was most likely caused by:

  1. Aryan invasions alone
  2. A combination of factors: climate change, flooding, river course changes, and possible epidemic
  3. Nuclear radiation (fringe theory)
  4. Complete drying up of all rivers

Answer: A combination of factors: climate change, flooding, river course changes, and possible epidemic

Explanation: Most modern scholars believe the IVC decline was multicausal: climate change causing drought, shifts in monsoon patterns, flooding, river course changes (Ghaggar-Hakra drying up), and possible trade collapse. The 'Aryan invasion' theory alone is now rejected.

14. The Indus Valley people traded with:

  1. China and Japan
  2. Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) — seals found in Ur and other Mesopotamian sites
  3. Egypt and Greece
  4. No external trade evidence

Answer: Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) — seals found in Ur and other Mesopotamian sites

Explanation: IVC traded with Mesopotamia (Sumer, Akkad). Harappan seals, weights, and carnelian beads found at Ur. Mesopotamian texts mention 'Meluhha' — believed to refer to the Indus region.

15. Which crop was NOT cultivated by the Indus Valley people?

  1. Wheat
  2. Barley
  3. Rice (at some sites)
  4. Maize

Answer: Maize

Explanation: Maize is a New World crop that reached India only after Columbus (post-1492). Indus people cultivated wheat, barley, cotton (first in the world!), rice (Lothal), dates, sesame, and mustard.

16. Cotton was first cultivated in the world by:

  1. Egyptians
  2. Mesopotamians
  3. Indus Valley people
  4. Chinese

Answer: Indus Valley people

Explanation: Cotton was first cultivated by the Indus Valley people around 3000 BCE. They also produced cotton textiles. Mesopotamians called it 'sindon' (from Sindh). India remained a major cotton producer throughout history.

17. The Rigveda mentions which river most frequently?

  1. Ganga
  2. Indus (Sindhu)
  3. Saraswati
  4. Yamuna

Answer: Saraswati

Explanation: The Saraswati River is the most praised river in the Rigveda, described as the greatest of rivers. The Sindhu (Indus) is also frequently mentioned. The Ganga appears only twice in the Rigveda.

18. The Harappan city known for its large dockyard is:

  1. Lothal
  2. Mohenjo-daro
  3. Harappa
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Lothal

Explanation: Lothal in Gujarat had a well-planned dockyard (170m×37m), indicating maritime trade with Mesopotamia. It is the only Harappan site with a dockyard.

19. Great Bath was found at which Harappan site?

  1. Mohenjo-daro
  2. Harappa
  3. Lothal
  4. Dholavira

Answer: Mohenjo-daro

Explanation: The Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro (11.88m×7.01m) is the most striking feature of the Indus Valley Civilization, used for ritualistic bathing.

20. The Indus script is:

  1. Undeciphered
  2. Brahmi-based
  3. Sanskrit-derived
  4. Dravidian

Answer: Undeciphered

Explanation: The Indus script (Harappan script) remains undeciphered despite many attempts. It contains over 400 signs and was written right-to-left.

21. Which Harappan site is located in Gujarat and is known for its water conservation systems?

  1. Dholavira
  2. Lothal
  3. Surkotada
  4. Rangpur

Answer: Dholavira

Explanation: Dholavira in Gujarat is known for its elaborate water conservation system with large reservoirs, stepwells, and dams — unique among all Harappan sites.

22. Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) culture preceded which period in India?

  1. Vedic period
  2. Gupta period
  3. Kushan period
  4. Mauryan period

Answer: Vedic period

Explanation: OCP culture (2000-1500 BCE) in the Ganga plains preceded the Vedic period and marks the transition from the Harappan civilization to early Vedic settlements.

23. Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) was co-founded by:

  1. Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad
  2. Gandhi and Nehru
  3. Subhas Bose and Tilak
  4. VOC and Subramania Bharati

Answer: Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad

Explanation: HSRA was reorganized by Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev, and others in 1928. They believed in socialist revolution through direct action against British imperialism.

24. The first cotton textile mill in India was established at:

  1. Bombay (1854)
  2. Calcutta (1830)
  3. Madras (1870)
  4. Ahmedabad (1860)

Answer: Bombay (1854)

Explanation: India's first modern cotton textile mill was established in Bombay in 1854 by Cowasjee Nanabhoy Davar. This marked the beginning of modern industrialization in India.

25. The word 'India' is derived from the river:

  1. Indus (Sindhu)
  2. Ganga
  3. Brahmaputra
  4. Kaveri

Answer: Indus (Sindhu)

Explanation: The name 'India' derives from the river Indus (Sanskrit: Sindhu). Ancient Persians and Greeks called the land east of the Indus as 'Hind' or 'India'.
