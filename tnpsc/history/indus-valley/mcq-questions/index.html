26. The term 'Second Urbanization' in Indian history refers to:

  1. Rise of cities along Ganga around 600 BCE
  2. Harappan Civilization
  3. Mughal city building
  4. British colonial cities

Answer: Rise of cities along Ganga around 600 BCE

Explanation: Second Urbanization (c. 600-300 BCE) refers to the rise of large cities (Pataliputra, Vaishali, Kashi, Ujjain) in the Gangetic plains, distinct from the first urbanization of the Indus Valley.

27. Firuz Shah Tughlaq is known for:

  1. Military conquests
  2. Welfare works — canals (including the 200km Rajasthan canal), hospitals (shifakhana), madrasas, rest houses, orchards; banned torture
  3. Introducing market reforms
  4. Inviting Timur to India

Answer: Welfare works — canals (including the 200km Rajasthan canal), hospitals (shifakhana), madrasas, rest houses, orchards; banned torture

Explanation: Firuz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388) built extensive infrastructure: 150 wells, 100 caravanserais, 30 reservoirs/canals, 100 hospitals, 150 bridges. Banned many cruel punishments. But also discriminated against Hindus.

28. The Indus Valley script has approximately how many signs?

  1. 400+
  2. 26
  3. 50-100
  4. 1000+

Answer: 400+

Explanation: The Indus Valley script has over 400 distinct signs, suggesting it is a logosyllabic system (like Egyptian hieroglyphics). It remains one of the world's great undeciphered writing systems.

29. Akbar's policy of 'Sulh-i-Kul' (Universal Peace) meant:

  1. Conquering all kingdoms
  2. Religious tolerance towards all faiths — abolishing jizya, allowing all religions to practice freely
  3. Peaceful coexistence only with Muslims
  4. Sanskrit-based governance

Answer: Religious tolerance towards all faiths — abolishing jizya, allowing all religions to practice freely

Explanation: Sulh-i-Kul was Akbar's (1556–1605) policy of universal harmony — abolishing jizya (non-Muslim tax), allowing free practice of all religions, marrying Rajput princess Jodha Bai, including Hindus in court, and starting Din-i-Ilahi (1582).

30. Aurangzeb's policy towards Hindus differed from Akbar's because:

  1. He was more generous with Hindus
  2. He reimposed jizya (1679), destroyed some Hindu temples, banned music, and applied strict Sharia — more orthodox Islamic policies
  3. He gave more Hindus positions in administration
  4. He merged Hindu and Muslim festivals

Answer: He reimposed jizya (1679), destroyed some Hindu temples, banned music, and applied strict Sharia — more orthodox Islamic policies

Explanation: Aurangzeb (1658–1707) reversed Akbar's tolerant policies: reimposed jizya (1679), destroyed some temples (Kashi Vishwanath, Mathura's Keshav Deo), banned music and dance, executed Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675). This led to Rajput and Sikh revolts.

31. The Swadeshi Movement (1905) involved:

  1. Promoting foreign goods
  2. Boycott of British goods and promotion of Indian-made goods — also included national education and Swadesh sanghas
  3. Only political speeches
  4. Violent uprisings only

Answer: Boycott of British goods and promotion of Indian-made goods — also included national education and Swadesh sanghas

Explanation: The Swadeshi Movement arose after the Partition of Bengal (1905). It involved: burning foreign cloth, boycotting British goods, promoting Indian industries (Swadeshi), national schools, and Swadesh Sanghas. Leaders: Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai (Lal-Bal-Pal), Aurobindo.

32. The Industrial Revolution first began in:

  1. England
  2. France
  3. Germany
  4. USA

Answer: England

Explanation: The Industrial Revolution began in Britain (England) in the 18th century (1760s-1840s) with mechanization of textile industry, steam engine, iron production, and railways.

33. The Industrial Revolution began in:

  1. France
  2. Germany
  3. Britain (England, 18th century)
  4. United States

Answer: Britain (England, 18th century)

Explanation: The Industrial Revolution began in Britain (c. 1760–1840), driven by coal, iron, steam power, and textile innovations (Spinning Jenny, power loom, steam engine by James Watt). Britain's colonial empire provided raw materials and markets.

34. Which of the following sites of the Indus Valley Civilization is located in the Indian state of Gujarat?

  1. Lothal
  2. Harappa
  3. Mohenjo-daro
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Lothal

Explanation: Lothal is a major IVC site in Gujarat, known for its dockyard and bead-making industry.

35. The Great Bath of the Indus Valley Civilization was discovered at which site?

  1. Mohenjo-daro
  2. Harappa
  3. Chanhudaro
  4. Dholavira

Answer: Mohenjo-daro

Explanation: The Great Bath, a large brick-lined public bathing structure, is located at Mohenjo-daro.

36. Which metal was NOT known to the people of the Indus Valley Civilization?

  1. Gold
  2. Silver
  3. Iron
  4. Copper

Answer: Iron

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilization was a Bronze Age culture; iron was not known to them.

37. Which animal is NOT depicted on the seals of the Indus Valley Civilization?

  1. Unicorn
  2. Tiger
  3. Horse
  4. Bull

Answer: Horse

Explanation: The horse was not native to the IVC and is absent from its seals and artifacts.

38. The Indus Valley Civilization belongs to which historical period?

  1. Palaeolithic
  2. Mesolithic
  3. Neolithic
  4. Bronze Age

Answer: Bronze Age

Explanation: The IVC is a Bronze Age civilization, evidenced by its use of bronze tools and weapons.

39. Which of the following is considered the most distinctive feature of the Indus Valley Civilization's town planning?

  1. Grid-like street layout
  2. Circular houses
  3. Pyramids
  4. Underground tunnels

Answer: Grid-like street layout

Explanation: The IVC cities, like Mohenjo-daro, followed a systematic grid pattern with straight streets cutting at right angles.

40. The famous 'Dancing Girl' bronze statue was found at which Indus Valley site?

  1. Harappa
  2. Lothal
  3. Mohenjo-daro
  4. Rakhigarhi

Answer: Mohenjo-daro

Explanation: The bronze 'Dancing Girl' figurine was discovered at Mohenjo-daro.

41. Which crop was NOT cultivated by the people of the Indus Valley Civilization?

  1. Wheat
  2. Barley
  3. Rice
  4. Cotton

Answer: Rice

Explanation: While wheat, barley, and cotton were grown, rice cultivation is not conclusively proven for the mature IVC.

42. What was the primary script used by the Indus Valley Civilization?

  1. Brahmi
  2. Kharosthi
  3. Indus script
  4. Pali

Answer: Indus script

Explanation: The IVC used a pictographic script, known as the Indus script, which remains largely undeciphered.

43. The Indus Valley Civilization declined around which approximate date?

  1. 2500 BCE
  2. 1900 BCE
  3. 1500 BCE
  4. 1000 BCE

Answer: 1900 BCE

Explanation: The mature phase of the IVC ended around 1900 BCE, followed by a gradual decline.

44. Which is the largest known site of the Indus Valley Civilization?

  3. Rakhigarhi

Answer: Rakhigarhi

Explanation: Rakhigarhi in Haryana is the largest Indus site, covering over 350 hectares.

  2. Elephant
  3. Unicorn (mythical)
