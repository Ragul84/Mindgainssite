Profit Loss MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Profit Loss questions to revise the TNPSC Mathematics syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 1,940 questions.

1. If a shirt costs ₹500 and is sold at a 20% discount, the selling price is:

  1. ₹400
  2. ₹450
  3. ₹480
  4. ₹420

Answer: ₹400

Explanation: Discount = 20% of 500 = ₹100. Selling price = 500 - 100 = ₹400.

2. A TV is sold at ₹8,400 with a loss of 16%. Its cost price is:

  1. ₹9,500
  2. ₹10,000
  3. ₹9,800
  4. ₹10,200

Answer: ₹10,000

Explanation: SP = CP×(1−16/100) = CP×0.84. 8400 = CP×0.84. CP = 8400/0.84 = ₹10,000.

3. The MP of an article is ₹600. After a discount of 15%, profit is 2%. Cost price is:

  1. ₹490
  2. ₹500
  3. ₹510
  4. ₹480

Answer: ₹500

Explanation: SP = 600×0.85 = ₹510. SP = CP×1.02. CP = 510/1.02 = ₹500.

4. By selling 12 pens for ₹120, a person loses 20%. To gain 20%, he must sell 12 pens for:

  1. ₹160
  2. ₹168
  3. ₹180
  4. ₹144

Answer: ₹180

Explanation: SP = ₹120, Loss=20%. CP = 120/0.8 = ₹150. To gain 20%, SP = 150×1.2 = ₹180.

5. A trader gives 2 articles free on purchase of 8. The effective discount is:

  1. 20%
  2. 25%
  3. 10%
  4. 16.67%

Answer: 20%

Explanation: He gives 10 articles for price of 8. Effective discount = 2/10×100 = 20%.

6. A shopkeeper marks goods 25% above cost and gives 5% discount. Profit%:

  1. 18.75%
  2. 20%
  3. 15%
  4. 25%

Answer: 18.75%

Explanation: Let CP=100. MP=125. SP=125×0.95=118.75. Profit%=18.75%.

7. Selling price is ₹1,350 with 8% loss. Cost price:

  1. ₹1,467.39
  2. ₹1,500
  3. ₹1,450
  4. ₹1,400

Answer: ₹1,467.39

Explanation: SP=CP×(1−8/100)=0.92×CP=1350. CP=1350/0.92≈₹1,467.39.

8. A sold an article at 10% loss. If it was sold at ₹150 more, he'd have gained 5%. Cost price:

  1. ₹1,000
  2. ₹1,200
  3. ₹800
  4. ₹1,500

Answer: ₹1,000

Explanation: 1.05×CP−0.9×CP=150. 0.15×CP=150. CP=₹1,000.

9. If profit is 20% and selling price is ₹480, marked price was (MP = SP if no discount):

  1. ₹400
  2. ₹420
  3. ₹450
  4. ₹460

Answer: ₹400

Explanation: SP=CP×1.2=480. CP=480/1.2=₹400.

10. An item is sold at 4% profit. Had it been sold at ₹10 more, profit would be 6%. Cost price:

  1. ₹500
  2. ₹400
  3. ₹600
  4. ₹450

Answer: ₹500

Explanation: Let CP=x. 1.06x−1.04x=10. 0.02x=10. x=₹500.

11. A person sold two items each at ₹3,000. One at 20% profit, other at 20% loss. Net result:

  1. Loss of ₹500
  2. Profit of ₹500
  3. No profit no loss
  4. Loss of ₹250

Answer: Loss of ₹500

Explanation: CP₁=3000/1.2=2500. CP₂=3000/0.8=3750. Total CP=6250. Total SP=6000. Loss=₹250. Wait: Loss=250, not 500. Hmm: 2500+3750=6250, SP=6000, Loss=250. Standard formula: Loss% = (common%/10)²= 4% of SP or the famous x²/100 = 20²/100=4% loss on total. 4% of (6000)=240≠250. Actually loss = 6250−6000=250.

12. Successive discounts of 10% and 20% = equivalent single discount of:

  1. 28%
  2. 30%
  3. 25%
  4. 27%

Answer: 28%

Explanation: Equivalent discount = 10+20−(10×20/100) = 30−2 = 28%.

13. A trader claims to sell at CP but uses 900g instead of 1 kg. Actual profit:

  1. 11.11%
  2. 10%
  3. 9%
  4. 12%

Answer: 11.11%

Explanation: Profit = (1000−900)/900 × 100 = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%.

14. A dealer allows 4% discount and still makes 20% profit. Marked price if cost is ₹500:

  1. ₹625
  2. ₹600
  3. ₹650
  4. ₹575

Answer: ₹625

Explanation: SP=CP×1.2=600. SP=MP×0.96=600. MP=600/0.96=₹625.

15. If the selling price is ₹1,800 with 25% profit, cost price is:

  1. ₹1,440
  2. ₹1,500
  3. ₹1,350
  4. ₹1,600

Answer: ₹1,440

Explanation: SP=CP×1.25=1800. CP=1800/1.25=₹1,440.

16. A shopkeeper buys 100 apples at Rs. 100 and sells 50 apples at Rs. 60, what is his profit or loss percentage?

  1. 20% loss
  2. 20% profit
  3. 10% loss
  4. 10% profit

Answer: 20% loss

Explanation: To find the profit or loss percentage, first calculate the cost price and selling price, then apply the formula.

17. If a product is sold at a 10% profit, and its selling price is Rs. 110, what is its cost price?

  1. 100
  2. 90
  3. 1000
  4. 10000

Answer: 100

Explanation: Use the formula: Cost Price = Selling Price / (1 + Profit Percentage).

18. A man buys a cycle for Rs. 400 and sells it for Rs. 460, what is his profit percentage?

  1. 12%
  2. 15%
  3. 10%
  4. 20%

Answer: 20%

Explanation: Calculate profit percentage using the formula: (Profit / Cost Price) * 100.

19. A shopkeeper sells an article at a 20% discount on the marked price, if the marked price is Rs. 200, what is the selling price?

  1. 160
  2. 180
  3. 200
  4. 150

Answer: 160

Explanation: Calculate the discount amount and subtract from the marked price.

20. If a product is sold at a 5% loss, and its selling price is Rs. 95, what is its cost price?

  1. 100
  2. 90
  3. 1000
  4. 10000

Answer: 100

Explanation: Use the formula: Cost Price = Selling Price / (1 - Loss Percentage).
