Earth Universe MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Earth Universe questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which state in India receives the highest rainfall?

  1. Assam
  2. Meghalaya
  3. Kerala
  4. West Bengal

Answer: Meghalaya

Explanation: Meghalaya receives the highest rainfall in India. Mawsynram in Meghalaya is the wettest place on Earth.

2. The Sundarbans delta is formed by which rivers?

  1. Ganga and Yamuna
  2. Ganga and Brahmaputra
  3. Indus and Chenab
  4. Krishna and Godavari

Answer: Ganga and Brahmaputra

Explanation: The Sundarbans delta — the world's largest mangrove forest — is formed by the Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers in West Bengal and Bangladesh.

3. The Atacama Desert — the driest place on Earth — is in which continent?

  1. Africa
  2. Asia
  3. South America
  4. Australia

Answer: South America

Explanation: The Atacama Desert, the driest non-polar desert on Earth, is in northern Chile (South America).

4. The latitude that divides India roughly into northern and southern halves passes through:

  1. Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)
  2. 30°N latitude
  3. Standard Meridian (82.5°E)
  4. Equator (0°)

Answer: Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N) passes through the middle of India, dividing it into tropical south and subtropical north.

5. The 'Roaring Forties', 'Furious Fifties', and 'Screaming Sixties' are associated with:

  1. Monsoon winds
  2. Polar easterlies
  3. Strong westerly winds in southern hemisphere
  4. Tropical cyclones

Answer: Strong westerly winds in southern hemisphere

Explanation: These are strong westerly winds that blow in the southern hemisphere between 40°-70°S latitude. They are unimpeded by land masses and are very strong.

6. The northernmost point of India is:

  1. Siachen Glacier
  2. Ladakh
  3. Indira Col (in Ladakh, J&K — administered)
  4. Leh

Answer: Indira Col (in Ladakh, J&K — administered)

Explanation: Indira Col (Indira Point) in the Karakoram Range, Ladakh (J&K) is the northernmost point of India at approximately 37°6'N latitude.

7. The Mangrove forests in India are most extensive in:

  1. Maharashtra coast
  2. Kerala backwaters
  3. Sundarbans (West Bengal/Bangladesh) — world's largest mangrove delta
  4. Tamil Nadu coast

Answer: Sundarbans (West Bengal/Bangladesh) — world's largest mangrove delta

Explanation: The Sundarbans in West Bengal (and Bangladesh) contain the world's largest mangrove forest (10,000 km²). India has significant mangrove coverage in Odisha, Gujarat (Gulf of Kutch), Tamil Nadu (Pichavaram), and Kerala (Vembanad).

8. Mangrove forests in India are largest in:

  1. Sundarbans (West Bengal)
  2. Kerala
  3. Goa
  4. Odisha

Answer: Sundarbans (West Bengal)

Explanation: India has the world's largest mangrove — the Sundarbans in West Bengal. Other mangrove areas: Godavari-Krishna delta (AP), Mahanadi delta (Odisha), Gulf of Kutch (Gujarat), and Andamans.

9. The world's largest ocean is:

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Pacific Ocean

Explanation: The Pacific Ocean (165 million sq km) is the world's largest ocean. It contains the Mariana Trench — the deepest point on Earth (11,034 m).

10. Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin) is where:

  1. Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, and Indian Ocean meet
  2. Two seas meet
  3. Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean meet
  4. Palk Strait meets Indian Ocean

Answer: Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, and Indian Ocean meet

Explanation: Kanyakumari (southernmost tip of Indian mainland) is the confluece point of the Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, and Indian Ocean. It is famous for the sunrise/sunset view and the Vivekananda Rock Memorial.

11. Which state has the largest reserves of iron ore in India?

  1. Odisha
  2. Jharkhand
  3. Chhattisgarh
  4. Karnataka

Answer: Odisha

Explanation: Odisha has the largest reserves of iron ore in India (about 33% of total). Major mines: Barbil-Sundergarh region. Karnataka (Bellary-Hospet) and Chhattisgarh-Jharkhand are also major producers.

12. The Western Ghats are a biodiversity hotspot. How many biodiversity hotspots are there globally?

  1. 36 hotspots
  2. 25 hotspots
  3. 50 hotspots
  4. 18 hotspots

Answer: 36 hotspots

Explanation: There are 36 global biodiversity hotspots as recognized by Conservation International. India has 4 hotspots: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma region, and Sundaland (includes Andaman & Nicobar).

13. The easternmost point of India is:

  1. Kibithu, Arunachal Pradesh
  2. Imphal, Manipur
  3. Kohima, Nagaland
  4. Agartala, Tripura

Answer: Kibithu, Arunachal Pradesh

Explanation: Kibithu in Anjaw district, Arunachal Pradesh (97.5°E) is the easternmost village/point of India. India's easternmost extent is about 97°25'E longitude.

14. The westernmost point of India is:

  1. Sir Creek area, Gujarat
  2. Lakshadweep Islands
  3. Dwaraka, Gujarat
  4. Kutch, Gujarat

Answer: Sir Creek area, Gujarat

Explanation: The westernmost point of India's mainland is the Sir Creek area/western Rann of Kutch, Gujarat (approximately 68°7'E). India's westernmost extent is about 68°7'E longitude.

15. Hirakud Dam (world's longest earthen dam) is on:

  1. Mahanadi river, Odisha
  2. Godavari
  3. Brahmani
  4. Baitarani

Answer: Mahanadi river, Odisha

Explanation: Hirakud Dam on the Mahanadi river in Odisha is one of the world's longest earthen dams at 25.8 km. It forms Hirakud reservoir, one of India's largest artificial lakes.

16. Yellow Revolution in India is associated with:

  1. Oilseed production (edible oils)
  2. Wheat production
  3. Pulses
  4. Sugar

Answer: Oilseed production (edible oils)

Explanation: Yellow Revolution refers to the rapid increase in oilseed production in India (primarily sunflower, mustard, groundnut, soybean). The Technology Mission on Oilseeds (1986) launched by PM Rajiv Gandhi triggered this.

17. The 'Prime Meridian' passes through:

  1. Greenwich, London (UK)
  2. Paris, France
  3. Madrid, Spain
  4. Cairo, Egypt

Answer: Greenwich, London (UK)

Explanation: The Prime Meridian (0° longitude) passes through the Greenwich Observatory in London, UK, established by international agreement in 1884. It divides the Earth into Eastern and Western hemispheres.

18. 'Isohalines' connect places having:

  1. Equal salinity in the sea
  2. Equal pressure
  3. Equal temperature
  4. Equal rainfall

Answer: Equal salinity in the sea

Explanation: Isohalines are imaginary lines connecting points of equal salinity in an ocean or sea. The ocean's salinity varies with latitude — highest near the tropics (due to evaporation) and lower at the equator (rainfall).

19. Madurai is known as the 'Temple City' for:

  1. Meenakshi Amman Temple (Arulmigu Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple)
  2. Having 1000 temples
  3. Being the oldest city in South India
  4. Being Pandya kingdom capital

Answer: Meenakshi Amman Temple (Arulmigu Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple)

Explanation: Madurai is called the 'Temple City' primarily for the Meenakshi Amman Temple (Arulmigu Meenakshi Sundareswarar) — one of the largest Hindu temples in the world with 14 gopurams. It was the ancient Pandya capital.

20. The Ganga-Brahmaputra delta (Sundarbans) is the world's:

  1. Largest river delta in the world
  2. Second largest delta
  3. Largest mangrove forest only
  4. Largest lagoon

Answer: Largest river delta in the world

Explanation: The Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna delta (Sundarbans) is the world's largest river delta, covering about 100,000 sq km across Bangladesh and West Bengal (India). It is also the world's largest mangrove forest.

21. The 'Chipko Movement' was related to:

  1. Forest conservation (hugging trees to prevent felling) in Uttarakhand
  2. Water conservation
  3. Wildlife protection
  4. Anti-pollution

Answer: Forest conservation (hugging trees to prevent felling) in Uttarakhand

Explanation: The Chipko Movement (1973) in Uttarakhand was a nonviolent protest where villagers (led by Sundarlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt) hugged trees to prevent logging. It influenced India's forest policy.

22. 'Great Circle' routes are used by aircraft because:

  1. They are the shortest paths between two points on a sphere
  2. They avoid turbulence
  3. They follow the equator
  4. They avoid mountains

Answer: They are the shortest paths between two points on a sphere

Explanation: Great Circle routes are the shortest paths between two points on a sphere (Earth). Aircraft flying long-distance routes follow great circle paths to save fuel and time, even though the routes may appear curved on flat maps.

23. The phenomenon of 'Aurora Borealis' (Northern Lights) is seen near:

  1. Arctic/polar regions due to solar wind interactions with Earth's magnetic field
  2. Tropical regions
  3. Near the equator
  4. Near the Tropic of Cancer

Answer: Arctic/polar regions due to solar wind interactions with Earth's magnetic field

Explanation: Aurora Borealis (Northern Lights) and Aurora Australis (Southern Lights) occur near the poles when charged particles from the sun (solar wind) interact with Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere, causing colored light displays.

24. The 'time zone' concept was introduced to standardize time because:

  1. Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours (15° per hour), causing local times to differ
  2. Different countries chose different times
  3. Trains needed scheduled departures
  4. The sun rises at different times

Answer: Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours (15° per hour), causing local times to differ

Explanation: Earth rotates 15° per hour (360°/24h). For every 15° of longitude, time differs by 1 hour. Time zones standardize this so regions use the same clock time. The 1884 Washington Conference established 24 time zones.

25. The Equator passes through which continents?

  1. South America, Africa, and Asia (through the islands of Indonesia)
  2. Only Africa and South America
  3. Only Africa
  4. All continents

Answer: South America, Africa, and Asia (through the islands of Indonesia)

Explanation: The Equator (0° latitude) passes through 3 continents: South America (Ecuador, Colombia, Brazil), Africa (Gabon, Congo, DR Congo, Uganda, Kenya, Somalia), and Asia (Indonesian islands — Sumatra, Borneo, Sulawesi).
