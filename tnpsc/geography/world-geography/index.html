TNPSC Geography practice

World Geography MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked World Geography questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which continent is India a part of?

  1. Africa
  2. Europe
  3. Asia
  4. Australia

Answer: Asia

Explanation: India is located in South Asia, which is a subregion of Asia.

2. India shares its longest international border with which country?

  1. China
  2. Nepal
  3. Pakistan
  4. Bangladesh

Answer: Bangladesh

Explanation: India shares its longest international border with Bangladesh (4,096 km).

3. India's total geographical area is approximately:

  1. 2.4 million km²
  2. 3.28 million km²
  3. 4.5 million km²
  4. 1.9 million km²

Answer: 3.28 million km²

Explanation: India's total area is 3.28 million km² (32.8 lakh sq km), making it the 7th largest country in the world. It accounts for about 2.4% of the world's total land area.

4. Lakshadweep is the smallest Union Territory of India. Its capital is:

  1. Kavaratti
  2. Agatti
  3. Minicoy
  4. Androth

Answer: Kavaratti

Explanation: Kavaratti is the capital of Lakshadweep, India's smallest Union Territory by area (32 sq km). Agatti island has the only airport. The islands are coral atolls in the Arabian Sea.

5. According to the 2011 census, India's population was approximately:

  1. 1.21 billion (121 crore)
  2. 1.35 billion
  3. 1 billion
  4. 1.15 billion

Answer: 1.21 billion (121 crore)

Explanation: India's population was 1.21 billion (121 crore) as per the 2011 census. India is projected to overtake China as the world's most populous country — which happened around 2023.

6. India shares the longest border with which country?

  1. Bangladesh
  2. China
  3. Pakistan
  4. Nepal

Answer: Bangladesh

Explanation: India shares its longest land border with Bangladesh (4,156 km), followed by China (3,488 km), Pakistan (3,323 km), Nepal (1,751 km), Myanmar (1,643 km), Bhutan (699 km), and Afghanistan (106 km, disputed).

7. India's Silicon Valley is:

  1. Bengaluru (Bangalore)
  2. Hyderabad
  3. Chennai
  4. Mumbai

Answer: Bengaluru (Bangalore)

Explanation: Bengaluru (Bangalore) is called India's Silicon Valley due to concentration of IT companies — Infosys, Wipro, HAL, ISRO are headquartered here. It is also called the 'Pub Capital', 'Garden City', 'Startup Capital of India'.

8. White Revolution in India is associated with:

  1. Milk production (Operation Flood)
  2. Sugar production
  3. Cotton production
  4. Salt production

Answer: Milk production (Operation Flood)

Explanation: White Revolution (Operation Flood, 1970-1996) under Dr. Verghese Kurien (Father of White Revolution) transformed India from a milk-deficient country to the world's largest milk producer through cooperative dairy development (AMUL model).

9. The 'continental shelf' is:

  1. The submerged extension of a continent under the shallow sea (up to 200 m depth)
  2. The edge of a continent
  3. A mountain range under the sea
  4. The abyssal plain

Answer: The submerged extension of a continent under the shallow sea (up to 200 m depth)

Explanation: The continental shelf is the gently sloping submerged extension of a continent from the shoreline to a depth of about 200 m. Beyond it is the steeper continental slope, then the ocean floor/abyssal plain.

10. Thanjavur is famous for:

  1. Brihadeeswarar Temple, Tanjore paintings, and Carnatic music tradition
  2. IT sector
  3. Leather industry
  4. Textiles

Answer: Brihadeeswarar Temple, Tanjore paintings, and Carnatic music tradition

Explanation: Thanjavur (Tanjore) — former Chola capital — is known for the Brihadeeswarar (Big) Temple (UNESCO World Heritage), Tanjore paintings, Tanjore bronze statues, Carnatic music, and rich cultural heritage.

11. Kancheepuram (Kanchi) is famous for:

  1. Silk sarees and ancient temples (Pallava capital)
  2. Cotton textiles
  3. Steel industry
  4. IT sector

Answer: Silk sarees and ancient temples (Pallava capital)

Explanation: Kancheepuram is the 'City of Thousand Temples' — famous for silk weaving (Kanchipuram silk sarees), and ancient Pallava temples (Ekambareswarar, Kailasanathar, Varadharaja Perumal). It was the Pallava kingdom capital.

12. The largest country in Africa by area is:

  1. Algeria
  2. Sudan
  3. DR Congo
  4. Libya

Answer: Algeria

Explanation: Algeria (2.38 million sq km) is the largest country in Africa by area and the 10th largest in the world, after the 2011 partition of Sudan (which created South Sudan — Sudan is now smaller).

13. Which Indian city has the largest natural harbour?

  1. Mumbai
  2. Vizag (Visakhapatnam)
  3. Kochi
  4. Chennai

Answer: Mumbai

Explanation: Mumbai (Bombay) has one of the best natural harbours in the world. This is a key reason why it became India's commercial capital — it allowed large ships to dock safely. Vizag is India's deepest port.

14. The island country of Sri Lanka was formerly known as:

  1. Ceylon
  2. Serendip
  3. Lanka
  4. Taprobane

Answer: Ceylon

Explanation: Sri Lanka was called Ceylon (in English, from Portuguese 'Ceilão') until 1972 when it became a republic and adopted the name Sri Lanka. The ancient name was Serendip (Arabian/Persian) and Taprobane (Greek).

15. The capital of Australia is:

  1. Canberra
  2. Sydney
  3. Melbourne
  4. Brisbane

Answer: Canberra

Explanation: Canberra is the capital of Australia — it was specifically chosen as a compromise between rival cities Sydney and Melbourne. Sydney and Melbourne are much larger, but Canberra was built as a planned federal capital (1913).

16. The capital of Canada is:

  1. Ottawa
  2. Toronto
  3. Vancouver
  4. Montreal

Answer: Ottawa

Explanation: Ottawa (Ontario) is the capital of Canada. Toronto is the largest city and financial capital. Ottawa was chosen as capital by Queen Victoria in 1857 as a compromise between Toronto (Ontario) and Montreal (Quebec).

17. The capital of Brazil is:

  1. Brasília
  2. São Paulo
  3. Rio de Janeiro
  4. Salvador

Answer: Brasília

Explanation: Brasília is the capital of Brazil — a planned capital built in just 41 months and inaugurated in 1960. Before Brasília, Rio de Janeiro was the capital. São Paulo is Brazil's largest city and economic center.

18. The capital of Japan is:

  1. Tokyo
  2. Osaka
  3. Kyoto
  4. Hiroshima

Answer: Tokyo

Explanation: Tokyo is the capital and largest city of Japan (population ~14 million city, ~38 million metro area — world's largest metropolitan area). Before 1868, Kyoto was Japan's capital.

19. Which is the only state in India with two capitals (summer and winter)?

  1. Himachal Pradesh (Shimla in summer, Dharamsala or Shimla all year; J&K — Srinagar/Jammu)
  2. Uttarakhand
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Tamil Nadu

Answer: Himachal Pradesh (Shimla in summer, Dharamsala or Shimla all year; J&K — Srinagar/Jammu)

Explanation: J&K had two capitals — Srinagar (summer) and Jammu (winter) — before becoming a UT. After J&K reorganization (2019), both Srinagar and Jammu serve as capitals of the J&K UT. Himachal Pradesh's capital is only Shimla.

20. Chandigarh is the capital of:

  1. Punjab and Haryana both (and a Union Territory itself)
  2. Only Punjab
  3. Only Haryana
  4. Himachal Pradesh

Answer: Punjab and Haryana both (and a Union Territory itself)

Explanation: Chandigarh is a Union Territory that serves as the shared capital of both Punjab and Haryana. Designed by Le Corbusier, it was established after Partition (Lahore went to Pakistan) as Punjab's new capital.

21. Itanagar is the capital of:

  1. Arunachal Pradesh
  2. Meghalaya
  3. Nagaland
  4. Manipur

Answer: Arunachal Pradesh

Explanation: Itanagar is the capital of Arunachal Pradesh. Arunachal Pradesh is India's largest northeastern state by area and borders China, Bhutan, and Myanmar. Its capital Itanagar is named after Ita Fort.

22. Dispur (part of Guwahati) is the capital of:

  1. Assam
  2. Meghalaya
  3. Nagaland
  4. Mizoram

Answer: Assam

Explanation: Dispur (a locality within Guwahati) is the capital of Assam. Guwahati itself is the largest city in Assam and Northeast India. Assam's state legislative assembly is in Dispur.

23. The Khyber Pass connects:

  1. Pakistan (Peshawar) and Afghanistan (Kabul) — historic invasion route into India
  2. India and Pakistan
  3. India and China
  4. Afghanistan and Iran

Answer: Pakistan (Peshawar) and Afghanistan (Kabul) — historic invasion route into India

Explanation: Khyber Pass (3.5 km wide at narrowest) connects Peshawar (Pakistan) with Kabul (Afghanistan). Historically the most important invasion route into the Indian subcontinent — used by Persians, Greeks, Mughals.

24. Pune (Maharashtra) is called:

  1. 'Oxford of the East' for its numerous educational institutions
  2. 'Silicon Valley of India'
  3. 'IT capital of India'
  4. 'Gateway of India'

Answer: 'Oxford of the East' for its numerous educational institutions

Explanation: Pune is called 'Oxford of the East' for its many universities and colleges (Pune University, Symbiosis, Fergusson College, etc.). It is also a major IT and automobile hub. Peshwas made it their capital.

25. The capital of India is:

  1. Mumbai
  2. Kolkata
  3. New Delhi
  4. Hyderabad

Answer: New Delhi

Explanation: New Delhi is the capital of India and houses the Parliament, President's House, and Supreme Court.
