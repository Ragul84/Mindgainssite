India Climate MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked India Climate questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The 'Monsoon' word is derived from which language?

  1. Portuguese
  2. Dutch
  3. Arabic
  4. Hindi

Answer: Arabic

Explanation: The word 'monsoon' is derived from the Arabic word 'mausim' meaning season or seasonal wind.

2. The Rann of Kutch is a seasonal salt marsh located in:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Gujarat
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: Gujarat

Explanation: The Rann of Kutch is a vast seasonal salt marsh in Gujarat, India, and Pakistan. It is the world's largest salt desert.

3. India's climate is classified as:

  1. Tropical monsoon climate
  2. Mediterranean climate
  3. Continental climate
  4. Desert climate

Answer: Tropical monsoon climate

Explanation: India has a tropical monsoon climate (Koppen: Am/Aw/Bsh). The word 'monsoon' comes from Arabic 'mausim' (season). The four seasons: Cold weather (Dec-Feb), Hot weather (Mar-May), Southwest monsoon (Jun-Sep), Retreating monsoon (Oct-Nov).

4. The wettest place in India (and one of the wettest in the world) is:

  1. Assam
  2. Cherrapunji/Sohra (and nearby Mawsynram), Meghalaya
  3. Mumbai
  4. Thiruvananthapuram

Answer: Cherrapunji/Sohra (and nearby Mawsynram), Meghalaya

Explanation: Mawsynram (11,873mm/year average) and Cherrapunji/Sohra (11,777mm/year) in Meghalaya receive the highest rainfall in India and among the highest in the world. This is due to orographic (relief) rainfall as moist Bay of Bengal winds hit the Khasi Hills.

5. El Niño affects Indian monsoon by:

  1. Strengthening the monsoon
  2. Weakening the southwest monsoon, often leading to deficient rainfall/drought in India
  3. Having no effect
  4. Causing floods in Tamil Nadu

Answer: Weakening the southwest monsoon, often leading to deficient rainfall/drought in India

Explanation: El Niño (warming of central/eastern Pacific Ocean) weakens the Walker Circulation, suppresses Indian monsoon convection, and typically leads to below-normal monsoon rainfall in India — causing droughts. La Niña has the opposite effect (stronger monsoon).

6. Laterite soil is formed in regions with:

  1. High rainfall and low temperature
  2. High temperature and high seasonal rainfall — leaching removes silica, iron and aluminium oxides remain
  3. Low rainfall, dry conditions
  4. River alluvium deposits

Answer: High temperature and high seasonal rainfall — leaching removes silica, iron and aluminium oxides remain

Explanation: Laterite soils form in hot, humid tropical regions with high seasonal rainfall (alternating wet-dry seasons). Leaching removes silica and bases; iron and aluminium oxides remain, giving red/brick-like colour. Found in Tamil Nadu hill stations, Kerala, Karnataka, Odisha, Assam.

7. The 'Kharif crops' are sown during:

  1. October-November
  2. April-May
  3. June-July (beginning of southwest monsoon) — harvested October-November
  4. January-February

Answer: June-July (beginning of southwest monsoon) — harvested October-November

Explanation: Kharif crops are sown at the start of the southwest monsoon (June-July) and harvested in Oct-Nov. Examples: rice, cotton, jowar (sorghum), bajra, groundnut, sugarcane, jute. Rabi crops are sown Oct-Nov, harvested March-April (wheat, mustard, barley).

8. Tamil Nadu's main crop and what it depends on:

  1. Wheat, depends on canal irrigation
  2. Rice, depends primarily on northeast monsoon (Oct-Dec) and Kaveri/other rivers
  3. Cotton, depends on Vaigai dam
  4. Sugarcane, depends on dams

Answer: Rice, depends primarily on northeast monsoon (Oct-Dec) and Kaveri/other rivers

Explanation: Rice is Tamil Nadu's main crop. Tamil Nadu's agriculture depends heavily on the northeast monsoon (Oct-Dec) unlike most of India (which depends on southwest monsoon). The Kaveri delta (Thanjavur) is the 'Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu'.

9. Vedanthangal Bird Sanctuary in Tamil Nadu is famous for:

  1. Tigers and leopards
  2. Water birds (Painted stork, Open-billed stork, Cormorants, Egrets) — Asia's oldest bird sanctuary
  3. Elephants
  4. Crocodiles

Answer: Water birds (Painted stork, Open-billed stork, Cormorants, Egrets) — Asia's oldest bird sanctuary

Explanation: Vedanthangal (Chengalpattu district) is Asia's oldest and one of India's most important bird sanctuaries. It hosts thousands of migratory and resident water birds during the breeding season (Nov-March). Established c. 1798 by local farmers.

10. Tamil Nadu receives most of its rainfall from:

  1. Northeast monsoon (October-December)
  2. Southwest monsoon
  3. Cyclones from Bay of Bengal
  4. Western disturbances

Answer: Northeast monsoon (October-December)

Explanation: Tamil Nadu is unique — it receives about 60% of its annual rainfall from the Northeast (Retreating) monsoon during October-December. This is why Tamil Nadu gets rain when most other states have post-monsoon dry weather.

11. The southwest monsoon arrives in India first in:

  1. Kerala (around 1 June)
  2. Tamil Nadu
  3. Mumbai
  4. Goa

Answer: Kerala (around 1 June)

Explanation: The southwest monsoon arrives in Kerala around 1 June each year and then progresses northward. Kerala is the first Indian State to receive the southwest monsoon.

12. Western Disturbances bring winter rainfall to:

  1. North India (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Jammu & Kashmir)
  2. South India
  3. Northeast India
  4. Rajasthan only

Answer: North India (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Jammu & Kashmir)

Explanation: Western Disturbances are cyclonic systems from the Mediterranean Sea that travel eastward, bringing winter rainfall to North India (especially Punjab, Haryana, UP) — beneficial for Rabi crops (wheat).

13. The retreat of the southwest monsoon begins from:

  1. Northwestern India (Rajasthan) in September
  2. Northern India in August
  3. South India in October
  4. Bay of Bengal in November

Answer: Northwestern India (Rajasthan) in September

Explanation: The southwest monsoon retreats (withdraws) from India starting from Rajasthan/northwestern India in September, and completely withdraws from India by early December (it retreats southward).

14. El Niño phenomenon is associated with:

  1. Unusual warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean warming
  3. Indian Ocean cooling
  4. Arctic warming

Answer: Unusual warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean

Explanation: El Niño is a climate pattern where the central and eastern Pacific Ocean warms unusually, disrupting global weather. It typically causes drought in India (weak monsoon), flooding in South America, and other global effects.

15. The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to limit global warming to:

  1. Well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels (pursuing 1.5°C)
  2. 5°C above pre-industrial levels
  3. 2.5°C above pre-industrial levels
  4. 1°C above pre-industrial levels

Answer: Well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels (pursuing 1.5°C)

Explanation: The Paris Agreement (COP21, December 2015) aims to hold global average temperature increase to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

16. 'Isotherms' connect places having:

  1. Equal temperature
  2. Equal pressure
  3. Equal rainfall
  4. Equal altitude

Answer: Equal temperature

Explanation: Isotherms are lines on a map connecting places having the same mean temperature at a given time or for a given period. They help visualize temperature patterns and climate zones.

17. The Rann of Kutch is a unique geographical feature because:

  1. It is a large salt marsh/seasonal lake (dry in summer, flooded in monsoon)
  2. It is the largest desert in India
  3. It is always flooded
  4. It is a cold desert

Answer: It is a large salt marsh/seasonal lake (dry in summer, flooded in monsoon)

Explanation: The Rann of Kutch (Gujarat) is a large seasonal salt marsh — dry and salty in summer (white expanse), and partially flooded during monsoon. It covers ~23,000 sq km. Famous for the Wild Ass Sanctuary and Rann Utsav.

18. The main Indian Ocean monsoon system is driven by:

  1. Differential heating of land and sea causing seasonal reversal of winds
  2. Trade winds alone
  3. Jet streams alone
  4. Coriolis effect only

Answer: Differential heating of land and sea causing seasonal reversal of winds

Explanation: The Indian monsoon is driven by differential heating — land heats faster than the sea. In summer, low pressure over the hot Indian landmass draws moisture-laden winds from the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal, causing rain.

19. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands experience heavy rainfall because:

  1. They receive both southwest and northeast monsoon rains
  2. They are near the equator only
  3. Cyclones alone
  4. Trade winds cause orographic rainfall

Answer: They receive both southwest and northeast monsoon rains

Explanation: Andaman and Nicobar Islands receive rainfall from both the southwest monsoon (May-October) and the northeast monsoon (November-December), giving them about 300 cm of annual rainfall. They are near the equator.

20. Which Indian state produces the most cotton?

  1. Gujarat
  2. Maharashtra
  3. Telangana
  4. Punjab

Answer: Gujarat

Explanation: Gujarat is India's largest cotton producer (about 35% of India's cotton). Maharashtra (Vidarbha) is second. India is the world's largest cotton producer. Cotton requires black cotton soil and moderate rainfall.

21. The Atacama Desert in South America (Chile/Peru) is the driest because:

  1. Rain shadow effect of Andes + cold Humboldt current causing subsiding dry air
  2. Distance from ocean
  3. Polar influence
  4. Trade wind pattern

Answer: Rain shadow effect of Andes + cold Humboldt current causing subsiding dry air

Explanation: Atacama is the world's driest non-polar desert due to: (1) rain shadow of the Andes blocking Atlantic moisture, and (2) cold Humboldt current stabilizing the atmosphere, preventing convective rainfall.

22. The largest salt lake in India is:

  1. Sambhar Lake, Rajasthan
  2. Chilika Lake, Odisha
  3. Pulicat Lake, Tamil Nadu/AP
  4. Pangong Tso, Ladakh

Answer: Sambhar Lake, Rajasthan

Explanation: Sambhar Lake (Rajasthan) is India's largest inland saltwater lake (about 230 sq km during monsoon, much smaller in summer). It supplies most of Rajasthan's salt. Chilika is brackish, not fully saline.

23. The 'Convergence Zone' where trade winds from both hemispheres meet is called:

  1. Polar Front
  2. Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)
  3. Sub-tropical High Pressure Belt
  4. Ferrel Cell

Answer: Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)

Explanation: The ITCZ (Intertropical Convergence Zone) is a band near the equator where northeast and southeast trade winds converge. It plays a major role in the Indian monsoon mechanism.

24. Kodaikanal hill station is known for:

  1. Star-shaped lake, shola forests, and pleasant climate
  2. Being highest hill station in South India
  3. Tiger population
  4. Coffee plantations only

Answer: Star-shaped lake, shola forests, and pleasant climate

Explanation: Kodaikanal (Princess of Hill Stations) in the Palani Hills, Tamil Nadu, is known for its star-shaped artificial lake, shola forests, grasslands, and pleasant climate. Silver Cascade waterfall is nearby.

25. The Dzukou Valley is in:

  1. Nagaland and Manipur border
  2. Arunachal Pradesh
  3. Sikkim
  4. Meghalaya

Answer: Nagaland and Manipur border

Explanation: Dzukou Valley (2,452 m) on the Nagaland-Manipur border is known as the 'Valley of Flowers of the Northeast' — famous for its seasonal flowers, especially the Dzukou lily. It is a biodiversity-rich valley.
