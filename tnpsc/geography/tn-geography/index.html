Tn Geography MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Tn Geography questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which is the largest state in India by area?

  1. Maharashtra
  2. Uttar Pradesh
  3. Madhya Pradesh
  4. Rajasthan

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: Rajasthan is the largest state in India by area (342,239 sq km), followed by Madhya Pradesh.

2. Which is the smallest state in India by area?

  1. Sikkim
  2. Goa
  3. Tripura
  4. Nagaland

Answer: Goa

Explanation: Goa is the smallest state in India by area (3,702 sq km).

3. The Thar Desert is located in which state?

  1. Gujarat
  2. Punjab
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Haryana

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: The Thar Desert (Great Indian Desert) is mainly in Rajasthan. It extends into parts of Gujarat, Punjab, and Haryana as well.

4. How many states does India have (as of 2024)?

  1. 25
  2. 28
  3. 29
  4. 30

Answer: 28

Explanation: India has 28 states and 8 Union Territories (as of 2024 after the reorganisation of J&K into two UTs in 2019).

5. The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?

  1. 6
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 9

Answer: 8

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.

6. Which Indian state is the leading producer of rubber?

  1. Tamil Nadu
  2. Andhra Pradesh
  3. Kerala
  4. Karnataka

Answer: Kerala

Explanation: Kerala is India's leading producer of natural rubber, accounting for around 77% of total production. India is the third-largest producer globally.

7. Which is the highest waterfall in India?

  1. Jog Falls
  2. Dudhsagar Falls
  3. Nohkalikai Falls
  4. Kunchikal Falls

Answer: Kunchikal Falls

Explanation: Kunchikal Falls in Karnataka (455 m) is the highest waterfall in India. Nohkalikai (340 m) in Meghalaya is often confused for this, but Kunchikal is higher.

8. The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through how many Indian states?

  1. 6
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 9

Answer: 8

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram. It also passes through West Bengal and Tripura.

9. India is the largest producer of which crop in the world?

  1. Wheat
  2. Rice
  3. Milk (and also jute, sugarcane, pulses, spices)
  4. Cotton

Answer: Milk (and also jute, sugarcane, pulses, spices)

Explanation: India is the world's largest producer of milk, jute, and spices; 2nd largest producer of rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton, fruits, and vegetables. Agriculture employs ~45% of India's workforce and contributes ~18% of GDP.

10. India's first National Park was:

  1. Ranthambore
  2. Kanha National Park
  3. Jim Corbett National Park (1936, originally Hailey National Park)
  4. Sariska

Answer: Jim Corbett National Park (1936, originally Hailey National Park)

Explanation: Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand) was established in 1936 as 'Hailey National Park' — India's first national park. Named after Jim Corbett (British hunter-naturalist). It is a Project Tiger reserve.

11. 'Project Tiger' was launched in India in:

  1. 1960
  2. 1973
  3. 1980
  4. 1992

Answer: 1973

Explanation: Project Tiger was launched on 1 April 1973 by PM Indira Gandhi under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972. The first reserve was Jim Corbett (Uttarakhand). India now has 54 tiger reserves (2024). The Bengal Tiger is India's national animal.

12. The National Animal of India is:

  1. Elephant
  2. Lion
  3. Bengal Tiger
  4. Leopard

Answer: Bengal Tiger

Explanation: The Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris tigris) is India's national animal (since 1973, replacing the lion). India has ~70% of the world's tiger population. Project Tiger (1973) helped increase numbers from 1,827 (1973) to 3,167+ (2022 census).

13. Ooty (Udhagamandalam) is in which district of Tamil Nadu?

  1. Nilgiris district
  2. Coimbatore
  3. Salem
  4. Dindigul

Answer: Nilgiris district

Explanation: Ooty (Udhagamandalam) is the district headquarters of The Nilgiris district of Tamil Nadu. Called 'Queen of Hill Stations', it was a summer capital during British India.

14. The Thar Desert is primarily located in:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Gujarat
  3. Haryana
  4. Punjab

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: The Thar Desert (Great Indian Desert) is primarily in western Rajasthan (India), extending into parts of Pakistan. In India, it also covers small areas of Gujarat, Haryana, and Punjab.

15. The first National Park in India is:

  1. Jim Corbett National Park (1936)
  2. Gir National Park (1975)
  3. Kaziranga National Park (1974)
  4. Bandipur National Park (1974)

Answer: Jim Corbett National Park (1936)

Explanation: Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand) was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park — India's and Asia's first national park. Renamed Jim Corbett in 1957 and became the first Project Tiger reserve in 1973.

16. Kaziranga National Park (Assam) is known for:

  1. One-horned Indian rhinoceros
  2. Bengal tiger only
  3. Asian elephant
  4. Snow leopard

Answer: One-horned Indian rhinoceros

Explanation: Kaziranga National Park in Assam has the world's largest population of the one-horned Indian rhinoceros (about 2,400 — 70% of the world's population). It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

17. The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve was India's first biosphere reserve, established in:

  1. 1986
  2. 1992
  3. 1980
  4. 1990

Answer: 1986

Explanation: The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve (covering parts of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka) was India's first biosphere reserve, established in 1986. It covers 5,520 sq km and is a UNESCO Man and Biosphere Reserve.

18. The Green Revolution was primarily associated with which crop?

  1. Wheat
  2. Rice
  3. Cotton
  4. Pulses

Answer: Wheat

Explanation: The Green Revolution (mid-1960s) was primarily about wheat — using HYV seeds in Punjab, Haryana, and western UP. M.S. Swaminathan and Norman Borlaug were key figures. India became self-sufficient in wheat.

19. India is the world's largest producer of:

  1. Milk and pulses
  2. Wheat
  3. Rice (all varieties)
  4. Sugar

Answer: Milk and pulses

Explanation: India is the world's largest producer of milk (Operation Flood, since 1998), pulses (about 24% of world production), and spices. India is also the second-largest producer of rice and wheat.

20. Mount Everest (8,849 m) is located in:

  1. Nepal (on Nepal-China border)
  2. India
  3. China (Tibet)
  4. Pakistan

Answer: Nepal (on Nepal-China border)

Explanation: Mount Everest sits on the Nepal-China (Tibet) border. First climbed by Edmund Hillary (New Zealand) and Tenzing Norgay Sherpa (India/Nepal) on 29 May 1953.

21. The southernmost point of the Indian mainland is:

  1. Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin)
  2. Indira Point
  3. Point Calimere
  4. Rameshwaram

Answer: Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin)

Explanation: Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin) is the southernmost tip of the Indian mainland. Indira Point on Great Nicobar Island (6.5°N) is the southernmost point of India's entire territory.

22. Tamil Nadu shares its longest border with which State?

  1. Andhra Pradesh
  2. Karnataka
  3. Kerala
  4. Puducherry

Answer: Andhra Pradesh

Explanation: Tamil Nadu shares borders with Andhra Pradesh (northeast), Karnataka (north-northwest), and Kerala (west). The border with Andhra Pradesh is the longest.

23. The state with the largest area in India is:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Madhya Pradesh
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Uttar Pradesh

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: Rajasthan (342,239 sq km) is the largest state by area in India, followed by Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra. UP is the most populous state.

24. The most populous state in India is:

  1. Uttar Pradesh
  2. Maharashtra
  3. Bihar
  4. West Bengal

Answer: Uttar Pradesh

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh (population ~200 million, 2011 census) is the most populous state in India, followed by Maharashtra and Bihar.

25. Which state/UT has the highest literacy rate in India (as per census)?

  1. Kerala
  2. Mizoram
  3. Goa
  4. Tripura

Answer: Kerala

Explanation: Kerala consistently has India's highest literacy rate — 96.2% as per 2011 census, and even higher in recent NFHS surveys. Kerala's high literacy is attributed to colonial-era missionary schools and state focus on education.
