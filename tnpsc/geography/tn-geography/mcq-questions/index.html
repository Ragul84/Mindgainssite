Tn Geography MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Tn Geography questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

26. The Kyoto Protocol (1997) was related to:

  1. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions
  2. Nuclear disarmament
  3. Protection of wetlands
  4. Ozone layer protection

Answer: Reducing greenhouse gas emissions

Explanation: The Kyoto Protocol (1997, entered force 2005) was the first binding international agreement on greenhouse gas emission reductions. It set targets for developed countries. USA never ratified it.

27. The most densely populated state in India is:

  1. Bihar
  2. West Bengal
  3. Uttar Pradesh
  4. Kerala

Answer: Bihar

Explanation: Bihar has the highest population density in India (1,106 persons/sq km as per 2011 census), followed by West Bengal (1,028). UP has the highest absolute population.

28. The sex ratio (females per 1000 males) in India as per 2011 census was:

  1. 940
  2. 927
  3. 966
  4. 933

Answer: 940

Explanation: India's sex ratio was 940 females per 1000 males (2011 census), up from 933 (2001). Kerala has the highest sex ratio (1084) and Haryana the lowest (879). Tamil Nadu's sex ratio is 995.

29. The average annual growth rate of India's population (2001-2011) was:

  1. 1.64%
  2. 2.5%
  3. 3%
  4. 1%

Answer: 1.64%

Explanation: India's population grew from 1.028 billion (2001) to 1.21 billion (2011), at an average annual growth rate of 1.64%. This was lower than the 1991-2001 rate of 1.93%, showing slowing growth.

30. The Radcliffe Line is the border between:

  1. India and Pakistan
  2. India and Bangladesh
  3. India and Nepal
  4. India and China

Answer: India and Pakistan

Explanation: The Radcliffe Line (drawn by Sir Cyril Radcliffe in 1947) is the boundary between India and Pakistan, and between India and (East) Bangladesh. It was drawn in 5 weeks and announced on 17 August 1947.

31. Banihal Pass is the route to:

  1. Kashmir Valley from Jammu (J&K)
  2. Ladakh from Kashmir
  3. Himachal Pradesh from Punjab
  4. Uttarakhand from UP

Answer: Kashmir Valley from Jammu (J&K)

Explanation: Banihal Pass (2,832 m) in J&K connects Jammu with the Kashmir Valley. The Banihal Tunnel (Jawahar Tunnel, 2.5 km) provides an all-weather road link. The Banihal-Qazigund rail tunnel is the longest in India.

32. The Zoji La Pass connects:

  1. Kashmir Valley and Ladakh
  2. Jammu and Kashmir
  3. Himachal Pradesh and Ladakh
  4. Kargil and Srinagar

Answer: Kashmir Valley and Ladakh

Explanation: Zoji La Pass (3,528 m) in J&K connects the Kashmir Valley (Srinagar) with Ladakh (Kargil, Leh). It is one of the highest motorable passes in India. The Zoji La Tunnel is under construction to provide all-weather connectivity.

33. India's first Special Economic Zone (SEZ) was established at:

  1. Kandla (Gujarat)
  2. NOIDA
  3. Mumbai
  4. Chennai

Answer: Kandla (Gujarat)

Explanation: India's first SEZ was established at Kandla (Gandhidham) in Gujarat in 1965, making it one of the world's first SEZs. The SEZ policy was formalized in 2000 and the SEZ Act was passed in 2005.

34. Operation Flood (White Revolution) was implemented by:

  1. National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under Dr. Verghese Kurien
  2. Food Corporation of India
  3. Ministry of Agriculture
  4. NABARD

Answer: National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under Dr. Verghese Kurien

Explanation: Operation Flood was implemented by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India'). AMUL (Anand Milk Union Limited) was the model cooperative.

35. Pink Revolution is associated with:

  1. Meat and poultry production (also prawn/onion in some contexts)
  2. Rose farming
  3. Tomato production
  4. Strawberry farming

Answer: Meat and poultry production (also prawn/onion in some contexts)

Explanation: Pink Revolution primarily refers to the increased production of meat (mutton, poultry) in India. Some sources also use it for prawn/shrimp farming or onion production. The colour analogy follows other agricultural revolutions.

36. India's largest tea-producing state is:

  1. Assam
  2. West Bengal
  3. Tamil Nadu
  4. Kerala

Answer: Assam

Explanation: Assam is India's largest tea-producing state (about 53% of India's tea). Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling), and Tamil Nadu (Nilgiris) are the three major tea-growing regions in India.

37. Nilgiris tea from Tamil Nadu is known for:

  1. High-grown tea with a bright, brisk flavor — used in blends
  2. The most expensive tea in India
  3. Only black tea production
  4. Being used for green tea only

Answer: High-grown tea with a bright, brisk flavor — used in blends

Explanation: Nilgiris tea (Tamil Nadu) is grown at 1,000-2,500 m altitude. It is known for its bright, brisk, and fragrant character. Used in blended teas. Ooty is the main center; Kotagiri and Coonoor are also major tea zones.

38. India is the world's largest producer of coffee in Asia. Major coffee-growing state is:

  1. Karnataka (70%+ of India's coffee)
  2. Tamil Nadu
  3. Kerala
  4. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: Karnataka (70%+ of India's coffee)

Explanation: Karnataka produces about 70% of India's coffee (primarily in Coorg/Kodagu, Chikmagalur, Hassan). Kerala (20%) and Tamil Nadu (5-6%) are other major producers. India grows both Arabica and Robusta.

39. Tobacco production in India is mainly in:

  1. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana
  2. Gujarat
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Karnataka

Answer: Andhra Pradesh and Telangana

Explanation: Andhra Pradesh (especially Guntur) and Telangana produce about 50%+ of India's tobacco (Virginia/flue-cured variety). India is the world's third-largest tobacco producer after China and Brazil.

40. Jute is mainly produced in:

  1. West Bengal (India's leading jute state) and Bangladesh
  2. Assam
  3. Bihar
  4. Odisha

Answer: West Bengal (India's leading jute state) and Bangladesh

Explanation: West Bengal is the leading jute producer in India (about 75% of India's jute). Assam and Bihar also produce jute. Bangladesh is the largest global producer. Jute is called 'Golden Fibre'.

41. The United Nations headquarters is in:

  1. New York, USA
  2. Geneva, Switzerland
  3. Vienna, Austria
  4. Paris, France

Answer: New York, USA

Explanation: The United Nations headquarters is in New York City, USA (Midtown Manhattan). The UN also has main offices in Geneva (UNOG), Nairobi (UNON), and Vienna (UNOV).

42. The G20 is a group of:

  1. 19 major economies + the European Union
  2. 20 countries only
  3. Richest 20 countries
  4. G7 + 13 developing countries

Answer: 19 major economies + the European Union

Explanation: G20 consists of 19 major economies (Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Turkey, UK, USA) plus the European Union.

43. The African Union headquarters is in:

  1. Addis Ababa, Ethiopia
  2. Cairo, Egypt
  3. Nairobi, Kenya
  4. Lagos, Nigeria

Answer: Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

Explanation: The African Union (AU) is headquartered in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Established in 2002 (replacing OAU), it has 55 member states — all African countries.

44. NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization) headquarters is in:

  1. Brussels, Belgium
  2. Washington DC, USA
  3. London, UK
  4. Paris, France

Answer: Brussels, Belgium

Explanation: NATO's political headquarters is in Brussels, Belgium (moved from Paris in 1967). Its military headquarters (Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe — SHAPE) is in Mons, Belgium.

45. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) is located in:

  1. The Hague, Netherlands
  2. Geneva, Switzerland
  3. New York, USA
  4. Vienna, Austria

Answer: The Hague, Netherlands

Explanation: The International Court of Justice (ICJ) — the principal judicial organ of the United Nations — is located in The Hague, Netherlands, in the Peace Palace. It settles disputes between countries.

46. 'Isobars' on a weather map connect places having:

  1. Equal atmospheric pressure
  2. Equal temperature
  3. Equal rainfall
  4. Equal altitude

Answer: Equal atmospheric pressure

Explanation: Isobars connect places having the same atmospheric pressure on a weather map. They help identify high-pressure (anticyclones) and low-pressure (cyclones) areas. Closely-spaced isobars indicate strong winds.

47. The Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur was built by:

  1. Rajaraja Chola I (1010 CE)
  2. Rajendra Chola
  3. Ashoka
  4. Krishnadevaraya

Answer: Rajaraja Chola I (1010 CE)

Explanation: Brihadeeswarar Temple (Rajarajeswarar Temple) was built by Rajaraja Chola I and consecrated in 1010 CE. It is dedicated to Lord Shiva, stands 66 m tall, and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

48. The highest waterfall in the world is:

  1. Angel Falls, Venezuela (979 m)
  2. Niagara Falls, USA/Canada
  3. Victoria Falls, Africa
  4. Yosemite Falls, USA

Answer: Angel Falls, Venezuela (979 m)

Explanation: Angel Falls (Salto Ángel) in Venezuela is the world's highest uninterrupted waterfall at 979 m. Named after American aviator Jimmie Angel who spotted it in 1933. Niagara is famous for volume, not height.

49. The IUCN Red List categories include:

  1. Extinct, Extinct in Wild, Critically Endangered, Endangered, Vulnerable, Near Threatened, Least Concern
  2. Only Endangered and Extinct
  3. Five categories only
  4. Only Critically Endangered, Endangered, and Vulnerable

Answer: Extinct, Extinct in Wild, Critically Endangered, Endangered, Vulnerable, Near Threatened, Least Concern

Explanation: The IUCN Red List has 9 categories: Extinct (EX), Extinct in the Wild (EW), Critically Endangered (CR), Endangered (EN), Vulnerable (VU), Near Threatened (NT), Least Concern (LC), Data Deficient (DD), Not Evaluated (NE).

50. Project Elephant was launched in India in:

  1. 1992
  2. 1973
  3. 1980
  4. 2005

Answer: 1992

Explanation: Project Elephant was launched in 1992 by the Government of India to protect elephant habitats, study elephant populations, and address human-elephant conflict. India has about 60% of the world's Asian elephants.
