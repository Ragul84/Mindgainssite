India Physical MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked India Physical questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The Western Ghats in India is also known as:

  1. Vindhya Range
  2. Sahyadri Mountains
  3. Aravalli Range
  4. Eastern Ghats

Answer: Sahyadri Mountains

Explanation: The Western Ghats are also called Sahyadri Mountains. They run parallel to the western coast of India.

2. Which is the highest peak in India?

  1. Nanda Devi
  2. K2
  3. Kanchenjunga
  4. Mount Everest

Answer: Kanchenjunga

Explanation: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m) is the highest peak in India and the third highest peak in the world. K2 is in Pakistan-administered Kashmir.

3. The Deccan Plateau is located in which part of India?

  1. Northern India
  2. Eastern India
  3. Southern/Peninsular India
  4. Western India

Answer: Southern/Peninsular India

Explanation: The Deccan Plateau is a large plateau in peninsular (southern) India. It covers most of southern and central India.

4. Which is the largest lake in India?

  1. Wular Lake
  2. Dal Lake
  3. Chilika Lake
  4. Vembanad Lake

Answer: Chilika Lake

Explanation: Chilika Lake (Odisha) is the largest coastal lagoon and the largest lake in India by area (around 1,100 sq km).

5. The Himalaya mountain range is in which part of India?

  1. South India
  2. West India
  3. North India
  4. East India

Answer: North India

Explanation: The Himalayas are in the northern part of India, running from west to east and forming a natural barrier.

6. The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous ranges that run along which coast?

  1. Western coast
  2. Eastern coast
  3. Northern coast
  4. Southern coast

Answer: Eastern coast

Explanation: The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous ranges running along the eastern coast (Coromandel Coast) of India, from Odisha to Tamil Nadu.

7. Which soil is most suitable for cotton cultivation in India?

  1. Alluvial soil
  2. Red soil
  3. Black (Regur) soil
  4. Laterite soil

Answer: Black (Regur) soil

Explanation: Black soil (Regur soil) is most suitable for cotton cultivation as it has good moisture retention. The Deccan Plateau is famous for this type of soil.

8. Which Indian state has the longest coastline?

  1. Maharashtra
  2. Kerala
  3. Andhra Pradesh
  4. Gujarat

Answer: Gujarat

Explanation: Gujarat has the longest coastline of any Indian state (approximately 1,600 km), followed by Andhra Pradesh.

9. Which type of rainfall is most common in Tamil Nadu during the northeast monsoon?

  1. Orographic rainfall
  2. Convectional rainfall
  3. Cyclonic rainfall
  4. Relief rainfall

Answer: Orographic rainfall

Explanation: Tamil Nadu receives most of its rainfall during the northeast monsoon (October-December) through orographic (relief) rainfall as winds hit the Eastern Ghats.

10. The highest plateau in the world is:

  1. Deccan Plateau
  2. Tibetan Plateau
  3. Colorado Plateau
  4. Patagonian Plateau

Answer: Tibetan Plateau

Explanation: The Tibetan Plateau (average elevation ~4,500 m) is the world's highest and largest plateau, often called the 'Roof of the World'.

11. The soil found in the Gangetic plains is:

  1. Red laterite soil
  2. Black regur soil
  3. Alluvial soil
  4. Desert soil

Answer: Alluvial soil

Explanation: Alluvial soil (brought by rivers) is found in the Gangetic plains. It is the most fertile soil type in India, ideal for wheat, rice, and sugarcane cultivation.

12. The Western Ghats are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. How many hotspot regions are in the Western Ghats?

  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 8
  4. 12

Answer: 8

Explanation: The Western Ghats has 8 hotspot clusters recognised by UNESCO (2012) as outstanding examples of biological diversity.

13. The Nilgiri Hills are located at the junction of which states?

  1. Kerala, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu
  2. Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu
  3. Kerala and Tamil Nadu only
  4. Karnataka and Tamil Nadu only

Answer: Kerala, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu

Explanation: The Nilgiri Hills (Blue Mountains) are located where Kerala, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu meet. Ooty (Udhagamandalam) is the main hill station here.

14. The Shillong Plateau is in which region of India?

  1. Western India
  2. Central India
  3. Northern India
  4. Northeastern India

Answer: Northeastern India

Explanation: The Shillong Plateau (also called the Meghalaya Plateau) is in Northeastern India, in the state of Meghalaya.

15. The 'El Niño' phenomenon is associated with abnormal warming of surface water in:

  1. Western Pacific Ocean
  2. Eastern Pacific Ocean (off South America)
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Atlantic Ocean

Answer: Eastern Pacific Ocean (off South America)

Explanation: El Niño is associated with abnormal warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean off the coast of South America, disrupting global weather patterns including Indian monsoon.

16. The concept of 'Rain Shadow' region is associated with which type of rainfall?

  1. Cyclonic rainfall
  2. Convectional rainfall
  3. Orographic (relief) rainfall
  4. Frontal rainfall

Answer: Orographic (relief) rainfall

Explanation: In orographic rainfall, moisture-laden winds rise over a mountain range, cool, and precipitate on the windward side. The leeward side remains dry — this is the 'rain shadow' region. E.g., Shillong is on the windward side; the Deccan Plateau is in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats.

17. The 'Deccan Traps' are formed by:

  1. Folding of land
  2. Faulting and rifting
  3. Massive volcanic eruptions (flood basalts)
  4. River deposition over millions of years

Answer: Massive volcanic eruptions (flood basalts)

Explanation: The Deccan Traps are formed by massive flood basalt eruptions that occurred at the end of the Cretaceous period (about 66 million years ago), covering much of the Deccan Plateau.

18. The southernmost tip of mainland India is:

  1. Kanyakumari
  2. Indira Point
  3. Rameshwaram
  4. Thiruvananthapuram

Answer: Kanyakumari

Explanation: Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin), Tamil Nadu is the southernmost tip of peninsular (mainland) India. Indira Point in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands is the southernmost point of Indian territory overall.

19. The Himalayas were formed by the collision of:

  1. African and Eurasian plates
  2. Indian (Indo-Australian) plate and Eurasian (Asian) plate — about 50 million years ago
  3. Pacific and Indian plates
  4. Arabian and Eurasian plates

Answer: Indian (Indo-Australian) plate and Eurasian (Asian) plate — about 50 million years ago

Explanation: The Himalayas are fold mountains formed c. 50 million years ago when the northward-moving Indo-Australian plate collided with the Eurasian plate. The Tethys Sea's sediments were folded up to form the Himalayas (still rising ~5mm/year).

20. The highest peak in India is:

  1. Mt. Everest
  2. Kanchenjunga
  3. K2 (Godwin-Austen)
  4. Nanga Parbat

Answer: K2 (Godwin-Austen)

Explanation: K2 (Godwin-Austen, 8611m) in the Karakoram Range is the highest peak in India (in the Pakistan-India disputed territory of Gilgit-Baltistan). Within undisputed India, Kanchenjunga (8586m) on the Sikkim-Nepal border is the highest. Mt. Everest is in Nepal.

21. The Himalayan mountain ranges from south to north are:

  1. Shiwaliks, Greater Himalayas, Lesser Himalayas
  2. Lesser Himalayas, Shiwaliks, Greater Himalayas
  3. Shiwaliks, Lesser Himalayas, Greater Himalayas
  4. Greater Himalayas, Lesser Himalayas, Shiwaliks

Answer: Shiwaliks, Lesser Himalayas, Greater Himalayas

Explanation: South to North: Shiwaliks (Outer/Sub-Himalayas, 600–1500m) → Lesser Himalayas/Himachal (Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, 1500–4500m) → Greater Himalayas/Himadri (Himalaya proper, 5000–8000m) → Trans-Himalayas (Karakoram, Ladakh).

22. The Western Ghats (Sahyadri) separate:

  1. Tamil Nadu and Karnataka
  2. The narrow coastal plains (Malabar/Konkan coast) from the Deccan Plateau
  3. Gujarat from Maharashtra
  4. The Gangetic plain from Deccan

Answer: The narrow coastal plains (Malabar/Konkan coast) from the Deccan Plateau

Explanation: The Western Ghats run ~1600 km parallel to the west coast of India (Gujarat to Kanyakumari). They separate the narrow Konkan/Malabar coastal plains from the broad Deccan Plateau. They receive heavy rainfall due to the southwest monsoon.

23. The highest peak of the Western Ghats is:

  1. Anaimudi (2695m) in Kerala/Tamil Nadu border
  2. Doddabetta (2637m) in Nilgiris
  3. Kodaikanal
  4. Palani Hills

Answer: Anaimudi (2695m) in Kerala/Tamil Nadu border

Explanation: Anaimudi (2695m), located in Eravikulam National Park, Kerala, is the highest peak of the Western Ghats and South India. It is also called the 'Everest of South India'.

24. The Eastern Ghats are interrupted by which rivers?

  1. Ganga and Yamuna
  2. Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri — these rivers cut through the Eastern Ghats
  3. Brahmaputra and Barak
  4. Tapti and Narmada

Answer: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri — these rivers cut through the Eastern Ghats

Explanation: Unlike the continuous Western Ghats, the Eastern Ghats are discontinuous (broken/dissected) because major Deccan rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Pennar, Kaveri) cut through them as they flow east to the Bay of Bengal.

25. The highest peak in Tamil Nadu is:

  1. Anaimudi
  2. Doddabetta (Nilgiris, 2637m)
  3. Palani Hills
  4. Kodaikanal

Answer: Doddabetta (Nilgiris, 2637m)

Explanation: Doddabetta (2637m) in the Nilgiri Hills near Ooty (Udhagamandalam) is the highest peak in Tamil Nadu. It is the second highest peak in South India (after Anaimudi, which is in Kerala).
