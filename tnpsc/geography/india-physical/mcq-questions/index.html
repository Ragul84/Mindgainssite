26. The Nilgiri Hills are a junction of:

  1. Eastern and Western Ghats
  2. Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, and Anamalai Hills
  3. Satpura and Aravalli ranges
  4. Himalayan foothills

Answer: Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, and Anamalai Hills

Explanation: The Nilgiri Hills are a junction point where the Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, and Anaimalai (Anamalai) Hills meet. UNESCO recognised the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve — one of India's richest biodiversity zones.

27. The southwest monsoon enters India first through:

  1. Mumbai
  2. Kolkata
  3. Thiruvananthapuram (Kerala coast) around 1 June
  4. Chennai

Answer: Thiruvananthapuram (Kerala coast) around 1 June

Explanation: The Southwest Monsoon first arrives at the Kerala coast (Thiruvananthapuram area) around 1 June each year. It then advances in two branches: Arabian Sea branch (west coast) and Bay of Bengal branch (northeast). Reaches Delhi by ~29 June, rest of India by 15 July.

28. The driest region of India is:

  1. Rajasthan Thar Desert (but Tamil Nadu's Ramanathapuram gets ~350mm)
  2. Leh, Ladakh — gets <100mm annual rainfall
  3. Rajasthan (Barmer area)
  4. Gujarat (Kutch area)

Answer: Leh, Ladakh — gets <100mm annual rainfall

Explanation: Leh (Ladakh) receives less than 100mm of precipitation annually, making it the driest inhabited region of India. The Thar Desert in Rajasthan gets 100–250mm. The Himalayan rain shadow effect keeps Ladakh extremely dry.

29. The northeast monsoon (October-December) brings rain mainly to:

  1. Mumbai and Karnataka
  2. Kerala and Goa
  3. Tamil Nadu coast and southern Andhra Pradesh
  4. Punjab and Haryana

Answer: Tamil Nadu coast and southern Andhra Pradesh

Explanation: Tamil Nadu receives most of its annual rainfall (about 60%) from the retreating Northeast Monsoon (Oct-Dec), as the Bay of Bengal branch brings moisture to the Coromandel coast. This is why Tamil Nadu has its main rainy season in Oct-Dec.

30. The rain shadow area (leeward side) of the Western Ghats includes:

  1. Konkan Coast
  2. Karnataka coast
  3. Deccan Plateau (parts of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh)
  4. Kerala coast

Answer: Deccan Plateau (parts of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh)

Explanation: The Western Ghats block the southwest monsoon. The windward (western) side gets heavy rainfall (Konkan, Malabar coasts). The leeward (eastern) side is a rain shadow — the Deccan Plateau of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh gets relatively less rainfall (300–700mm).

31. The 'Loo' winds in North India are:

  1. Cool mountain winds
  2. Hot, dry, dusty winds blowing from the west/northwest in May-June in the northern plains (temperatures up to 45-48°C)
  3. Coastal sea breezes
  4. Monsoon winds

Answer: Hot, dry, dusty winds blowing from the west/northwest in May-June in the northern plains (temperatures up to 45-48°C)

Explanation: 'Loo' winds are hot, dry, dust-laden local winds that blow in the afternoon over the North Indian Plains (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan) in May-June. They can cause heatstroke and death. They result from intense heating of the Thar Desert.

32. Black cotton soil (Regur) is mainly found in:

  1. Gangetic plain
  2. Tamil Nadu coastal areas
  3. Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat)
  4. Northeast India

Answer: Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat)

Explanation: Black soil (Regur/Black Cotton Soil) covers about 5.46 lakh km² of the Deccan plateau. It is derived from basaltic Deccan Trap rock. Rich in iron, lime, magnesium. Highly moisture-retentive — ideal for cotton. Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka, Gujarat, AP.

33. The most fertile soil in India is:

  1. Black soil
  2. Red and Yellow soil
  3. Alluvial soil (found in Indo-Gangetic plains)
  4. Laterite soil

Answer: Alluvial soil (found in Indo-Gangetic plains)

Explanation: Alluvial soil (deposited by rivers) is the most fertile in India. It covers the Gangetic plains, Brahmaputra valley, and coastal plains. Rich in potash and lime. Supports wheat, rice, sugarcane, oilseeds. Divided into Khadar (new, lighter) and Bangar (old, darker).

34. Which Indian river flows westward (into the Arabian Sea) rather than eastward?

  1. Godavari
  2. Ganga
  3. Narmada and Tapti
  4. Krishna

Answer: Narmada and Tapti

Explanation: The Narmada and Tapti (Tapi) rivers flow westward through the Narmada and Tapti rift valleys and empty into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat). Most Peninsular rivers flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal.

35. The longest river in India is:

  1. Ganga
  2. Brahmaputra
  3. Godavari
  4. Indus

Answer: Ganga

Explanation: The Ganga (2525 km in India) is the longest river flowing within Indian territory. The Indus (3180km total) is longer overall but most of it flows through Pakistan. The Godavari (1465km) is the longest Peninsular river.

36. The Godavari is also called:

  1. Ganges of the South
  2. Dakshina Ganga (Ganga of the South)
  3. Cauvery of the North
  4. River of Five Tributaries

Answer: Dakshina Ganga (Ganga of the South)

Explanation: The Godavari (1465km) is the longest Peninsular river and is called 'Dakshina Ganga' (Ganga of the South) due to its sacred importance. It originates in Nashik, Maharashtra, and flows into the Bay of Bengal through Andhra Pradesh.

37. The Tamiraparani river is significant because:

  1. It flows through Chennai
  2. It is the only perennial river in Tamil Nadu's southern region (Tirunelveli)
  3. It feeds Chennai's water supply
  4. It forms the boundary between Tamil Nadu and Kerala

Answer: It is the only perennial river in Tamil Nadu's southern region (Tirunelveli)

Explanation: The Tamiraparani (216km) originates in the Agastyamalai Hills (Western Ghats), Tirunelveli. It is the only perennial river in southern Tamil Nadu. It flows through Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi before joining the Bay of Bengal.

38. India has the world's largest reserves of:

  1. Coal
  2. Iron ore
  3. Thorium (in monazite sand, coastal Kerala and Tamil Nadu)
  4. Petroleum

Answer: Thorium (in monazite sand, coastal Kerala and Tamil Nadu)

Explanation: India has the world's largest known reserves of thorium — found in monazite sands on the Kerala and Tamil Nadu coasts (Chavara, Kerala; Manavalakurichi, Tamil Nadu). India also has 4th largest coal reserves globally.

39. Point Calimere (Kodikkarai) Wildlife Sanctuary in Tamil Nadu is famous for:

  1. One-horned rhinoceros
  2. Flamingos, migratory birds, and Blackbuck (Great Indian Antelope)
  3. Nilgiri Tahr
  4. Saltwater crocodile

Answer: Flamingos, migratory birds, and Blackbuck (Great Indian Antelope)

Explanation: Point Calimere (Kodikkarai, Nagapattinam district) is a coastal sanctuary on the Bay of Bengal, famous for flamingos, other migratory birds (from Central Asia, Siberia), and one of the few habitats of Blackbuck in Tamil Nadu.

40. The Godavari is called the 'Dakshin Ganga' because:

  1. It is the largest river in Peninsular India
  2. It flows south
  3. It is sacred like Ganga
  4. It originates from the Himalayas

Answer: It is the largest river in Peninsular India

Explanation: Godavari is called Dakshin Ganga (Ganga of the South) because it is the largest and most sacred river in Peninsular India, originating from Trimbak near Nashik, Maharashtra, flowing 1,465 km to the Bay of Bengal.

41. The highest peak within India's undisputed territory is:

  1. Kangchenjunga (Sikkim)
  2. Nanda Devi (Uttarakhand)
  3. K2 (Karakoram)
  4. Kamet (Uttarakhand)

Answer: Kangchenjunga (Sikkim)

Explanation: Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) on the Sikkim-Nepal border is the highest peak within India's undisputed territory. K2 (8,611 m) is higher but lies in POK (disputed). World's third-highest mountain.

42. The Western Ghats are also known as:

  1. Sahyadri
  2. Eastern Ghats
  3. Vindhya
  4. Aravalli

Answer: Sahyadri

Explanation: The Western Ghats are also known as the Sahyadri. They run parallel to the western coast of India for about 1,600 km, from Gujarat to Kanyakumari. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

43. The highest peak in the Western Ghats is:

  1. Anamudi (2,695 m)
  2. Doddabetta (2,637 m)
  3. Kudremukh (1,892 m)
  4. Brahmagiri (1,608 m)

Answer: Anamudi (2,695 m)

Explanation: Anamudi (2,695 m) in the Anamalai Hills of Kerala is the highest peak in the Western Ghats and also the highest peak in South India outside the Himalayas.

44. The Nilgiri Hills are located at the junction of:

  1. Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Kerala
  2. Tamil Nadu and Karnataka only
  3. Only Tamil Nadu
  4. Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu

Answer: Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Kerala

Explanation: The Nilgiri Hills (Blue Mountains) are at the tri-junction of Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Kerala. Ooty (Udhagamandalam) is the major hill station. Doddabetta (2,637 m) is the highest peak.

45. The Deccan Plateau is bounded in the north by:

  1. Vindhya and Satpura Ranges
  2. Aravalli Range
  3. Eastern Ghats
  4. Himalayas

Answer: Vindhya and Satpura Ranges

Explanation: The Deccan Plateau is bounded by the Vindhya and Satpura Ranges in the north, the Western Ghats in the west, and the Eastern Ghats in the east.

46. The Aravalli Range runs through:

  1. Rajasthan and Gujarat (to Delhi)
  2. Only Rajasthan
  3. Rajasthan and MP
  4. Punjab and Rajasthan

Answer: Rajasthan and Gujarat (to Delhi)

Explanation: The Aravalli Range (about 800 km) runs from Gujarat (Palanpur) through Rajasthan to Delhi-Haryana border. It is one of the oldest fold mountain ranges in the world. Mount Abu (1,722 m) is the highest peak.

47. The Eastern Ghats are not continuous because:

  1. Major rivers cut across them as they flow to Bay of Bengal
  2. They were formed differently
  3. Glaciers eroded them
  4. They are much older than Western Ghats

Answer: Major rivers cut across them as they flow to Bay of Bengal

Explanation: Major rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Pennar) cut across the Eastern Ghats as they flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal, making them discontinuous. Western Ghats are not cut by major rivers.

48. Mawsynram (Meghalaya) is famous for:

  1. Highest rainfall in the world
  2. Highest mountain
  3. Largest lake
  4. Most cyclones

Answer: Highest rainfall in the world

Explanation: Mawsynram in Meghalaya receives about 11,872 mm annually — the highest in the world. Cherrapunji (Sohra), nearby, also holds many rainfall records. Both are on the Shillong Plateau facing the Bay of Bengal.

49. The 'Loo' is a hot dry wind blowing over:

  1. North Indian plains in May-June
  2. Rajasthan only
  3. Tamil Nadu coast
  4. Deccan plateau

Answer: North Indian plains in May-June

Explanation: The Loo is a hot, dry, dust-laden wind that blows over the North Indian plains (Rajasthan, UP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana) during May-June. Temperatures can reach 45-48°C during Loo conditions.

50. Black cotton soil (Regur) is most suitable for growing:

  1. Cotton and wheat
  2. Rice
  3. Jute
  4. Tea

Answer: Cotton and wheat

Explanation: Black cotton soil (Regur) — found in the Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, Karnataka) — is highly fertile for cotton. It retains moisture well, has high clay content, and swells when wet.
