India Rivers MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked India Rivers questions to revise the TNPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which is the longest river in India?

  1. Brahmaputra
  2. Yamuna
  3. Ganga
  4. Godavari

Answer: Ganga

Explanation: The Ganga (Ganges), approximately 2,525 km long, is the longest river in India.

2. Which river is known as the 'Sorrow of Bihar'?

  1. Ganga
  2. Son
  3. Kosi
  4. Mahanadi

Answer: Kosi

Explanation: The Kosi river is known as the 'Sorrow of Bihar' due to devastating floods it causes by frequently changing its course.

3. Which is the longest river entirely within India?

  1. Ganga
  2. Godavari
  3. Mahanadi
  4. Narmada

Answer: Godavari

Explanation: The Godavari (1,465 km) is the longest river that flows entirely within India. The Ganga's source is in India but it also flows through Bangladesh.

4. The river Brahmaputra enters India from:

  1. China (Tibet)
  2. Nepal
  3. Bangladesh
  4. Bhutan

Answer: China (Tibet)

Explanation: The Brahmaputra (called Tsangpo in Tibet) originates in Tibet, China, and enters India through Arunachal Pradesh.

5. Which is the national river of India?

  1. Yamuna
  2. Ganga
  3. Brahmaputra
  4. Godavari

Answer: Ganga

Explanation: Ganga was declared the National River of India in 2008. It has deep religious, cultural, and economic significance.

6. The Kaveri river originates from which state?

  1. Kerala
  2. Tamil Nadu
  3. Karnataka
  4. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: Karnataka

Explanation: The Kaveri river originates from the Brahmagiri hills in Kodagu (Coorg) district of Karnataka and flows through Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

7. The Narmada river flows into which sea/ocean?

  1. Bay of Bengal
  2. Arabian Sea
  3. Indian Ocean directly
  4. Palk Bay

Answer: Arabian Sea

Explanation: The Narmada river flows westward and empties into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat/Cambay).

8. India's largest river island is:

  1. Majuli
  2. Wheeler Island
  3. Sriharikota
  4. Neil Island

Answer: Majuli

Explanation: Majuli in Assam is India's largest river island, located on the Brahmaputra river. It is also the world's largest inhabited river island.

9. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are separated from the southeast Asian countries by:

  1. Strait of Malacca
  2. Ten Degree Channel
  3. Nine Degree Channel
  4. Duncan Passage

Answer: Strait of Malacca

Explanation: The Strait of Malacca separates the Andaman and Nicobar Islands from Malaysia and Indonesia. The Ten Degree Channel separates the Andamans from the Nicobars.

10. The Ganga originates from:

  1. Yamunotri glacier
  2. Gangotri glacier (Bhagirathi river)
  3. Kedarnath
  4. Badrinath

Answer: Gangotri glacier (Bhagirathi river)

Explanation: The Ganga originates as the Bhagirathi river from Gangotri glacier at Gomukh (3892m), Uttarakhand. The Bhagirathi and Alaknanda rivers meet at Devprayag to form the Ganga. The river flows ~2525 km before meeting the Bay of Bengal.

11. The Brahmaputra river is called 'Tsangpo' in:

  1. India (Assam)
  2. Bangladesh
  3. Tibet (China)
  4. Bhutan

Answer: Tibet (China)

Explanation: The Brahmaputra originates as the Tsangpo river in Tibet (China). It enters India through Arunachal Pradesh (called Dihang/Siang), flows through Assam as Brahmaputra, and enters Bangladesh as Jamuna before joining the Ganga-Padma.

12. The Kaveri river originates from:

  1. Kodagu (Coorg) district, Karnataka — Brahmagiri Hills
  2. Nilgiri Hills
  3. Mysore Plateau
  4. Anaimalai Hills

Answer: Kodagu (Coorg) district, Karnataka — Brahmagiri Hills

Explanation: The Kaveri (Cauvery) river originates at Talakaveri in Kodagu (Coorg) district, Karnataka, at 1341m altitude in the Brahmagiri Hills. It flows ~765 km and empties into the Bay of Bengal near Kaveripattinam.

13. Red and yellow soil is predominant in:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Eastern Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand
  3. Western Ghats
  4. Delta regions

Answer: Eastern Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand

Explanation: Red and Yellow soils (formed from ancient crystalline/metamorphic rocks under oxidation) are found in eastern Tamil Nadu (non-Cauvery delta areas), Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, western Andhra Pradesh. Red colour from iron oxide.

14. The river Vaigai flows through:

  1. Salem and Namakkal
  2. Madurai and Ramanathapuram — emptying into Palk Bay
  3. Coimbatore and Erode
  4. Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi

Answer: Madurai and Ramanathapuram — emptying into Palk Bay

Explanation: The Vaigai river originates in the Varushanad Hills (Dindigul), flows through Madurai, Sivaganga, and Ramanathapuram before emptying into the Palk Bay. It is a significant river in southern Tamil Nadu with the Vaigai Dam at Vaigai.

15. The Mettur Dam is built across which river?

  1. Vaigai
  2. Kaveri (Stanley Reservoir)
  3. Bhavani
  4. Tamiraparani

Answer: Kaveri (Stanley Reservoir)

Explanation: Mettur Dam (Stanley Reservoir) is built across the river Kaveri in Salem district, Tamil Nadu. Constructed in 1934, it is one of the largest dams in India. Water is released for Kuruvai cultivation in the delta.

16. The 'Pichavaram Mangrove Forest' in Tamil Nadu is located near:

  1. Chennai
  2. Rameswaram
  3. Cuddalore (Chidambaram)
  4. Kanyakumari

Answer: Cuddalore (Chidambaram)

Explanation: Pichavaram Mangrove Forest (1100 hectares) is located near Chidambaram (Cuddalore district), Tamil Nadu. It is one of the world's largest mangrove forests and lies between two estuaries (Vellar and Coleroon/Kollidam rivers).

17. The 'Thanjavur delta' (Cauvery delta) is called:

  1. Granary of South India
  2. Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu
  3. Garden of Tamil Nadu
  4. Breadbasket of India

Answer: Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu

Explanation: The Thanjavur (Tanjore) district and the Kaveri delta region is called the 'Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu' due to extensive paddy cultivation. It has a well-developed canal irrigation network from the Kaveri river.

18. The Chambal river is a tributary of:

  1. Yamuna
  2. Ganga
  3. Ghaghra
  4. Son

Answer: Yamuna

Explanation: The Chambal river (originating from Janapav hills, Vindhya Range, MP) is a major right-bank tributary of the Yamuna. It flows through MP and Rajasthan before joining the Yamuna in UP.

19. The river Brahmaputra is known as 'Tsangpo' in:

  1. Tibet (China)
  2. Bhutan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. Arunachal Pradesh

Answer: Tibet (China)

Explanation: The Brahmaputra originates from Chemayungdung glacier in Tibet (China) as the Tsangpo. It enters India in Arunachal Pradesh (called Siang/Dihang), flows through Assam as Brahmaputra, and enters Bangladesh as Jamuna.

20. The Indus river originates from:

  1. Mansarovar Lake area, Tibet
  2. Gangotri glacier
  3. Amarkantak
  4. Trimbak, Nashik

Answer: Mansarovar Lake area, Tibet

Explanation: The Indus (Sindhu) originates from Sengge Zangbo springs near Mansarovar Lake in Tibet (China). It flows through Ladakh (India), Pakistan, and drains into the Arabian Sea.

21. Which river is called 'Sorrow of Bihar'?

  1. Kosi
  2. Son
  3. Gandak
  4. Bagmati

Answer: Kosi

Explanation: The Kosi river is called the 'Sorrow of Bihar' because of the devastating floods it causes annually. It frequently changes course and causes enormous loss of life and property.

22. The river Narmada flows into:

  1. Arabian Sea
  2. Bay of Bengal
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Gulf of Mannar

Answer: Arabian Sea

Explanation: The Narmada (originating from Amarkantak, MP) flows westward through the Vindhya-Satpura rift valley and drains into the Arabian Sea near Bharuch in Gujarat.

23. Duncan Passage separates:

  1. South Andaman and Little Andaman
  2. Andaman and Nicobar
  3. Great Nicobar and Little Nicobar
  4. North and South Andaman

Answer: South Andaman and Little Andaman

Explanation: Duncan Passage separates South Andaman from Little Andaman. The Ten Degree Channel separates Andaman Islands from Nicobar Islands. The Nine Degree Channel separates Lakshadweep Islands.

24. The Cauvery delta region in Tamil Nadu is called:

  1. Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu
  2. Cotton Belt
  3. Sugarcane Belt
  4. Granary of South India

Answer: Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu

Explanation: The Cauvery delta region (Thanjavur, Tiruvarur, Nagapattinam districts) is called the 'Rice Bowl of Tamil Nadu' and historically the 'Granary of South India' due to high paddy productivity.

25. The world's longest river is:

  1. Nile
  2. Amazon
  3. Yangtze
  4. Mississippi

Answer: Nile

Explanation: The Nile (6,650 km) is the world's longest river, flowing through northeastern Africa into the Mediterranean Sea. The Amazon is the largest by volume/discharge.
