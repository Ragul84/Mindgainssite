Constitution Making MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Constitution Making questions to revise the TNPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Who is known as the Father of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  4. Sardar Patel

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution and is called its Father.

2. Which fundamental right is called the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution?

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Constitutional Remedies
  4. Right to Education

Answer: Right to Constitutional Remedies

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution.

3. The Indian Constitution was adopted on:

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 November 1949
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 1 January 1949

Answer: 26 November 1949

Explanation: The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day/Samvidhan Divas). It came into force on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months, 18 days.

4. The Constituent Assembly was chaired by:

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Dr. Rajendra Prasad
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Explanation: Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President (permanent chairman) of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the chairman of the Drafting Committee and is called the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

5. Article 32 is called the 'Heart and Soul of the Constitution' by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar because:

  1. It establishes the Supreme Court
  2. It gives the right to move Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights — right to constitutional remedies
  3. It defines fundamental duties
  4. It establishes Parliament

Answer: It gives the right to move Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights — right to constitutional remedies

Explanation: Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) allows any citizen to move the Supreme Court directly if their Fundamental Rights are violated. The SC can issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari). Ambedkar called it the 'very soul of the Constitution'.

6. The Constitution of India was drafted by the Constituent Assembly in how many days?

  1. 2 years 11 months 18 days
  2. 3 years 5 months
  3. 2 years 6 months
  4. 1 year 11 months

Answer: 2 years 11 months 18 days

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days (December 9, 1946 to November 26, 1949) to draft the Constitution, holding 11 sessions with 165 sitting days.

7. Article 32 is called the 'soul of the Constitution' by:

  1. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Sardar Patel
  4. Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'heart and soul of the Constitution'. It allows citizens to directly approach the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights.

8. Inter-State River Water Disputes are adjudicated by:

  1. Tribunals under Article 262 and Inter-State Water Disputes Act
  2. Supreme Court directly
  3. Central Water Commission
  4. State Governments jointly

Answer: Tribunals under Article 262 and Inter-State Water Disputes Act

Explanation: Article 262 empowers Parliament to provide for adjudication of disputes relating to use, distribution, or control of inter-state rivers. Parliament enacted the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956.

9. The Constituent Assembly of India was set up under which plan?

  1. Cabinet Mission Plan 1946
  2. Cripps Mission 1942
  3. Mountbatten Plan 1947
  4. Simon Commission 1927

Answer: Cabinet Mission Plan 1946

Explanation: The Cabinet Mission Plan (May 1946) provided for the formation of the Constituent Assembly. Elections to the Constituent Assembly were held in July-August 1946.

10. How many sessions did the Constituent Assembly hold before adopting the Constitution?

  1. 11 sessions
  2. 5 sessions
  3. 7 sessions
  4. 15 sessions

Answer: 11 sessions

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly held 11 sessions over 2 years, 11 months and 18 days (9 December 1946 – 26 November 1949) before adopting the Constitution.

11. The Objectives Resolution was moved in the Constituent Assembly by:

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Rajendra Prasad
  4. Sardar Patel

Answer: Jawaharlal Nehru

Explanation: Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946, which outlined the aims and objectives of the Constitution. It was adopted on 22 January 1947.

12. The total number of members who signed the Constitution on 24 January 1950 was:

  1. 284
  2. 389
  3. 299
  4. 308

Answer: 284

Explanation: 284 members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution on 24 January 1950. The total strength of the Constituent Assembly was 389 (including 4 seats for Chief Commissioners' provinces).

13. Which Fundamental Right was described as the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' by Ambedkar?

  1. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32)
  2. Right to Equality (Art 14)
  3. Right to Freedom (Art 19)
  4. Right to Life (Art 21)

Answer: Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32)

Explanation: Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' as it gives the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

14. The Maneka Gandhi case (1978) expanded the meaning of Article 21 by holding:

  1. 'Procedure established by law' must be fair, just, and reasonable
  2. Article 21 applies only to citizens
  3. Personal liberty does not include freedom of travel
  4. Article 21 cannot be read with Article 19

Answer: 'Procedure established by law' must be fair, just, and reasonable

Explanation: Before Maneka Gandhi, 'procedure established by law' meant any procedure enacted by legislature (Gopalan case, 1950). After Maneka Gandhi, the procedure must be fair, just, and reasonable — linking Art 21 with Art 14 and 19.

15. The directive to provide free legal aid to ensure equal justice is under:

  1. Article 39A
  2. Article 39
  3. Article 41
  4. Article 43A

Answer: Article 39A

Explanation: Article 39A (inserted by 42nd Amendment, 1976) directs the State to ensure equal justice and free legal aid to citizens, especially the poor. Under this, the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 was enacted.

16. Citizenship in India is dealt with in:

  1. Part II (Articles 5-11)
  2. Part III (Articles 12-35)
  3. Part IV (Articles 36-51)
  4. Part I (Articles 1-4)

Answer: Part II (Articles 5-11)

Explanation: Part II of the Constitution (Articles 5-11) deals with citizenship at the time of commencement of the Constitution. Parliament enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955 to provide for acquisition and loss of citizenship.

17. The Prevention of Atrocities Act (SC/ST Act) was enacted in:

  1. 1989
  2. 1955
  3. 1978
  4. 2000

Answer: 1989

Explanation: The Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 was enacted to prevent crimes against SCs and STs and provide special courts for speedy trial. It was amended in 2015 to strengthen it.

18. The three-tier Panchayati Raj structure recommended by the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee consists of:

  1. Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad
  2. Gram Panchayat, Block Panchayat, District Board
  3. Village Council, Taluk Board, District Council
  4. Gram Sabha, Gram Panchayat, District Panchayat

Answer: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad

Explanation: The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) recommended a 3-tier structure: Gram Panchayat (village level), Panchayat Samiti (block level), and Zila Parishad (district level). This was formally adopted by the 73rd Amendment as the model.

19. The Comptroller and Auditor General's role was described as 'the most important officer under the Constitution' by:

  1. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Sardar Patel
  4. Rajendra Prasad

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described the CAG as the most important officer under the Constitution of India because the CAG ensures financial accountability and that public money is spent as Parliament intended.

20. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act was enacted in:

  1. 2003
  2. 2000
  3. 2005
  4. 2010

Answer: 2003

Explanation: The FRBM Act, 2003 mandates fiscal discipline by setting targets for reducing fiscal deficit, revenue deficit, and controlling government borrowings. It promotes macro-economic stability and sound public finance management.

21. Which article of the Indian Constitution provides for the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights?

  1. Article 30
  2. Article 32
  3. Article 226
  4. Article 300

Answer: Article 32

Explanation: Article 32 gives the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Dr. Ambedkar called it the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution.

22. How many members does India's Constituent Assembly have when the Constitution was adopted?

  1. 189
  2. 207
  3. 284
  4. 299

Answer: 284

Explanation: 284 members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution on 24 January 1950. The full Constituent Assembly had 299 members.

23. The SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act was enacted in:

  1. 1989
  2. 1978
  3. 1993
  4. 1995

Answer: 1989

Explanation: Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 provides stringent punishment for crimes against SCs and STs. It was amended in 2018 to overcome dilution by Supreme Court.

24. Which Article provides for the resolution of inter-state river water disputes?

  1. Article 262
  2. Article 263
  3. Article 252
  4. Article 256

Answer: Article 262

Explanation: Article 262 provides Parliament can make law for adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-state rivers. Under this, Parliament enacted the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956.

25. Article 267 relates to:

  1. Contingency Fund of India
  2. Consolidated Fund
  3. Public Account
  4. Finance Commission

Answer: Contingency Fund of India

Explanation: Article 267 establishes the Contingency Fund of India — placed at the disposal of the President for unforeseen expenditures. Parliament has enacted the Contingency Fund of India Act, 1950 for this purpose.
