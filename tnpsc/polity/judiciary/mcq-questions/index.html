Judiciary MCQs with answers

26. The retirement age of a Supreme Court judge is:

  1. 65 years
  2. 60 years
  3. 62 years
  4. 70 years

Answer: 65 years

Explanation: Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years (Article 124). High Court judges retire at 62 years. This was the only age limit originally — fixed at 65 by the Constitution.

27. 'Public Interest Litigation' (PIL) was developed by:

  1. Supreme Court of India
  2. Parliament
  3. President
  4. Law Commission

Answer: Supreme Court of India

Explanation: PIL was developed by the Supreme Court of India in the 1980s, especially through Justices V.R. Krishna Iyer and P.N. Bhagwati, allowing any citizen to approach courts for public interest.

28. Who is the custodian of the Constitution?

  1. Supreme Court of India
  2. President of India
  3. Parliament of India
  4. Attorney General of India

Answer: Supreme Court of India

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India is the custodian and final interpreter of the Constitution. It has the power of judicial review to strike down any law or executive action that violates the Constitution.

29. The tenure of the Chief Election Commissioner is:

  1. 6 years or till age 65, whichever is earlier
  2. 5 years or till age 65
  3. 6 years or till age 70
  4. Fixed 5 years

Answer: 6 years or till age 65, whichever is earlier

Explanation: The Chief Election Commissioner holds office for 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier. The same applies to Election Commissioners. They can be removed only like a Supreme Court judge.

30. The Indra Sawhney case (1992) ruled that total reservation cannot exceed:

  1. 50%
  2. 33%
  3. 49%
  4. 60%

Answer: 50%

Explanation: In Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (Mandal case, 1992), the Supreme Court upheld OBC reservation but set the ceiling at 50% for all reservations combined (with exceptions for extraordinary situations).

31. The three organs of the Indian Government are:

  1. President, PM, Parliament
  2. Legislature, Executive, Judiciary
  3. Central, State, Local
  4. Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Supreme Court

Answer: Legislature, Executive, Judiciary

Explanation: The three organs of government are: Legislature (makes laws), Executive (implements laws), and Judiciary (interprets laws).

32. NOTA (None of the Above) option on EVMs was introduced in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2010
  3. 2004
  4. 2014

Answer: 2013

Explanation: NOTA was introduced following a Supreme Court order (PUCL case, 2013). Voters can press the NOTA button to reject all candidates while exercising their right to vote.

33. Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the establishment of the Supreme Court?

  1. Article 124
  2. Article 32
  3. Article 136
  4. Article 143

Answer: Article 124

Explanation: Article 124 establishes the Supreme Court of India.

34. Who appoints the judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts?

  1. President of India
  2. Prime Minister
  3. Chief Justice of India
  4. Law Minister

Answer: President of India

Explanation: The President of India appoints judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts under Articles 124 and 217.

35. What is the minimum age required for a person to be appointed as a judge of the Supreme Court?

  1. 35 years
  2. 40 years
  3. 45 years
  4. No minimum age prescribed

Answer: No minimum age prescribed

Explanation: The Constitution does not prescribe any minimum age for appointment as a Supreme Court judge.

36. The power of the Supreme Court to declare a law unconstitutional is called:

  1. Judicial review
  2. Judicial activism
  3. Writ jurisdiction
  4. Appellate jurisdiction

Answer: Judicial review

Explanation: Judicial review is the power to declare laws or executive actions unconstitutional.

37. Under which Article can the President of India seek the opinion of the Supreme Court on a question of law or fact?

  1. Article 142
  2. Article 143
  3. Article 144
  4. Article 145

Answer: Article 143

Explanation: Article 143 empowers the President to consult the Supreme Court for advisory opinion.

38. Which of the following writs is issued to prevent a subordinate court from exceeding its jurisdiction?

  1. Habeas Corpus
  2. Mandamus
  3. Prohibition
  4. Certiorari

Answer: Prohibition

Explanation: Writ of Prohibition is issued by a higher court to prevent a lower court from acting beyond its jurisdiction.

39. A judge of the Supreme Court can be removed from office on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity by a motion passed by:

  1. Majority in Lok Sabha only
  2. Majority in Rajya Sabha only
  3. Special majority in both Houses
  4. Simple majority in both Houses

Answer: Special majority in both Houses

Explanation: Removal requires a special majority in both Houses of Parliament under Article 124(4).

40. The concept of 'basic structure' of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which landmark case?

  1. Kesavananda Bharati case
  2. Golak Nath case
  3. Minerva Mills case
  4. Maneka Gandhi case

Answer: Kesavananda Bharati case

Explanation: The basic structure doctrine was established in the Kesavananda Bharati (1973) case.

41. Which High Court in India was the first to be established?

  1. Bombay High Court
  2. Madras High Court
  3. Calcutta High Court
  4. Allahabad High Court

Answer: Calcutta High Court

Explanation: The Calcutta High Court was established in 1862 as the first High Court in India.

42. The term 'judicial review' in the Indian Constitution is considered part of:

  1. Fundamental Rights
  2. Directive Principles
  3. Preamble
  4. Basic Structure of the Constitution

Answer: Basic Structure of the Constitution

Explanation: Judicial review is a part of the basic structure of the Constitution and cannot be abrogated.

43. What is the retirement age of a judge of the Supreme Court of India?

  1. 65 years
  2. 62 years
  3. 60 years
  4. 70 years

Answer: 65 years

Explanation: Article 124(2) of the Constitution sets the retirement age of Supreme Court judges at 65 years.

44. Which Article of the Indian Constitution grants the Supreme Court the power of judicial review?

  1. Article 32
  2. Article 13
  3. Article 226
  4. Article 136

Answer: Article 13

Explanation: Article 13 declares any law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights as void, forming the basis of judicial review.

45. What is the current sanctioned strength of judges in the Supreme Court of India (including the Chief Justice)?

  1. 31
  2. 34
  3. 25
  4. 28

Answer: 34

Explanation: As per the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019, the sanctioned strength is 34 judges.

46. Who appoints the judges of the Supreme Court of India?

  1. President of India
  2. Chief Justice of India
  3. Prime Minister
  4. Law Minister

Answer: President of India

Explanation: The President appoints Supreme Court judges in consultation with the Chief Justice and other judges as per Article 124.

47. What is the procedure for the removal of a Supreme Court judge?

  1. By a majority in Lok Sabha only
  2. By an order of the President
  3. By impeachment in both Houses with special majority
  4. By a resolution of the Cabinet

Answer: By impeachment in both Houses with special majority

Explanation: A Supreme Court judge can be removed only by impeachment passed by both Houses with a two-thirds majority.

48. Which of the following cases falls under the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court?

  1. Disputes between two states
  2. Appeal from High Court
  3. Criminal cases
  4. Revenue matters

Answer: Disputes between two states

Explanation: Article 131 gives the Supreme Court original jurisdiction in disputes between the Government of India and states or between states.

49. Under which Article can a citizen directly approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights?

  1. Article 226
  2. Article 32
  3. Article 136
  4. Article 14

Answer: Article 32

Explanation: Article 32 guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

50. Which court is the highest court of appeal in India?

  1. High Court
  2. Supreme Court
  3. District Court
  4. Session Court

Answer: Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court is the apex court and the final court of appeal under Article 136.
