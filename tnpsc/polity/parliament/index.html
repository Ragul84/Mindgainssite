TNPSC Polity practice

Parliament MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Parliament questions to revise the TNPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The Indian Parliament consists of how many Houses?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 4

Answer: 2

Explanation: Indian Parliament has two Houses: Lok Sabha (Lower House) and Rajya Sabha (Upper House), plus the President.

2. What is the maximum strength of Lok Sabha?

  1. 543
  2. 545
  3. 550
  4. 552

Answer: 550

Explanation: The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is 550: up to 530 members from the States and 20 from the UTs (Article 81). It was 552 until the 104th Amendment (2019) ended the nomination of two Anglo-Indian members from January 2020. The present House has 543 elected seats.

3. Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?

  1. President of India
  2. Prime Minister
  3. Vice President of India
  4. Speaker of Lok Sabha

Answer: Vice President of India

Explanation: The Vice President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

4. The Rajya Sabha is also called the:

  1. House of the People
  2. Council of States
  3. Legislative Assembly
  4. Council of Ministers

Answer: Council of States

Explanation: Rajya Sabha is officially called the 'Council of States'. Lok Sabha is called the 'House of the People'.

5. What is the term of a Rajya Sabha member?

  1. 5 years
  2. 6 years
  3. 4 years
  4. Lifetime

Answer: 6 years

Explanation: Rajya Sabha members serve 6-year terms. One-third retire every 2 years. Rajya Sabha is a permanent body.

6. The President of India is elected by:

  1. All citizens of India
  2. Members of Parliament only
  3. Elected members of Parliament and State Legislatures
  4. Governors of all States

Answer: Elected members of Parliament and State Legislatures

Explanation: The President is elected by an Electoral College consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of all State Legislative Assemblies.

7. Which article empowers the Parliament to create new States or alter the boundaries of existing States?

  1. Article 1
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 3
  4. Article 4

Answer: Article 3

Explanation: Article 3 empowers Parliament to form new States by separation, union, or merger of existing States, or by altering boundaries, names, or areas.

8. What is the quorum required to conduct business in Lok Sabha?

  1. 1/5th of total members
  2. 1/10th of total members
  3. 1/3rd of total members
  4. Half the members

Answer: 1/10th of total members

Explanation: The quorum to constitute a meeting of either House is 1/10th of total membership.

9. How many members can the President nominate to the Rajya Sabha?

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 10
  4. 12

Answer: 12

Explanation: The President nominates 12 members to Rajya Sabha who have distinguished themselves in literature, science, art, or social service.

10. Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the formation of new States?

  1. Article 1
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 3
  4. Article 5

Answer: Article 3

Explanation: Article 3 empowers Parliament to admit new States, form new States from existing ones, alter boundaries or change names.

11. What is the minimum age for a person to become a member of Rajya Sabha?

  1. 21 years
  2. 25 years
  3. 30 years
  4. 35 years

Answer: 30 years

Explanation: A person must be at least 30 years old to be elected to the Rajya Sabha.

12. How is the Speaker of Lok Sabha elected?

  1. Appointed by the President
  2. Elected by all Lok Sabha members
  3. Appointed by the Prime Minister
  4. Elected by senior members only

Answer: Elected by all Lok Sabha members

Explanation: The Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected from among the members of the Lok Sabha by its members.

13. The Indian Parliament consists of:

  1. Only Lok Sabha
  2. Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha
  3. President, Lok Sabha, and Rajya Sabha
  4. Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, and Supreme Court

Answer: President, Lok Sabha, and Rajya Sabha

Explanation: The Indian Parliament consists of the President + Lok Sabha (House of the People) + Rajya Sabha (Council of States). Article 79. The President is an integral part of Parliament (though not a member of either House).

14. The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is:

  1. 500
  2. 542
  3. 550 (530 from States + 20 from UTs; the 2 Anglo-Indian seats ended in 2020)
  4. 545

Answer: 550 (530 from States + 20 from UTs; the 2 Anglo-Indian seats ended in 2020)

Explanation: The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is 550: up to 530 members from the States and 20 from the UTs (Article 81). It was 552 until the 104th Amendment (2019) ended the nomination of two Anglo-Indian members from January 2020. The present House has 543 elected seats.

15. The term of the Lok Sabha is:

  1. 4 years
  2. 5 years (can be extended during National Emergency)
  3. 6 years
  4. 3 years

Answer: 5 years (can be extended during National Emergency)

Explanation: The normal term of the Lok Sabha is 5 years from the date of its first sitting after a general election (Article 83). During a National Emergency (Article 352), Parliament can extend its term by 1 year at a time by law.

16. The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body because:

  1. Its members serve for life
  2. It is never dissolved — only 1/3 of its members retire every 2 years
  3. It has more powers than Lok Sabha
  4. Its members are elected for 10 years

Answer: It is never dissolved — only 1/3 of its members retire every 2 years

Explanation: Rajya Sabha is a permanent body (Article 83) — it is never dissolved. Each member serves a 6-year term, and 1/3 of its members retire every 2 years. Currently has 245 members (238 elected + 12 nominated by President for expertise in arts, science, literature, social service).

17. A Money Bill can only be introduced in:

  1. Rajya Sabha
  2. Lok Sabha (Article 110)
  3. Either House
  4. Joint sitting of both Houses

Answer: Lok Sabha (Article 110)

Explanation: A Money Bill (Article 110) can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha. After Lok Sabha passes it, it is sent to Rajya Sabha which can only make recommendations (not amend/reject). If no action in 14 days, it is deemed passed. Lok Sabha has final say on Money Bills.

18. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is elected by:

  1. President of India
  2. Members of Lok Sabha from among themselves
  3. Members of both Houses
  4. Prime Minister

Answer: Members of Lok Sabha from among themselves

Explanation: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is elected by the members of the Lok Sabha from among themselves (Article 93). The Speaker maintains order, presides over proceedings, and is the guardian of the powers and privileges of the House.

19. A 'No Confidence Motion' can be moved against:

  1. The President
  2. The Council of Ministers (the Cabinet) in the Lok Sabha
  3. Individual ministers only
  4. The Speaker

Answer: The Council of Ministers (the Cabinet) in the Lok Sabha

Explanation: A No Confidence Motion (or Motion of No Confidence) is moved in the Lok Sabha against the Council of Ministers. If passed, the Prime Minister and Cabinet must resign. Requires support of at least 50 Lok Sabha members to be admitted.

20. The Vice-President of India is elected by:

  1. Members of Lok Sabha only
  2. Members of Electoral College (same as President)
  3. Members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) — not state legislatures
  4. Members of Rajya Sabha only

Answer: Members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) — not state legislatures

Explanation: The Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament (Article 66) — Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha. State legislators are NOT part of this electoral process (unlike President's election).

21. The Prime Minister is appointed by:

  1. Elected by Lok Sabha members
  2. President of India — who appoints the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha
  3. Rajya Sabha
  4. Electoral College

Answer: President of India — who appoints the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha

Explanation: The President appoints the Prime Minister (Article 75). Conventionally, the President appoints the leader of the party/coalition that commands majority in the Lok Sabha. The PM is the head of the Council of Ministers and real executive head.

22. President's Rule (State Emergency) is imposed under:

  1. Article 352
  2. Article 356 (if state government cannot function according to Constitution — on Governor's report or otherwise)
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 361

Answer: Article 356 (if state government cannot function according to Constitution — on Governor's report or otherwise)

Explanation: Article 356 (President's Rule/State Emergency): The President can take over state government if state constitutional machinery fails. Proclaimed on Governor's report (or other information). Parliament must approve every 6 months. Maximum: 3 years.

23. A judge of the Supreme Court can be removed by:

  1. President on the recommendation of PM
  2. An address of Parliament (both Houses) by special majority presented to the President (impeachment)
  3. Chief Justice of India
  4. Directly by the President

Answer: An address of Parliament (both Houses) by special majority presented to the President (impeachment)

Explanation: A Supreme Court judge can only be removed by the President on an address of Parliament — both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha must pass the resolution by a special majority (majority of total membership + 2/3 of members present and voting). This is called impeachment.

24. The Seventh Schedule of the Constitution contains:

  1. Forms of oaths
  2. Languages in the 8th Schedule
  3. Three lists — Union List (97 subjects), State List (66 subjects), Concurrent List (47 subjects)
  4. Names of the States

Answer: Three lists — Union List (97 subjects), State List (66 subjects), Concurrent List (47 subjects)

Explanation: The Seventh Schedule (Article 246) contains three legislative lists: Union List (97 subjects on which only Parliament can legislate), State List (66 subjects for states), and Concurrent List (47 subjects where both can legislate — in conflict, Union law prevails).

25. The Parliamentary form of government in India is borrowed from:

  1. British Constitution
  2. US Constitution
  3. Australian Constitution
  4. French Constitution

Answer: British Constitution

Explanation: Parliamentary system (Cabinet government, Prime Minister's role, collective responsibility) is borrowed from the British (Westminster) model. Also known as Westminster Model.
