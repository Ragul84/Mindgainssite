Parliament MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Parliament questions to revise the TNPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

26. The maximum gap between two sessions of Parliament is:

  1. 6 months
  2. 3 months
  3. 1 year
  4. 2 months

Answer: 6 months

Explanation: The Constitution requires that Parliament must meet at least twice a year and the gap between two sessions must not exceed 6 months (Article 85).

27. Money Bills can be introduced only in:

  1. Lok Sabha
  2. Rajya Sabha
  3. Both Houses
  4. Either House

Answer: Lok Sabha

Explanation: Money Bills (Article 110) can be introduced only in Lok Sabha (Article 109). Rajya Sabha can only delay it for 14 days but cannot amend or reject it.

28. The Rajya Sabha is a:

  1. Permanent House (never dissolved)
  2. Temporary House
  3. House elected every 5 years
  4. House with fixed 250 members

Answer: Permanent House (never dissolved)

Explanation: Rajya Sabha is a permanent body — it is never dissolved as a whole. One-third of its members retire every two years (6-year term for each member).

29. Maximum membership of Lok Sabha:

  1. 552
  2. 543
  3. 545
  4. 550

Answer: 550

Explanation: The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is 550: up to 530 members from the States and 20 from the UTs (Article 81). It was 552 until the 104th Amendment (2019) ended the nomination of two Anglo-Indian members from January 2020. The present House has 543 elected seats.

30. The Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected by:

  1. Members of Lok Sabha
  2. President of India
  3. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  4. Council of Ministers

Answer: Members of Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected by the members of Lok Sabha from among themselves (Article 93). Similarly, the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha is elected by its members.

31. Zero Hour in Parliament refers to:

  1. Questions asked without prior notice
  2. 12:00 noon parliamentary business
  3. Period after oath taking
  4. Vote on account discussion

Answer: Questions asked without prior notice

Explanation: Zero Hour (informal institution) begins at noon (12:00) after Question Hour, allowing members to raise urgent public matters without prior notice to the Speaker.

32. A joint sitting of Parliament is presided over by:

  1. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  2. President of India
  3. Vice President
  4. Prime Minister

Answer: Speaker of Lok Sabha

Explanation: Joint sitting of both Houses (Article 108) in case of deadlock is presided over by the Speaker of Lok Sabha (or Deputy Speaker in Speaker's absence).

33. The Budget is presented by the:

  1. Finance Minister in Lok Sabha
  2. Prime Minister
  3. President
  4. Planning Commission

Answer: Finance Minister in Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Union Budget (Annual Financial Statement) is presented by the Finance Minister in the Lok Sabha (traditionally on the last working day of February, now changed to February 1).

34. Quorum in the Lok Sabha is:

  1. 1/10th of total membership
  2. 1/2 of total membership
  3. Simple majority
  4. 2/3rd majority

Answer: 1/10th of total membership

Explanation: Quorum for Lok Sabha (Article 100): 1/10th of total membership = 55 members (out of 543). Similarly for Rajya Sabha: 1/10th = 25 members (out of 250).

35. Which type of majority is required to pass an Ordinary Bill?

  1. Simple majority (majority of members present and voting)
  2. Absolute majority
  3. Special majority (2/3rd)
  4. Unanimous vote

Answer: Simple majority (majority of members present and voting)

Explanation: Ordinary Bills require simple majority — majority of members present and voting (not absolute majority of total membership). Constitutional Amendments require special majority.

36. Minimum age to become President of India:

  1. 35 years
  2. 25 years
  3. 30 years
  4. 40 years

Answer: 35 years

Explanation: To be eligible for President: Must be a citizen of India, minimum 35 years of age, qualified to be a member of Lok Sabha (Article 58).

37. Which article gives Parliament the power to create new states?

  1. Article 3
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 4
  4. Article 5

Answer: Article 3

Explanation: Article 3 empowers Parliament to form new states, increase/decrease areas, alter boundaries or change names of existing states. Such bills require Presidential recommendation.

38. The minimum number of members required to sign a notice of No-Confidence Motion is:

  1. 50 members
  2. 100 members
  3. 25 members
  4. Simple majority

Answer: 50 members

Explanation: A No-Confidence Motion can be introduced in Lok Sabha if at least 50 members support it (notice signed by minimum 50 members). The Speaker then admits the motion.

39. Parliamentary privileges are codified in:

  1. Articles 105 and 194
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 14
  4. Not codified, in Articles 105 and 194

Answer: Not codified, in Articles 105 and 194

Explanation: Parliamentary privileges are mentioned in Articles 105 (Parliament) and 194 (State Legislatures). They are NOT fully codified — courts have ruled that privileges exist as they did in British Parliament in 1947.

40. The Speaker of Lok Sabha vacates office:

  1. When new Lok Sabha meets for the first time
  2. After 5 years
  3. On completion of term
  4. When new government is formed

Answer: When new Lok Sabha meets for the first time

Explanation: The Speaker vacates office not when Lok Sabha is dissolved, but 'immediately before the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha' (Article 94). The Speaker thus continues for the first sitting of the new House.

41. Under which article can Parliament legislate on State List subjects?

  1. Articles 249, 250, 252, 253
  2. Article 246
  3. Article 248
  4. Article 245

Answer: Articles 249, 250, 252, 253

Explanation: Parliament can legislate on State List under: Art. 249 (RS resolution for national interest), Art. 250 (during Emergency), Art. 252 (2 or more states request), Art. 253 (for treaty/agreement).

42. The maximum size of the Council of Ministers cannot exceed:

  1. 15% of Lok Sabha's total strength
  2. 10% of total MPs
  3. 25 members
  4. 20% of Lok Sabha

Answer: 15% of Lok Sabha's total strength

Explanation: 91st Constitutional Amendment (2003): Council of Ministers strength shall not exceed 15% of total membership of the Lower House (Lok Sabha). Similarly for state cabinets.

43. Rajya Sabha's special power to authorize Parliament to legislate on State subjects is under:

  1. Article 249
  2. Article 246
  3. Article 248
  4. Article 252

Answer: Article 249

Explanation: Article 249: If Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by 2/3rd majority that it is necessary in national interest, Parliament can legislate on a State List subject for 1 year (renewable).

44. Rajya Sabha can authorize creation of new All India Services under:

  1. Article 312
  2. Article 315
  3. Article 309
  4. Article 310

Answer: Article 312

Explanation: Article 312: Rajya Sabha can authorize Parliament to create new All India Services (like IAS, IPS, IFS) by 2/3rd majority resolution, if it declares such services as necessary in national interest.

45. A Constitutional Amendment Bill rejected by Rajya Sabha:

  1. Cannot be passed through joint sitting
  2. Goes for joint sitting
  3. Fails permanently
  4. President can refer to SC

Answer: Cannot be passed through joint sitting

Explanation: Constitutional Amendment Bills CANNOT be passed through a joint sitting of Parliament (unlike ordinary bills which can). Rajya Sabha's rejection of a Constitutional Amendment Bill means it fails.

46. A political party gets National Party status if:

  1. Wins 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 states OR recognized in 4 states
  2. Wins elections in 4 states
  3. Gets 10% of votes nationally
  4. Has presence in all states

Answer: Wins 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 states OR recognized in 4 states

Explanation: A party is recognized as a National Party if: (a) Secures at least 6% of votes in 4 states and 4 Lok Sabha seats, OR (b) Wins at least 2% of total Lok Sabha seats from at least 3 different states.

47. The Delimitation Commission is established by:

  1. Delimitation Act passed by Parliament
  2. Constitutional provision
  3. President's order
  4. Election Commission

Answer: Delimitation Act passed by Parliament

Explanation: Delimitation Commission is established by an Act of Parliament (Delimitation Act). It determines boundaries of constituencies for Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

48. 'First Past the Post' electoral system used in India means:

  1. Candidate with most votes wins even if less than 50%
  2. Winner must get 50%+1 votes
  3. Proportional representation
  4. Second round if no majority

Answer: Candidate with most votes wins even if less than 50%

Explanation: First Past the Post (FPTP) or Simple Plurality System: The candidate with the highest number of votes wins, even if they have less than 50% of votes. Used for Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha elections.

49. Election to Rajya Sabha is conducted through:

  1. Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote
  2. Direct election by people
  3. Appointment by President
  4. Nomination by state legislatures

Answer: Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote

Explanation: Rajya Sabha members are elected by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies through Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV).

50. VVPATs (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trails) were introduced in India in:

  1. 2013 (Nagaland state election)
  2. 2004
  3. 2009
  4. 2014

Answer: 2013 (Nagaland state election)

Explanation: VVPATs were first used in India in the Nagaland state election in 2013. They were used in all Lok Sabha constituencies from 2019 elections.
