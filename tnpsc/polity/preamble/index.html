Preamble MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Preamble questions to revise the TNPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 280 questions.

1. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution begins with which words?

  1. We, the People of India
  2. We, the Citizens of India
  3. We, the Sovereign People
  4. We, the Democratic People

Answer: We, the People of India

Explanation: The Preamble begins with 'WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA', emphasizing popular sovereignty.

2. What does 'Secular' in the Preamble mean?

  1. India has an official religion
  2. India has no official religion
  3. All citizens must follow Hinduism
  4. Government controls religion

Answer: India has no official religion

Explanation: Secular means the State does not favour any religion. All religions are treated equally.

3. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) is called:

  1. The Democratic Amendment
  2. The Mini Constitution
  3. The Social Amendment
  4. The Economic Amendment

Answer: The Mini Constitution

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment is called the 'Mini Constitution' because it made extensive changes, including adding 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble.

4. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution was amended by which amendment?

  1. 24th Amendment
  2. 42nd Amendment
  3. 44th Amendment
  4. 52nd Amendment

Answer: 42nd Amendment

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment (1976) added three words to the Preamble: 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity'.

5. On which date did the Indian Constitution come into force?

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 November 1949
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 2 October 1950

Answer: 26 January 1950

Explanation: The Indian Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950, celebrated as Republic Day.

6. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution begins with:

  1. 'We, the Parliament of India'
  2. 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'
  3. 'India is a democratic country'
  4. 'In the name of God'

Answer: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'

Explanation: The Preamble begins: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic and to secure to all its citizens JUSTICE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY, and FRATERNITY...'

7. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
  2. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  3. 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
  4. 73rd Amendment Act, 1992

Answer: 42nd Amendment Act, 1976

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) during Emergency (PM Indira Gandhi) added 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble, making India a 'Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic'.

8. The Preamble is considered to be:

  1. A part of the Constitution (Supreme Court's Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)
  2. Not a part of the Constitution
  3. A directive principle
  4. An ordinary statute

Answer: A part of the Constitution (Supreme Court's Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)

Explanation: Initially in Berubari Union Case (1960), the SC said Preamble is not part of the Constitution. But in Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973), the SC held that the Preamble IS a part of the Constitution and can be amended (but not the basic structure).

9. The Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973) established the doctrine of:

  1. Equality of laws
  2. Basic Structure of the Constitution — Parliament cannot amend the basic structure
  3. Separation of powers
  4. Federal supremacy

Answer: Basic Structure of the Constitution — Parliament cannot amend the basic structure

Explanation: Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala (1973): 13-judge bench held (7:6) that Parliament can amend ANY part of the Constitution under Article 368, BUT cannot alter the 'basic structure' (essential features). Basic structure includes: supremacy of Constitution, democracy, republicanism, secular character, federal character, judicial review.

10. The Preamble of India's Constitution begins with:

  1. We, the People of India
  2. We, the Citizens of India
  3. The People of India
  4. We, the Constituent Assembly

Answer: We, the People of India

Explanation: The Preamble begins with 'WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into...' — reflecting popular sovereignty.

11. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by:

  1. 42nd Amendment (1976)
  2. 44th Amendment (1978)
  3. 25th Amendment
  4. 1st Amendment (1951)

Answer: 42nd Amendment (1976)

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble during the Emergency period under Indira Gandhi.

12. The Preamble to the Constitution was amended by:

  1. Only once (42nd Amendment)
  2. Twice
  3. Never
  4. Three times

Answer: Only once (42nd Amendment)

Explanation: The Preamble has been amended only once — by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act 1976 — which added 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity'.

13. 'Justice — Social, Economic, and Political' in the Preamble was inspired by:

  1. Russian Revolution
  2. French Revolution
  3. American Revolution
  4. British Democracy

Answer: Russian Revolution

Explanation: The concept of Social, Economic, and Political Justice in the Preamble was inspired by the Russian Revolution (1917) and the ideals of socialism.

14. The Preamble declares India as a:

  1. Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic
  2. Sovereign, Democratic, Federal, Secular Republic
  3. Democratic, Federal, Secular, Socialist Republic
  4. Federal, Secular, Socialist, Democratic Republic

Answer: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic

Explanation: Preamble declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic (SSSDR). Sovereign=independent; Socialist=social justice; Secular=no state religion; Democratic=elected government; Republic=no hereditary head.

15. Is the Preamble a part of the Constitution?

  1. Yes, per Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)
  2. No, per Berubari Case (1960)
  3. Not settled by Supreme Court
  4. Only after 42nd Amendment

Answer: Yes, per Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)

Explanation: Initially in Berubari case (1960) SC said Preamble is not part of Constitution. Later in Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) SC ruled Preamble IS part of the Constitution and can be amended.

16. The S.R. Bommai case (1994) significantly restricted:

  1. Misuse of Article 356 (President's Rule)
  2. Right to property
  3. Emergency powers
  4. Inter-state disputes

Answer: Misuse of Article 356 (President's Rule)

Explanation: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994): 9-judge bench held that: (a) President's Rule is subject to judicial review, (b) Floor test must be held before dismissing state governments, (c) Secularism is Basic Structure.

17. The 42nd Amendment (1976) is called:

  1. Mini Constitution
  2. Fundamental Amendment
  3. Emergency Amendment
  4. Peoples' Amendment

Answer: Mini Constitution

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment (1976), passed during the Emergency, is called the 'Mini Constitution' as it made sweeping changes — added 'Socialist', 'Secular', 'Integrity' to Preamble, added Fundamental Duties, curtailed judicial review, etc.

18. The 'Doctrine of Basic Structure' means:

  1. Parliament cannot amend certain fundamental features of the Constitution
  2. Courts cannot review constitutional amendments
  3. All constitutional amendments require 2/3rd majority
  4. State governments have basic powers that Parliament cannot take away

Answer: Parliament cannot amend certain fundamental features of the Constitution

Explanation: The Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) limits Parliament's power to amend the Constitution. Certain features (supremacy of Constitution, republican form, separation of powers, federalism, secular character, judicial review, FRs) form the basic structure and cannot be amended.

19. The S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) case relates to:

  1. Imposition of President's Rule under Art 356
  2. Judicial review of constitutional amendments
  3. OBC reservations
  4. Anti-defection law

Answer: Imposition of President's Rule under Art 356

Explanation: S.R. Bommai (1994) — 9-judge bench — held that President's Rule is subject to judicial review, the floor test must be the only way to determine majority, and secularism is part of Basic Structure.

20. The term 'sovereign' in the Indian Preamble means:

  1. India is free from external control and has supreme power internally
  2. India is under no international obligation
  3. India has a monarch
  4. India is independent of all treaties

Answer: India is free from external control and has supreme power internally

Explanation: 'Sovereign' in the Preamble means India is not subject to any external authority — no foreign power controls India. Internally, the Constitution is supreme. India's membership in Commonwealth or UN does not affect sovereignty.

21. The term 'Republic' in the Preamble means:

  1. The head of state is elected, not hereditary
  2. India has no monarchy
  3. India has no President
  4. India is a democracy

Answer: The head of state is elected, not hereditary

Explanation: 'Republic' means the head of state (President) is an elected person and not a hereditary monarch. This distinguishes India from a monarchy like UK where the King/Queen is the constitutional head.

22. The 42nd Amendment (1976) is called the 'mini-Constitution' because:

  1. It was a small amendment
  2. It made sweeping changes — added 'socialist', 'secular' to Preamble, added DPSPs, Fundamental Duties, extended Emergency powers, curtailed judicial review
  3. It was the 42nd attempt to amend
  4. It was related to education

Answer: It made sweeping changes — added 'socialist', 'secular' to Preamble, added DPSPs, Fundamental Duties, extended Emergency powers, curtailed judicial review

Explanation: 42nd Amendment (Indira Gandhi during Emergency, 1976) was the most comprehensive amendment: added 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to Preamble, added 10 Fundamental Duties, gave primacy to DPSPs over Fundamental Rights, curtailed SC/HC powers, extended Lok Sabha/state assembly terms to 6 years.

23. Parliamentary Sovereignty in India means:

  1. Parliament has supreme power within Constitutional limits
  2. Parliament is above the Constitution
  3. Parliament overrides Supreme Court
  4. Parliament can do anything

Answer: Parliament has supreme power within Constitutional limits

Explanation: Unlike UK's absolute Parliamentary sovereignty, India has limited parliamentary sovereignty — Parliament is supreme within constitutional limits. Courts can strike down laws violating the Constitution.

24. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution was amended to add the words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' by:

  1. 42nd Amendment, 1976
  2. 44th Amendment, 1978
  3. 74th Amendment, 1992
  4. 46th Amendment, 1982

Answer: 42nd Amendment, 1976

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) — during Emergency — added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble. The Preamble has been amended only once.

25. In a democracy, the ultimate power rests with:

  1. The President
  2. The Prime Minister
  3. The Supreme Court
  4. The People

Answer: The People

Explanation: In a democracy, ultimate sovereignty rests with the people, who exercise power through their elected representatives.
