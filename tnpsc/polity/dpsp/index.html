TNPSC Polity practice

DPSP MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked DPSP questions to revise the TNPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?

  1. Part II
  2. Part III
  3. Part IV
  4. Part V

Answer: Part IV

Explanation: Directive Principles of State Policy are in Part IV (Articles 36-51). They are non-justiciable but fundamental in governance.

2. How many Fundamental Duties are listed in the Indian Constitution?

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 11
  4. 12

Answer: 11

Explanation: There are 11 Fundamental Duties. Initially 10 were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976); the 11th (related to education for children) was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

3. The concept of 'Directive Principles of State Policy' was borrowed from:

  1. USA
  2. UK
  3. Ireland (Irish Constitution of 1937)
  4. USSR

Answer: Ireland (Irish Constitution of 1937)

Explanation: DPSP (Part IV, Articles 36–51) was borrowed from the Irish Constitution (Éire) of 1937. DPSPs are non-justiciable (not enforceable by courts) but fundamental to governance.

4. Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code) is a:

  1. Fundamental Right
  2. Directive Principle of State Policy — non-justiciable directive to the state to endeavour to secure a UCC for all citizens
  3. Constitutional Amendment procedure
  4. Emergency provision

Answer: Directive Principle of State Policy — non-justiciable directive to the state to endeavour to secure a UCC for all citizens

Explanation: Article 44 (DPSP): 'The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India.' It is non-justiciable — cannot be enforced through courts. Goa has a common civil code (Goa Civil Code, inherited from Portuguese rule).

5. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are in:

  1. Part III (Articles 12-35)
  2. Part IV (Articles 36-51)
  3. Part V
  4. Part VI

Answer: Part IV (Articles 36-51)

Explanation: DPSPs are in Part IV (Articles 36–51). They are non-justiciable (cannot be enforced by court), but they are 'fundamental to governance.' Inspired by Irish Constitution and the UN's Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

6. The DPSP that requires the state to minimize inequalities in income and status is:

  1. Article 39
  2. Article 38
  3. Article 40
  4. Article 41

Answer: Article 38

Explanation: Article 38 directs the State to secure a social order for promotion of welfare and to minimize inequalities in income, status, facilities, and opportunities. Article 39 lists specific economic principles (equal pay, no concentration of wealth, etc.).

7. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 (Part IVA, Article 51A)
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 73rd Amendment
  4. 86th Amendment

Answer: 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 (Part IVA, Article 51A)

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment Act (1976) added Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A) with 10 duties based on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. The 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th duty — to provide opportunities for education to children aged 6-14.

8. How many Fundamental Duties are there currently?

  1. 10
  2. 11
  3. 12
  4. 8

Answer: 11

Explanation: There are currently 11 Fundamental Duties under Article 51A. Originally 10 were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). The 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th duty: for parents/guardians to provide opportunities for education to children aged 6-14.

9. The Constitution of India borrowed the idea of Directive Principles from:

  1. Irish Constitution
  2. US Constitution
  3. British Constitution
  4. Australian Constitution

Answer: Irish Constitution

Explanation: Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36-51) were borrowed from the Irish Constitution (1937). The concept was inspired by the Spanish Constitution of 1931.

10. Directive Principles are:

  1. Non-justiciable but fundamental to governance
  2. Justiciable and enforceable by courts
  3. Neither justiciable nor fundamental
  4. Justiciable only for SCs/STs

Answer: Non-justiciable but fundamental to governance

Explanation: DPSPs are non-justiciable (courts cannot enforce them) but are fundamental in governance — the state 'shall endeavor' to implement them. Courts use them to interpret laws.

11. Article 44 of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Uniform Civil Code
  2. Equal pay for equal work
  3. Free legal aid
  4. Panchayati Raj

Answer: Uniform Civil Code

Explanation: Article 44 DPSP directs the state to secure a Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for all citizens — one set of personal laws regardless of religion for marriage, divorce, inheritance.

12. Article 48A (Protection of environment) was added by:

  1. 42nd Amendment (1976)
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 52nd Amendment
  4. 61st Amendment

Answer: 42nd Amendment (1976)

Explanation: Article 48A (protection of environment, forests, and wildlife) was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment 1976. Article 51A(g) imposes corresponding duty on citizens.

13. Which DPSP relates to 'Equal pay for equal work for men and women'?

  1. Article 39(d)
  2. Article 41
  3. Article 43
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 39(d)

Explanation: Article 39(d) DPSP directs equal pay for equal work for both men and women — aimed at eliminating gender-based wage discrimination.

14. Fundamental Duties are contained in:

  1. Article 51A (Part IVA)
  2. Article 51 (Part IV)
  3. Article 19 (Part III)
  4. Article 52 (Part V)

Answer: Article 51A (Part IVA)

Explanation: Fundamental Duties are in Article 51A (Part IVA), added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) based on Swaran Singh Committee recommendations. 11th duty added by 86th Amendment (2002).

15. Originally Fundamental Duties were:

  1. 10 in number
  2. 11 in number
  3. 8 in number
  4. 12 in number

Answer: 10 in number

Explanation: Originally 10 Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). The 11th Fundamental Duty (provide education to children aged 6-14) was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

16. Fundamental Duties were added on the recommendation of:

  1. Swaran Singh Committee
  2. Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
  3. Sarkaria Commission
  4. Shah Commission

Answer: Swaran Singh Committee

Explanation: Swaran Singh Committee (1976) recommended adding Fundamental Duties to the Constitution. The 42nd Amendment implemented 10 of these duties.

17. The Constitution of India borrowed the concept of Directive Principles of State Policy from:

  1. Ireland
  2. UK
  3. USA
  4. Australia

Answer: Ireland

Explanation: The Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) were borrowed from the Irish Constitution of 1937, which itself was influenced by the Spanish Constitution.

18. The 86th Amendment (2002) relates to:

  1. Right to Education — Art 21A
  2. GST
  3. Reservation for OBCs
  4. Anti-defection

Answer: Right to Education — Art 21A

Explanation: The 86th Amendment (2002) inserted Art 21A (Right to Education for 6-14 year olds), amended Art 45 (early childhood care and education up to 6 years), and added Art 51A(k) as a parental duty.

19. Which Article of Directive Principles directs the State to secure equal pay for equal work for both men and women?

  1. Article 39(d)
  2. Article 39(a)
  3. Article 41
  4. Article 43

Answer: Article 39(d)

Explanation: Article 39(d) directs the State to ensure that there is equal pay for equal work for both men and women. The Supreme Court has derived right to equal pay as a right under Art 14/16 applying DPSP.

20. Article 44 of the Constitution directs the State to secure:

  1. Uniform Civil Code for citizens
  2. Equal pay for equal work
  3. Free legal aid
  4. Village panchayats

Answer: Uniform Civil Code for citizens

Explanation: Article 44 directs the State to endeavor to secure a Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for citizens throughout India. This remains one of the most politically sensitive DPSPs yet to be implemented.

21. Article 48 of the Directive Principles deals with:

  1. Agriculture and animal husbandry (prohibition of cow slaughter)
  2. Environmental protection
  3. Village panchayats
  4. Legal aid

Answer: Agriculture and animal husbandry (prohibition of cow slaughter)

Explanation: Article 48 directs the State to organize agriculture and animal husbandry on modern scientific lines and prohibit slaughter of cows and calves and other milch and draught cattle. Art 48A (42nd Amendment) adds environment protection.

22. Which DPSP article relates to promotion of cottage industries in rural areas?

  1. Article 43
  2. Article 41
  3. Article 39
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 43

Explanation: Article 43 directs the State to secure to all workers a living wage, decent standard of life and to promote cottage industries on individual or cooperative basis in rural areas.

23. Article 51A of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Fundamental Duties of citizens
  2. Directive Principles
  3. Fundamental Rights
  4. Amendment procedure

Answer: Fundamental Duties of citizens

Explanation: Article 51A (Part IV-A) contains the Fundamental Duties of citizens, added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). Originally 10 duties; an 11th duty (relating to education for children) was added by 86th Amendment (2002).

24. Article 368 of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution
  2. Emergency provisions
  3. Fundamental Rights
  4. Directive Principles

Answer: Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution

Explanation: Article 368 (Part XX) empowers Parliament to amend the Constitution. It provides three methods: simple majority, special majority (2/3rd + majority of total membership), and special majority with ratification by at least half the State Legislatures.

25. How many Fundamental Duties are listed in Article 51A of the Constitution?

  1. 11
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 8

Answer: 11

Explanation: Currently there are 11 Fundamental Duties in Art 51A. The original 42nd Amendment (1976) added 10 duties. The 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th duty — parents must provide opportunities for education to children aged 6-14.
