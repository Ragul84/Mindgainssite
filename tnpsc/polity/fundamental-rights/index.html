TNPSC Polity practice

Fundamental Rights MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Fundamental Rights questions to revise the TNPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. What is the supreme law of India?

  1. Indian Penal Code
  2. The Constitution of India
  3. Civil Procedure Code
  4. The Indian Evidence Act

Answer: The Constitution of India

Explanation: The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the land. All other laws must be consistent with it.

2. How many fundamental rights are guaranteed by the Indian Constitution?

  1. 5
  2. 6
  3. 7
  4. 9

Answer: 6

Explanation: The Indian Constitution guarantees 6 Fundamental Rights under Part III (Articles 12-35).

3. How many languages are listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution?

  1. 18
  2. 20
  3. 22
  4. 24

Answer: 22

Explanation: The Eighth Schedule lists 22 official languages of India.

4. Right to Education is guaranteed under which article?

  1. Article 19
  2. Article 21
  3. Article 21A
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 21A

Explanation: Article 21A (added by 86th Amendment, 2002) provides free and compulsory education to children aged 6-14 years.

5. What is the national animal of India?

  1. Lion
  2. Elephant
  3. Tiger
  4. Peacock

Answer: Tiger

Explanation: Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris tigris) is the national animal of India.

6. Which writ is issued by a court to compel a public authority to perform its legal duty?

  1. Habeas Corpus
  2. Mandamus
  3. Certiorari
  4. Quo Warranto

Answer: Mandamus

Explanation: Mandamus (meaning 'We command') is issued to a public authority, lower court, or corporation to perform a public or statutory duty.

7. Which writ is used to produce a detained person before a court?

  1. Mandamus
  2. Certiorari
  3. Habeas Corpus
  4. Prohibition

Answer: Habeas Corpus

Explanation: Habeas Corpus (meaning 'You may have the body') is issued to free a person from illegal detention.

8. How many schedules does the Indian Constitution have?

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 14

Answer: 12

Explanation: The Indian Constitution originally had 8 schedules; it now has 12 schedules after various amendments.

9. Which schedule of the Constitution deals with anti-defection law?

  1. Eighth Schedule
  2. Ninth Schedule
  3. Tenth Schedule
  4. Eleventh Schedule

Answer: Tenth Schedule

Explanation: The Tenth Schedule (added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985) contains provisions for disqualification on grounds of defection.

10. Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees:

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right against Exploitation
  3. Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion

Answer: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty

Explanation: Article 21 states: 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.'

11. The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by which amendment?

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 46th Amendment
  4. 52nd Amendment

Answer: 44th Amendment

Explanation: The 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) removed the Right to Property from Fundamental Rights and made it a legal right under Article 300A.

12. Which constitutional body is known as the 'watchdog of public finance'?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Comptroller and Auditor General
  3. Finance Commission
  4. SEBI

Answer: Comptroller and Auditor General

Explanation: The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG), under Article 148, audits all government accounts and is called the watchdog of public finance.

13. Which article guarantees freedom of speech and expression in India?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 17
  3. Article 19(1)(a)
  4. Article 21

Answer: Article 19(1)(a)

Explanation: Article 19(1)(a) guarantees all citizens the right to freedom of speech and expression.

14. The Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with:

  1. Languages
  2. Anti-defection law
  3. Distribution of powers between Centre and States
  4. Tribal areas

Answer: Distribution of powers between Centre and States

Explanation: The Seventh Schedule contains three lists — Union List, State List, and Concurrent List — which distribute legislative powers between the Centre and States.

15. Which amendment reduced the voting age from 21 to 18 years?

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 61st Amendment
  4. 73rd Amendment

Answer: 61st Amendment

Explanation: The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act (1988) reduced the voting age from 21 to 18 years.

16. The Constitution of India originally had:

  1. 395 articles, 8 schedules
  2. 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules (1950)
  3. 448 articles, 12 schedules
  4. 300 articles, 7 schedules

Answer: 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules (1950)

Explanation: The original Constitution (1950) had 395 articles, 22 parts, and 8 schedules. After amendments, it now has 448 articles, 25 parts, and 12 schedules. It is the world's lengthiest written constitution.

17. India borrowed the concept of 'Fundamental Rights' from:

  1. UK
  2. USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)
  3. USSR
  4. Australia

Answer: USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)

Explanation: India borrowed the concept of Fundamental Rights from the US Constitution (First 10 Amendments — Bill of Rights). The idea of judicial review was also borrowed from the USA.

18. Fundamental Rights are enshrined in:

  1. Part II (Articles 5–11)
  2. Part III (Articles 12–35)
  3. Part IV (Articles 36–51)
  4. Part I (Articles 1–4)

Answer: Part III (Articles 12–35)

Explanation: Fundamental Rights are in Part III (Articles 12–35) of the Indian Constitution. They are justiciable (enforceable by courts). Originally 7, now 6 (Right to Property removed by 44th Amendment 1978, made a legal right under Article 300A).

19. The six Fundamental Rights are:

  1. Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies
  2. Right to Property, Right to Education, Right to Equality, Right to Vote, Right to Work, Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Life, Right to Liberty, Right to Property, Right to Religion, Right to Speech, Right to Assembly
  4. Right to Equality, Right to Work, Right to Education, Right to Food, Right to Health, Right to Housing

Answer: Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies

Explanation: The 6 Fundamental Rights: 1) Right to Equality (Art 14-18), 2) Right to Freedom (Art 19-22), 3) Right against Exploitation (Art 23-24), 4) Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25-28), 5) Cultural and Educational Rights (Art 29-30), 6) Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32).

20. Article 14 of the Indian Constitution guarantees:

  1. Right to Freedom of Speech
  2. Right to Equality before law and Equal protection of laws
  3. Right to Life and Personal Liberty
  4. Right against Exploitation

Answer: Right to Equality before law and Equal protection of laws

Explanation: Article 14: 'The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.' It is available to citizens AND non-citizens.

21. Article 19 guarantees freedom to all CITIZENS. Which of these is NOT in Article 19?

  1. Freedom of speech and expression
  2. Freedom to assemble peacefully
  3. Freedom to reside and settle anywhere in India
  4. Right to life and personal liberty

Answer: Right to life and personal liberty

Explanation: Article 19 gives citizens 6 freedoms: speech/expression, peaceful assembly (without arms), forming associations/unions, free movement, residence/settlement, and practising any profession/occupation. Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) is separate.

22. Article 21 guarantees:

  1. Freedom of speech
  2. Protection of life and personal liberty — 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law'
  3. Right to property
  4. Right to education

Answer: Protection of life and personal liberty — 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law'

Explanation: Article 21 is one of the most fundamental rights. Through judicial interpretation, it now covers: right to privacy, right to livelihood, right to health, right to clean environment, right to speedy trial, right to dignity, etc. Article 21A guarantees free education for children 6-14 years.

23. The Right to Education (Article 21A) was added by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 86th Amendment Act, 2002
  4. 93rd Amendment

Answer: 86th Amendment Act, 2002

Explanation: The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act (2002) inserted Article 21A: 'The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years.' The Right to Education Act 2009 gives effect to this.

24. 'Habeas Corpus' literally means:

  1. We command
  2. You may have the body — a writ ordering the production of a detained person before court to determine if detention is lawful
  3. A question of right — challenging a person's claim to public office
  4. It is prohibited — to stop lower court/tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction

Answer: You may have the body — a writ ordering the production of a detained person before court to determine if detention is lawful

Explanation: Habeas Corpus ('you may have the body') is a writ ordering the production of a detained person before a court to determine if the detention is lawful. It protects against arbitrary arrest and detention.

25. The writ 'Mandamus' is issued to:

  1. Lower courts to stop exceeding jurisdiction
  2. A public official or body to perform a legal duty they have neglected or refused to perform
  3. A person to justify why they hold a public office
  4. Produce a detained person before court

Answer: A public official or body to perform a legal duty they have neglected or refused to perform

Explanation: Mandamus ('we command') is a writ directing a public official, lower court, government body, or corporation to perform a mandatory public duty that it has failed or refused to perform. Cannot be issued against a private person.
