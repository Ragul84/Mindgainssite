Fundamental Rights MCQs with answers

26. Equal pay for equal work for both men and women is under:

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 15
  3. Article 39(d) — DPSP
  4. Article 16

Answer: Article 39(d) — DPSP

Explanation: Article 39(d) DPSP: 'equal pay for equal work for both men and women.' Though not a Fundamental Right, the Supreme Court has interpreted Article 39(d) in conjunction with Article 14 and 16 to enforce equal pay.

27. Question Hour in Parliament is held:

  1. On Wednesdays only
  2. During the first hour of every sitting day (11 AM-12 noon)
  3. Once a month
  4. After passing of budget only

Answer: During the first hour of every sitting day (11 AM-12 noon)

Explanation: Question Hour is the first hour (11 AM-12 noon) of each Parliamentary sitting. Members can ask oral questions (starred — answered orally, supplementaries can follow) and written questions (unstarred — written answers). Zero Hour immediately follows.

28. The President of India can declare National Emergency under:

  1. Article 352 — if national security is threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion
  2. Article 356
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 368

Answer: Article 352 — if national security is threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion

Explanation: Article 352 allows the President to proclaim National Emergency if the security of India is threatened by war, external aggression, or 'armed rebellion' (changed from 'internal disturbance' by 44th Amendment). Cabinet must advise in writing. Requires 2/3 special majority in Parliament.

29. The 44th Amendment Act (1978) restricted the use of National Emergency by:

  1. Requiring approval of state legislatures
  2. Requiring a written recommendation by the Cabinet + 2/3 majority of total membership in each House (changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion')
  3. Abolishing the provision
  4. Giving it only to Parliament

Answer: Requiring a written recommendation by the Cabinet + 2/3 majority of total membership in each House (changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion')

Explanation: The 44th Amendment (1978, post-Emergency) changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion' (harder standard), required Cabinet's written recommendation, required approval by special majority (2/3 of total membership), and gave Lok Sabha power to revoke Emergency by simple majority.

30. India is described as:

  1. A Federation
  2. A Unitary State
  3. A Union of States (quasi-federal — strong centre, federal features)
  4. A Confederation

Answer: A Union of States (quasi-federal — strong centre, federal features)

Explanation: Article 1 describes India as 'a Union of States'. India has federal features (two levels of government, written constitution, division of powers, independent judiciary) but also strong unitary features (single citizenship, integrated judiciary, emergency powers). K.C. Wheare called it 'quasi-federal'.

31. The concept of Fundamental Rights in Indian Constitution is borrowed from:

  1. US Constitution
  2. British Constitution
  3. Irish Constitution
  4. Canadian Constitution

Answer: US Constitution

Explanation: Fundamental Rights (Part III) were largely inspired by the Bill of Rights in the US Constitution (First 10 Amendments). The judicial review mechanism also came from the USA.

32. The concept of 'Concurrent List' in Indian Constitution is borrowed from:

  1. Australian Constitution
  2. US Constitution
  3. British Constitution
  4. Canadian Constitution

Answer: Australian Constitution

Explanation: The three-list system (Union, State, Concurrent) and specifically the Concurrent List was borrowed from the Australian Constitution.

33. India's Constitution is the world's longest written constitution with originally how many articles?

  1. 395 articles
  2. 442 articles
  3. 448 articles
  4. 444 articles

Answer: 395 articles

Explanation: Originally the Constitution had 395 articles in 22 parts and 8 schedules. Currently it has 448 articles (as amended) in 25 parts and 12 schedules (as of 2023).

34. The original Constitution had how many Schedules?

  1. 8
  2. 12
  3. 10
  4. 9

Answer: 8

Explanation: Original Constitution (1950) had 8 Schedules. Currently it has 12 Schedules (4 new schedules added by various amendments).

35. Which schedule lists the official languages of India?

  1. 8th Schedule
  2. 7th Schedule
  3. 9th Schedule
  4. 6th Schedule

Answer: 8th Schedule

Explanation: 8th Schedule originally listed 14 official languages. Currently it has 22 languages. Tamil was one of the original 14 languages.

36. The 9th Schedule was added by which Constitutional Amendment?

  1. 1st Amendment (1951)
  2. 42nd Amendment
  3. 24th Amendment
  4. 44th Amendment

Answer: 1st Amendment (1951)

Explanation: 9th Schedule was added by the 1st Constitutional Amendment (1951) to protect certain land reform laws from judicial review. Laws in 9th Schedule are immune from fundamental rights challenge.

37. Right to Education (RTE) is a Fundamental Right under which Article?

  1. Article 21A
  2. Article 21
  3. Article 29
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 21A

Explanation: Article 21A (inserted by 86th Amendment, 2002) makes free and compulsory education for children 6-14 years a Fundamental Right. The RTE Act 2009 gives effect to this right.

38. Article 19 guarantees freedom of:

  1. Speech, assembly, movement, profession, and more (6 freedoms)
  2. Religion
  3. Equality
  4. Education

Answer: Speech, assembly, movement, profession, and more (6 freedoms)

Explanation: Article 19 guarantees 6 freedoms: (a) Speech & Expression, (b) Peaceful Assembly, (c) Form Associations, (d) Move freely, (e) Reside anywhere, (g) Practice any profession/trade.

39. The Right to Property was removed as a Fundamental Right by:

  1. 44th Amendment (1978)
  2. 42nd Amendment (1976)
  3. 25th Amendment
  4. 1st Amendment

Answer: 44th Amendment (1978)

Explanation: 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights (Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31). It became a legal right under Article 300A.

40. Freedom of religion is guaranteed by Articles:

  1. 25-28
  2. 19-22
  3. 14-18
  4. 23-24

Answer: 25-28

Explanation: Articles 25-28 deal with Freedom of Religion: Art. 25 (right to profess, practice, propagate), Art. 26 (manage religious affairs), Art. 27 (no tax for religion), Art. 28 (no religious instruction in state schools).

41. Which Fundamental Right is available to both citizens and non-citizens?

  1. Right to Equality (Article 14)
  2. Right to vote
  3. Right to property
  4. Right to contest elections

Answer: Right to Equality (Article 14)

Explanation: Article 14 (Equality before law) applies to 'any person' — both citizens and non-citizens. Similarly, Articles 20, 21, 21A, 22, 25-28 apply to all persons, not just citizens.

42. Which Fundamental Right cannot be suspended even during Emergency?

  1. Articles 20 and 21
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 14
  4. Article 32

Answer: Articles 20 and 21

Explanation: Under Article 359, during Emergency, rights under Article 19 can be suspended, but Articles 20 (protection against conviction for offenses) and 21 (right to life) CANNOT be suspended even during Emergency.

43. The 'Golden Triangle' of Fundamental Rights refers to:

  1. Articles 14, 19, 21
  2. Articles 14, 15, 16
  3. Articles 19, 20, 21
  4. Articles 32, 226, 136

Answer: Articles 14, 19, 21

Explanation: Articles 14 (equality), 19 (freedoms), and 21 (life and personal liberty) are called the 'Golden Triangle' as they are most frequently invoked and mutually reinforce each other.

44. The Minerva Mills case (1980) established that:

  1. Parliament cannot destroy Fundamental Rights by amending DPSPs
  2. DPSPs override Fundamental Rights
  3. Parliament has unlimited amendment power
  4. Preamble is not part of Constitution

Answer: Parliament cannot destroy Fundamental Rights by amending DPSPs

Explanation: Minerva Mills case (1980): SC held that Parliament cannot destroy the balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs — the 42nd Amendment's attempt to give DPSPs absolute primacy was unconstitutional.

45. Which Parliamentary procedure is associated with 'starred questions'?

  1. Questions requiring oral answers
  2. Questions requiring written answers
  3. Questions about budget
  4. Questions about international affairs

Answer: Questions requiring oral answers

Explanation: Starred questions (marked with *) require oral answers and are followed by supplementary questions. Unstarred questions require written answers and no supplementary questions are allowed.

46. The President's term of office is:

  1. 5 years
  2. 6 years
  3. 4 years
  4. Until death

Answer: 5 years

Explanation: The President holds office for 5 years (Article 56). Can be re-elected any number of times. Can resign by writing to Vice President.

47. Cut motions on Demands for Grants are of how many types?

  1. 3 types
  2. 2 types
  3. 4 types
  4. 5 types

Answer: 3 types

Explanation: Three types of Cut Motions: (1) Disapproval of Policy Cut (reduce to ₹1), (2) Economy Cut (reduce by specific amount), (3) Token Cut (reduce by ₹100 to ventilate grievance).

48. The concept of 'due process of law' in India was elaborated in:

  1. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
  2. Kesavananda Bharati case
  3. Golaknath case
  4. A.K. Gopalan case

Answer: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)

Explanation: Maneka Gandhi case (1978): SC interpreted Article 21 broadly — procedure for depriving life/liberty must be fair, just, and reasonable (not mere 'procedure established by law' as literally stated).

49. Which case established that Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right?

  1. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)
  2. Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
  3. Maneka Gandhi (1978)
  4. Vishaka v. Rajasthan (1997)

Answer: Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)

Explanation: Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017): 9-judge bench unanimously held that Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right under Article 21.

50. Article 13 of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void
  2. Right to equality
  3. Rights of minorities
  4. Abolition of untouchability

Answer: Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void

Explanation: Article 13: Any law that takes away or abridges Fundamental Rights is void to the extent of inconsistency. It also prohibits the State from making such laws — foundation of judicial review.
