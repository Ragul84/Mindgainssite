Money Banking MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Money Banking questions to revise the UPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 700 questions.

1. FDI stands for:

  1. Foreign Direct Investment
  2. Federal Direct Income
  3. Financial Development Index
  4. Foreign Debt Instrument

Answer: Foreign Direct Investment

Explanation: FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) is investment made by a foreign entity in a business or production in another country, providing control or significant influence.

2. The 'Green Revolution' in India was associated with:

  1. Increase in forest cover
  2. High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds for food grains (wheat and rice)
  3. Development of renewable energy
  4. Growth of vegetable production

Answer: High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds for food grains (wheat and rice)

Explanation: The Green Revolution (1960s-70s) introduced HYV seeds (especially wheat by Norman Borlaug and M.S. Swaminathan), modern irrigation, and fertilisers, leading to a dramatic increase in food grain production.

3. The WTO (World Trade Organisation) replaced which organisation?

  1. IMF
  2. GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)
  3. World Bank
  4. UNCTAD

Answer: GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)

Explanation: WTO replaced GATT on 1 January 1995. It administers multilateral trade agreements and provides a forum for trade negotiations and dispute resolution.

4. The 'Gini Coefficient' measures:

  1. National income
  2. Inequality of income distribution (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality)
  3. Standard of living
  4. Economic growth rate

Answer: Inequality of income distribution (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality)

Explanation: Gini Coefficient (0 to 1 or 0 to 100) measures income inequality. 0 = perfect equality (everyone has same income); 1 = perfect inequality (one person has all income). India's Gini is approximately 0.35-0.38.

5. The 'Mundell-Fleming Model' extends Keynesian IS-LM analysis to:

  1. Closed economies
  2. Open economies with capital flows and exchange rates
  3. Socialist economies
  4. Agricultural economies

Answer: Open economies with capital flows and exchange rates

Explanation: The Mundell-Fleming model (early 1960s) extends IS-LM to open economies, incorporating international capital flows and exchange rate policies. It shows that monetary and fiscal policy effectiveness depends on the exchange rate regime.

6. Economics is defined as the study of:

  1. How individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants
  2. Only money and banking
  3. Only government finance
  4. Only international trade

Answer: How individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants

Explanation: Economics (from Greek 'oikonomia' = household management): study of how scarce resources (land, labor, capital, enterprise) are allocated to satisfy unlimited human wants. Alfred Marshall: 'Science of material welfare.' Lionel Robbins: 'Science of choice under scarcity.'

7. Inflation refers to:

  1. Sustained increase in the general price level over time — reduces purchasing power of money
  2. Decrease in prices (deflation)
  3. Increase in production
  4. Increase in employment

Answer: Sustained increase in the general price level over time — reduces purchasing power of money

Explanation: Inflation: persistent rise in the general price level → purchasing power of money falls. Measured by: CPI (Consumer Price Index, India's headline inflation measure), WPI (Wholesale Price Index). RBI's inflation target: 4% ± 2% (2-6% band). Causes: demand-pull (excess demand), cost-push (higher costs), monetary (excess money supply).

8. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures:

  1. Change in retail prices of a basket of goods and services purchased by typical households — India's main inflation indicator since 2014
  2. Change in wholesale prices (WPI)
  3. Change in stock market prices
  4. Change in property prices

Answer: Change in retail prices of a basket of goods and services purchased by typical households — India's main inflation indicator since 2014

Explanation: CPI: measures change in average retail prices paid by consumers for a fixed basket of goods/services. Base year: 2012. Categories: food & beverages (weight ~46%), housing, fuel, clothing, misc. RBI uses CPI-Combined (Urban + Rural) as the headline inflation measure. Annual CPI target: 4% (with ±2% tolerance band).

9. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was established in:

  1. 1935 (April 1) — under the RBI Act 1934; nationalized in 1949; headquartered in Mumbai
  2. 1947
  3. 1969
  4. 1950

Answer: 1935 (April 1) — under the RBI Act 1934; nationalized in 1949; headquartered in Mumbai

Explanation: RBI: established April 1, 1935, as a private shareholders' bank under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 (based on Hilton-Young Commission recommendations). Nationalized January 1, 1949. Governor: Shaktikanta Das (26th Governor). HQ: Mumbai. Key functions: monetary policy, currency issue, banking regulation, forex management.

10. The Repo Rate is:

  1. Rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks for the short term (against government securities as collateral)
  2. Rate at which banks lend to customers
  3. Rate at which banks accept deposits
  4. Rate of interest on government bonds

Answer: Rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks for the short term (against government securities as collateral)

Explanation: Repo Rate (Repurchase Rate): rate at which RBI provides short-term funds to commercial banks by purchasing their government securities (with agreement to repurchase). Increasing repo rate → commercial banks raise lending rates → reduces borrowing → controls inflation. Reverse Repo Rate: rate at which RBI borrows from banks (currently = Repo - 25 bps).

11. The Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is:

  1. Percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (deposits) that commercial banks must maintain as cash with RBI (earns no interest)
  2. Interest rate on loans
  3. Tax on bank profits
  4. Minimum capital requirement

Answer: Percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (deposits) that commercial banks must maintain as cash with RBI (earns no interest)

Explanation: CRR: RBI requires commercial banks to hold a certain % of their total deposits (NDTL) as cash with RBI. Current CRR: 4% (as of 2024). High CRR → banks have less money to lend → reduces money supply → controls inflation. RBI uses CRR as a quantitative monetary policy tool.

12. The Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) requires banks to:

  1. Maintain a certain percentage of their NDTL in liquid assets — gold, cash, or approved government securities (currently 18%)
  2. Only maintain cash with RBI
  3. Pay tax to the government
  4. Keep a minimum balance with other banks

Answer: Maintain a certain percentage of their NDTL in liquid assets — gold, cash, or approved government securities (currently 18%)

Explanation: SLR: banks must hold a minimum % of their deposits (NDTL) in liquid assets: cash, gold, and approved securities (mainly government bonds). Current SLR: 18% (2024). This ensures banks always have liquid assets to meet customer demands. SLR also channels funds to government borrowing. SLR + CRR together limit banks' funds available for lending.

13. India's Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in:

  1. January 2015 — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) chaired by the Prime Minister, unlike Planning Commission
  2. 2010
  3. 2000
  4. 2020

Answer: January 2015 — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) chaired by the Prime Minister, unlike Planning Commission

Explanation: NITI Aayog: established January 1, 2015, replacing the Planning Commission (est. 1950). NITI Aayog is a policy think tank (not a fund-allocating body like Planning Commission). Chaired by PM. CEO runs daily operations. Prepares national strategies and provides technical advice. Planning Commission's 5-year plans replaced by 3-year, 7-year strategies.

14. The White Revolution in India refers to:

  1. Operation Flood (1970-1996) — massive increase in milk production; led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India') through AMUL cooperative model
  2. Green Revolution
  3. Blue Revolution (fisheries)
  4. Pink Revolution (meat/poultry)

Answer: Operation Flood (1970-1996) — massive increase in milk production; led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India') through AMUL cooperative model

Explanation: White Revolution/Operation Flood: Dr. Verghese Kurien (NDDB — National Dairy Development Board) set up milk cooperatives (AMUL model) to eliminate middlemen, give fair price to farmers, and collect/process milk efficiently. India became world's largest milk producer. AMUL started in Anand, Gujarat, 1946.

15. The World Trade Organization (WTO) was established in:

  1. 1995 — replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1947); HQ in Geneva, Switzerland; 164 members; deals with international trade rules
  2. 1947
  3. 2000
  4. 1990

Answer: 1995 — replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1947); HQ in Geneva, Switzerland; 164 members; deals with international trade rules

Explanation: WTO (January 1, 1995): replaced GATT (1947) after Uruguay Round negotiations (1986-1994). HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. 164 member countries. Functions: sets and enforces global trade rules, resolves trade disputes, negotiates new trade agreements. India is a founding member. Doha Development Round (2001): stalled over agricultural subsidies.

16. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) was established at:

  1. Bretton Woods Conference (1944), operative 1945 — provides financial assistance to countries facing balance of payments crises; HQ: Washington DC; 190 members
  2. 1950
  3. 1960
  4. Geneva, 1947

Answer: Bretton Woods Conference (1944), operative 1945 — provides financial assistance to countries facing balance of payments crises; HQ: Washington DC; 190 members

Explanation: IMF: established at Bretton Woods, New Hampshire (July 1944), alongside World Bank. Came into existence 1945, HQ: Washington DC. Functions: balance of payments assistance, macroeconomic surveillance, technical assistance. Special Drawing Rights (SDR) is IMF's reserve currency. India's largest borrowing from IMF: 1991 crisis ($2.2 billion, mortgaged 67 tons of gold).

17. The World Bank Group's main lending arm for developing countries is:

  1. IBRD (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development) — lends to middle-income countries; IDA (International Development Association) — concessional loans to poorest countries
  2. IMF
  3. WTO
  4. ADB

Answer: IBRD (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development) — lends to middle-income countries; IDA (International Development Association) — concessional loans to poorest countries

Explanation: World Bank Group (est. Bretton Woods, 1944): IBRD (1945): loans/guarantees to middle-income/creditworthy countries. IDA (1960): interest-free/low-interest loans to poorest countries (including India for many years, now graduated). IFC: private sector lending. MIGA: political risk insurance. HQ: Washington DC. India is the largest borrower from World Bank.

18. Bank nationalization in India (major phase) occurred in:

  1. 1969 — PM Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 major private banks (those with deposits > Rs 50 crore); second phase 1980: 6 more banks nationalized
  2. 1947
  3. 1991
  4. 1935

Answer: 1969 — PM Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 major private banks (those with deposits > Rs 50 crore); second phase 1980: 6 more banks nationalized

Explanation: Bank Nationalization 1969: PM Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 private banks with deposits over Rs 50 crore (Central Bank, Bank of India, Canara Bank, etc.) to direct credit to priority sectors (agriculture, small industry). Second phase 1980: 6 more nationalized. SBI: nationalized 1955 (formerly Imperial Bank). RBI: 1949.

19. Sen's Capability Approach to development was proposed by:

  1. Amartya Sen — Nobel Prize in Economics 1998; argues development should expand people's capabilities and freedoms, not just income
  2. Paul Samuelson
  3. Milton Friedman
  4. John Maynard Keynes

Answer: Amartya Sen — Nobel Prize in Economics 1998; argues development should expand people's capabilities and freedoms, not just income

Explanation: Amartya Sen (Nobel 1998): capabilities approach — development as freedom/expansion of real opportunities. Focus on what people are actually able to do and be (capabilities), not just income. Influenced HDI design. Major works: 'Development as Freedom' (1999), 'The Idea of Justice'. Bengal famine research: famines caused by lack of entitlements, not just food scarcity.

20. The term 'balance of payments' refers to:

  1. Systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a given period (Current Account + Capital Account)
  2. Bank's income vs expenditure
  3. Government revenue vs expenditure (Budget)
  4. Export value vs import value (Trade Balance)

Answer: Systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a given period (Current Account + Capital Account)

Explanation: Balance of Payments (BoP): complete record of all monetary transactions between a country and the rest of the world. Current Account: goods (trade balance), services, income, current transfers. Capital Account: FDI, FPI/FII, loans, banking capital. BoP always balances (accounting identity). BoP crisis: when foreign reserves are insufficient.

21. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) is:

  1. Investment by a foreign entity in a business in another country with significant control (typically 10%+ ownership) — in plant, machinery, or management control
  2. Portfolio investment (FPI/FII — just buying shares)
  3. Loans from foreign banks
  4. Government-to-government aid

Answer: Investment by a foreign entity in a business in another country with significant control (typically 10%+ ownership) — in plant, machinery, or management control

Explanation: FDI: foreign entity invests in a domestic company (typically 10%+ equity = significant control). Brings: capital, technology, management expertise, global market access. FPI/FII: foreign portfolio investment (buying shares without control). India attracted $70 billion+ FDI in 2021-22. Top FDI sectors: services, computer, telecom, construction.

22. SENSEX is the benchmark stock market index of:

  1. Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) — comprises 30 largest, most actively traded companies; launched January 1, 1986 (base year 1978-79 = 100)
  2. National Stock Exchange (NSE — that's NIFTY 50)
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. Finance Ministry

Answer: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) — comprises 30 largest, most actively traded companies; launched January 1, 1986 (base year 1978-79 = 100)

Explanation: SENSEX (Sensitive Index): BSE's 30-stock index. NSE's benchmark: NIFTY 50 (50 stocks). BSE (1875): world's oldest stock exchange in Asia, at Dalal Street, Mumbai. NSE (1992): India's largest exchange by trading volume. SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India, 1992): regulator of securities market.

23. The Kuznets Curve (Environmental Kuznets Curve) suggests:

  1. Environmental degradation first increases then decreases as per capita income rises (inverted U-shape) — poor countries can't afford clean tech; rich countries can
  2. Income inequality always rises with growth
  3. Pollution is always increasing
  4. Environment improves immediately with development

Answer: Environmental degradation first increases then decreases as per capita income rises (inverted U-shape) — poor countries can't afford clean tech; rich countries can

Explanation: Environmental Kuznets Curve (EKC): as income rises, pollution/environmental degradation first increases (industrialization), reaches a peak, then decreases (clean technology, service economy, environmental regulations). Named after Simon Kuznets. The original Kuznets Curve deals with income inequality (inverted U) as economic development progresses.

24. The term 'multiplier effect' in economics means:

  1. An initial change in spending leads to a larger final change in income — if multiplier = 2, then Rs 100 crore investment → Rs 200 crore income increase
  2. Prices multiplying during inflation
  3. Population growth multiplier
  4. Export multiplier only

Answer: An initial change in spending leads to a larger final change in income — if multiplier = 2, then Rs 100 crore investment → Rs 200 crore income increase

Explanation: Keynesian Multiplier: k = 1/(1-MPC) = 1/MPS. Where MPC = Marginal Propensity to Consume (fraction of extra income spent). If MPC = 0.8, k = 1/(1-0.8) = 5. An injection of Rs 100 crore in government spending creates Rs 500 crore in total income (as each round of spending becomes income for someone else).

25. Microfinance refers to:

  1. Provision of small loans (micro-credit), savings, insurance, and other financial services to low-income people who lack access to traditional banking
  2. Large loans to industry
  3. Export finance
  4. Home loans

Answer: Provision of small loans (micro-credit), savings, insurance, and other financial services to low-income people who lack access to traditional banking

Explanation: Microfinance: pioneered by Muhammad Yunus (Grameen Bank, Bangladesh, 1983 — Nobel Peace Prize 2006). In India: SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (RBI/NABARD, 1992) — women form self-help groups, save collectively, access bank credit. MFIs (Microfinance Institutions) like SKS/Bandhan. India has world's largest microfinance program.
