26. Which of the following best describes the 'Base Effect' in the context of calculating inflation in India?

  1. The impact of the previous year's price levels on the current inflation rate
  2. The effect of base year revision on the Consumer Price Index
  3. The influence of core inflation on the headline inflation rate
  4. The impact of minimum support prices on food inflation

Answer: The impact of the previous year's price levels on the current inflation rate

Explanation: Base effect refers to the distortion in current inflation rates caused by the price levels of the corresponding period in the previous year.

27. If the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) decides to increase the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR), what is the most likely immediate impact on inflation?

  1. Inflation will increase due to higher costs of borrowing
  2. Inflation will decrease as liquidity in the banking system is reduced
  3. Inflation will remain unchanged as CRR only affects bank profitability
  4. Inflation will increase as banks will increase lending rates to recover losses

Answer: Inflation will decrease as liquidity in the banking system is reduced

Explanation: Increasing CRR reduces the loanable funds available with banks, lowering aggregate demand and helping to curb inflation.

28. In the context of the Indian economy, 'Core Inflation' is calculated by excluding which of the following from the headline inflation?

  1. Fuel and manufactured goods
  2. Services and food prices
  3. Food and fuel prices
  4. Imported goods and services

Answer: Food and fuel prices

Explanation: Core inflation excludes volatile components like food and fuel to provide a clearer picture of long-term inflation trends.

29. Which of the following scenarios would lead to 'Cost-Push Inflation' in the Indian economy?

  1. A sudden increase in government spending on infrastructure
  2. A sharp rise in global crude oil prices
  3. An increase in the money supply by the central bank
  4. A surge in consumer demand during the festive season

Answer: A sharp rise in global crude oil prices

Explanation: Cost-push inflation occurs when rising production costs, such as raw materials or oil, force producers to increase prices.

30. Under the Flexible Inflation Targeting framework adopted by the RBI, what is the primary objective of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)?

  1. To maintain a zero-inflation environment
  2. To ensure price stability while keeping growth in mind
  3. To prioritize exchange rate stability over price stability
  4. To regulate the fiscal deficit of the central government

Answer: To ensure price stability while keeping growth in mind

Explanation: The MPC aims to maintain price stability (targeting 4% +/- 2%) while supporting economic growth.

31. Which of the following is a characteristic of 'Stagflation'?

  1. High economic growth accompanied by high inflation
  2. Low inflation accompanied by high unemployment
  3. High inflation accompanied by stagnant growth and high unemployment
  4. Low inflation accompanied by stagnant growth

Answer: High inflation accompanied by stagnant growth and high unemployment

Explanation: Stagflation is a rare economic condition characterized by stagnant growth, high unemployment, and high inflation.

32. What is the likely impact of 'Sterilized Intervention' by the RBI when it buys foreign currency to prevent rupee appreciation?

  1. It increases the money supply and fuels inflation
  2. It neutralizes the effect on the domestic money supply to prevent inflation
  3. It leads to a decrease in the interest rates of government securities
  4. It forces the government to increase the fiscal deficit

Answer: It neutralizes the effect on the domestic money supply to prevent inflation

Explanation: Sterilization involves offsetting the increase in money supply from forex purchases by selling government securities to mop up liquidity.

33. Which of the following measures is a 'Fiscal' tool to control inflation, rather than a 'Monetary' tool?

  1. Increasing the Repo Rate
  2. Selling government securities in the Open Market Operations
  3. Increasing direct taxes to reduce disposable income
  4. Raising the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

Answer: Increasing direct taxes to reduce disposable income

Explanation: Taxation is a tool of fiscal policy managed by the government, whereas repo rates and SLR are monetary tools managed by the RBI.

34. When the RBI uses 'Open Market Operations' (OMO) to sell government securities, what is the intended effect on inflation?

  1. To increase liquidity and stimulate growth
  2. To decrease liquidity and put downward pressure on inflation
  3. To stabilize the exchange rate of the rupee
  4. To encourage commercial banks to lend more to the MSME sector

Answer: To decrease liquidity and put downward pressure on inflation

Explanation: Selling securities mops up excess liquidity from the system, reducing the money supply and helping to lower inflation.

35. In the context of the Indian economy, which of the following is the most likely consequence of 'Cost-Push Inflation'?

  1. An increase in the aggregate demand for goods and services
  2. A decrease in the overall output and real GDP
  3. A shift in the demand curve to the right
  4. An increase in the purchasing power of the domestic currency

Answer: A decrease in the overall output and real GDP

Explanation: Cost-push inflation occurs when production costs rise, leading to lower supply and reduced real GDP.

36. Which of the following actions by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is most effective in controlling demand-pull inflation?

  1. Reducing the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
  2. Decreasing the Repo Rate
  3. Increasing the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
  4. Buying government securities through Open Market Operations

Answer: Increasing the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

Explanation: Increasing CRR reduces the loanable funds available with banks, thereby curtailing aggregate demand.

37. How does a significant depreciation of the Indian Rupee against the US Dollar typically impact domestic inflation?

  1. It leads to deflation due to increased export competitiveness
  2. It causes imported inflation by increasing the cost of crude oil and imports
  3. It has no impact as India is a self-sufficient economy
  4. It reduces inflation by increasing the supply of foreign goods

Answer: It causes imported inflation by increasing the cost of crude oil and imports

Explanation: Currency depreciation makes imports more expensive, which increases domestic prices, known as imported inflation.

38. Which of the following statements best describes the 'Inflation Targeting' framework adopted by the RBI?

  1. The RBI targets a zero-inflation rate to ensure price stability
  2. The government sets a fixed price for all essential commodities
  3. The RBI aims to maintain CPI inflation at 4% with a tolerance band of +/- 2%
  4. The focus is solely on the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) to control inflation

Answer: The RBI aims to maintain CPI inflation at 4% with a tolerance band of +/- 2%

Explanation: India uses a flexible inflation targeting framework based on the Consumer Price Index (CPI).

39. What is the primary difference between the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) and the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in India?

  1. WPI includes services, while CPI only includes goods
  2. CPI captures the price changes at the retail level, while WPI captures them at the wholesale level
  3. WPI is used by the RBI for inflation targeting, whereas CPI is not
  4. CPI excludes food items, while WPI includes all food categories

Answer: CPI captures the price changes at the retail level, while WPI captures them at the wholesale level

Explanation: CPI reflects the cost of living for consumers, while WPI reflects the cost of goods for wholesalers.

40. If the RBI conducts 'Sterilized Intervention' to manage the impact of capital inflows, what is the primary objective?

  1. To increase the money supply to stimulate growth
  2. To prevent the appreciation of the rupee from causing domestic deflation
  3. To offset the effect of foreign exchange interventions on the domestic money supply
  4. To encourage more foreign portfolio investment in the equity market

Answer: To offset the effect of foreign exchange interventions on the domestic money supply

Explanation: Sterilization prevents the increase in domestic liquidity that occurs when the RBI buys foreign currency.

41. Which of the following scenarios would lead to 'Stagflation' in an economy?

  1. High inflation accompanied by high economic growth
  2. Low inflation accompanied by high unemployment
  3. High inflation accompanied by stagnant growth and high unemployment
  4. Low inflation accompanied by stagnant growth

Answer: High inflation accompanied by stagnant growth and high unemployment

Explanation: Stagflation is a rare combination of stagnant economic growth, high unemployment, and high inflation.

42. What is the likely impact of an increase in the 'Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)' rate on inflation?

  1. It increases liquidity in the banking system, raising inflation
  2. It makes borrowing costlier for banks, helping to curb inflation
  3. It leads to a decrease in interest rates for retail borrowers
  4. It encourages banks to lend more to the agricultural sector

Answer: It makes borrowing costlier for banks, helping to curb inflation

Explanation: A higher MSF rate increases the cost of emergency borrowing for banks, reducing overall liquidity.

43. In the context of inflation, what does the 'Base Effect' refer to?

  1. The impact of the base year selection on the calculation of current inflation
  2. The minimum interest rate set by the RBI
  3. The effect of fiscal deficit on the price level
  4. The impact of global oil prices on the domestic WPI

Answer: The impact of the base year selection on the calculation of current inflation

Explanation: The base effect occurs when the price level of the previous year affects the percentage change in current inflation.

44. Which of the following is a 'fiscal' measure to control inflation, as opposed to a 'monetary' measure?

  1. Increasing the Repo Rate
  2. Selling government securities in the open market
  3. Increasing direct taxes to reduce disposable income
  4. Raising the Cash Reserve Ratio

Answer: Increasing direct taxes to reduce disposable income

Explanation: Taxation is a tool of fiscal policy managed by the government, whereas interest rates are monetary tools.

45. In the context of the Indian economy, which of the following best describes the 'Inflation Targeting' framework adopted by the Reserve Bank of India?

  1. Fixing the exchange rate of the Rupee against the US Dollar to curb imported inflation
  2. A flexible inflation targeting framework where the RBI aims to maintain CPI inflation at 4% with a band of +/- 2%
  3. A system where the government sets a fixed price ceiling for all essential agricultural commodities
  4. Using only the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) to control the growth of broad money

Answer: A flexible inflation targeting framework where the RBI aims to maintain CPI inflation at 4% with a band of +/- 2%

Explanation: India uses a flexible inflation targeting framework focusing on Consumer Price Index (CPI) to maintain price stability.

46. If the Reserve Bank of India decides to conduct an Open Market Operation (OMO) by selling government securities in the open market, what is the most likely intended impact on inflation?

  1. Increase in liquidity, leading to higher inflation
  2. Decrease in liquidity, helping to lower inflation
  3. No impact on liquidity, but an increase in the exchange rate
  4. Increase in the money supply, leading to lower interest rates

Answer: Decrease in liquidity, helping to lower inflation

Explanation: Selling securities absorbs liquidity from the banking system, reducing the money supply and curbing inflationary pressure.

47. Which of the following scenarios would most likely lead to 'Cost-Push Inflation' in the Indian economy?

  1. A significant increase in government spending on infrastructure projects
  2. A sudden surge in the global prices of crude oil
  3. An increase in the disposable income of the urban middle class
  4. A decrease in the statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) by the RBI

Answer: A sudden surge in the global prices of crude oil

Explanation: Cost-push inflation occurs when rising production costs, like crude oil, force producers to increase prices.

48. Consider the impact of Rupee depreciation on inflation. Which of the following statements is correct?

  1. It reduces the cost of imported raw materials, thereby lowering inflation
  2. It leads to 'imported inflation' by making imports more expensive
  3. It has no impact on inflation as India is primarily an agrarian economy
  4. It decreases the price of exports, which directly lowers domestic inflation

Answer: It leads to 'imported inflation' by making imports more expensive

Explanation: Depreciation makes imports costlier, increasing the domestic price of goods and services, known as imported inflation.

49. Which of the following is the primary difference between the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) and the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in India?

  1. WPI includes services, while CPI only includes goods
  2. CPI includes services and reflects the retail price, while WPI tracks goods at the wholesale level
  3. WPI is used by the RBI for inflation targeting, whereas CPI is used by the Ministry of Finance
  4. CPI tracks price changes at the factory gate, while WPI tracks the end-consumer price

Answer: CPI includes services and reflects the retail price, while WPI tracks goods at the wholesale level

Explanation: CPI captures the price of a basket of goods and services consumed by households, unlike WPI which focuses on wholesale goods.

50. What is the most likely effect of an increase in the Repo Rate by the RBI on the inflation rate?

  1. Borrowing becomes cheaper, increasing consumption and inflation
  2. Borrowing becomes costlier, reducing aggregate demand and lowering inflation
  3. It leads to an immediate increase in the money supply in the economy
  4. It encourages banks to lower their lending rates to stimulate growth

Answer: Borrowing becomes costlier, reducing aggregate demand and lowering inflation

Explanation: Higher repo rates increase borrowing costs for banks and consumers, reducing spending and cooling down inflation.
