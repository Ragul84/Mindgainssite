Inflation MCQs with answers

Use these 5 worked Inflation questions to revise the UPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 700 questions.

1. The 'Philips Curve' depicts the relationship between:

  1. GDP growth and inflation
  2. Unemployment rate and inflation rate (inverse relationship)
  3. Interest rate and investment
  4. Trade deficit and exchange rate

Answer: Unemployment rate and inflation rate (inverse relationship)

Explanation: The Phillips Curve (1958) shows an inverse relationship between unemployment and inflation — lower unemployment is associated with higher inflation and vice versa, but this relationship broke down in the 1970s stagflation.

2. The law of demand states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)
  2. When price rises, demand rises
  3. Demand is always constant
  4. Supply determines demand

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)

Explanation: Law of Demand (Alfred Marshall): price and quantity demanded have an inverse relationship — as price rises, quantity demanded falls (consumers buy less; some switch to substitutes). Exceptions (Giffen goods, Veblen goods, necessities, wartime hoarding). Demand curve slopes downward.

3. The law of supply states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)
  2. When price rises, supply falls
  3. Supply is constant regardless of price
  4. Demand determines supply

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)

Explanation: Law of Supply: price and quantity supplied have a direct relationship — as price rises, producers are willing to supply more (higher profit incentive). Supply curve slopes upward. Exceptions: agricultural products (time lag), antiques/art (fixed supply). Market equilibrium: demand = supply.

4. The Wholesale Price Index (WPI) in India measures:

  1. Price changes at the wholesale/producer level (not retail) — covers primary articles (food, minerals), fuel & power, manufactured products; base year 2011-12; used to measure producer-level inflation
  2. Retail prices (CPI measures that)
  3. Import prices
  4. Stock prices

Answer: Price changes at the wholesale/producer level (not retail) — covers primary articles (food, minerals), fuel & power, manufactured products; base year 2011-12; used to measure producer-level inflation

Explanation: WPI: measures average change in prices of goods at first point of bulk sale (wholesale). Base year: 2011-12. Three groups: Primary Articles (22.6% weight — food, non-food, minerals), Fuel & Power (13.2%), Manufactured Products (64.2%). WPI has been replaced by CPI as India's headline inflation indicator (since 2014). WPI useful for PPI (Producer Price Index) analysis.

5. The term 'stagflation' was coined during:

  1. 1970s OPEC oil embargo crisis — when oil price quadrupled, causing supply-side cost-push inflation AND recession simultaneously; coined by UK politician Iain Macleod (1965)
  2. 1929 Great Depression
  3. 1990s financial crisis
  4. 2008 global financial crisis

Answer: 1970s OPEC oil embargo crisis — when oil price quadrupled, causing supply-side cost-push inflation AND recession simultaneously; coined by UK politician Iain Macleod (1965)

Explanation: 'Stagflation' coined by British politician Iain Macleod (1965, combined 'stagnation' + 'inflation'). Major episode: 1973-74 OPEC oil embargo quadrupled oil prices → cost-push inflation worldwide + recession (less production) → stagflation. Challenged Keynesian consensus that inflation and unemployment are inversely related (Phillips Curve). Led to Monetarism (Friedman) gaining prominence.
