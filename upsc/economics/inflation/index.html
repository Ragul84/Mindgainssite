Inflation MCQs with answers

1. The 'Philips Curve' depicts the relationship between:

  1. GDP growth and inflation
  2. Unemployment rate and inflation rate (inverse relationship)
  3. Interest rate and investment
  4. Trade deficit and exchange rate

Answer: Unemployment rate and inflation rate (inverse relationship)

Explanation: The Phillips Curve (1958) shows an inverse relationship between unemployment and inflation — lower unemployment is associated with higher inflation and vice versa, but this relationship broke down in the 1970s stagflation.

2. The law of demand states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)
  2. When price rises, demand rises
  3. Demand is always constant
  4. Supply determines demand

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)

Explanation: Law of Demand (Alfred Marshall): price and quantity demanded have an inverse relationship — as price rises, quantity demanded falls (consumers buy less; some switch to substitutes). Exceptions (Giffen goods, Veblen goods, necessities, wartime hoarding). Demand curve slopes downward.

3. The law of supply states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)
  2. When price rises, supply falls
  3. Supply is constant regardless of price
  4. Demand determines supply

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)

Explanation: Law of Supply: price and quantity supplied have a direct relationship — as price rises, producers are willing to supply more (higher profit incentive). Supply curve slopes upward. Exceptions: agricultural products (time lag), antiques/art (fixed supply). Market equilibrium: demand = supply.

4. The Wholesale Price Index (WPI) in India measures:

  1. Price changes at the wholesale/producer level (not retail) — covers primary articles (food, minerals), fuel & power, manufactured products; base year 2011-12; used to measure producer-level inflation
  2. Retail prices (CPI measures that)
  3. Import prices
  4. Stock prices

Answer: Price changes at the wholesale/producer level (not retail) — covers primary articles (food, minerals), fuel & power, manufactured products; base year 2011-12; used to measure producer-level inflation

Explanation: WPI: measures average change in prices of goods at first point of bulk sale (wholesale). Base year: 2011-12. Three groups: Primary Articles (22.6% weight — food, non-food, minerals), Fuel & Power (13.2%), Manufactured Products (64.2%). WPI has been replaced by CPI as India's headline inflation indicator (since 2014). WPI useful for PPI (Producer Price Index) analysis.

5. The term 'stagflation' was coined during:

  1. 1970s OPEC oil embargo crisis — when oil price quadrupled, causing supply-side cost-push inflation AND recession simultaneously; coined by UK politician Iain Macleod (1965)
  2. 1929 Great Depression
  3. 1990s financial crisis
  4. 2008 global financial crisis

Answer: 1970s OPEC oil embargo crisis — when oil price quadrupled, causing supply-side cost-push inflation AND recession simultaneously; coined by UK politician Iain Macleod (1965)

Explanation: 'Stagflation' coined by British politician Iain Macleod (1965, combined 'stagnation' + 'inflation'). Major episode: 1973-74 OPEC oil embargo quadrupled oil prices → cost-push inflation worldwide + recession (less production) → stagflation. Challenged Keynesian consensus that inflation and unemployment are inversely related (Phillips Curve). Led to Monetarism (Friedman) gaining prominence.

6. The 'Dutch Flower Auction' pricing mechanism is used for:

  1. Price starting high and descending until a buyer accepts — also called 'descending auction' or 'clock auction'; used for flowers, fish, tobacco, and some treasury securities
  2. Price starting low and going up (English auction)
  3. Fixed price by government
  4. Price set by producer only

Answer: Price starting high and descending until a buyer accepts — also called 'descending auction' or 'clock auction'; used for flowers, fish, tobacco, and some treasury securities

Explanation: Dutch (Descending) Auction: auctioneer starts at high price, lowers until a buyer bids. First bidder who accepts becomes the buyer. Advantage: fast (one round). Used in: Dutch flower market (hence name), fish markets, US Treasury auctions (actually uniform price), Google's IPO. Contrasts with English (ascending) auction where price rises until one bidder remains.

7. The term 'agflation' was coined during:

  1. 2007-08 global food crisis — combination of agriculture + inflation; food prices rose 80%+ globally due to biofuel demand, droughts, export restrictions, and financial speculation
  2. 1970s oil crisis
  3. 1991 India crisis
  4. 2008 financial crisis

Answer: 2007-08 global food crisis — combination of agriculture + inflation; food prices rose 80%+ globally due to biofuel demand, droughts, export restrictions, and financial speculation

Explanation: Agflation: price surge driven by agricultural commodity prices (food inflation). 2007-08 food crisis: wheat prices rose 130%, rice 74%, corn 56%. Causes: biofuel mandates (US corn for ethanol), droughts in Australia/Ukraine, export bans (India, Vietnam, Egypt for rice), financial speculators in commodity markets. Affected food security of poor countries most.

8. The Wholesale Price Index (WPI) in India has a base year of:

  1. 2011-12 (revised from 2004-05 in 2017) — covers 697 commodities; primary articles 22.6%, fuel 13.2%, manufactured products 64.2%; monthly release with 14-day lag
  2. 2000-01
  3. 2004-05 (still current)
  4. 2019-20

Answer: 2011-12 (revised from 2004-05 in 2017) — covers 697 commodities; primary articles 22.6%, fuel 13.2%, manufactured products 64.2%; monthly release with 14-day lag

Explanation: WPI base year revised to 2011-12 (released in May 2017) from 2004-05. Coverage: 697 commodities (vs 676 earlier). Weight: Primary Articles 22.62% (food: 15.26%, non-food: 4.29%, minerals: 3.07%), Fuel & Power 13.15%, Manufactured Products 64.23%. Released by Office of Economic Adviser (Ministry of Commerce). 14-day lag (faster than CPI's 12-day lag).

9. India's Wholesale Price Index (WPI) inflation measures prices at:

  1. First point of bulk sale/wholesale level — factory gate or farm gate prices — leads retail prices by 2-3 months (retailer passes on cost increases later)
  2. Consumer/retail level (CPI measures that)
  3. Import prices
  4. Export prices

Answer: First point of bulk sale/wholesale level — factory gate or farm gate prices — leads retail prices by 2-3 months (retailer passes on cost increases later)

Explanation: WPI: price changes at wholesale/bulk sale point — producer/factory/farm gate prices, before retail markup. WPI typically leads CPI (retail) by 2-3 months as cost increases take time to pass through supply chain. WPI doesn't include services (CPI includes services). WPI relevant for: business contracts (inflation indexation), commodity analysis.

10. A Giffen good is one where demand increases as price increases because:

  1. It has high brand value (Veblen effect)
  2. It is an inferior good where the income effect dominates the substitution effect
  3. Supply decreases with price
  4. It has no close substitutes

Answer: It is an inferior good where the income effect dominates the substitution effect

Explanation: Giffen goods (Robert Giffen): inferior goods where price rise reduces real income so severely that consumers buy more (cannot afford the superior substitute). Classic example: bread in 19th-century England.

11. India's Wholesale Price Index (WPI) is published by:

  1. RBI
  2. CSO/NSO
  3. Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Office of the Economic Adviser)
  4. MOSPI

Answer: Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Office of the Economic Adviser)

Explanation: WPI: published monthly by Office of the Economic Adviser (OEA), Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry. Base year 2011-12, 697 commodities.

12. India's Consumer Price Index (CPI) gives the highest weight to which category?

  1. Housing
  2. Miscellaneous
  3. Food and beverages
  4. Fuel and light

Answer: Food and beverages

Explanation: CPI (Combined, Base 2012): Food and beverages have the highest weight ~45.86% (rural ~54.18%, urban ~36.29%). Housing: ~10.07% (urban only in combined). This high food weight makes India's CPI sensitive to food inflation.

13. What is 'stagflation' and when was it first observed?

  1. Simultaneous stagnation (high unemployment, low growth) + inflation (rising prices) — first observed in 1970s OPEC oil crisis, contradicting Phillips Curve assumption of inverse relationship
  2. Only seen in developing countries
  3. Observed in all recessions
  4. Common economic phenomenon always present

Answer: Simultaneous stagnation (high unemployment, low growth) + inflation (rising prices) — first observed in 1970s OPEC oil crisis, contradicting Phillips Curve assumption of inverse relationship

Explanation: Stagflation: simultaneous recession and inflation. Phillips Curve (1958): inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment (when inflation is high, unemployment is low). 1973 OPEC oil embargo: oil prices quadrupled → cost-push inflation PLUS economic recession → stagflation (high inflation + high unemployment simultaneously). Disproved simple Phillips Curve. Led to Monetarism (Friedman) and supply-side economics (Reagan).

14. The 'Phillips Curve' in economics (A.W. Phillips, 1958) shows:

  1. Inverse relationship between unemployment rate and inflation rate — when unemployment is low (economy is booming), wages/prices rise faster; when unemployment is high, inflation is low
  2. Direct relationship between unemployment and inflation
  3. No relationship between unemployment and inflation
  4. Relationship between GDP and inflation only

Answer: Inverse relationship between unemployment rate and inflation rate — when unemployment is low (economy is booming), wages/prices rise faster; when unemployment is high, inflation is low

Explanation: Phillips Curve (1958): negative correlation between unemployment and wage inflation in UK data 1861-1957. Policy implication: policymakers can trade-off (accept more inflation for less unemployment). Stagflation of 1970s disproved simple Phillips Curve. Long-run Phillips Curve (Friedman-Phelps): vertical at Natural Rate of Unemployment (NAIRU) — no long-run trade-off.

15. The term 'Stagflation' was coined during which decade and event?

  1. 1930s Great Depression
  2. 1950s post-war boom
  3. 1970s OPEC oil crisis
  4. 1990s dot-com bust

Answer: 1970s OPEC oil crisis

Explanation: Stagflation = stagnation + inflation. Coined by UK politician Iain Macleod in 1965, but the phenomenon prominently occurred in the 1970s OPEC oil crises (1973, 1979). Contradicted the Phillips Curve.

16. Which of the following is the primary driver of inflation in the Indian economy?

  1. Demand-pull factors
  2. Cost-push factors
  3. Supply-side constraints in agriculture
  4. External sector shocks

Answer: Supply-side constraints in agriculture

Explanation: Supply-side constraints in agriculture, such as monsoon failures or procurement inefficiencies, frequently contribute to food inflation in India.

17. What is the main objective of the Reserve Bank of India's open market operations?

  1. To regulate the exchange rate
  2. To control liquidity in the banking system
  3. To directly finance government deficits
  4. To manage public sector undertakings

Answer: To control liquidity in the banking system

Explanation: Open market operations involve buying/selling government securities to inject or absorb liquidity, influencing interest rates and inflation.

18. How does a depreciation of the Indian rupee typically impact domestic inflation?

  1. Reduces inflation by making exports cheaper
  2. Increases inflation due to higher import costs
  3. Has no direct impact on inflation
  4. Only affects capital inflows

Answer: Increases inflation due to higher import costs

Explanation: A weaker rupee raises the cost of imported goods, contributing to cost-push inflation, especially in fuel and raw materials.

19. In sterilized intervention by the RBI, what does the central bank do to offset the impact of foreign exchange purchases?

  1. Raises the repo rate
  2. Sells government bonds to absorb excess liquidity
  3. Reduces the cash reserve ratio
  4. Increases lending rates

Answer: Sells government bonds to absorb excess liquidity

Explanation: Sterilization involves selling bonds to neutralize the rupee liquidity created from forex inflows, preventing inflationary pressure.

20. Which fiscal indicator is most directly linked to inflation in the Indian context?

  1. Fiscal deficit
  2. Current account deficit
  3. Revenue deficit
  4. Budget deficit

Answer: Fiscal deficit

Explanation: A high fiscal deficit often leads to increased government spending, driving aggregate demand and inflation.

21. What is the likely impact of persistent high inflation on India's balance of payments?

  1. Improves current account balance
  2. Worsens current account due to reduced export competitiveness
  3. Strengthens the rupee
  4. Reduces import dependence

Answer: Worsens current account due to reduced export competitiveness

Explanation: High inflation makes Indian exports less competitive internationally, widening the current account deficit.

22. Which agricultural reform is most likely to reduce food inflation in India?

  1. Increase in Minimum Support Price (MSP)
  2. Reduction in procurement by the government
  3. Liberalization of agricultural markets
  4. Subsidizing fertilizers

Answer: Liberalization of agricultural markets

Explanation: Liberalizing agricultural markets improves supply efficiency and reduces price volatility, curbing food inflation.

23. Why is the Consumer Price Index (CPI) considered more relevant than the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) for inflation targeting in India?

  1. CPI reflects producer prices
  2. CPI measures retail prices affecting households directly
  3. WPI is more volatile
  4. CPI is easier to calculate

Answer: CPI measures retail prices affecting households directly

Explanation: CPI captures the price changes faced by consumers, making it more aligned with household welfare and policy objectives.

24. What is the primary mechanism through which the RBI's repo rate influences inflation?

  1. Directly controls money supply
  2. Influences borrowing costs and aggregate demand
  3. Regulates foreign exchange reserves
  4. Adjusts fiscal spending

Answer: Influences borrowing costs and aggregate demand

Explanation: A higher repo rate reduces borrowing costs for banks, lowering aggregate demand and inflationary pressures.

25. How does high inflation disproportionately affect social indicators in India?

  1. Reduces fiscal deficit
  2. Increases unemployment in the formal sector
  3. Worsens income inequality and erodes purchasing power
  4. Improves rural infrastructure

Answer: Worsens income inequality and erodes purchasing power

Explanation: Inflation reduces real incomes, hits the poor harder (due to higher food weight in CPI), and exacerbates inequality.
