Budget Fiscal MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Budget Fiscal questions to revise the UPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 700 questions.

1. Poverty line in India is determined based on:

  1. Income only
  2. Calorie intake / consumption expenditure
  3. Asset ownership
  4. Employment status

Answer: Calorie intake / consumption expenditure

Explanation: India's poverty line is primarily based on minimum required calorie intake (2,400 kcal/day rural; 2,100 kcal/day urban) and the expenditure needed to achieve it. The Tendulkar Committee revised this methodology.

2. The 'Laffer Curve' illustrates the relationship between:

  1. Tax rate and tax revenue — showing that revenue peaks at an optimal tax rate
  2. Inflation and unemployment (Phillips Curve)
  3. Money supply and interest rates (LM Curve)
  4. Investment and income (IS Curve)

Answer: Tax rate and tax revenue — showing that revenue peaks at an optimal tax rate

Explanation: The Laffer Curve shows that both 0% and 100% tax rates result in zero revenue. Tax revenue peaks at some optimal rate. It is used to argue for tax cuts to increase revenue.

3. The 'Tobin Tax' is proposed for:

  1. Taxing corporate profits
  2. Small tax on international financial transactions to reduce speculation
  3. Taxing carbon emissions
  4. Wealth tax on billionaires

Answer: Small tax on international financial transactions to reduce speculation

Explanation: The Tobin Tax (proposed by James Tobin, 1972) is a small tax on international currency transactions to reduce speculative short-term financial flows ('hot money') without impeding long-term investment.

4. According to Keynes, during recession the government should:

  1. Reduce spending and raise taxes (austerity)
  2. Increase government spending and reduce taxes (expansionary fiscal policy)
  3. Reduce money supply
  4. Increase interest rates

Answer: Increase government spending and reduce taxes (expansionary fiscal policy)

Explanation: Keynesian economics recommends expansionary fiscal policy during recession — increase government spending (even deficit spending) and reduce taxes to stimulate aggregate demand and employment.

5. India's Union Budget is presented by the:

  1. Finance Minister of India in Parliament (Lok Sabha) — normally on February 1 since 2017; first Indian Union Budget: 1860 (James Wilson)
  2. Prime Minister
  3. RBI Governor
  4. President of India

Answer: Finance Minister of India in Parliament (Lok Sabha) — normally on February 1 since 2017; first Indian Union Budget: 1860 (James Wilson)

Explanation: Union Budget: presented by Finance Minister in Parliament (Lok Sabha) on February 1 (changed from last day of February in 2017, by Finance Minister Arun Jaitley). Consists of Revenue Budget (tax/non-tax income + current expenditure) and Capital Budget (assets/liabilities). First Budget: James Wilson (1860) under British. P. Chidambaram presented most Budgets (9 times).

6. Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented in India on:

  1. July 1, 2017 — replaced multiple central and state taxes; 101st Constitutional Amendment; 'One Nation One Tax'
  2. July 1, 2015
  3. January 1, 2017
  4. April 1, 2018

Answer: July 1, 2017 — replaced multiple central and state taxes; 101st Constitutional Amendment; 'One Nation One Tax'

Explanation: GST launched on 1 July 2017 under the 101st Constitutional Amendment (2016), replacing central excise, service tax, VAT, CST, octroi and other state taxes. Since 22 September 2025 its main rates are 5% and 18%, with 40% for luxury and sin goods (earlier 5, 12, 18 and 28%).

7. The Economic Survey of India is published by:

  1. Ministry of Finance (Economic Division) — usually released the day before the Union Budget; provides comprehensive analysis of Indian economy
  2. RBI
  3. NITI Aayog
  4. Finance Commission

Answer: Ministry of Finance (Economic Division) — usually released the day before the Union Budget; provides comprehensive analysis of Indian economy

Explanation: Economic Survey: prepared by the Economic Division of India's Ministry of Finance (typically under the Chief Economic Adviser to the Government). Released one day before the Union Budget. Provides: macroeconomic overview, sector-wise analysis, fiscal data, and policy recommendations. Since 2017, it has been published in two volumes.

8. The GST Council is chaired by:

  1. Union Finance Minister of India — GST Council has Finance Ministers of all States as members; decisions by 3/4th majority (Centre has 1/3rd vote, States collectively 2/3rd)
  2. Prime Minister
  3. RBI Governor
  4. President of India

Answer: Union Finance Minister of India — GST Council has Finance Ministers of all States as members; decisions by 3/4th majority (Centre has 1/3rd vote, States collectively 2/3rd)

Explanation: GST Council (Article 279A): joint body of Centre and States. Chaired by Union FM. Members: State FMs/Finance Deputies. Voting: Centre's votes = 1/3rd, States' votes combined = 2/3rd. Decision requires 3/4th majority. Makes recommendations on: GST rates, exemptions, rules, cross-border supply. Any decision requires broad consensus.

9. The PM-KISAN (Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi) scheme provides:

  1. Rs 6,000 per year (Rs 2,000 per 4-month installment) directly to farmer families' bank accounts — covers all land-holding farmers; launched 2018
  2. Free seeds
  3. MSP guarantee
  4. Crop insurance free

Answer: Rs 6,000 per year (Rs 2,000 per 4-month installment) directly to farmer families' bank accounts — covers all land-holding farmers; launched 2018

Explanation: PM-KISAN (December 2018): income support of Rs 6,000/year in 3 installments of Rs 2,000 each to all farmer families (initially small/marginal, then extended to all landholding farmers). Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) to Aadhaar-linked bank accounts. About 9 crore+ beneficiaries. Annual expenditure: ~Rs 60,000 crore.

10. The term 'externality' in economics refers to:

  1. Cost or benefit that falls on a third party not involved in the transaction — negative externality (e.g., pollution from factory); positive externality (e.g., education benefits society)
  2. Foreign trade impact
  3. Government regulation effect
  4. Market competition effect

Answer: Cost or benefit that falls on a third party not involved in the transaction — negative externality (e.g., pollution from factory); positive externality (e.g., education benefits society)

Explanation: Externalities: spillover effects on uninvolved parties. Negative: factory pollutes river (costs borne by community, not factory). Positive: education (benefits society beyond individual). Externalities cause market failure (market produces too much pollution, too little education). Government response: taxes (Pigouvian tax on negative), subsidies (positive), regulation.

11. Special Economic Zones (SEZs) in India offer:

  1. Tax concessions, duty-free imports, simplified regulations to attract export-oriented industries; first SEZ: Kandla (1965, now called KASEZ); SEZ Act 2005
  2. Only tax exemptions
  3. Only land subsidies
  4. Access to government funds

Answer: Tax concessions, duty-free imports, simplified regulations to attract export-oriented industries; first SEZ: Kandla (1965, now called KASEZ); SEZ Act 2005

Explanation: SEZs: designated areas with different economic regulations to promote exports and attract investment. Benefits: duty-free import of capital goods/raw materials, income tax exemptions (sunset clauses), 100% FDI allowed, simplified labor/environmental regulations. India's first SEZ: Kandla (1965, then Asia's first export processing zone). SEZ Act 2005: rapid expansion. India has 200+ SEZs.

12. The Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) was launched to:

  1. Incentivize states to increase investment in agriculture — 11th Plan scheme with 100% Central funding for states that increased agriculture budget share; restructured in 2017 as RAFTAAR
  2. Provide crop insurance
  3. Fix MSP
  4. Export agricultural produce

Answer: Incentivize states to increase investment in agriculture — 11th Plan scheme with 100% Central funding for states that increased agriculture budget share; restructured in 2017 as RAFTAAR

Explanation: RKVY (Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana, 2007): launched under 11th Five Year Plan. States qualifying (those increasing agriculture budget share above 3-year moving average) got 100% central grants for agriculture projects. States had flexibility. Restructured in 2017 as RKVY-RAFTAAR (Remunerative Approaches for Agriculture and Allied sector Rejuvenation).

13. The National Sample Survey (NSS) / PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey) provides data on:

  1. Employment, unemployment, wages, living standards, consumer expenditure in India — PLFS replaced NSS on employment from 2017; annual survey with quarterly urban data
  2. Industrial production
  3. GDP only
  4. Agricultural output

Answer: Employment, unemployment, wages, living standards, consumer expenditure in India — PLFS replaced NSS on employment from 2017; annual survey with quarterly urban data

Explanation: NSO conducts: PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey, 2017): quarterly urban data + annual rural+urban data on employment, wages, migration patterns. HCES (Household Consumer Expenditure Survey): spending patterns (last conducted 2022-23 after gap since 2011-12 — 2017-18 data was withheld due to controversy). These surveys inform poverty estimates and policy.

14. India's 'Startup India' initiative was launched to:

  1. Build a strong startup ecosystem with tax exemptions, simplified regulations, and funding support; launched January 2016 by PM Modi — 90-day company registration, 3-year tax holiday, Rs 10,000 crore Fund of Funds
  2. Replace MSME policy
  3. Export tech startups
  4. Only support IT startups

Answer: Build a strong startup ecosystem with tax exemptions, simplified regulations, and funding support; launched January 2016 by PM Modi — 90-day company registration, 3-year tax holiday, Rs 10,000 crore Fund of Funds

Explanation: Startup India (January 16, 2016): 19-point action plan. Features: simplify incorporation (24-hour process via Startup India Hub), 3-year income tax holiday, capital gains exemption for investments, Rs 10,000 crore Fund of Funds (via SIDBI), self-certification for 9 labor and environmental laws, faster patent examination. India now 3rd largest startup ecosystem globally.

15. The Union Budget is prepared by which Ministry?

  1. Ministry of Finance — specifically the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) and Department of Revenue coordinate the budget; Finance Minister presents to Parliament
  2. Prime Minister's Office
  3. Ministry of Commerce
  4. Planning Commission (abolished)

Answer: Ministry of Finance — specifically the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) and Department of Revenue coordinate the budget; Finance Minister presents to Parliament

Explanation: Union Budget preparation: Ministry of Finance (DEA — Budget Division) issues budget circular to all ministries for expenditure estimates; Department of Revenue handles tax proposals (Direct Tax: CBDT, Indirect Tax: CBIC). Finance Minister consults stakeholders, states, industry. Finance Bill (tax changes) + Appropriation Bill (expenditure) presented together.

16. The role of the Finance Commission of India is to:

  1. Recommend distribution of tax revenues between the Centre and States (vertical devolution) and among states (horizontal devolution) — constituted every 5 years under Article 280
  2. Set tax rates
  3. Prepare Union Budget
  4. Regulate banking sector

Answer: Recommend distribution of tax revenues between the Centre and States (vertical devolution) and among states (horizontal devolution) — constituted every 5 years under Article 280

Explanation: Finance Commission (FC): constitutional body (Article 280), appointed every 5 years by President. 15th FC (NK Singh, 2021-26): recommended 41% of divisible pool to states; performance grants; centrally sponsored scheme reforms. Also recommends grants-in-aid for states. Divisible pool: all central taxes except cess and surcharge.

17. The concept of 'Zero Based Budgeting' (ZBB) means:

  1. Every budget cycle, all expenditures must be freshly justified from a 'zero base' — no automatic continuation of last year's budget; each program must prove its value
  2. Starting from last year's budget
  3. Only capital expenditure is justified
  4. Budget starts from previous year's surplus

Answer: Every budget cycle, all expenditures must be freshly justified from a 'zero base' — no automatic continuation of last year's budget; each program must prove its value

Explanation: Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB, Peter Pyhrr, 1970): all spending must be justified for each new period, starting from zero. Opposite of incremental budgeting (adjusting last year's budget by a %). ZBB helps eliminate wasteful programs. India adopted modified ZBB approach in the late 1980s but reverted. Companies like Kraft-Heinz, Unilever use ZBB to cut costs.

18. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) follows which principle?

  1. Destination-based consumption tax — GST is levied where goods/services are consumed (destination), not where produced (origin); revenue goes to consuming state not producing state
  2. Origin-based principle
  3. Production-based principle
  4. Import-based principle

Answer: Destination-based consumption tax — GST is levied where goods/services are consumed (destination), not where produced (origin); revenue goes to consuming state not producing state

Explanation: GST is destination-based: taxes collected where goods/services are finally consumed. This is why Integrated GST (IGST) on interstate trade is given to the consuming state (not the producing state). Before GST: CST (Central Sales Tax) was origin-based — producing states got revenue. GST corrects this inter-state revenue distortion.

19. India's EXIM Policy (Foreign Trade Policy 2023-28) focuses on:

  1. Boosting merchandise exports to $2 trillion and services exports to $1 trillion by 2030 — streamlines export processes, introduces amnesty scheme for old cases, and promotes e-commerce exports
  2. Import substitution only
  3. Import promotion
  4. Reducing exports to control inflation

Answer: Boosting merchandise exports to $2 trillion and services exports to $1 trillion by 2030 — streamlines export processes, introduces amnesty scheme for old cases, and promotes e-commerce exports

Explanation: Foreign Trade Policy 2023-28 (released April 2023): targets $2 trillion merchandise + $1 trillion services exports by 2030. Features: Towns of Export Excellence (12 new cities), Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme, duty credit scrips streamlined, e-commerce exports promoted (single window, simplified procedures). SEIS and MEIS merged into RoDTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products).

20. The 'Laffer Curve' in economics shows:

  1. There is an optimal tax rate that maximizes tax revenue — too low rate = too little revenue; too high rate = economic disincentive, less activity, less revenue; both extremes give zero revenue
  2. Tax revenue always increases with higher tax rate
  3. Higher taxes always improve welfare
  4. Tax rates have no effect on economy

Answer: There is an optimal tax rate that maximizes tax revenue — too low rate = too little revenue; too high rate = economic disincentive, less activity, less revenue; both extremes give zero revenue

Explanation: Laffer Curve (Arthur Laffer, 1974): at 0% tax rate, revenue = 0; at 100% rate, nobody works, revenue = 0; somewhere in between is the revenue-maximizing rate. Basis for supply-side economics (Reagan's tax cuts). Critics argue the actual peak is above current rates for most countries. Empirical support: mixed.

21. The Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) is:

  1. Fraction of additional income spent on consumption (ΔC/ΔY); MPC + MPS (Marginal Propensity to Save) = 1; if MPC = 0.8, 80 paise of each extra rupee of income is consumed
  2. Total consumption divided by total income
  3. Savings rate
  4. Fixed cost ratio

Answer: Fraction of additional income spent on consumption (ΔC/ΔY); MPC + MPS (Marginal Propensity to Save) = 1; if MPC = 0.8, 80 paise of each extra rupee of income is consumed

Explanation: MPC = ΔC/ΔY (change in consumption / change in income). MPS = ΔS/ΔY. MPC + MPS = 1. In Keynesian economics, MPC determines the fiscal multiplier: k = 1/(1-MPC) = 1/MPS. Higher MPC → larger multiplier → larger impact of government spending on income. Poor people have higher MPC (spend more of extra income) than rich (who save more).

22. Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures:

  1. Percentage change in quantity demanded / Percentage change in price — elastic: |PED|>1 (luxury goods, many substitutes); inelastic: |PED|<1 (necessities, few substitutes like salt, insulin)
  2. Total demand
  3. Consumer income levels
  4. Production capacity

Answer: Percentage change in quantity demanded / Percentage change in price — elastic: |PED|>1 (luxury goods, many substitutes); inelastic: |PED|<1 (necessities, few substitutes like salt, insulin)

Explanation: PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP. Elastic (|PED|>1): consumer responds strongly to price changes — luxury goods (gold jewelry, cars). Inelastic (|PED|<1): quantity barely changes with price — necessities (salt, insulin, petrol). Unit elastic (|PED|=1): %ΔQd = %ΔP. Key for tax policy: taxing inelastic goods (petrol, tobacco) raises more revenue with less market distortion.

23. The Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN) scheme, earlier known as:

  1. Mid-Day Meal Scheme — provides free lunch to students in government/aided schools (Classes I-VIII) to improve enrollment, attendance, and nutrition; one of world's largest school feeding programs
  2. ICDS (Integrated Child Development Services, for 0-6 age group)
  3. Antyodaya Anna Yojana
  4. Food Security Act only

Answer: Mid-Day Meal Scheme — provides free lunch to students in government/aided schools (Classes I-VIII) to improve enrollment, attendance, and nutrition; one of world's largest school feeding programs

Explanation: PM POSHAN (renamed from Mid-Day Meal Scheme 2021): provides one hot meal per school day to 11.8 crore students in Classes I-VIII in government/government-aided schools. Benefits: attendance ↑ (incentive), dropout ↓, nutrition ↑, social equity (reduces caste discrimination around food). One of world's largest feeding programs. Annual budget: ~Rs 12,000 crore.

24. Fiscal Federalism in India involves:

  1. Division of taxing powers and expenditure responsibilities between Centre and States — Centre has more taxes (GST, income tax, customs), States have some exclusive taxes (stamp duty, excise on liquor)
  2. Only Centre controlling all finances
  3. States having complete fiscal autonomy
  4. No central government role in finance

Answer: Division of taxing powers and expenditure responsibilities between Centre and States — Centre has more taxes (GST, income tax, customs), States have some exclusive taxes (stamp duty, excise on liquor)

Explanation: Fiscal Federalism: distribution of financial powers. Centre: income tax, corporate tax, customs, GST (shared), cess/surcharge. States: stamp duty, excise on liquor, land revenue, profession tax, entry tax (now subsumed in GST). Shared: GST (CGST to Centre, SGST to State). Finance Commission determines revenue sharing. States depend significantly on central transfers (devolution + grants).

25. The Sansad TV (earlier Rajya Sabha TV + Lok Sabha TV merged) and its role in India's democracy is:

  1. Parliamentary broadcast channel — telecast proceedings of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, committees, and informational programs — merged in 2021 to form Sansad TV
  2. News channel
  3. Entertainment channel
  4. Regional language channel

Answer: Parliamentary broadcast channel — telecast proceedings of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, committees, and informational programs — merged in 2021 to form Sansad TV

Explanation: Sansad TV (2021, merger of Lok Sabha TV and Rajya Sabha TV): 24/7 parliamentary television. Functions: live telecast of both Houses' proceedings, select committee meetings, budget speech; educational content (legislation, governance, history). Free-to-air on DD Free Dish and cable. Promotes parliamentary transparency.
