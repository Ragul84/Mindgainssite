1. India is classified as a:

  1. Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)
  2. Pure socialist economy
  3. Pure capitalist economy
  4. Closed economy

Answer: Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)

Explanation: India's mixed economy (as in the Constitution's Directive Principles): public sector controls strategic industries (defense, rail, atomic energy), while private sector operates in most areas. Since 1991 reforms, India has moved significantly toward market economy with reduced government control.

2. Pension reforms in India were initiated with the establishment of:

  1. PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority, 2003) — regulates NPS, APY, and other pension schemes; statutory status 2014 under PFRDA Act
  2. LIC
  3. RBI
  4. SEBI

Answer: PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority, 2003) — regulates NPS, APY, and other pension schemes; statutory status 2014 under PFRDA Act

Explanation: PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority): interim PFRDA set up 2003 to manage NPS rollout for new central government employees. PFRDA Act 2013 (in force 2014) gave it statutory status. Regulates: NPS (National Pension System — for government and private sector), APY (Atal Pension Yojana — for unorganized sector). Fund managers: SBI, LIC, HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Kotak, UTI, Aditya Birla.

3. Privatization of Air India was completed in:

  1. January 2022 — Tata Sons (through Talace Pvt Ltd) acquired Air India for Rs 18,000 crore; ending government ownership since 1953 nationalization; Air India's homecoming to Tatas (original founder JRD Tata)
  2. 2019
  3. 2015
  4. 2010

Answer: January 2022 — Tata Sons (through Talace Pvt Ltd) acquired Air India for Rs 18,000 crore; ending government ownership since 1953 nationalization; Air India's homecoming to Tatas (original founder JRD Tata)

Explanation: Air India disinvestment: launched 2020, completed January 27, 2022. Tata Sons won the bid with enterprise value of Rs 18,000 crore (Rs 2,700 crore cash + Rs 15,300 crore debt assumption). Air India was founded by J.R.D. Tata (1932 as Tata Airlines) → nationalized 1953 → returned to Tatas after 69 years. Tata also owns Vistara, Air Asia India (merged into Air India).

4. The 'Big Bang' approach to economic reforms (as opposed to 'gradualism') involves:

  1. Slow, phased implementation of reforms
  2. Rapid, simultaneous implementation of multiple major reforms
  3. Only monetary reforms
  4. Only trade reforms

Answer: Rapid, simultaneous implementation of multiple major reforms

Explanation: Big Bang reforms: comprehensive, rapid liberalization across sectors simultaneously (Poland 1990, Russia early 1990s). Contrasts with China's and India's gradual approach. India's 1991 reforms were bold but not full 'big bang.'

5. The National Monetization Pipeline (NMP) was announced by:

  1. FM Nirmala Sitharaman in August 2021 — plan to monetize Rs 6 lakh crore of brownfield government infrastructure assets (roads, railways, power lines, gas pipelines, airports) over 4 years
  2. PM Modi in 2019
  3. Planning Commission in 2015
  4. NITI Aayog in 2016

Answer: FM Nirmala Sitharaman in August 2021 — plan to monetize Rs 6 lakh crore of brownfield government infrastructure assets (roads, railways, power lines, gas pipelines, airports) over 4 years

Explanation: NMP (National Monetization Pipeline, 2021-2025): aims to raise Rs 6 lakh crore by transferring operation of existing government assets (brownfield) to private sector for a fixed period (not selling assets). Assets: NHAI roads, railway stations/freight terminals, power lines (PGCIL), gas pipelines (GAIL), airports (AAI), ports. Asset monetization ≠ privatization.
