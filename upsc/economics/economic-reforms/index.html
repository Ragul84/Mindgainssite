Economic Reforms MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Economic Reforms questions to revise the UPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 699 questions.

1. India is classified as a:

  1. Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)
  2. Pure socialist economy
  3. Pure capitalist economy
  4. Closed economy

Answer: Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)

Explanation: India's mixed economy (as in the Constitution's Directive Principles): public sector controls strategic industries (defense, rail, atomic energy), while private sector operates in most areas. Since 1991 reforms, India has moved significantly toward market economy with reduced government control.

2. Pension reforms in India were initiated with the establishment of:

  1. PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority, 2003) — regulates NPS, APY, and other pension schemes; statutory status 2014 under PFRDA Act
  2. LIC
  3. RBI
  4. SEBI

Answer: PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority, 2003) — regulates NPS, APY, and other pension schemes; statutory status 2014 under PFRDA Act

Explanation: PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority): interim PFRDA set up 2003 to manage NPS rollout for new central government employees. PFRDA Act 2013 (in force 2014) gave it statutory status. Regulates: NPS (National Pension System — for government and private sector), APY (Atal Pension Yojana — for unorganized sector). Fund managers: SBI, LIC, HDFC, ICICI Prudential, Kotak, UTI, Aditya Birla.

3. Privatization of Air India was completed in:

  1. January 2022 — Tata Sons (through Talace Pvt Ltd) acquired Air India for Rs 18,000 crore; ending government ownership since 1953 nationalization; Air India's homecoming to Tatas (original founder JRD Tata)
  2. 2019
  3. 2015
  4. 2010

Answer: January 2022 — Tata Sons (through Talace Pvt Ltd) acquired Air India for Rs 18,000 crore; ending government ownership since 1953 nationalization; Air India's homecoming to Tatas (original founder JRD Tata)

Explanation: Air India disinvestment: launched 2020, completed January 27, 2022. Tata Sons won the bid with enterprise value of Rs 18,000 crore (Rs 2,700 crore cash + Rs 15,300 crore debt assumption). Air India was founded by J.R.D. Tata (1932 as Tata Airlines) → nationalized 1953 → returned to Tatas after 69 years. Tata also owns Vistara, Air Asia India (merged into Air India).

4. The 'Big Bang' approach to economic reforms (as opposed to 'gradualism') involves:

  1. Slow, phased implementation of reforms
  2. Rapid, simultaneous implementation of multiple major reforms
  3. Only monetary reforms
  4. Only trade reforms

Answer: Rapid, simultaneous implementation of multiple major reforms

Explanation: Big Bang reforms: comprehensive, rapid liberalization across sectors simultaneously (Poland 1990, Russia early 1990s). Contrasts with China's and India's gradual approach. India's 1991 reforms were bold but not full 'big bang.'

5. The National Monetization Pipeline (NMP) was announced by:

  1. FM Nirmala Sitharaman in August 2021 — plan to monetize Rs 6 lakh crore of brownfield government infrastructure assets (roads, railways, power lines, gas pipelines, airports) over 4 years
  2. PM Modi in 2019
  3. Planning Commission in 2015
  4. NITI Aayog in 2016

Answer: FM Nirmala Sitharaman in August 2021 — plan to monetize Rs 6 lakh crore of brownfield government infrastructure assets (roads, railways, power lines, gas pipelines, airports) over 4 years

Explanation: NMP (National Monetization Pipeline, 2021-2025): aims to raise Rs 6 lakh crore by transferring operation of existing government assets (brownfield) to private sector for a fixed period (not selling assets). Assets: NHAI roads, railway stations/freight terminals, power lines (PGCIL), gas pipelines (GAIL), airports (AAI), ports. Asset monetization ≠ privatization.

6. The Model Agricultural Produce Marketing Committee (APMC) Act, 2003, introduced by the central government, primarily aimed to:

  1. Nationalize all agricultural markets
  2. Allow private sector participation in agricultural marketing
  3. Abolish minimum support prices for all crops
  4. Impose quantitative restrictions on agricultural exports

Answer: Allow private sector participation in agricultural marketing

Explanation: The Model APMC Act 2003 sought to allow private markets, direct purchases, and contract farming, breaking the monopoly of state APMCs.

7. Which of the following is NOT a quantitative tool of monetary policy used by the Reserve Bank of India?

  1. Bank Rate
  2. Open Market Operations
  3. Credit Authorization Scheme
  4. Marginal Standing Facility

Answer: Credit Authorization Scheme

Explanation: Credit Authorization Scheme is a qualitative (selective) credit control tool, whereas Bank Rate, OMO, and MSF are quantitative instruments.

8. The difference between the fiscal deficit and the interest payments of the government is known as:

  1. Revenue deficit
  2. Primary deficit
  3. Capital deficit
  4. Budgetary deficit

Answer: Primary deficit

Explanation: Primary deficit equals fiscal deficit minus interest payments, indicating current year's borrowing net of past debt obligations.

9. In India, the headline inflation rate that serves as the nominal anchor for monetary policy is currently measured by:

  1. Wholesale Price Index (WPI)
  2. Consumer Price Index (CPI)
  3. GDP Deflator
  4. Producer Price Index (PPI)

Answer: Consumer Price Index (CPI)

Explanation: Since 2014, the RBI uses CPI (Combined) as the inflation anchor under flexible inflation targeting, replacing WPI.

10. A persistent surplus in the current account of India's balance of payments indicates that:

  1. The country is a net borrower from the rest of the world
  2. Exports of goods, services, and transfers exceed imports
  3. The country has a deficit in the capital account
  4. The rupee is depreciating against the dollar

Answer: Exports of goods, services, and transfers exceed imports

Explanation: A current account surplus means exports of goods, services, and net transfers are greater than imports of the same.

11. Consider the following effects of rupee depreciation against the US dollar: 1. Imports become costlier 2. Exports become more competitive 3. Foreign currency-denominated debt becomes cheaper to repay 4. Inflation may rise due to imported inflation. Which of the above are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 1, 2, and 4 only
  3. 1, 2, and 3 only
  4. 1, 2, 3, and 4

Answer: 1, 2, and 4 only

Explanation: Depreciation makes imports costlier, boosts export competitiveness, and triggers imported inflation; however, foreign debt becomes costlier, not cheaper.

12. 'Sterilized intervention' by a central bank in the foreign exchange market refers to:

  1. Buying foreign exchange without affecting domestic money supply
  2. Selling foreign exchange to prevent currency appreciation
  3. Offsetting the monetary impact of forex operations on domestic money supply
  4. Restricting cross-border capital flows

Answer: Offsetting the monetary impact of forex operations on domestic money supply

Explanation: Sterilization means the central bank neutralizes the impact of forex inflows/outflows on domestic money supply through offsetting open market operations.

13. The New Industrial Policy of 1991, which marked the beginning of economic reforms in India, was based on which of the following approaches?

  1. Import substitution and tight licensing
  2. Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization
  3. Complete nationalization of industries
  4. Centrally planned economy

Answer: Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization

Explanation: The 1991 reforms under PM Narasimha Rao and FM Manmohan Singh adopted the LPG framework to shift from a controlled to a market-oriented economy.

14. The strategic sale of public sector enterprises in India, as opposed to minority disinvestment, aims to:

  1. Retain government control while raising revenue
  2. Transfer management control to private entities and raise substantial revenue
  3. Issue employee stock options only
  4. Convert public debt into equity

Answer: Transfer management control to private entities and raise substantial revenue

Explanation: Strategic disinvestment involves selling a substantial stake (often over 50%), resulting in transfer of management control to the private buyer.

15. Despite high economic growth after the 1991 economic reforms, the gap between the rich and poor in India widened significantly. This phenomenon is referred to as:

  1. Trickle-down effect
  2. Inclusive growth
  3. Growth paradox or rising inequality
  4. Vicious circle of poverty

Answer: Growth paradox or rising inequality

Explanation: Post-1991 India experienced a 'growth paradox' where high GDP growth did not translate proportionally into poverty reduction, leading to widening inequality.

16. In the context of India's 1991 Economic Reforms, which of the following best describes the primary objective of 'Devaluation' of the Rupee?

  1. To increase the cost of imports to discourage luxury consumption
  2. To make Indian exports more competitive in the global market
  3. To reduce the domestic inflation rate by limiting imports
  4. To increase the value of foreign exchange reserves through direct purchase

Answer: To make Indian exports more competitive in the global market

Explanation: Devaluation makes domestic goods cheaper for foreign buyers, thereby boosting exports and improving the current account balance.

17. Which of the following is a direct consequence of 'Sterilized Intervention' conducted by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)?

  1. A decrease in the overall money supply to curb inflation
  2. The neutralization of the impact of foreign exchange inflows on domestic liquidity
  3. An automatic increase in the value of the Rupee against the Dollar
  4. A mandatory increase in the Cash Reserve Ratio for commercial banks

Answer: The neutralization of the impact of foreign exchange inflows on domestic liquidity

Explanation: Sterilization involves offsetting the increase in money supply caused by forex purchases by selling government securities.

18. Regarding the 'Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act', what is the primary conceptual goal of targeting a specific fiscal deficit limit?

  1. To ensure that the government spends more on capital assets than revenue expenditure
  2. To eliminate the need for external borrowing from the IMF
  3. To ensure long-term macroeconomic stability by limiting government borrowing
  4. To mandate a balanced budget where expenditure exactly equals revenue

Answer: To ensure long-term macroeconomic stability by limiting government borrowing

Explanation: The FRBM Act aims to institutionalize financial discipline to ensure sustainability of public debt and stability.

19. If the RBI decides to increase the 'Weighted Average Call Rate' through open market operations, what is the most likely impact on the economy?

  1. Cost of short-term borrowing for banks increases, potentially raising lending rates
  2. The Rupee will immediately depreciate against the US Dollar
  3. Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) will decrease due to higher costs
  4. The government's fiscal deficit will automatically widen

Answer: Cost of short-term borrowing for banks increases, potentially raising lending rates

Explanation: Higher call rates increase the cost of inter-bank borrowing, which typically trickles down to higher lending rates for consumers.

20. Which of the following statements correctly describes the impact of a 'depreciating Rupee' on India's Balance of Payments?

  1. It leads to a decrease in the value of remittances sent by NRIs
  2. It generally makes imports cheaper, improving the trade balance
  3. It may improve the trade balance by making exports cheaper and imports costlier
  4. It leads to an automatic decrease in the Capital Account deficit

Answer: It may improve the trade balance by making exports cheaper and imports costlier

Explanation: Depreciation makes exports more attractive to foreigners and imports more expensive for locals, potentially reducing the trade deficit.

21. In the context of agricultural reforms, 'Contract Farming' is primarily intended to address which of the following systemic issues?

  1. The lack of land ownership among marginal farmers
  2. Price volatility and lack of assured markets for farmers
  3. The over-reliance on chemical fertilizers and pesticides
  4. The inefficiency of the irrigation systems in rain-fed areas

Answer: Price volatility and lack of assured markets for farmers

Explanation: Contract farming provides price certainty and market access by linking farmers directly with buyers before sowing.

22. Which of the following is a characteristic of 'Inflation Targeting' as adopted by the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) of India?

  1. Fixing the exchange rate of the Rupee against a basket of currencies
  2. Using the Repo Rate as the primary tool to keep inflation within a specified range
  3. Setting a fixed price for essential commodities to prevent price hikes
  4. Prioritizing GDP growth over price stability during economic downturns

Answer: Using the Repo Rate as the primary tool to keep inflation within a specified range

Explanation: Inflation targeting focuses on maintaining CPI inflation within a target band using the repo rate as the main instrument.

23. What is the conceptual difference between 'Primary Deficit' and 'Fiscal Deficit'?

  1. Primary deficit excludes the interest payments on previous borrowings
  2. Fiscal deficit excludes the revenue deficit from the total
  3. Primary deficit includes only the external debt payments
  4. Fiscal deficit represents only the borrowing from the RBI

Answer: Primary deficit excludes the interest payments on previous borrowings

Explanation: Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit minus Interest Payments, showing the borrowing needs excluding past debt obligations.

24. Which of the following best explains the 'Crowding Out' effect in the context of government borrowing?

  1. Increased government spending leading to a surge in private investment
  2. High government borrowing increasing interest rates and reducing private investment
  3. Foreign investors exiting the market due to high domestic inflation
  4. The replacement of manual labor by automation in the industrial sector

Answer: High government borrowing increasing interest rates and reducing private investment

Explanation: When government borrows heavily, it competes for limited loanable funds, driving up interest rates and deterring private investment.

25. Under the 'Goods and Services Tax (GST)' reform, the 'Input Tax Credit' mechanism is designed to prevent which of the following?

  1. The accumulation of trade deficits with trading partners
  2. The cascading effect of taxes (tax on tax)
  3. The increase in the overall price of luxury goods
  4. The shift from formal to informal economic sectors

Answer: The cascading effect of taxes (tax on tax)

Explanation: Input Tax Credit allows sellers to claim credit for taxes paid on inputs, eliminating the cascading effect of multiple taxes.
