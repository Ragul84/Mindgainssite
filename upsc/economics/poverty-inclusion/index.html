Poverty Inclusion MCQs with answers

1. The Atal Pension Yojana (APY) targets:

  1. Unorganized sector workers — provides guaranteed pension of Rs 1,000-5,000/month at age 60; administered by PFRDA; co-contribution by government for eligible subscribers
  2. Government employees
  3. Corporate sector workers
  4. Farmers only

Answer: Unorganized sector workers — provides guaranteed pension of Rs 1,000-5,000/month at age 60; administered by PFRDA; co-contribution by government for eligible subscribers

Explanation: APY (2015, replaced Swavalamban scheme): for unorganized sector workers. Subscribers aged 18-40 contribute monthly; at 60, receive guaranteed pension of Rs 1,000/2,000/3,000/4,000/5,000 per month. Government (co-contribution of 50% of contribution or Rs 1,000/year, whichever lower) for eligible subscribers for 5 years. Administered by PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority).

2. The UDAN (Ude Desh Ka Aam Nagrik) scheme is related to:

  1. Regional Air Connectivity Scheme — makes air travel affordable on unserved/underserved routes; caps airfare for 1-hour flight; launched 2016
  2. Road connectivity
  3. Rail connectivity
  4. Port development

Answer: Regional Air Connectivity Scheme — makes air travel affordable on unserved/underserved routes; caps airfare for 1-hour flight; launched 2016

Explanation: UDAN (Ude Desh Ka Aam Nagrik — Let the Common Citizen Fly) 2016: provides affordable air connectivity to Tier-2/Tier-3 cities. Viability Gap Funding (VGF) provided to airlines operating on unserved routes. Capped airfare: Rs 2,500 for 1-hour flight. Revived and developed many regional airports. Over 400 routes operationalized.

3. The Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY scheme provides:

  1. Health insurance of Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalization to ~10 crore poor/vulnerable families (50 crore beneficiaries) — launched 2018
  2. Primary health care only
  3. Cash transfer to sick persons
  4. Free medicines only

Answer: Health insurance of Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalization to ~10 crore poor/vulnerable families (50 crore beneficiaries) — launched 2018

Explanation: Ayushman Bharat - Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY, 2018): world's largest health assurance scheme. Covers Rs 5 lakh per family per year for hospitalization. Target: 10.74 crore poor/vulnerable families (50 crore beneficiaries) — bottom 40% of population. Cashless treatment at empanelled private and public hospitals. SECC 2011 data used for beneficiary identification.

4. The Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA) was launched to:

  1. Universalize elementary education (Classes 1-8) — free and compulsory education for all children 6-14 years; merged into Samagra Shiksha in 2018
  2. Provide higher education
  3. Provide skill training
  4. Build universities

Answer: Universalize elementary education (Classes 1-8) — free and compulsory education for all children 6-14 years; merged into Samagra Shiksha in 2018

Explanation: SSA (2001): flagship program for universalizing elementary education (RTE Act 2009 gave it constitutional backing). Established/upgraded schools in unserved habitations, recruited teachers, built classrooms. Mid-Day Meal Scheme complemented SSA. In 2018, SSA, RMSA (secondary), and Teacher Education merged into Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan.

5. The National Pension System (NPS) replaced the Old Pension Scheme (OPS) for Central Government employees from:

  1. January 1, 2004 (for employees joining on/after that date) — contributory scheme where employee contributes 10% and government 14% of basic+DA; managed by PFRDA
  2. 2010
  3. 1999
  4. 2008

Answer: January 1, 2004 (for employees joining on/after that date) — contributory scheme where employee contributes 10% and government 14% of basic+DA; managed by PFRDA

Explanation: NPS (National Pension System, 2004): replaced defined benefit OPS for central government employees joining from January 1, 2004 onwards. Contributory (employee: 10%, employer: 14% for central govt). Market-linked returns. Managed by PFRDA. NPS extended to all citizens in 2009 (Swavalamban/NPS-Lite for unorganized sector).

6. Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY) provides:

  1. Accidental death/disability insurance cover of Rs 2 lakh at Rs 20 premium per year — for bank account holders aged 18-70; launched 2015
  2. Health insurance
  3. Life insurance (non-accident)
  4. Pension

Answer: Accidental death/disability insurance cover of Rs 2 lakh at Rs 20 premium per year — for bank account holders aged 18-70; launched 2015

Explanation: PMSBY (2015): accidental death/permanent total disability: Rs 2 lakh; permanent partial disability: Rs 1 lakh. Annual premium: just Rs 20 (for 18-70 year olds with bank accounts). Auto-debit from bank account. Covers road accidents, drowning, accidental falls etc. Part of Jan Suraksha schemes. Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY): life insurance Rs 2 lakh, Rs 436/year.

7. The 'poverty trap' is a situation where:

  1. Poor people cannot escape poverty because they lack resources to invest in health, education, or productive assets — low income → low savings → low investment → low income (vicious cycle)
  2. Only high taxation causes poverty
  3. Poverty is self-fulfilling prophecy due to geography
  4. Lack of democracy causes poverty

Answer: Poor people cannot escape poverty because they lack resources to invest in health, education, or productive assets — low income → low savings → low investment → low income (vicious cycle)

Explanation: Poverty Trap (Jeffrey Sachs): poor people/countries stuck in poverty because: too poor to save/invest → capital doesn't accumulate → productivity stays low → income stays low. Solutions: 'Big Push' (massive external aid to break the cycle), conditional cash transfers, microfinance. Counter-view (Daron Acemoglu, James Robinson): institutions matter more than external shocks.

8. The concept of 'disguised unemployment' is different from 'seasonal unemployment' in that:

  1. Disguised unemployment: excess labor with zero marginal product (mainly in agriculture, year-round). Seasonal unemployment: workers unemployed during off-season (only part of year)
  2. They are the same
  3. Seasonal is worse than disguised
  4. Disguised unemployment only in cities

Answer: Disguised unemployment: excess labor with zero marginal product (mainly in agriculture, year-round). Seasonal unemployment: workers unemployed during off-season (only part of year)

Explanation: Disguised Unemployment (structural): workers appear employed but contribute zero output (farm has 5 workers when 3 suffice). Zero marginal product. Removing 2 workers doesn't reduce output. Seasonal Unemployment: workers unemployed part of year (farmers during off-season, tourism workers in lean season). Cyclic unemployment: due to recession. Frictional: between jobs.

9. The Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) scheme provides:

  1. Package of 6 services to children 0-6 years and pregnant/lactating mothers: supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-up, referral services, pre-school education, nutrition & health education
  2. Only food to children
  3. Only education
  4. Only vaccination

Answer: Package of 6 services to children 0-6 years and pregnant/lactating mothers: supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-up, referral services, pre-school education, nutrition & health education

Explanation: ICDS (1975): world's largest and most comprehensive early childhood development program. 6 services through Anganwadi Centres: (1) Supplementary nutrition, (2) Immunization, (3) Health check-up, (4) Referral services, (5) Pre-school non-formal education (3-6 years), (6) Nutrition & health education (for women). 1.4 million Anganwadis, 14 crore beneficiaries.

10. The index 'Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)' measures poverty using:

  1. Three dimensions: health (nutrition, child mortality), education (years of schooling, school attendance), and living standards (cooking fuel, sanitation, water, electricity, housing, assets) — UNDP/OPHI
  2. Only income below $1.90/day
  3. Only calorie intake
  4. Only income inequality

Answer: Three dimensions: health (nutrition, child mortality), education (years of schooling, school attendance), and living standards (cooking fuel, sanitation, water, electricity, housing, assets) — UNDP/OPHI

Explanation: MPI (UNDP and OPHI — Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative): multidimensional poverty measure using 10 indicators across 3 dimensions. A household is 'deprived' in each indicator; 'multidimensionally poor' if deprived in >33% of weighted indicators. India's MPI: 415 million exited poverty 2005-06 to 2019-21 (massive reduction). India publishes National MPI annually.

11. India's 'One Nation One Ration Card' (ONORC) scheme allows:

  1. PDS (ration card) beneficiaries to claim their subsidized food entitlement from any Fair Price Shop in India — especially beneficial for migrant workers who move to different states for work
  2. Only local ration card use
  3. Multiple ration cards
  4. Free food without card

Answer: PDS (ration card) beneficiaries to claim their subsidized food entitlement from any Fair Price Shop in India — especially beneficial for migrant workers who move to different states for work

Explanation: ONORC (2019-2021, nationwide rollout): using Aadhaar-linked biometric authentication at FPS (Fair Price Shop), beneficiary can claim PDS quota from any FPS in India. Critical for: migrant workers (can claim entitlement at destination state), seasonal migrants. All 36 states/UTs joined ONORC by 2021. 80+ crore beneficiaries. Managed through Integrated Management of PDS (IM-PDS) portal.

12. The Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS) provides:

  1. Rs 945 crore seed funding to early-stage startups through incubators — for proof of concept, prototype development, trials, market entry; Max Rs 20 lakh grant or Rs 50 lakh soft loan per startup
  2. Venture capital for large startups
  3. Government equity in startups
  4. Export subsidies for startups

Answer: Rs 945 crore seed funding to early-stage startups through incubators — for proof of concept, prototype development, trials, market entry; Max Rs 20 lakh grant or Rs 50 lakh soft loan per startup

Explanation: SISFS (2021-25): Rs 945 crore over 4 years. Grants through selected incubators: Rs 20 lakh for proof of concept/validation (PoC, prototype, trials); Rs 50 lakh as soft loan for commercialization/market entry. Incubators apply to DPIIT; approved incubators select startups. Fills the gap between angel investment and formal VC: 'seed funding' stage.

13. Frictional unemployment occurs when:

  1. The economy is in recession
  2. Workers are between jobs or searching for better jobs
  3. Jobs exist but skills don't match
  4. Seasonal work ends

Answer: Workers are between jobs or searching for better jobs

Explanation: Frictional unemployment: temporary unemployment while workers transition between jobs or search for better ones. It's natural and exists even in a healthy economy (part of 'natural rate of unemployment').

14. The Soil Health Card scheme launched in 2015 aims to:

  1. Subsidize fertilizers
  2. Provide farmers with soil nutrient status and fertilizer recommendations
  3. Promote organic certification
  4. Map agricultural land via satellite

Answer: Provide farmers with soil nutrient status and fertilizer recommendations

Explanation: Soil Health Card: issued every 2 years to farmers; provides information on 12 parameters (N, P, K, pH, micronutrients) with crop-wise recommendations to optimize fertilizer use.

15. Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme provides short-term credit to farmers for:

  1. Land purchase only
  2. Purchase of farm machinery only
  3. Crop cultivation, post-harvest expenses, maintenance, and allied activities
  4. Long-term investment in irrigation

Answer: Crop cultivation, post-harvest expenses, maintenance, and allied activities

Explanation: KCC (1998, NABARD): provides revolving credit for crop cultivation, post-harvest expenses, allied activities (fisheries, animal husbandry since 2018-19), and maintenance needs at subsidized rates.

16. Which index measures the inequality of income or wealth distribution in a country?

  1. Human Development Index (HDI)
  2. Gini Coefficient
  3. Consumer Price Index (CPI)
  4. Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)

Answer: Gini Coefficient

Explanation: Gini Coefficient (Corrado Gini, 1912): 0 = perfect equality; 1 = maximum inequality. Derived from Lorenz Curve. India's Gini ~0.36 (income). Used alongside HDI to measure inclusive development.

17. The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) is a scheme for:

  1. Crop insurance — provides financial support to farmers suffering crop loss due to natural calamities, pests, or disease; launched 2016, low premium (2% for kharif, 1.5% rabi)
  2. Free seeds to farmers
  3. Minimum support price guarantee
  4. Agricultural credit

Answer: Crop insurance — provides financial support to farmers suffering crop loss due to natural calamities, pests, or disease; launched 2016, low premium (2% for kharif, 1.5% rabi)

Explanation: PMFBY (February 2016): replaced NAIS and MNAIS. Features: uniform premium of 2% for kharif crops, 1.5% for rabi crops, 5% for commercial/horticultural crops; remaining premium paid by Centre and State equally. Uses satellite, remote sensing, and smartphone apps for rapid loss assessment. Covers pre-sowing to post-harvest losses.

18. India's Urban Employment Guarantee Scheme (UGEGS) for urban poor was introduced in:

  1. 2020 during COVID-19 pandemic as 'PM Garib Kalyan Rojgar Abhiyan' for migrant workers — some states like Rajasthan have their own urban employment guarantee schemes
  2. 2005 (same time as MGNREGA)
  3. 2010
  4. 2015

Answer: 2020 during COVID-19 pandemic as 'PM Garib Kalyan Rojgar Abhiyan' for migrant workers — some states like Rajasthan have their own urban employment guarantee schemes

Explanation: Urban employment guarantee: the PM Garib Kalyan Rojgar Abhiyan (June 2020) provided 125 days of work to returning migrant workers in 116 districts. Rajasthan's Indira Gandhi Shahri Rozgar Guarantee Yojana (2022): 100 days urban employment. Kerala has similar scheme. No national-level urban employment guarantee act yet.

19. POSHAN 2.0 (2021) merged which earlier schemes?

  1. ICDS and MGNREGA
  2. POSHAN Abhiyaan and Supplementary Nutrition Programme and other nutrition schemes
  3. NHM and ICDS
  4. Anganwadi and PM POSHAN

Answer: POSHAN Abhiyaan and Supplementary Nutrition Programme and other nutrition schemes

Explanation: POSHAN 2.0: merged POSHAN Abhiyaan, ICDS (Supplementary Nutrition), National Creche Scheme, Kishori Shakti Yojana into an integrated nutrition support programme under Mission Saksham Anganwadi.

20. PM POSHAN scheme (renamed from Mid-Day Meal in 2021) covers students up to:

  1. Class V
  2. Class VIII
  3. Class X
  4. Class XII

Answer: Class VIII

Explanation: PM POSHAN: covers students from pre-primary (Balvatika) to Class VIII in government and government-aided schools. ~11.8 crore students across ~11.2 lakh schools.

21. Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY (Jan Arogya Yojana) provides health cover of how much per family per year?

  1. Rs 1 lakh
  2. Rs 3 lakh
  3. Rs 5 lakh
  4. Rs 10 lakh

Answer: Rs 5 lakh

Explanation: PM-JAY (launched September 23, 2018): Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care hospitalization. World's largest government health insurance scheme. ~55 crore beneficiaries (50 crore poor).

22. The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) launched in 2016 replaced which earlier crop insurance schemes?

  1. RKVY and NAIS
  2. NAIS (National Agricultural Insurance Scheme) and MNAIS
  3. PM-KISAN and RKVY
  4. PMRY and NAIS

Answer: NAIS (National Agricultural Insurance Scheme) and MNAIS

Explanation: PMFBY (January 2016): replaced NAIS (1999) and MNAIS (2010). Premium rates: Kharif 2%, Rabi 1.5%, commercial/horticultural crops 5%. Actuarial premium difference borne by Centre and State.

23. Which committee recommended the rationalization of centrally sponsored schemes (CSS) in India?

  1. Rangarajan Committee
  2. Kelkar Committee
  3. B.K. Chaturvedi Committee
  4. Shivraj Patil Committee

Answer: B.K. Chaturvedi Committee

Explanation: B.K. Chaturvedi Committee (2011): recommended rationalizing and restructuring CSS. Later, Sub-Group of CMs under CM Shivraj Singh Chouhan (2015) recommended reducing CSS from 66 to 28 for more flexibility.

24. What is the primary objective of monetary policy in the context of poverty and inclusion in India?

  1. Price stability and economic growth
  2. Fiscal deficit reduction
  3. Balance of trade improvement
  4. Exchange rate management

Answer: Price stability and economic growth

Explanation: Monetary policy aims to achieve price stability and promote economic growth, which can help reduce poverty.

25. Which of the following fiscal indicators is most closely related to poverty reduction in India?

  1. Fiscal deficit as a percentage of GDP
  2. Revenue deficit
  3. Capital expenditure on social sectors
  4. Total government expenditure

Answer: Capital expenditure on social sectors

Explanation: Capital expenditure on social sectors directly impacts poverty reduction.
