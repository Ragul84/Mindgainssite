26. An 'inclusive growth' strategy targets:

  1. Economic growth that benefits all sections of society — particularly poor, marginalized, women, tribals, minorities — through social protection, education, health, employment generation
  2. Only GDP growth
  3. Only industrial growth
  4. Only agricultural growth

Answer: Economic growth that benefits all sections of society — particularly poor, marginalized, women, tribals, minorities — through social protection, education, health, employment generation

Explanation: Inclusive Growth: broad-based growth where benefits reach the bottom of the pyramid. India's Planning Commission and NITI Aayog emphasize inclusive growth. Indicators: HDI improvement, poverty reduction, gender parity, social mobility, access to basic services. Related: sustainable development goals (SDGs) — 'leaving no one behind.'

27. Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) are loans where:

  1. Borrower has not made interest or principal repayment for more than 90 days — classified as Substandard (< 1 year), Doubtful (1-3 years), Loss (>3 years)
  2. Borrower is making regular payments
  3. Bank has invested in unproductive assets
  4. Government loans not repaid

Answer: Borrower has not made interest or principal repayment for more than 90 days — classified as Substandard (< 1 year), Doubtful (1-3 years), Loss (>3 years)

Explanation: NPA: loan where interest/principal is overdue for 90+ days. Classification: Substandard (NPA for <1 year), Doubtful (NPA for 1-3 years, collateral insufficient), Loss (uncollectable, to be written off). High NPAs reduce banks' profitability and ability to lend. India's NPA problem peaked ~2018 (GNPAs of ~11.2% of advances). IBC 2016 helped resolve many NPAs.

28. BRICS grouping includes:

  1. Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa — represents major emerging economies; expanded in 2024 to include UAE, Ethiopia, Egypt, Iran; BRICS+ has 9 members
  2. Only Asian countries
  3. Only G20 members
  4. Only Commonwealth members

Answer: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa — represents major emerging economies; expanded in 2024 to include UAE, Ethiopia, Egypt, Iran; BRICS+ has 9 members

Explanation: BRICS: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa. Jim O'Neill (Goldman Sachs) coined 'BRIC' (2001, without South Africa). BRICS New Development Bank (NDB) established 2014, HQ Shanghai. BRICS expanded (2024): UAE, Ethiopia, Egypt, Iran joined. BRICS+ represents ~40% of world GDP (PPP) and ~44% of world population.

29. The 'velocity of money' in economics refers to:

  1. The rate at which money circulates in the economy — high velocity means each rupee is spent many times; MV = PQ (Fisher's Quantity Theory of Money)
  2. The speed of money printing
  3. The speed of bank transfers
  4. The rate of inflation

Answer: The rate at which money circulates in the economy — high velocity means each rupee is spent many times; MV = PQ (Fisher's Quantity Theory of Money)

Explanation: Velocity of Money (V): average number of times a unit of currency is spent in a period. Fisher's Equation of Exchange: MV = PQ (M = money supply, V = velocity, P = price level, Q = real output). If velocity falls (people hoard money), money supply must increase to maintain same GDP. Velocity fell sharply during COVID-19 (people hoarded).

30. Inflation targeting in India means:

  1. RBI is mandated to keep CPI inflation at 4% (with ±2% band = 2-6%) — if missed for 3 consecutive quarters, RBI must explain to government — framework adopted 2016
  2. RBI targets WPI only
  3. Government sets all prices
  4. RBI targets stock market returns

Answer: RBI is mandated to keep CPI inflation at 4% (with ±2% band = 2-6%) — if missed for 3 consecutive quarters, RBI must explain to government — framework adopted 2016

Explanation: Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) framework adopted 2016 (RBI Amendment Act): RBI's primary objective is price stability while keeping growth in mind. Target: CPI inflation 4% ±2% (i.e., 2-6%). Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) decides policy rate. If inflation exceeds band for 3 consecutive quarters, RBI must explain reasons and remedial actions to government.

31. The concept of 'transfer payments' in national accounting includes:

  1. Government payments where no goods/services are received in return — pensions, unemployment benefits, scholarships, subsidies — NOT included in GDP (as no production)
  2. All government expenditure
  3. Export payments
  4. Bank loans

Answer: Government payments where no goods/services are received in return — pensions, unemployment benefits, scholarships, subsidies — NOT included in GDP (as no production)

Explanation: Transfer Payments: one-way payment with no corresponding output — included in government budget but NOT in GDP calculation. Examples: pensions, unemployment benefits, scholarships, welfare payments, food subsidies. These transfer purchasing power from government to beneficiaries but don't create new output. Included in recipients' income but not in GDP via expenditure method.

32. The 'Production Possibility Frontier' (PPF) concept illustrates:

  1. Maximum combinations of two goods that an economy can produce given limited resources — points on the curve = efficient; inside = inefficient; outside = currently unattainable (requires growth)
  2. Consumer demand curve
  3. Government budget allocation
  4. International trade limits

Answer: Maximum combinations of two goods that an economy can produce given limited resources — points on the curve = efficient; inside = inefficient; outside = currently unattainable (requires growth)

Explanation: PPF (Production Possibility Curve): illustrates scarcity and trade-offs. Economy with fixed resources and technology can produce combinations along the PPF frontier. Opportunity cost: producing more of one good requires producing less of another (resources are transferred). Economic growth (more resources, better technology) shifts PPF outward. Key concept in macroeconomics.

33. India's Growth Model in the 2000s was characterized by:

  1. Services-led growth (IT, telecom, financial services) rather than manufacturing-led growth like China; GDP grew 8-9% annually in 2003-2008; criticised for being 'Jobless growth' in manufacturing
  2. Agriculture-led growth
  3. Manufacturing-led growth
  4. Export-only led growth

Answer: Services-led growth (IT, telecom, financial services) rather than manufacturing-led growth like China; GDP grew 8-9% annually in 2003-2008; criticised for being 'Jobless growth' in manufacturing

Explanation: India's post-1991 growth: IT/ITES sector led the services boom. Unlike East Asian economies (manufacturing-led export growth), India's strength is in high-skill services. 2003-2008: India's 'golden decade' with 8-9% GDP growth. Manufacturing remains underdeveloped (~14-17% of GDP vs China's ~27%). PLI scheme aims to boost manufacturing.

34. The 'trickle-down effect' in economics has been criticized by economists like:

  1. Joseph Stiglitz, Amartya Sen, and Paul Krugman — empirical evidence shows benefits don't automatically flow to poor; inequality often worsens with growth unless redistributive policies are in place
  2. Milton Friedman
  3. Friedrich Hayek
  4. Adam Smith

Answer: Joseph Stiglitz, Amartya Sen, and Paul Krugman — empirical evidence shows benefits don't automatically flow to poor; inequality often worsens with growth unless redistributive policies are in place

Explanation: IMF's 2015 research (Ostry, Berg, Tsangarides): found that higher income share of top 20% is associated with lower GDP growth; while higher income share of bottom 20% is associated with faster growth. This challenges trickle-down thesis. UNDP, World Bank now emphasize inclusive growth, direct investment in health/education for poor.

35. Which Five Year Plan was the most successful in India in terms of achieving its growth target?

  1. Sixth Five Year Plan (1980-85) — achieved 5.7% vs target of 5.2% growth; under PM Indira Gandhi; focused on poverty alleviation, self-reliance; Jawahar Rozgar Yojana launched
  2. First FYP
  3. Second FYP
  4. Eleventh FYP

Answer: Sixth Five Year Plan (1980-85) — achieved 5.7% vs target of 5.2% growth; under PM Indira Gandhi; focused on poverty alleviation, self-reliance; Jawahar Rozgar Yojana launched

Explanation: Sixth FYP (1980-85): India's most successful plan — exceeded its growth target. Focus: poverty alleviation, self-reliance, minimum needs program. Also notable: Third FYP (1961-66) was derailed by 1962 India-China war, 1965 India-Pakistan war, and drought → 'plan holiday' 1966-69. Eleventh FYP (2007-12) achieved 7.9% growth.

36. National Income at market prices - Indirect Taxes + Subsidies =

  1. National Income at Factor Cost (NI at FC) — removes the tax wedge to measure actual factor payments (wages, rent, interest, profit) received by factors of production
  2. GDP
  3. GNP
  4. Gross National Disposable Income

Answer: National Income at Factor Cost (NI at FC) — removes the tax wedge to measure actual factor payments (wages, rent, interest, profit) received by factors of production

Explanation: National Income at Factor Cost (NIFC) = NI at Market Price - Indirect Taxes + Subsidies. Factor cost = prices paid to factors of production (wages, rent, interest, profit), excluding indirect taxes paid to government and adding subsidies received from government. India previously reported NI at factor cost; now reports at basic prices (similar but slightly different treatment of taxes/subsidies).

  1. Provide tap water connections to every rural household by 2024 (extended to 2026) — 19 crore rural households; 55 litre per capita per day; Rs 3.60 lakh crore outlay; launched August 2019
  2. Provide bottled water
  3. Build water treatment plants only
  4. Rainwater harvesting only

Answer: Provide tap water connections to every rural household by 2024 (extended to 2026) — 19 crore rural households; 55 litre per capita per day; Rs 3.60 lakh crore outlay; launched August 2019

Explanation: Jal Jeevan Mission (Aug 15, 2019): 'Har Ghar Jal' — tap water (Functional Household Tap Connection/FHTC) to every rural household. Target: 55 litres per capita per day (lpcd) potable water. Progress: from 17% coverage (2019) to ~75%+ (2023). Total outlay: Rs 3.60 lakh crore. Implementation: Ministry of Jal Shakti with State governments.

38. India's current account in BoP is in deficit mainly because of:

  1. Large merchandise trade deficit (India imports more goods than it exports — oil, gold, electronics are major imports) partially offset by services surplus and large remittances
  2. Government budget deficit
  3. Banking sector deficit
  4. Agricultural deficit

Answer: Large merchandise trade deficit (India imports more goods than it exports — oil, gold, electronics are major imports) partially offset by services surplus and large remittances

Explanation: India's Current Account Deficit (CAD): Main contributor is merchandise trade deficit (imports: crude oil ~20-25% of total imports, gold ~8%, electronic items; exports: engineering goods, petroleum products, chemicals). Partly offset by: services surplus (IT exports ~$250B), remittances (~$100B from NRIs). CAD typically 1-3% of GDP, manageable.

  2. PMGSY road scheme
  3. Smart Cities scheme only

Answer: Rs 111 lakh crore infrastructure investment plan for 2019-2025 — covers energy, roads, railways, urban, and rural sectors — implemented by public and private sectors

40. The 'Monetary Policy Transmission' problem in India means:

  1. RBI rate cuts/hikes are not fully or quickly passed on to borrowers/depositors by commercial banks — due to: fixed-rate deposits, MCLR vs EBLR mismatch, bank risk aversion, excess liquidity
  2. RBI cannot change repo rate
  3. Banks do not lend
  4. Government controls all interest rates

Answer: RBI rate cuts/hikes are not fully or quickly passed on to borrowers/depositors by commercial banks — due to: fixed-rate deposits, MCLR vs EBLR mismatch, bank risk aversion, excess liquidity

Explanation: Monetary Policy Transmission: when RBI cuts repo rate, commercial banks should lower their lending rates (to stimulate growth). Problems in India: banks have legacy fixed-rate deposits (can't reduce cost immediately), MCLR-linked loans take time vs EBLR-linked (quicker). Also: banks may not cut rates if they have high NPAs (risk aversion). EBLR regime (2019): faster transmission for retail loans.

41. India's per capita income (NNP at factor cost, current prices) in 2022-23 was approximately:

  1. ~Rs 1.97 lakh per year (approximately $2,400 at market exchange rate) — making India a lower-middle income country by World Bank classification
  2. Rs 50,000 per year
  3. Rs 10 lakh per year
  4. Rs 5 lakh per year

Answer: ~Rs 1.97 lakh per year (approximately $2,400 at market exchange rate) — making India a lower-middle income country by World Bank classification

Explanation: India's per capita NNP (National Income per person) at current prices: ~Rs 1.97 lakh (2022-23) ≈ $2,400 at market exchange rate. At PPP: ~$8,000. World Bank classification (2022-23): Lower-Middle Income ($1,086-$4,255 per capita GNI). India crossed the $2,000 per capita mark. Contrast: Norway/USA ~$65,000+ per capita. Wide intra-India variation (Goa ~$5,500 vs Bihar ~$700).

42. The difference between 'economic growth' and 'economic development' is:

  1. Growth = quantitative increase in GDP/output. Development = qualitative improvement in living standards, institutions, human capital, and well-being (HDI, literacy, health indicators) — growth is necessary but not sufficient for development
  2. They are exactly the same
  3. Development is only about GDP
  4. Growth includes only agricultural GDP

Answer: Growth = quantitative increase in GDP/output. Development = qualitative improvement in living standards, institutions, human capital, and well-being (HDI, literacy, health indicators) — growth is necessary but not sufficient for development

Explanation: Economic Growth: increase in real GDP or per capita GDP (quantitative, narrowly measured). Economic Development: broader — includes growth PLUS structural transformation (industrialization, urbanization), improvement in HDI indicators (health, education, longevity), reduction in poverty/inequality, improvement in institutions, sustainability. Example: Gulf states have high per capita income but lower development (limited democracy, women's rights).

43. The 'Kuznets Curve' on income inequality suggests:

  1. As economies develop, income inequality first rises (industrialization pulls labor from low-productivity agriculture to higher-paying industry — creates urban/rural gap) then falls (as education, welfare state mature) — inverted U shape
  2. Inequality always rises with development
  3. Inequality is always falling
  4. No relationship between inequality and development

Answer: As economies develop, income inequality first rises (industrialization pulls labor from low-productivity agriculture to higher-paying industry — creates urban/rural gap) then falls (as education, welfare state mature) — inverted U shape

Explanation: Kuznets Curve (Simon Kuznets, Nobel 1971): inequality vs per capita income forms an inverted U. Early development: inequality rises (structural shift, unequal gains). Later: inequality falls (education spreads, welfare state, minimum wages, service economy). Critics: doesn't apply universally (Latin America stayed unequal; China/East Asia grew with initially low inequality). Environment Kuznets Curve: similar pattern for pollution.

Answer: Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) — compiles National Accounts (GDP), conducts NSS surveys, PLFS (employment), and population/agriculture statistics

  3. 150% of GDP
  4. 10% of GDP

47. The 'Gross Value Added' (GVA) concept in India's national accounts means:

  1. Value of output minus value of intermediate inputs — GVA + taxes on products - subsidies on products = GDP at market prices; GVA used for sector-wise analysis
  2. Same as GDP
  3. Only industrial output
  4. Agricultural output only

Answer: Value of output minus value of intermediate inputs — GVA + taxes on products - subsidies on products = GDP at market prices; GVA used for sector-wise analysis

Explanation: GVA = GDP at basic prices = value of output (production) minus value of intermediate consumption (inputs used). GDP at market prices = GVA + Net Taxes on Products (taxes - subsidies on products). India's 2011-12 base year revision introduced GVA-based reporting. GVA by sector: Agriculture, Industry (manufacturing, mining, utilities, construction), Services.

48. The Keynesian 'paradox of thrift' states that:

  1. If all individuals save more (rational individual behavior), aggregate demand falls, incomes fall, and total savings may actually decrease (irrational collective outcome)
  2. Saving is always beneficial for the economy
  3. More saving always leads to more investment
  4. Thrift is never useful

Answer: If all individuals save more (rational individual behavior), aggregate demand falls, incomes fall, and total savings may actually decrease (irrational collective outcome)

Explanation: Paradox of Thrift (Keynes): individually rational (save more for future) but collectively irrational (if everyone saves, consumption falls → aggregate demand falls → GDP falls → incomes fall → total savings end up lower). During recessions, fiscal stimulus is needed to break this deflationary spiral — government must spend when private sector cuts back.

49. India's National Accounts are compiled using which base year?

  1. 2011-12 (revised from 2004-05 in January 2015) — allows better capture of new industries (IT, telecom, financial services) and improved methodology
  2. 2000-01
  3. 2004-05 still
  4. 1993-94

Answer: 2011-12 (revised from 2004-05 in January 2015) — allows better capture of new industries (IT, telecom, financial services) and improved methodology

Explanation: India's National Accounts base year revision: 2004-05 → 2011-12 (January 2015). Changes: MCA21 database (corporate sector), better coverage of financial services, improved price deflators, GVA (Gross Value Added) reported at basic prices instead of factor cost. Revision made India's GDP estimates higher than earlier (better coverage of organized sector).
