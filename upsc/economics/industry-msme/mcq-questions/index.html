26. The 'Champions' portal' launched by the Government of India is primarily aimed at which of the following objectives?

  1. Providing direct subsidies for electricity consumption
  2. Grievance redressal and facilitating the growth of MSMEs into larger enterprises
  3. Regulating the import of raw materials for small industries
  4. Managing the payroll of employees in the informal sector

Answer: Grievance redressal and facilitating the growth of MSMEs into larger enterprises

Explanation: The Champions portal focuses on grievance redressal and helping MSMEs scale up to become 'Champions'.

27. Which of the following is a key characteristic of 'Udyam Registration' for MSMEs in India?

  1. It is a manual process requiring physical verification of the factory
  2. It is a permanent registration based on self-declaration via an online portal
  3. It requires a minimum paid-up capital of 10 lakh rupees
  4. It is only applicable to enterprises located in Special Economic Zones

Answer: It is a permanent registration based on self-declaration via an online portal

Explanation: Udyam Registration is a fully online, paperless, and self-declaration-based permanent registration process.

28. In the context of the Indian economy, what is the most likely impact of a significant depreciation of the Indian Rupee on MSMEs primarily focused on export-oriented manufacturing?

  1. It makes their exports more expensive in the global market
  2. It increases the cost of domestic labor, reducing competitiveness
  3. It generally makes their products more price-competitive in international markets
  4. It leads to an automatic increase in the cost of domestic raw materials

Answer: It generally makes their products more price-competitive in international markets

Explanation: A weaker rupee makes Indian exports cheaper for foreign buyers, increasing the competitiveness of export-oriented MSMEs.

29. Which of the following is the primary objective of the 'Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises' (CGTMSE)?

  1. To provide direct grants to MSMEs for technology upgrades
  2. To provide collateral-free credit to micro and small enterprises
  3. To act as a regulatory body for MSME pricing
  4. To provide insurance against fire and natural disasters

Answer: To provide collateral-free credit to micro and small enterprises

Explanation: CGTMSE provides credit guarantees to lenders to encourage them to provide collateral-free loans to MSMEs.

30. The concept of 'Cluster Development' in the MSME sector is intended to achieve which of the following?

  1. The concentration of all MSMEs in a single metropolitan city
  2. Economies of scale through shared infrastructure and collective bargaining
  3. The elimination of competition between small-scale units
  4. The conversion of all MSMEs into Public Sector Undertakings

Answer: Economies of scale through shared infrastructure and collective bargaining

Explanation: Cluster development allows MSMEs to share common facility centers and reduce costs through collective resource utilization.

31. Which of the following statements regarding the 'Priority Sector Lending' (PSL) norms is correct in relation to MSMEs?

  1. Banks are not required to lend to MSMEs under PSL
  2. MSME lending is a component of PSL, ensuring targeted credit flow to the sector
  3. PSL norms only apply to the agricultural sector, not the industrial sector
  4. PSL mandates that all MSME loans must be interest-free

Answer: MSME lending is a component of PSL, ensuring targeted credit flow to the sector

Explanation: PSL mandates that a specific percentage of bank lending must go to sectors like agriculture and MSMEs to ensure inclusive growth.

32. What is the primary purpose of the 'ZED' (Zero Defect Zero Effect) scheme for MSMEs?

  1. To ensure zero employees are laid off during economic downturns
  2. To encourage quality manufacturing (Zero Defect) and environmental sustainability (Zero Effect)
  3. To eliminate all taxes for small-scale industries
  4. To ensure zero dependence on imported machinery

Answer: To encourage quality manufacturing (Zero Defect) and environmental sustainability (Zero Effect)

Explanation: ZED aims to improve quality standards to reduce waste and minimize the environmental footprint of MSME manufacturing.

33. Which monetary policy tool involves the central bank injecting liquidity into the economy by purchasing government securities?

  1. Open Market Operations
  2. Bank Rate Policy
  3. Cash Reserve Ratio Adjustment
  4. Statutory Liquidity Ratio Adjustment

Answer: Open Market Operations

Explanation: Open Market Operations (OMO) involve buying/selling government securities to regulate liquidity. Purchasing securities injects liquidity into the banking system.

34. Which of the following is a direct fiscal indicator of India's economic health?

  1. GDP Growth Rate
  2. Fiscal Deficit
  3. Current Account Deficit
  4. Inflation Rate

Answer: Fiscal Deficit

Explanation: Fiscal Deficit measures the gap between government revenue and expenditure, excluding non-debt expenditure. It directly reflects fiscal policy sustainability.

35. Which sectoral policy has the greatest impact on the competitiveness of MSMEs?

  1. Trade Liberalization
  2. Monetary Tightening
  3. Interest Rate Cuts
  4. Corporate Tax Reforms

Answer: Trade Liberalization

Explanation: Trade liberalization reduces import competition and enhances export opportunities for MSMEs by lowering barriers to international trade.

36. A depreciation of the Indian rupee against the US dollar is most likely to

  1. Increase exports
  2. Decrease imports
  3. Worsen inflation
  4. Improve trade balance

Answer: Worsen inflation

Explanation: Rupee depreciation raises import costs, contributing to inflation. While exports may rise, the immediate impact is often higher inflationary pressure.

37. Which of the following is a component of India's balance of payments?

  1. FDI Inflows
  2. Capital Account
  3. Statutory Liquidity Ratio
  4. Fiscal Deficit

Answer: Capital Account

Explanation: Capital Account records capital transfers and acquisition/disposal of non-financial assets. It is a key component of balance of payments.

38. Which of the following is a demand-pull inflationary factor?

  1. Cost-push inflation
  2. Monopoly power
  3. High taxation
  4. Excess demand

Answer: Excess demand

Explanation: Demand-pull inflation occurs when aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply, leading to rising prices due to excess demand.

39. Which fiscal indicator reflects the government's ability to meet short-term obligations?

  1. Gross Domestic Product
  2. Primary Deficit
  3. Tax Revenue
  4. Monetization of Debt

Answer: Primary Deficit

Explanation: Primary Deficit (excluding interest payments) indicates the government's capacity to fund current expenditures without relying on past borrowing.

40. Which of the following is a structural reform aimed at improving India's trade balance?

  1. Export Promotion Councils
  2. Import Duties
  3. Domestic Subsidies
  4. Monetary Tightening

Answer: Export Promotion Councils

Explanation: Export Promotion Councils support MSMEs and industries to boost exports, directly improving the trade balance.

41. Which of the following is a consequence of sterilized open market operations?

  1. Increased money supply
  2. Reduced inflation
  3. Increased interest rates
  4. No change in money supply

Answer: No change in money supply

Explanation: Sterilized OMO involves offsetting liquidity injections through other measures, ensuring no net change in money supply.

42. Which of the following is a key factor in the rising non-performing assets (NPAs) in the Indian banking sector?

  1. Over-leveraged MSMEs
  2. Weak inflation targeting
  3. High fiscal deficit
  4. Currency depreciation

Answer: Over-leveraged MSMEs

Explanation: Over-leveraged MSMEs default on loans due to high debt levels, contributing to NPAs in commercial banks.

43. Which of the following is a direct impact of a rise in the Bank Rate?

  1. Increased credit availability
  2. Lower inflation
  3. Higher borrowing costs
  4. Increased money supply

Answer: Higher borrowing costs

Explanation: An increase in Bank Rate raises the cost of borrowing for banks, leading to higher interest rates for consumers and businesses.

44. With reference to the revised definition of MSMEs in India, which of the following is the primary basis for the new classification?

  1. Investment in plant and machinery only
  2. Annual turnover only
  3. Composite criteria of investment and annual turnover
  4. Number of employees and paid-up capital

Answer: Composite criteria of investment and annual turnover

Explanation: The 2020 revision moved from investment-only to a composite criterion of both investment and annual turnover to encourage growth without losing benefits.

45. How does the 'Udyam Registration' portal primarily assist in the formalization of the MSME sector in India?

  1. By providing mandatory equity funding to all registered units
  2. By creating a permanent digital identity for seamless access to government schemes
  3. By exempting all registered MSMEs from GST compliance
  4. By automating the export-import licensing process

Answer: By creating a permanent digital identity for seamless access to government schemes

Explanation: Udyam provides a permanent registration number, facilitating easier access to priority sector lending and government support.

46. In the context of Indian industrial policy, what is the primary objective of the 'Champions' project' launched by the Ministry of MSME?

  1. To provide direct cash grants to all micro-enterprises
  2. To transform quality MSMEs into 'Champions' for global competitiveness
  3. To replace the existing MSME classification system
  4. To nationalize small-scale industries for better coordination

Answer: To transform quality MSMEs into 'Champions' for global competitiveness

Explanation: The project aims to upgrade MSMEs through technology and innovation to make them globally competitive.

47. Which of the following is a likely impact of an increase in the 'Cash Reserve Ratio' (CRR) on the MSME sector?

  1. Increased availability of working capital loans
  2. Reduced cost of borrowing for small industries
  3. Potential tightening of credit availability due to reduced loanable funds
  4. Automatic increase in the turnover of micro-enterprises

Answer: Potential tightening of credit availability due to reduced loanable funds

Explanation: Higher CRR reduces the liquidity available with banks, which often leads to tighter credit conditions for credit-dependent MSMEs.

48. Consider the 'Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises' (CGTMSE). What is its fundamental purpose?

  1. To provide direct loans to entrepreneurs without interest
  2. To provide collateral-free credit by offering guarantees to lenders
  3. To manage the equity shares of small-scale industries
  4. To regulate the interest rates charged by NBFCs

Answer: To provide collateral-free credit by offering guarantees to lenders

Explanation: CGTMSE reduces the risk for banks, encouraging them to lend to MSMEs who lack sufficient collateral.

49. How does a significant depreciation of the Indian Rupee typically affect the MSME sector focused on exports?

  1. Makes exports more expensive and less competitive
  2. Increases the cost of raw materials for purely domestic producers
  3. Makes Indian exports more price-competitive in the global market
  4. Decreases the total value of foreign exchange earnings

Answer: Makes Indian exports more price-competitive in the global market

Explanation: A weaker rupee makes Indian goods cheaper for foreign buyers, thereby boosting export competitiveness.

50. Which of the following statements regarding 'Priority Sector Lending' (PSL) is correct in the context of MSMEs?

  1. Banks are not required to lend to MSMEs under PSL
  2. MSME lending is a mandatory target for scheduled commercial banks
  3. PSL only applies to agricultural loans, not industrial loans
  4. Only government-owned banks are bound by PSL norms

Answer: MSME lending is a mandatory target for scheduled commercial banks

Explanation: MSMEs are a key component of Priority Sector Lending, ensuring they receive a mandated share of bank credit.
