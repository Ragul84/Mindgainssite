1. The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme was introduced to:

  1. Incentivize companies to increase domestic manufacturing — financial incentives based on incremental sales; initially 13 sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles
  2. Replace GST
  3. Provide employment guarantee
  4. Support agriculture

Answer: Incentivize companies to increase domestic manufacturing — financial incentives based on incremental sales; initially 13 sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles

Explanation: PLI Scheme (2020-21): government gives manufacturers financial incentives (3-5% of incremental production over a base year) to boost domestic manufacturing. Initially 13 sectors: mobile phones (Apple, Samsung set up factories), pharmaceuticals (API), automobiles, textiles, food processing, etc. Aims to make India a global manufacturing hub.

2. The Make in India initiative was launched by:

  1. PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol
  2. PM Manmohan Singh
  3. NITI Aayog independently
  4. Ministry of Commerce

Answer: PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol

Explanation: Make in India (September 25, 2014): India as global design and manufacturing hub. 25 focus sectors: automobiles, aviation, chemicals, IT, pharma, construction, food processing, textiles, etc. The iconic logo: walking lion made of cogwheels (industry symbol). Complemented by: Invest India, Startup India, Skill India, Digital India.

3. India's largest public sector undertaking (PSU) by revenue is:

  1. Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) — petroleum refining and marketing; consistently India's largest company by revenue; followed by ONGC, BPCL
  2. Life Insurance Corporation (LIC)
  3. State Bank of India
  4. NTPC

Answer: Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) — petroleum refining and marketing; consistently India's largest company by revenue; followed by ONGC, BPCL

Explanation: Indian Oil Corporation (IOC): India's largest company by revenue (~Rs 8 lakh crore). It is a Fortune 500 company (ranked ~94th globally). Refines and markets petroleum products through its extensive pipeline and retail network (~30,000 outlets). Other large PSUs: ONGC (oil exploration), BPCL, HPCL, Coal India, SBI, LIC, NTPC.

4. The phenomenon of 'Dutch Disease' refers to:

  1. Boom in one sector (e.g., natural resource discovery) causing currency appreciation and decline in other export sectors (especially manufacturing) — named after Netherlands' natural gas boom in 1960s
  2. A type of financial crisis
  3. An agricultural disease
  4. Import of cheap manufactured goods

Answer: Boom in one sector (e.g., natural resource discovery) causing currency appreciation and decline in other export sectors (especially manufacturing) — named after Netherlands' natural gas boom in 1960s

Explanation: Dutch Disease: named after Netherlands' North Sea gas discovery (1959). Large foreign currency inflows from resource boom → currency appreciates → manufacturing becomes uncompetitive → deindustrialization. Examples: Nigeria (oil), Venezuela (oil). Prevention: sovereign wealth funds (Norway), diversification strategies.

5. 'Disinvestment' in Indian public sector means:

  1. Government selling its equity stake in PSUs (Public Sector Undertakings) — partial disinvestment: retains majority; strategic disinvestment: transfers management control; 'privatization' used when full control is transferred
  2. Nationalization
  3. Government investing more in PSUs
  4. PSU borrowing funds

Answer: Government selling its equity stake in PSUs (Public Sector Undertakings) — partial disinvestment: retains majority; strategic disinvestment: transfers management control; 'privatization' used when full control is transferred

Explanation: Disinvestment: government sells equity shares in PSUs to raise revenue and improve PSU efficiency. Types: Minority disinvestment (retains >51%, no control change), Majority disinvestment (retains <51%), Strategic Disinvestment (control transferred — like Air India sale to Tata 2022). DIPAM (Dept of Investment and Public Asset Management) manages disinvestment. Annual target: Rs 50,000-60,000 crore.
