Industry Msme MCQs with answers

1. The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme was introduced to:

  1. Incentivize companies to increase domestic manufacturing — financial incentives based on incremental sales; initially 13 sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles
  2. Replace GST
  3. Provide employment guarantee
  4. Support agriculture

Answer: Incentivize companies to increase domestic manufacturing — financial incentives based on incremental sales; initially 13 sectors including mobile phones, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles

Explanation: PLI Scheme (2020-21): government gives manufacturers financial incentives (3-5% of incremental production over a base year) to boost domestic manufacturing. Initially 13 sectors: mobile phones (Apple, Samsung set up factories), pharmaceuticals (API), automobiles, textiles, food processing, etc. Aims to make India a global manufacturing hub.

2. The Make in India initiative was launched by:

  1. PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol
  2. PM Manmohan Singh
  3. NITI Aayog independently
  4. Ministry of Commerce

Answer: PM Narendra Modi in September 2014 — to make India a global manufacturing hub; focuses on 25 sectors; lion (Ashoka Chakra wheel as wheels) is its symbol

Explanation: Make in India (September 25, 2014): India as global design and manufacturing hub. 25 focus sectors: automobiles, aviation, chemicals, IT, pharma, construction, food processing, textiles, etc. The iconic logo: walking lion made of cogwheels (industry symbol). Complemented by: Invest India, Startup India, Skill India, Digital India.

3. India's largest public sector undertaking (PSU) by revenue is:

  1. Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) — petroleum refining and marketing; consistently India's largest company by revenue; followed by ONGC, BPCL
  2. Life Insurance Corporation (LIC)
  3. State Bank of India
  4. NTPC

Answer: Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) — petroleum refining and marketing; consistently India's largest company by revenue; followed by ONGC, BPCL

Explanation: Indian Oil Corporation (IOC): India's largest company by revenue (~Rs 8 lakh crore). It is a Fortune 500 company (ranked ~94th globally). Refines and markets petroleum products through its extensive pipeline and retail network (~30,000 outlets). Other large PSUs: ONGC (oil exploration), BPCL, HPCL, Coal India, SBI, LIC, NTPC.

4. The phenomenon of 'Dutch Disease' refers to:

  1. Boom in one sector (e.g., natural resource discovery) causing currency appreciation and decline in other export sectors (especially manufacturing) — named after Netherlands' natural gas boom in 1960s
  2. A type of financial crisis
  3. An agricultural disease
  4. Import of cheap manufactured goods

Answer: Boom in one sector (e.g., natural resource discovery) causing currency appreciation and decline in other export sectors (especially manufacturing) — named after Netherlands' natural gas boom in 1960s

Explanation: Dutch Disease: named after Netherlands' North Sea gas discovery (1959). Large foreign currency inflows from resource boom → currency appreciates → manufacturing becomes uncompetitive → deindustrialization. Examples: Nigeria (oil), Venezuela (oil). Prevention: sovereign wealth funds (Norway), diversification strategies.

5. 'Disinvestment' in Indian public sector means:

  1. Government selling its equity stake in PSUs (Public Sector Undertakings) — partial disinvestment: retains majority; strategic disinvestment: transfers management control; 'privatization' used when full control is transferred
  2. Nationalization
  3. Government investing more in PSUs
  4. PSU borrowing funds

Answer: Government selling its equity stake in PSUs (Public Sector Undertakings) — partial disinvestment: retains majority; strategic disinvestment: transfers management control; 'privatization' used when full control is transferred

Explanation: Disinvestment: government sells equity shares in PSUs to raise revenue and improve PSU efficiency. Types: Minority disinvestment (retains >51%, no control change), Majority disinvestment (retains <51%), Strategic Disinvestment (control transferred — like Air India sale to Tata 2022). DIPAM (Dept of Investment and Public Asset Management) manages disinvestment. Annual target: Rs 50,000-60,000 crore.

6. The 'Economic corridor' concept in India refers to:

  1. Integrated industrial and logistics infrastructure along transportation routes — Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC), Chennai-Bengaluru (CBIC), Amritsar-Kolkata (AKIC), Bangalore-Mumbai (BMIC)
  2. Only road corridors
  3. Trade routes with neighboring countries
  4. Exclusive economic zones at sea

Answer: Integrated industrial and logistics infrastructure along transportation routes — Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC), Chennai-Bengaluru (CBIC), Amritsar-Kolkata (AKIC), Bangalore-Mumbai (BMIC)

Explanation: Industrial Corridors: Delhi-Mumbai IC (DMIC, flagship: 1483 km along DFC — Dedicated Freight Corridor, 24 manufacturing nodes, $90 billion investment plan). Chennai-Bengaluru IC (CBIC). Amritsar-Kolkata IC (AKIC). Hyderabad-Nagpur, Hyderabad-Warangal IC. Funded by: National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation (NICDC).

7. Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana (PMBJP) provides:

  1. Quality generic medicines at affordable prices through Jan Aushadhi Kendras (pharmacies) — launched 2008, expanded 2015; ~10,000+ Kendras in 2023; medicines priced 50-90% lower than branded drugs
  2. Free medicines everywhere
  3. Only vaccines
  4. Only surgical equipment

Answer: Quality generic medicines at affordable prices through Jan Aushadhi Kendras (pharmacies) — launched 2008, expanded 2015; ~10,000+ Kendras in 2023; medicines priced 50-90% lower than branded drugs

Explanation: PMBJP (Bureau of Pharma PSUs of India — BPPI): network of Jan Aushadhi Kendras selling 1,900+ generic medicines and 300+ surgical items at prices 50-90% below branded alternatives. Ensures quality through testing (ISP and NABL-accredited labs). Benefited: affordable medicines for common diseases (diabetes, hypertension, cardiac, cancer, TB). Also: SuJaK (Sugam Jan Aushadi Kendras) for remote areas.

8. The concept of 'infant industry protection' argues:

  1. New/young industries in developing countries need temporary tariff protection to grow and become internationally competitive — used by USA (19th century), Germany (Bismarck era), Japan, South Korea, and now by developing economies including India
  2. All industries need permanent protection
  3. Free trade is never beneficial
  4. Only agricultural industries need protection

Answer: New/young industries in developing countries need temporary tariff protection to grow and become internationally competitive — used by USA (19th century), Germany (Bismarck era), Japan, South Korea, and now by developing economies including India

Explanation: Infant Industry Argument (Friedrich List, 1841 — 'National System of Political Economy'): nascent industries with potential scale economies need temporary protection (tariffs, subsidies) against established foreign competitors until they achieve global competitiveness. USA protected its manufacturing (1800s-1900s) with high tariffs. South Korea's POSCO steel. Counter: protection tends to become permanent, breeds inefficiency.

9. The National Credit Guarantee Trust (NCGTC) oversees:

  1. Credit guarantees for MSME loans (without collateral) — provides guarantee cover to lending institutions so they lend to MSMEs; also operates PM Mudra Yojana and PM SVANidhi guarantees
  2. Housing loan guarantees
  3. Agricultural loan waivers
  4. Export credit guarantees (that's ECGC)

Answer: Credit guarantees for MSME loans (without collateral) — provides guarantee cover to lending institutions so they lend to MSMEs; also operates PM Mudra Yojana and PM SVANidhi guarantees

Explanation: NCGTC (National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company, 2014): provides credit guarantees for MSME loans through: Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE), Credit Guarantee Fund for Skill Development (CGFSD), Credit Guarantee Fund for Educational Loans (CGFEL), Credit Guarantee Scheme for Start-Ups. Borrowers don't need collateral; banks get guaranteed coverage.

10. India's 'National Mission on Transformative Mobility and Battery Storage' (NMTMBS) aims at:

  1. Accelerating adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) — policies for EV manufacturing, charging infrastructure, battery recycling; FAME-II scheme for EV subsidies; targets: 30% EV penetration by 2030 for private cars
  2. Petroleum refining
  3. Public transport only
  4. Two-wheelers only

Answer: Accelerating adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) — policies for EV manufacturing, charging infrastructure, battery recycling; FAME-II scheme for EV subsidies; targets: 30% EV penetration by 2030 for private cars

Explanation: EV Mission: FAME India (Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of Electric Vehicles): Phase-I (2015-19), Phase-II (2019-24, Rs 10,000 crore): subsidies for EV purchase, charging infrastructure. PLI for Advanced Chemistry Cell batteries (ACC-PLI, Rs 18,100 crore). NMTMBS: inter-ministerial mission for EV ecosystem. India wants 30% EV by 2030 for all categories; 100% EV for 2-wheelers and 3-wheelers.

11. Structural unemployment is caused by:

  1. Economic downturns reducing demand
  2. Seasonal variations in production
  3. Mismatch between workers' skills and available jobs due to technological change
  4. Workers voluntarily changing jobs

Answer: Mismatch between workers' skills and available jobs due to technological change

Explanation: Structural unemployment: skills mismatch due to technology changes, industry shifts, or globalization. Long-term; requires retraining. E.g., manufacturing workers displaced by automation.

12. The Bombay Plan (1944) was drafted by Indian industrialists including Tata and Birla, proposing:

  1. Free market economy with no government intervention
  2. Central planning with significant state investment in industry
  3. Complete nationalization of all industries
  4. Agricultural-led growth model

Answer: Central planning with significant state investment in industry

Explanation: Bombay Plan: 8 industrialists (JRD Tata, GD Birla, etc.) proposed a 15-year plan with heavy state investment in infrastructure and industry, import substitution, and mixed economy framework.

13. The MSME sector in India is defined under the MSME Development (Amendment) Act 2020 based on:

  1. Number of employees only
  2. Investment in plant & machinery/equipment AND annual turnover
  3. Annual turnover only
  4. Capital employed only

Answer: Investment in plant & machinery/equipment AND annual turnover

Explanation: MSME 2020 definition: Micro (Investment ≤Rs 1 cr, Turnover ≤Rs 5 cr), Small (Investment ≤Rs 10 cr, Turnover ≤Rs 50 cr), Medium (Investment ≤Rs 50 cr, Turnover ≤Rs 250 cr). Composite criteria (investment + turnover).

14. The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) is responsible for:

  1. Collecting import/export duties
  2. Formulating and implementing India's Foreign Trade Policy (FTP)
  3. Regulating foreign exchange transactions
  4. Negotiating trade agreements

Answer: Formulating and implementing India's Foreign Trade Policy (FTP)

Explanation: DGFT: under Ministry of Commerce and Industry. Implements FTP, issues Importer Exporter Code (IEC), administers export promotion schemes (EPCG, Advance Authorization, MEIS/RODTEP). Also issues export/import licenses.

15. The 'Smile Curve' concept in international trade (coined by Stan Shih of Acer) shows that:

  1. Profits are highest in the middle of the value chain (manufacturing)
  2. Profits are highest at both ends of the value chain (R&D/design and marketing/services), not in manufacturing
  3. Developing countries benefit most from trade
  4. Small economies grow faster

Answer: Profits are highest at both ends of the value chain (R&D/design and marketing/services), not in manufacturing

Explanation: Smile Curve: value added is highest at R&D/design (upstream) and brand/marketing/services (downstream) ends; manufacturing in the middle captures less value. Explains why Apple earns more than Foxconn despite Foxconn making iPhones.

16. India's National Monetization Pipeline (NMP) aims to monetize government/PSU brownfield assets worth approximately:

  1. Rs 1 lakh crore
  2. Rs 3 lakh crore
  3. Rs 6 lakh crore
  4. Rs 10 lakh crore

Answer: Rs 6 lakh crore

Explanation: NMP (August 2021): Rs 6 lakh crore worth of brownfield infrastructure assets to be monetized over 4 years (FY2022-25). Assets: NHAI roads, railway stations, airports, power lines, gas pipelines, telecom towers. NITI Aayog designed it.

17. Privatization means:

  1. Transfer of ownership or management of state-owned enterprises to private sector — through disinvestment (selling government equity), strategic sale, or outright sale
  2. Government taking over private companies
  3. Increase in government spending
  4. Tax reduction only

Answer: Transfer of ownership or management of state-owned enterprises to private sector — through disinvestment (selling government equity), strategic sale, or outright sale

Explanation: Privatization: government ownership/control transferred to private sector. Disinvestment: government sells part of its equity in PSUs (Public Sector Undertakings). Strategic sale: transfer of management control + equity. Full privatization: 100% sale. India's disinvestment target: Rs 65,000 crore (2023-24). Air India sold to Tata Sons (2022) is a major strategic disinvestment.

18. The concept of 'Comparative Advantage' in international trade was developed by:

  1. David Ricardo (1817, 'On the Principles of Political Economy and Taxation') — countries should specialize in producing goods they produce relatively more efficiently, even if less efficient absolutely
  2. Adam Smith (Absolute Advantage)
  3. John Keynes
  4. Paul Krugman (New Trade Theory)

Answer: David Ricardo (1817, 'On the Principles of Political Economy and Taxation') — countries should specialize in producing goods they produce relatively more efficiently, even if less efficient absolutely

Explanation: Comparative Advantage (Ricardo): a country should specialize in goods where it has the lowest opportunity cost relative to other goods, not necessarily the lowest absolute cost. Even if one country is more efficient in all goods, both benefit from trade through specialization. Basis for international free trade theory. Example: India in IT services, China in manufacturing.

19. The Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC) is a joint initiative with which country?

  1. USA
  2. China
  3. Japan
  4. Germany

Answer: Japan

Explanation: DMIC: India-Japan joint project along the 1,483-km Western Dedicated Freight Corridor. 24 manufacturing cities/nodes planned across 6 states. Japan invested in DMIC Development Corporation.

20. The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme provides incentive as a percentage of:

  1. Fixed capital investment
  2. Incremental sales over a base year
  3. Employment generated
  4. Export value

Answer: Incremental sales over a base year

Explanation: PLI: companies receive incentive (4-20% depending on sector) on incremental sales above their base year (2019-20). Covers 14 sectors. Aims to attract investment, enhance manufacturing, and boost exports.

21. Who is known as the 'Father of Indian Economic Planning'?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. P.C. Mahalanobis
  3. Manmohan Singh
  4. V.K.R.V. Rao

Answer: P.C. Mahalanobis

Explanation: P.C. Mahalanobis (1893-1972): statistician and planner, architect of India's second Five Year Plan (1956-61). Founder of ISI (Indian Statistical Institute). His 'two-sector' model emphasized heavy industry.

22. The term 'Dutch Disease' in economics refers to:

  1. Economic downturn caused by flood/natural disaster
  2. Decline in manufacturing sector caused by a large discovery/boom in natural resources (e.g., oil)
  3. High inflation in a welfare state
  4. Economic recession caused by high wages

Answer: Decline in manufacturing sector caused by a large discovery/boom in natural resources (e.g., oil)

Explanation: Dutch Disease: named after Netherlands' 1960s natural gas discovery. Resource boom → currency appreciates → exports of other sectors (manufacturing) become uncompetitive → deindustrialization. Affects many resource-rich nations.

23. Under the revised MSME definition, which of the following is the primary criterion used to classify an enterprise as a 'Micro' enterprise?

  1. Only the total number of employees
  2. Combination of investment in plant and machinery and annual turnover
  3. Only the annual turnover of the enterprise
  4. Investment in fixed assets regardless of turnover

Answer: Combination of investment in plant and machinery and annual turnover

Explanation: The revised definition uses a composite criterion of both investment and annual turnover to classify Micro, Small, and Medium enterprises.

24. Which of the following best describes the 'TReDS' (Trade Receivables Discounting System) platform in the context of MSMEs?

  1. A portal for registering new MSME units
  2. A mechanism for providing equity funding to startups
  3. An online platform to facilitate the discounting of trade receivables to solve liquidity issues
  4. A credit rating agency for small-scale industrial units

Answer: An online platform to facilitate the discounting of trade receivables to solve liquidity issues

Explanation: TReDS is an institutional mechanism to address the problem of delayed payments to MSMEs by discounting their invoices.

25. How does an increase in the 'Cash Reserve Ratio' (CRR) by the RBI typically impact the operational capacity of MSMEs?

  1. It increases the availability of working capital loans
  2. It reduces the cost of borrowing for small industries
  3. It may lead to a liquidity crunch by reducing the lendable resources of banks
  4. It has no impact as MSMEs do not rely on commercial bank credit

Answer: It may lead to a liquidity crunch by reducing the lendable resources of banks

Explanation: Higher CRR reduces the loanable funds available with banks, potentially tightening credit flow to credit-dependent MSMEs.
