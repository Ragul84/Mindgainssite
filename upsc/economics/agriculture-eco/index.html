1. India's Five Year Plans were a feature of which economic model?

  1. Socialist/Mixed economy planning — Soviet-influenced centralized planning to guide economic development (1951-2017)
  2. Pure capitalist economy
  3. Free market economy
  4. Laissez-faire economy

Answer: Socialist/Mixed economy planning — Soviet-influenced centralized planning to guide economic development (1951-2017)

Explanation: India's Five-Year Plans (1951-2017, 12 plans total) were inspired by Soviet Union's central planning model. First Plan (1951-56): agriculture focus (Bhakra dam, community development). Second Plan (Nehru-Mahalanobis): heavy industry. 12th Plan ended 2017. NITI Aayog replaced with strategy documents.

2. The First Five Year Plan of India (1951-56) focused on:

  1. Agriculture, irrigation, and power (dams: Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Damodar Valley); based on Harrod-Domar growth model
  2. Heavy industrialization
  3. IT sector
  4. Infrastructure only

Answer: Agriculture, irrigation, and power (dams: Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Damodar Valley); based on Harrod-Domar growth model

Explanation: First FYP (1951-56): priority on agriculture, irrigation, and power generation to overcome food shortages and post-partition economic disruption. Planned outlay: Rs 2,069 crore. Major projects: Bhakra-Nangal (Punjab), Damodar Valley Corporation, Hirakud Dam. Also focused on land reforms.

3. Public Distribution System (PDS) in India is meant to:

  1. Distribute subsidized food grains (rice, wheat, sugar, kerosene) to Below Poverty Line (BPL) families through ration shops (Fair Price Shops)
  2. Distribute fertilizers
  3. Manage mid-day meals in schools
  4. Export food grains

Answer: Distribute subsidized food grains (rice, wheat, sugar, kerosene) to Below Poverty Line (BPL) families through ration shops (Fair Price Shops)

Explanation: PDS: network of about 5.3 lakh Fair Price Shops (ration shops) distributing subsidized food. Managed jointly by central (Food Corporation of India procures and allocates) and state governments (distributes). NFSA 2013 (National Food Security Act) extended PDS to ~81 crore people (75% rural, 50% urban population).

4. The Minimum Support Price (MSP) is:

  1. Government-guaranteed price for agricultural produce — ensures farmers get fair price and are not exploited by market forces; recommended by CACP
  2. Market price of commodities
  3. Minimum wage for agricultural workers
  4. Tax on agricultural income

Answer: Government-guaranteed price for agricultural produce — ensures farmers get fair price and are not exploited by market forces; recommended by CACP

Explanation: MSP: announced by Central Government (based on CACP — Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices recommendations) before sowing season. Government agencies (FCI, NAFED) purchase crops at MSP if market price falls below it. MSP for 23 major crops including paddy, wheat, pulses, oilseeds, and cotton. Not legally binding on private buyers.

5. The National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) guarantees:

  1. 100 days of unskilled manual work per year to any rural household that demands it — minimum wage paid, unemployed allowance if work not provided within 15 days
  2. 200 days of work
  3. Employment to urban workers
  4. Skilled employment only

Answer: 100 days of unskilled manual work per year to any rural household that demands it — minimum wage paid, unemployed allowance if work not provided within 15 days

Explanation: MGNREGA (2005, implemented 2006): provides legal right to 100 days of unskilled work per household per year at minimum wage. If work not provided within 15 days, unemployment allowance paid. Works include road construction, water conservation, land development. Among world's largest social protection programs. Renamed to include Gandhi: MGNREGS.

6. The Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) aims at:

  1. Financial inclusion — opening zero-balance bank accounts for unbanked sections with RuPay debit card, overdraft facility, and insurance
  2. Free LPG connections
  3. Healthcare for poor
  4. Rural electrification

Answer: Financial inclusion — opening zero-balance bank accounts for unbanked sections with RuPay debit card, overdraft facility, and insurance

Explanation: PMJDY (August 28, 2014): world's largest financial inclusion initiative. Features: zero-balance savings account (Jan Dhan account), RuPay debit card with Rs 2 lakh accident insurance, Rs 30,000 life insurance, Rs 10,000 overdraft facility. By 2024: 52+ crore accounts opened, mostly in rural areas.

7. The NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) was established in:

  1. 1982 — apex bank for agricultural and rural credit; provides refinance to cooperative banks, RRBs, and commercial banks for agricultural loans
  2. 1935
  3. 1969
  4. 2000

Answer: 1982 — apex bank for agricultural and rural credit; provides refinance to cooperative banks, RRBs, and commercial banks for agricultural loans

Explanation: NABARD (est. July 12, 1982) based on B. Sivaraman Committee recommendations. HQ: Mumbai. Functions: refinancing apex cooperative banks, RRBs (Regional Rural Banks), and commercial banks for agriculture/rural development; rural infrastructure development (RIDF); watershed development; Self-Help Group (SHG) - Bank Linkage Programme.

8. The Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY) aims to provide:

  1. Housing for All — affordable housing to urban and rural poor by 2022 (extended to 2024); includes PMAY-Urban and PMAY-Gramin
  2. Free food for all
  3. Employment guarantee
  4. Healthcare for all

Answer: Housing for All — affordable housing to urban and rural poor by 2022 (extended to 2024); includes PMAY-Urban and PMAY-Gramin

Explanation: PMAY (2015): targets housing for all urban slum dwellers and rural homeless. PMAY-Urban: credit-linked subsidy scheme for home loans (for EWS/LIG/MIG). PMAY-Gramin: provides Rs 1.2 lakh (plain areas) or Rs 1.3 lakh (NE, hilly) grant for rural house construction. Completed 3 crore+ houses.

9. The term 'poverty line' in India is determined by:

  1. Minimum monthly per capita expenditure needed to meet basic food, clothing, and shelter needs — currently based on Tendulkar Committee methodology (modified by Rangarajan Committee)
  2. Annual income below Rs 1 lakh
  3. BPL card possession
  4. Urban/rural classification only

Answer: Minimum monthly per capita expenditure needed to meet basic food, clothing, and shelter needs — currently based on Tendulkar Committee methodology (modified by Rangarajan Committee)

Explanation: India's Poverty Line: Tendulkar Committee (2009): Rs 816/month urban, Rs 672/month rural (2011-12 prices). Rangarajan Committee (2014): higher threshold — Rs 1407 urban, Rs 972 rural. SECC (Socio-Economic and Caste Census) used for targeting welfare programs. About 21% of population below poverty line (World Bank $1.90/day PPP criterion).

10. Remittances from Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) contribute to India's:

  1. Current Account (in Balance of Payments) — India is the world's largest remittance recipient (~$100 billion annually)
  2. Capital Account only
  3. Trade Balance (goods exports)
  4. Fiscal deficit

Answer: Current Account (in Balance of Payments) — India is the world's largest remittance recipient (~$100 billion annually)

Explanation: NRI remittances: India consistently receives the highest remittances globally (~$100 billion in 2022-23). Recorded under 'transfers' in the Current Account of BoP. Major sources: USA, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UK. They help finance India's current account deficit and support rural household incomes.

11. The National Rural Health Mission (NHM) was launched in:

  1. 2005 — to improve healthcare in rural India; later merged with National Urban Health Mission to form NHM in 2013; ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) are key workers
  2. 2000
  3. 2010
  4. 1995

Answer: 2005 — to improve healthcare in rural India; later merged with National Urban Health Mission to form NHM in 2013; ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) are key workers

Explanation: NRHM (National Rural Health Mission, 2005): flagship health scheme for rural areas. Key feature: ASHA workers — trained female community health activists (one per 1000 population) who link communities with health facilities. NRHM + National Urban Health Mission merged into NHM (National Health Mission) in 2013.

12. The Atal Innovation Mission (AIM) was established under:

  1. NITI Aayog — to promote innovation and entrepreneurship across India through Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) in schools and Atal Incubation Centres (AICs)
  2. Ministry of Finance
  3. CSIR
  4. RBI

Answer: NITI Aayog — to promote innovation and entrepreneurship across India through Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) in schools and Atal Incubation Centres (AICs)

Explanation: AIM (2016, under NITI Aayog): flagship initiative to promote innovation. Key programs: Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) — maker spaces in schools (Grades 6-12) with 3D printers, robotics kits, etc. Atal Incubation Centres (AICs): support startups. Atal Community Innovation Centres (ACICs): tier-2/3 cities and rural areas.

13. The Eleventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution lists subjects under:

  1. Panchayati Raj institutions (29 subjects including agriculture, land, roads, drinking water, education, poverty alleviation) after 73rd Amendment 1992
  2. State legislature subjects
  3. Union List subjects
  4. Concurrent List subjects

Answer: Panchayati Raj institutions (29 subjects including agriculture, land, roads, drinking water, education, poverty alleviation) after 73rd Amendment 1992

Explanation: 73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992): gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj (3-tier local self-government). 11th Schedule: 29 subjects devolved to panchayats including: agriculture, land reforms, rural electrification, water management, primary education, primary health, roads, poverty alleviation. States choose which subjects to actually transfer.

14. The Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (Clean India Mission) was launched on:

  1. October 2, 2014 (Gandhi Jayanti) — to achieve Open Defecation Free (ODF) India; 9 crore+ toilets built; PM Modi started by personally sweeping Valmiki colony, New Delhi
  2. August 15, 2014
  3. January 26, 2015
  4. April 5, 2016

Answer: October 2, 2014 (Gandhi Jayanti) — to achieve Open Defecation Free (ODF) India; 9 crore+ toilets built; PM Modi started by personally sweeping Valmiki colony, New Delhi

Explanation: Swachh Bharat Mission (Oct 2, 2014, Gandhi's 145th birthday): two sub-missions — SBM-Grameen (rural, MoDRWS) and SBM-Urban (MoHUA). Phase 1 (2014-19): ODF (Open Defecation Free) goal — 60 crore people stopped open defecation. Phase 2 (2020-25): ODF+, solid/liquid waste management, plastic waste management.

15. The Bharat Net project aims to:

  1. Provide broadband connectivity to all Gram Panchayats (2.5 lakh panchayats) using OFC, radio, and satellite — run by BSNL/DOT; enable Digital India in rural areas
  2. Build highways
  3. Develop ports
  4. Electrify villages (that's Saubhagya scheme)

Answer: Provide broadband connectivity to all Gram Panchayats (2.5 lakh panchayats) using OFC, radio, and satellite — run by BSNL/DOT; enable Digital India in rural areas

Explanation: BharatNet (2011, earlier NOFN — National Optical Fibre Network): world's largest rural broadband connectivity project. Connects all 2.5 lakh gram panchayats with optical fiber (OFC). Implemented by BSNL through state governments. Enables: e-governance, telemedicine, e-education, e-commerce in rural India. Essential backbone for Digital India.

16. Food Corporation of India (FCI) was established to:

  1. Procure food grains from farmers at MSP, store, and distribute them through PDS — established 1965; HQ: New Delhi; India's largest food storage network
  2. Export food grains
  3. Process food products
  4. Regulate food prices directly

Answer: Procure food grains from farmers at MSP, store, and distribute them through PDS — established 1965; HQ: New Delhi; India's largest food storage network

Explanation: FCI (1965, FCI Act 1964): functions: procurement of wheat and rice from farmers at MSP (mainly Punjab, Haryana, UP), building and managing strategic food reserves (buffer stocks), distributing to state governments for PDS at Central Issue Price (CIP). FCI's buffer stock of grain helps stabilize prices and ensure food security.

17. The Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) is a scheme for:

  1. Skill development — free short-term training to youth in industry-relevant skills; recognition of prior learning (RPL); National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) implements it
  2. Mid-day meals
  3. Employment guarantee
  4. Housing for youth

Answer: Skill development — free short-term training to youth in industry-relevant skills; recognition of prior learning (RPL); National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) implements it

Explanation: PMKVY (2015): flagship skill development scheme under Skill India Mission. Free short-term training across 300+ job roles in 38 sectors (construction, IT, healthcare, agriculture, etc.). On successful training + assessment: placement assistance and Rs 8,000 reward. PMKVY 4.0: focus on Industry 4.0 skills, digital and green jobs.

18. The Bharat Broadband Network Limited (BBNL) was merged with BSNL to implement:

  1. BharatNet project — providing optical fiber broadband to all gram panchayats; BBNL was Special Purpose Vehicle set up 2012; merged with BSNL 2023
  2. 4G rollout in cities
  3. Satellite internet
  4. National telecom policy

Answer: BharatNet project — providing optical fiber broadband to all gram panchayats; BBNL was Special Purpose Vehicle set up 2012; merged with BSNL 2023

Explanation: BBNL (Bharat Broadband Network Limited, 2012): Special Purpose Vehicle to implement BharatNet (initially NOFN). In 2023, government merged BBNL with BSNL to streamline the BharatNet project and leverage BSNL's existing infrastructure, workforce, and right-of-way permissions for faster rural broadband rollout.

19. The Asian Development Bank (ADB) is headquartered in:

  1. Manila, Philippines — established 1966; provides loans/technical assistance to developing Asian countries; India is the largest borrower from ADB
  2. Tokyo
  3. Singapore
  4. Beijing

Answer: Manila, Philippines — established 1966; provides loans/technical assistance to developing Asian countries; India is the largest borrower from ADB

Explanation: ADB (Asian Development Bank, 1966): HQ in Manila, Philippines. 68 member countries (49 from Asia-Pacific). Provides: project loans, policy-based loans, technical assistance, equity investments. India is one of ADB's largest borrowers (infrastructure, urban development, agriculture). USA and Japan are largest shareholders.

20. The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) was launched to:

  1. Provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected habitations (500+ population in plain areas, 250+ in hilly/tribal areas) — launched 2000, PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee
  2. Build national highways
  3. Build expressways
  4. Develop urban roads only

Answer: Provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected habitations (500+ population in plain areas, 250+ in hilly/tribal areas) — launched 2000, PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee

Explanation: PMGSY (2000): flagship scheme to provide all-weather road connectivity to unconnected rural habitations. Target: 500+ population habitations in plain areas, 250+ in NE/hilly/tribal. World's largest rural road program. PMGSY Phase III (2019): upgrade 1.25 lakh km of existing rural roads. Funded 60% Centre: 40% State (90:10 for NE/special category).

21. NABARD's function of refinancing is:

  1. Providing funds to cooperative banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and commercial banks against their agriculture/rural lending portfolio — enabling them to extend more credit to farmers
  2. Providing loans directly to farmers only
  3. Setting agricultural prices
  4. Managing FCI stocks

Answer: Providing funds to cooperative banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), and commercial banks against their agriculture/rural lending portfolio — enabling them to extend more credit to farmers

Explanation: NABARD refinancing: Short-term credit (seasonal agricultural operations: crop loans) and Long-term credit (land development, farm machinery, irrigation) to: District/State Cooperative Banks, Regional Rural Banks, Commercial Banks. NABARD also gives direct loans to state governments (RIDF — Rural Infrastructure Development Fund) for rural projects.

22. The Agri-Stack or Digital Agriculture Mission aims to:

  1. Create a digital public infrastructure (DPI) for agriculture — farmer registry, crop sown registry, farm boundaries using satellite/GIS to enable precision farming and targeted subsidies
  2. Replace all agricultural workers with robots
  3. Automate MSP procurement
  4. Create digital agricultural exports only

Answer: Create a digital public infrastructure (DPI) for agriculture — farmer registry, crop sown registry, farm boundaries using satellite/GIS to enable precision farming and targeted subsidies

Explanation: Digital Agriculture Mission / Agri-Stack (2021-2025): federated farmer database (Farmer ID linked to land records, Aadhaar), Crop Sown Registry (what crops are grown, digitized land records), Remote Sensing and GIS maps of farm plots. Enables: targeted input delivery (seeds, fertilizer), precision advisory (weather, crop insurance, agri-credit).

23. The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) is:

  1. Multilateral development bank proposed by China (2013), established 2015 — 106 members; HQ Beijing; funds infrastructure in Asia; India is the 2nd largest shareholder
  2. ADB branch
  3. World Bank subsidiary
  4. IMF initiative

Answer: Multilateral development bank proposed by China (2013), established 2015 — 106 members; HQ Beijing; funds infrastructure in Asia; India is the 2nd largest shareholder

Explanation: AIIB (Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, 2015): proposed by China's Xi Jinping. 57 founding members. HQ: Beijing. Focus: infrastructure (energy, transport, urban, rural, communications) in Asia. China: largest shareholder (~26%); India: 2nd largest (~8%). The US and Japan did not join (seeing it as China's geopolitical tool). India has received several AIIB loans for transport and urban infrastructure.

24. India's first Regional Rural Bank (RRB) was:

  1. Prathama Grameen Bank, established October 2, 1975 in Moradabad, Uttar Pradesh — sponsored by Syndicate Bank
  2. SBI Gramin Bank
  3. Syndicate Bank RRB
  4. Punjab National Bank Rural Bank

Answer: Prathama Grameen Bank, established October 2, 1975 in Moradabad, Uttar Pradesh — sponsored by Syndicate Bank

Explanation: Prathama Bank (now Prathama UP Gramin Bank after merger): first RRB established October 2, 1975, Gandhi Jayanti, in Moradabad, UP. Sponsored by Syndicate Bank. 5 RRBs simultaneously established as pilot. Based on Narasimham Committee recommendations (1975). Total 5 initial RRBs: Prathama (Moradabad), Gorakhpur Kshetriya Gramin (UP), Haryana Kshetriya Gramin (Bhiwani), Jaipur Nagaur Anchalik (Rajasthan), Singhbhum (Bihar).

25. India's 'Silent Revolution' in agriculture refers to:

  1. Second Green Revolution for eastern India (Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, UP) and diversification into horticulture, animal husbandry, and fisheries; also called 'Evergreen Revolution' by M.S. Swaminathan
  2. Original Green Revolution
  3. White Revolution
  4. Blue Revolution

Answer: Second Green Revolution for eastern India (Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, UP) and diversification into horticulture, animal husbandry, and fisheries; also called 'Evergreen Revolution' by M.S. Swaminathan

Explanation: Second Green Revolution/Evergreen Revolution (M.S. Swaminathan): need to extend agricultural productivity gains to eastern India and dryland farming regions while maintaining ecological sustainability. Also: shift from cereal monoculture to diversification (horticulture, dairying, poultry, fisheries) for nutritional security and farmer income.
