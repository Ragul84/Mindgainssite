26. India's 'Unified Portal for Agricultural Statistics' (UPAg) was launched to:

  1. Provide real-time agricultural data — crop sowing area, production estimates, MSP, market prices — to farmers, policymakers, and researchers via a single platform
  2. Replace FCI
  3. Automate MSP procurement
  4. Provide agri loans directly

Answer: Provide real-time agricultural data — crop sowing area, production estimates, MSP, market prices — to farmers, policymakers, and researchers via a single platform

Explanation: UPAg (2022, Ministry of Agriculture): first-of-its-kind single platform integrating agricultural statistics from multiple agencies. Data: crop sowing/area, production, trade, price (wholesale, retail, MSP), input costs, import/export. Makes data accessible to farmers via mobile app. Aims to improve crop planning and reduce information asymmetry.

27. The term 'crop diversification' in agriculture means:

  1. Shifting from monoculture to growing multiple crops including horticulture, spices, oilseeds, pulses — reduces risk, improves nutrition, and can increase farmer income compared to wheat/rice only
  2. Growing only wheat and rice
  3. Farming in multiple countries
  4. Diversification of farm ownership

Answer: Shifting from monoculture to growing multiple crops including horticulture, spices, oilseeds, pulses — reduces risk, improves nutrition, and can increase farmer income compared to wheat/rice only

Explanation: Crop Diversification: growing a variety of crops (different from traditional cereal monoculture). Benefits: reduces market and weather risk, improves soil health, nutrition security, higher value crops (horticulture → higher income than cereals). Government promotes with MSP for pulses and oilseeds, cluster development programs, and horticulture development programs.

28. The 'double cropping' system in agriculture refers to:

  1. Growing two crops per year on the same piece of land — kharif (June-November, monsoon crop: paddy, cotton, maize) + rabi (November-April, winter crop: wheat, mustard, chickpea)
  2. Growing crops in two countries
  3. Two varieties of same crop simultaneously
  4. Mixed cropping of two types

Answer: Growing two crops per year on the same piece of land — kharif (June-November, monsoon crop: paddy, cotton, maize) + rabi (November-April, winter crop: wheat, mustard, chickpea)

Explanation: Double Cropping: two crops sequentially in a year. India's cropping seasons: Kharif (sown with SW monsoon, harvested Oct-Nov): paddy, cotton, maize, soybean, groundnut. Rabi (sown Nov, harvested April): wheat, mustard, chickpea, barley. Zaid (short summer season, March-June): watermelon, cucumber, vegetables. Irrigation enables double/triple cropping (Punjab, Haryana, UP).

29. The Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY) is:

  1. Skill training and placement program for rural poor youth (15-35 years, BPL families) — provides free training in 250+ trades, placement guarantee; part of MGNREGA complementary scheme
  2. Scholarship for rural students
  3. Employment guarantee (MGNREGA is that)
  4. Urban skill development

Answer: Skill training and placement program for rural poor youth (15-35 years, BPL families) — provides free training in 250+ trades, placement guarantee; part of MGNREGA complementary scheme

Explanation: DDU-GKY (2014, renamed Aajeevika Skills/NRLM Skills): targets rural BPL youth. Free skill training, boarding, lodging during training, and placement assistance. Coverage: 15-35 years (extended to 45 for some groups). Target: place 75%+ trained youth in paid jobs. Implemented by Ministry of Rural Development through State Rural Livelihoods Missions.

30. The National Rural Livelihoods Mission (NRLM/Aajeevika) focuses on:

  1. Organizing rural poor into Self-Help Groups (SHGs) — credit linking, livelihood promotion, skill training; targets 10 crore rural poor women in 600 districts; launched 2011
  2. Only individual farmers
  3. Only agricultural credit
  4. Only employment guarantee (MGNREGA does that)

Answer: Organizing rural poor into Self-Help Groups (SHGs) — credit linking, livelihood promotion, skill training; targets 10 crore rural poor women in 600 districts; launched 2011

Explanation: NRLM (2011, renamed Aajeevika, now renamed DAY-NRLM — Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana): world's largest poverty alleviation program. Promotes SHGs (12-20 women) → federations (village organization) → cluster level federations → block/district level federations. Provides: bank credit linkage (SHG-BLP), livelihood promotion, skill training, financial literacy.

31. The Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM-AASHA) scheme comprises:

  1. Three sub-schemes: Price Support Scheme (PSS), Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS), and Private Procurement & Stockist Scheme (PPSS) — to ensure farmers get MSP even in market
  2. Only MSP announcement
  3. Only crop insurance
  4. Only direct income support

Answer: Three sub-schemes: Price Support Scheme (PSS), Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS), and Private Procurement & Stockist Scheme (PPSS) — to ensure farmers get MSP even in market

Explanation: PM-AASHA (2018): umbrella scheme for MSP implementation. PSS: government agencies physically procure crops when prices fall below MSP (for oilseeds, pulses, copra). PDPS: farmers sell in market, government pays difference between MSP and market price directly (for oilseeds). PPSS: private traders can also procure at MSP after paying security deposit (pilot for oilseeds).

32. The 'Lead Bank Scheme' in India was launched to:

  1. Assign one bank as 'lead bank' for each district — responsible for coordinating credit plans, financial inclusion, and SHG promotion in that district; launched 1969 on Narashimham Committee recommendation
  2. Provide leadership training to bankers
  3. Make one bank responsible for all banking in a district
  4. Replace cooperative banks

Answer: Assign one bank as 'lead bank' for each district — responsible for coordinating credit plans, financial inclusion, and SHG promotion in that district; launched 1969 on Narashimham Committee recommendation

Explanation: Lead Bank Scheme (1969): one bank (public sector) designated as lead bank per district (covering all talukas). Functions: preparing annual credit plan (ACP) for the district, coordinating with other banks, promoting financial inclusion, leading SLBC (State Level Bankers' Committee) work in the district. Essential for priority sector credit planning and rural financial inclusion.

33. India's Agriculture Export Policy 2018 targets:

  1. $60 billion agricultural exports by 2022 (now extended to 2025) from ~$30 billion baseline — through cluster-based approach, value addition, brand building (GI tags), and export infrastructure
  2. Only rice exports
  3. Only organic farming exports
  4. Import substitution

Answer: $60 billion agricultural exports by 2022 (now extended to 2025) from ~$30 billion baseline — through cluster-based approach, value addition, brand building (GI tags), and export infrastructure

Explanation: Agriculture Export Policy 2018 (Ministry of Commerce): targets doubling agri exports. Key features: Agri Export Zones (AEZ) concept, cluster-based approach (integrated 12 clusters initially), GI (Geographical Indication) products promotion (Darjeeling tea, Basmati rice, Alphonso mango), value addition promotion, infrastructure (cold chain, testing labs), e-commerce for agri exports.

34. The concept of 'mixed economy' in India post-1991 means:

  1. Market forces determine most resource allocation; government intervenes in: externalities, public goods, infrastructure, strategic sectors, social sector — less government control than pre-1991 but more than pure laissez-faire
  2. Pure market economy
  3. Pure socialist planned economy
  4. Government controlling all prices

Answer: Market forces determine most resource allocation; government intervenes in: externalities, public goods, infrastructure, strategic sectors, social sector — less government control than pre-1991 but more than pure laissez-faire

Explanation: Post-1991 India: hybrid economy — most industries open to private sector with market-based pricing, but government retains: defence production (DRDO, HAL), atomic energy, nuclear power, railways (mostly), strategic minerals, coal. Also intervenes: agricultural prices (MSP), petroleum prices (partially regulated), utilities (electricity distribution tariff regulation), social sector (healthcare, education). 'Market-friendly' rather than 'market-only.'

35. The Green Revolution (1960s-70s) was associated with:

  1. Norman Borlaug (Nobel Peace Prize 1970) — developed semi-dwarf HYV wheat varieties; worked with CIMMYT; brought to India by M.S. Swaminathan and promoted by PM Indira Gandhi's government
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru only
  3. Planning Commission alone
  4. NABARD's initiative

Answer: Norman Borlaug (Nobel Peace Prize 1970) — developed semi-dwarf HYV wheat varieties; worked with CIMMYT; brought to India by M.S. Swaminathan and promoted by PM Indira Gandhi's government

Explanation: Green Revolution: Norman Borlaug (USA, Nobel 1970) developed semi-dwarf wheat varieties (higher yield, resistant to lodging — bending with wind). Introduced to India by M.S. Swaminathan (who worked with IARI) under Agriculture Minister C. Subramaniam and PM Indira Gandhi's government. Punjab/Haryana became wheat-surplus states. Pakistan, Mexico also benefited. Also: IR-8 'miracle rice' (IRRI, Philippines).

36. Priority Sector Lending (PSL) requires Indian commercial banks to lend what percentage of ANBC to priority sectors?

  1. 25%
  2. 30%
  3. 40%
  4. 50%

Answer: 40%

Explanation: PSL target: 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) for domestic scheduled commercial banks. Priority sectors: Agriculture (18%), MSMEs, Education, Housing, Weaker Sections (10%).

37. The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) in India is conducted by:

  1. NITI Aayog
  2. Ministry of Labour
  3. National Statistical Office (NSO)
  4. NSSO under MOSPI

Answer: National Statistical Office (NSO)

Explanation: PLFS is conducted by NSO (National Statistical Office) under MoSPI. Launched 2017-18, provides quarterly urban + annual rural employment/unemployment estimates.

38. The Agriculture Produce Market Committee (APMC) Act was designed to:

  1. Export agricultural produce
  2. Protect farmers by regulating markets and preventing exploitation by middlemen
  3. Provide crop insurance
  4. Promote organic farming

Answer: Protect farmers by regulating markets and preventing exploitation by middlemen

Explanation: APMC Acts (state subject) created regulated mandis to ensure fair prices for farmers. Critics say they create cartels. The three Farm Acts 2020 (later repealed) aimed to allow sales outside APMCs.

39. E-NAM (electronic National Agriculture Market) is a pan-India online trading platform launched in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2016
  3. 2018
  4. 2020

Answer: 2016

Explanation: e-NAM launched April 14, 2016 by SFAC (Small Farmers Agribusiness Consortium) under Ministry of Agriculture. Connects APMC mandis online for transparent price discovery.

40. NABARD's Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) provides loans to:

  1. Farmers directly for crop loans
  2. State governments for rural infrastructure projects
  3. Commercial banks for priority sector shortfall
  4. Microfinance institutions

Answer: State governments for rural infrastructure projects

Explanation: RIDF: state governments and state-owned corporations borrow from NABARD to build rural infrastructure (roads, bridges, irrigation). Funded by shortfall in banks' PSL targets.

41. A price floor is effective (binding) only when it is set:

  1. Below the equilibrium price
  2. At the equilibrium price
  3. Above the equilibrium price
  4. At zero

Answer: Above the equilibrium price

Explanation: Price floor (minimum price) is binding only above equilibrium price, creating a surplus (supply > demand). E.g., MSP set above market price creates surplus procurement. Below equilibrium = non-binding.

42. The National Food Security Act (NFSA) 2013 provides subsidized food grains to what percentage of India's population?

  1. 50%
  2. 67%
  3. 75%
  4. 81%

Answer: 67%

Explanation: NFSA 2013: covers up to 75% rural + 50% urban population = ~67% of total population (~81.35 crore people). Provides 5 kg/person/month at subsidized rates: Rice Rs 3, Wheat Rs 2, Coarse grains Rs 1/kg.

43. Disguised unemployment is most commonly found in:

  1. Agricultural sector — more workers than needed; marginal productivity of labor is zero; removing them does not reduce output
  2. Manufacturing sector
  3. Services sector
  4. Government sector

Answer: Agricultural sector — more workers than needed; marginal productivity of labor is zero; removing them does not reduce output

Explanation: Disguised (concealed) unemployment: more people employed than necessary; additional workers add nothing to output (zero marginal product). Common in Indian agriculture (joint farming by family members). Unlike open unemployment (person seeks work but can't find it), disguised unemployment involves apparently 'working' people.

44. India's agricultural marketing is regulated through:

  1. State-level APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) Acts — mandated that farmers sell through regulated mandis; APMC reforms (Model APMC Act 2003) and Farm Laws 2020 (repealed 2021) aimed to allow direct selling
  2. Central government alone
  3. RBI
  4. NABARD directly

Answer: State-level APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) Acts — mandated that farmers sell through regulated mandis; APMC reforms (Model APMC Act 2003) and Farm Laws 2020 (repealed 2021) aimed to allow direct selling

Explanation: APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) system: most states have APMCs that regulate agricultural markets (mandis). Farmers had to sell produce only through licensed commission agents in regulated markets. Critiqued for middleman exploitation. Model APMC Act 2003: allow private markets, direct farmer-buyer links. Farm Laws 2020 (allowing direct selling, contract farming) were repealed November 2021 after farmer protests.

45. The NITI Aayog's 'Aspirational Districts Programme' targets:

  1. 112 most underdeveloped districts (selected based on composite index of health, nutrition, education, agriculture, financial inclusion, infrastructure, skill development) to rapidly improve outcomes
  2. All 740 districts
  3. Only tribal districts
  4. Only Northeast districts

Answer: 112 most underdeveloped districts (selected based on composite index of health, nutrition, education, agriculture, financial inclusion, infrastructure, skill development) to rapidly improve outcomes

Explanation: Aspirational Districts Programme (2018): 112 districts ranked on 49 indicators across 5 themes: Health & Nutrition, Education, Agriculture & Water Resources, Financial Inclusion & Skill Development, Basic Infrastructure. Real-time monitoring on Championing Change portal. 'Competitive and cooperative federalism' — districts compete and learn from each other. Success: significant improvement in health, nutrition, education indicators.

46. The ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist) programme was launched under which mission?

  1. ICDS
  2. National Health Mission (NHM)
  3. Ayushman Bharat
  4. National Rural Livelihood Mission

Answer: National Health Mission (NHM)

Explanation: ASHA workers: introduced under National Rural Health Mission (NRHM, 2005) — now NHM. ~10 lakh ASHAs serve as community health workers, linking villages to the healthcare system.

47. Minimum Support Price (MSP) for agricultural crops in India is recommended by which of the following bodies?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. NITI Aayog
  3. Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)
  4. National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD)

Answer: Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)

Explanation: CACP recommends MSP for 22 mandatory crops and sugarcane (where FRP applies), based on factors like cost of production, demand-supply, and international prices.

48. The primary objective of establishing Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) mandis was to:

  1. Promote contract farming exclusively
  2. Eliminate all middlemen from agricultural trade
  3. Regulate markets and protect farmers from exploitation by intermediaries
  4. Facilitate direct export of farm produce

Answer: Regulate markets and protect farmers from exploitation by intermediaries

Explanation: APMC mandis were set up to ensure fair prices, prevent exploitation by middlemen, and regulate agricultural trade through licensed buyers and regulated market yards.

49. The Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS) was introduced in India with the objective of:

  1. Providing free food grains to all citizens
  2. Restricting food subsidy to identified poor households
  3. Privatizing the public distribution system
  4. Replacing food grains with cash transfers to all farmers

Answer: Restricting food subsidy to identified poor households

Explanation: TPDS, introduced in 1997, focused subsidies on identified Below Poverty Line (BPL) families, distinguishing them from Above Poverty Line (APL) households to reduce fiscal burden.

50. The buffer stock operations of the Food Corporation of India (FCI) are primarily aimed at:

  1. Exporting food grains to earn foreign exchange
  2. Supporting industrial use of food grains
  3. Meeting contingencies and ensuring national food security
  4. Providing raw materials to agro-based industries

Answer: Meeting contingencies and ensuring national food security

Explanation: FCI maintains buffer stocks to meet exigencies like natural calamities, production shortfalls, and ensure food security through the Public Distribution System.
