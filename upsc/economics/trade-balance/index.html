Trade Balance MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Trade Balance questions to revise the UPSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 553 questions.

1. India's economic liberalisation was initiated in:

  1. 1947
  2. 1951
  3. 1991
  4. 2001

Answer: 1991

Explanation: India adopted LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation) reforms in 1991 under PM Narasimha Rao and FM Manmohan Singh, triggered by a balance of payments crisis.

2. India's largest trading partner (exports + imports combined) in recent years is:

  1. USA (largest export destination), China (largest import source) — China tops as overall trading partner by value
  2. Pakistan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. UAE

Answer: USA (largest export destination), China (largest import source) — China tops as overall trading partner by value

Explanation: India's top trading partners: USA is the largest export destination (~18% of India's exports). China is the largest source of imports (~15% of India's imports). As a bilateral trading partner (exports + imports combined), China and USA alternately top the list. UAE and Saudi Arabia are also major partners.

3. The term 'trade surplus' means:

  1. Exports > Imports (country earns more from exports than it spends on imports) — opposite of trade deficit
  2. Imports > Exports (trade deficit)
  3. Equal exports and imports
  4. High foreign exchange reserves

Answer: Exports > Imports (country earns more from exports than it spends on imports) — opposite of trade deficit

Explanation: Trade Balance = Exports - Imports. Trade Surplus: Exports > Imports (positive balance). Trade Deficit: Imports > Exports (negative balance). India consistently runs a trade deficit (imports more than exports). India's trade deficit ~$240 billion (2022-23). Offset partially by services surplus, remittances. China and Germany have large trade surpluses.

4. The One District One Product (ODOP) scheme promotes:

  1. Each district of India to develop and market one unique product — integrates local crafts, agriculture, and industry into value chain; 761 products from 761 districts identified
  2. One crop per district
  3. One factory per district
  4. One bank per district

Answer: Each district of India to develop and market one unique product — integrates local crafts, agriculture, and industry into value chain; 761 products from 761 districts identified

Explanation: ODOP (2018, DPIIT under Ministry of Commerce): identifies and promotes one distinctive product per district. Products range from handicrafts (Pashmina, Kanjeevaram silk) to food products (Rasgulla of Kolkata, Darjeeling tea) to agricultural products. Creates a brand identity, links artisans/farmers to markets, and promotes exports.

5. The Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (ECGC) of India provides:

  1. Credit insurance to Indian exporters against risk of non-payment by foreign buyers, and guarantees to banks that finance exports — established 1957, HQ Mumbai
  2. Direct export finance
  3. Import finance
  4. Port services

Answer: Credit insurance to Indian exporters against risk of non-payment by foreign buyers, and guarantees to banks that finance exports — established 1957, HQ Mumbai

Explanation: ECGC (Export Credit Guarantee Corporation, 1957, Ministry of Commerce): provides export credit insurance to protect Indian exporters against payment default by foreign buyers (due to commercial/political risk). Also gives counter-guarantees/guarantees to banks providing export finance. Enables Indian companies to export to risky markets with insurance protection.

6. The term 'dumping' in international trade means:

  1. Exporting goods to a foreign market at prices below production cost or below domestic price — considered unfair trade practice; subject to anti-dumping duties under WTO rules
  2. Disposing industrial waste
  3. Selling expired goods
  4. Selling goods at higher price abroad

Answer: Exporting goods to a foreign market at prices below production cost or below domestic price — considered unfair trade practice; subject to anti-dumping duties under WTO rules

Explanation: Dumping: selling exported goods below cost of production (or below home market price) to gain market share in foreign country. Damages domestic industries in the importing country. WTO's Anti-Dumping Agreement: allows importing country to impose anti-dumping duties to offset price difference. India frequently imposes anti-dumping duties on Chinese goods.

7. India's Merchandise Exports have the following major categories:

  1. Engineering goods (~26%), petroleum products (~17%), gems & jewellery (~12%), chemicals (~12%), textiles & apparel (~10%) are top categories
  2. Only IT services
  3. Only agricultural goods
  4. Only automobiles

Answer: Engineering goods (~26%), petroleum products (~17%), gems & jewellery (~12%), chemicals (~12%), textiles & apparel (~10%) are top categories

Explanation: India's top merchandise export categories: Engineering goods (machinery, electronics, metal products), Petroleum products (refined petroleum), Gems and Jewellery (cut diamonds, gold jewellery), Chemicals and pharmaceuticals, Textiles and apparel. Services exports are separate and even larger: IT/ITES (~$250 billion). Total merchandise exports: ~$450 billion; services: ~$250+ billion.

8. India's largest agricultural export in recent years has been:

  1. Marine products (seafood — shrimps/prawns: ~70% of marine exports) and rice (both non-basmati and basmati); also engineering goods, petroleum products compete for top position
  2. Tea only
  3. Coffee only
  4. Spices only

Answer: Marine products (seafood — shrimps/prawns: ~70% of marine exports) and rice (both non-basmati and basmati); also engineering goods, petroleum products compete for top position

Explanation: India's top agricultural exports: Non-basmati rice (became no. 1 rice exporter globally), Marine products (mainly vannamei shrimps to USA, Japan, China), Basmati rice, Spices, Sugar, Cotton, Fresh fruits/vegetables. APEDA oversees agricultural exports. India's total agri exports: ~$50 billion (2022-23).

9. In India's federal structure, 'Concurrent List' (List III, 7th Schedule) includes:

  1. Subjects where both Centre and State can legislate — education, forests, family planning, labour, trade unions, price control, bankruptcy, drugs, courts, population control — Centre's law prevails in conflict
  2. Subjects only Centre can legislate (Union List — 97 items)
  3. Subjects only States can legislate (State List — 66 items)
  4. Local government subjects

Answer: Subjects where both Centre and State can legislate — education, forests, family planning, labour, trade unions, price control, bankruptcy, drugs, courts, population control — Centre's law prevails in conflict

Explanation: Concurrent List (52 subjects): both Parliament and State Legislatures can make laws. In case of conflict: Central law prevails (repugnancy doctrine, Article 254). Examples: education (42nd Amendment moved from State to Concurrent), forests, labour laws, trade unions, social security, price control, commercial and industrial monopolies. States have more practical importance in Concurrent subjects.

10. The term 'terms of trade' in international economics refers to:

  1. Ratio of export price index to import price index — if terms of trade improve (exports become relatively more expensive), country can import more with same exports; important for commodity exporters
  2. Trade agreements
  3. Trade volumes
  4. Trade deficits

Answer: Ratio of export price index to import price index — if terms of trade improve (exports become relatively more expensive), country can import more with same exports; important for commodity exporters

Explanation: Terms of Trade (ToT) = (Export Price Index / Import Price Index) × 100. If ToT rises: country's exports buy more imports → welfare improves. If ToT falls: exports buy fewer imports → welfare falls. Important for developing countries exporting primary commodities (prices volatile, tend to fall relative to manufactured goods — Prebisch-Singer hypothesis). India: ToT typically negative due to oil imports.

11. India's current account in BoP became a surplus briefly in which year?

  1. 2020-21 (April-September, COVID quarters) — unprecedented surplus due to collapse of oil imports (lockdown, travel), surge in IT services demand, decline in gold imports, and massive remittances
  2. 1991 crisis year
  3. 2008 global crisis
  4. Every 5 years

Answer: 2020-21 (April-September, COVID quarters) — unprecedented surplus due to collapse of oil imports (lockdown, travel), surge in IT services demand, decline in gold imports, and massive remittances

Explanation: India typically runs current account deficit. Exception: 2020-21 Q1 (Apr-Jun): surplus of $19.1 billion (unprecedented) due to: COVID lockdowns → oil import collapse, gold import collapse; while IT service exports boomed (work from home), remittances remained strong. As economy reopened and imports recovered, deficit returned in 2021-22.

12. Who introduced the concept of Absolute Advantage in international trade?

  1. David Ricardo
  2. Adam Smith
  3. John Stuart Mill
  4. Alfred Marshall

Answer: Adam Smith

Explanation: Adam Smith in 'The Wealth of Nations' (1776) introduced Absolute Advantage — a country should export goods it can produce more efficiently (absolutely) than others.

13. The Heckscher-Ohlin theorem states that a country will export goods that are intensive in its:

  1. Scarce factor of production
  2. Abundant factor of production
  3. Advanced technology
  4. Cheapest imports

Answer: Abundant factor of production

Explanation: The H-O theorem: a country exports goods that intensively use its abundant factors. E.g., labour-abundant India exports labour-intensive goods.

14. The Leontief Paradox showed that the USA, a capital-abundant nation, exported:

  1. Capital-intensive goods
  2. Labour-intensive goods
  3. Technology-intensive goods
  4. Agricultural goods

Answer: Labour-intensive goods

Explanation: Wassily Leontief (1953) found the USA paradoxically exported labour-intensive goods, contradicting the H-O theorem. This is called the Leontief Paradox.

15. Currency depreciation differs from devaluation in that depreciation is:

  1. Government-decreed reduction in exchange rate
  2. Market-driven fall in currency value
  3. IMF-mandated adjustment
  4. Fixed exchange rate change

Answer: Market-driven fall in currency value

Explanation: Depreciation = market-driven fall in currency value (floating exchange rates). Devaluation = government-decreed reduction under fixed/pegged exchange rate system.

16. Which component of Balance of Payments records foreign direct investment and portfolio investment?

  1. Current Account
  2. Capital Account
  3. Financial Account
  4. Trade Account

Answer: Financial Account

Explanation: The Financial Account records FDI, FPI (portfolio investment), and other investment flows. The Capital Account records capital transfers and non-produced assets.

17. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) theory suggests that exchange rates should equalize:

  1. Interest rates across countries
  2. Wage rates across countries
  3. Price levels across countries
  4. Inflation rates across countries

Answer: Price levels across countries

Explanation: PPP: exchange rates adjust so that identical goods cost the same across countries. The 'Big Mac Index' by The Economist is a popular PPP measure.

18. The current account of Balance of Payments includes all EXCEPT:

  1. Merchandise trade
  2. Services trade
  3. Income (salaries/dividends)
  4. Foreign Direct Investment

Answer: Foreign Direct Investment

Explanation: FDI belongs to the Financial Account. The Current Account covers: (1) merchandise trade, (2) services, (3) primary income (wages/investment income), (4) secondary income (remittances).

19. Dadabhai Naoroji's 'Drain Theory' argued that British rule caused economic drain through:

  1. Cultural imperialism
  2. Forced export of raw materials and remittance of profits/salaries to Britain without equivalent return
  3. Military conquest
  4. Religious conversion

Answer: Forced export of raw materials and remittance of profits/salaries to Britain without equivalent return

Explanation: Naoroji (1867): calculated India's 'drain' — export surplus not returned as imports but as remittances to Britain (Home Charges, profit repatriation). Estimated drain as ~£15-30 million/year.

20. Export-Import Bank of India (EXIM Bank) was established to:

  1. Finance and facilitate India's international trade and export-oriented industries; established 1982, HQ Mumbai; provides buyer's/supplier's credit
  2. Finance agriculture
  3. Provide personal loans
  4. Manage foreign exchange

Answer: Finance and facilitate India's international trade and export-oriented industries; established 1982, HQ Mumbai; provides buyer's/supplier's credit

Explanation: EXIM Bank (Export-Import Bank of India, 1982, HQ Mumbai): fully government-owned. Functions: long-term export credit to foreign buyers of Indian goods (buyer's credit), supplier's credit to Indian exporters, project export finance, overseas investment finance, and Lines of Credit to foreign governments for import of Indian goods.

21. The One District One Product (ODOP) initiative under Aatmanirbhar Bharat promotes:

  1. Agricultural products from one district per state
  2. Local products, crafts, and industries that each district is known for
  3. One factory per district
  4. Uniform product standards across districts

Answer: Local products, crafts, and industries that each district is known for

Explanation: ODOP: each district identifies a signature product (handicraft, GI-tagged item, agricultural produce) for promotion, branding, and export. Started in UP (2018), scaled nationally under Aatmanirbhar Bharat.

22. India's Foreign Trade Policy 2023-28 sets a target for merchandise and services exports by 2030 of:

  1. $1 trillion merchandise + $1 trillion services
  2. $2 trillion merchandise + $1 trillion services
  3. $1 trillion total exports
  4. $2 trillion total exports

Answer: $2 trillion merchandise + $1 trillion services

Explanation: FTP 2023-28 (effective April 1, 2023): target $2 trillion merchandise + $1 trillion services exports by 2030 = $3 trillion total. Key focus: e-commerce exports, ease of doing business, districts as export hubs.

23. What is the primary impact of rupee depreciation on India's trade balance?

  1. Increase in exports and decrease in imports
  2. Decrease in exports and increase in imports
  3. No impact on trade balance
  4. Increase in both exports and imports

Answer: Increase in exports and decrease in imports

Explanation: Rupee depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports costlier, thus increasing exports and decreasing imports.

24. Which of the following is a component of the balance of payments that affects the trade balance?

  1. Capital account
  2. Current account
  3. Financial account
  4. Official reserve account

Answer: Current account

Explanation: The current account includes the trade balance, which comprises exports and imports of goods and services.

25. What is the effect of sterilized interventions by the RBI on the trade balance?

  1. Increase in money supply, decrease in trade balance
  2. Decrease in money supply, increase in trade balance
  3. No impact on trade balance
  4. Increase in both money supply and trade balance

Answer: Decrease in money supply, increase in trade balance

Explanation: Sterilized interventions reduce money supply, which can lead to higher interest rates and increased trade balance.
