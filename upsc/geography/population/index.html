Population MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Population questions to revise the UPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Kaziranga National Park (Assam) is known for:

  1. One-horned Indian rhinoceros
  2. Bengal tiger only
  3. Asian elephant
  4. Snow leopard

Answer: One-horned Indian rhinoceros

Explanation: Kaziranga National Park in Assam has the world's largest population of the one-horned Indian rhinoceros (about 2,400 — 70% of the world's population). It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

2. The most populous state in India is:

  1. Uttar Pradesh
  2. Maharashtra
  3. Bihar
  4. West Bengal

Answer: Uttar Pradesh

Explanation: Uttar Pradesh (population ~200 million, 2011 census) is the most populous state in India, followed by Maharashtra and Bihar.

3. Which state/UT has the highest literacy rate in India (as per census)?

  1. Kerala
  2. Mizoram
  3. Goa
  4. Tripura

Answer: Kerala

Explanation: Kerala consistently has India's highest literacy rate — 96.2% as per 2011 census, and even higher in recent NFHS surveys. Kerala's high literacy is attributed to colonial-era missionary schools and state focus on education.

4. The most densely populated state in India is:

  1. Bihar
  2. West Bengal
  3. Uttar Pradesh
  4. Kerala

Answer: Bihar

Explanation: Bihar has the highest population density in India (1,106 persons/sq km as per 2011 census), followed by West Bengal (1,028). UP has the highest absolute population.

5. The sex ratio (females per 1000 males) in India as per 2011 census was:

  1. 940
  2. 927
  3. 966
  4. 933

Answer: 940

Explanation: India's sex ratio was 940 females per 1000 males (2011 census), up from 933 (2001). Kerala has the highest sex ratio (1084) and Haryana the lowest (879). Tamil Nadu's sex ratio is 995.

6. The average annual growth rate of India's population (2001-2011) was:

  1. 1.64%
  2. 2.5%
  3. 3%
  4. 1%

Answer: 1.64%

Explanation: India's population grew from 1.028 billion (2001) to 1.21 billion (2011), at an average annual growth rate of 1.64%. This was lower than the 1991-2001 rate of 1.93%, showing slowing growth.

7. Project Elephant was launched in India in:

  1. 1992
  2. 1973
  3. 1980
  4. 2005

Answer: 1992

Explanation: Project Elephant was launched in 1992 by the Government of India to protect elephant habitats, study elephant populations, and address human-elephant conflict. India has about 60% of the world's Asian elephants.

8. The Great Indian Bustard is critically endangered and primarily found in:

  1. Rajasthan (and parts of Gujarat, Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka, AP)
  2. Assam
  3. Kerala
  4. Tamil Nadu

Answer: Rajasthan (and parts of Gujarat, Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka, AP)

Explanation: The Great Indian Bustard (Ardeotis nigriceps) is critically endangered — only about 100-150 remain. Primary habitat: Desert National Park in Rajasthan, with small populations in Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Karnataka.

9. The One-horned Rhinoceros in India is mainly in:

  1. Kaziranga (Assam) and Manas National Park
  2. Sundarbans
  3. Jim Corbett
  4. Gir

Answer: Kaziranga (Assam) and Manas National Park

Explanation: The Indian One-horned Rhinoceros population is mainly in Kaziranga National Park, Assam (~2,400 rhinos — 70% of the world's population), Manas (Assam), and Jaldapara (West Bengal).

10. Which state has the most number of tiger reserves in India?

  1. Madhya Pradesh
  2. Uttarakhand
  3. Karnataka
  4. Maharashtra

Answer: Madhya Pradesh

Explanation: Madhya Pradesh has the most tiger reserves (7) and the highest tiger population in India. Kanha, Bandhavgarh, Pench, Satpura, Panna, Bori-Satpura, and Ratapani are in MP. MP is called the 'Tiger State of India'.

11. Which state has the highest population density in India (Census 2011)?

  1. Uttar Pradesh
  2. Maharashtra
  3. Bihar (1102 persons/km²)
  4. West Bengal

Answer: Bihar (1102 persons/km²)

Explanation: Bihar had the highest population density of 1102 persons/km² in Census 2011. West Bengal was second (1029/km²). Among Union Territories, Delhi has the highest density. Arunachal Pradesh has the lowest (17/km²).

12. The largest continent in the world is:

  1. Asia
  2. Africa
  3. North America
  4. Antarctica

Answer: Asia

Explanation: Asia is the world's largest continent (44.6 million sq km), home to about 60% of the world's population. Africa is the second largest (30.4 million sq km).

13. The smallest country in the world (by area) is:

  1. Vatican City
  2. Monaco
  3. San Marino
  4. Nauru

Answer: Vatican City

Explanation: Vatican City (0.44 sq km) is the world's smallest country by both area and population. It is an independent city-state within Rome, Italy, and is the headquarters of the Roman Catholic Church.

14. The most populous country in the world (as of 2024) is:

  1. India
  2. China
  3. USA
  4. Indonesia

Answer: India

Explanation: India surpassed China in population in 2023, becoming the world's most populous country with about 1.44 billion people. China's population is declining due to its one-child policy effects.

15. What is the primary factor influencing population distribution in mountainous regions?

  1. Climate
  2. Relief
  3. Vegetation
  4. Soil

Answer: Relief

Explanation: Relief is the primary factor as it affects accessibility and settlement patterns.

16. Which of the following rivers has the largest population living in its basin?

  1. Ganga
  2. Brahmaputra
  3. Indus
  4. Godavari

Answer: Ganga

Explanation: Ganga river basin has the largest population due to fertile plains.

17. What is the term for the study of the geographical distribution of population?

  1. Demography
  2. Geography
  3. Anthropology
  4. Sociology

Answer: Demography

Explanation: Demography is the study of population statistics and distribution.

18. Which monsoon mechanism affects population settlement patterns in India?

  1. Trade Winds
  2. Westerlies
  3. Southwest Monsoon
  4. Northeast Monsoon

Answer: Southwest Monsoon

Explanation: Southwest Monsoon influences settlement due to seasonal rainfall patterns.

19. What is the population phenomenon where people move from rural to urban areas?

  1. Urbanization
  2. Ruralization
  3. Migration
  4. Displacement

Answer: Urbanization

Explanation: Urbanization is the process of people moving to cities for better opportunities.

20. Which strait separates the Indian subcontinent from Sri Lanka?

  1. Palk Strait
  2. Malacca Strait
  3. Bosphorus Strait
  4. Sunda Strait

Answer: Palk Strait

Explanation: Palk Strait separates India from Sri Lanka.

21. What is the term for areas with high population density and urban development?

  1. Megalopolis
  2. Metropolis
  3. Conurbation
  4. Agglomeration

Answer: Megalopolis

Explanation: Megalopolis refers to a large urban region with high population density.

22. Which climate pattern affects population distribution in the Thar Desert?

  1. Tropical
  2. Temperate
  3. Desert
  4. Alpine

Answer: Desert

Explanation: Desert climate affects population due to harsh conditions and limited resources.

23. What is the process of people moving to a new country for permanent residence?

  1. Immigration
  2. Emigration
  3. Migration
  4. Refuge

Answer: Immigration

Explanation: Immigration is the process of moving to a new country for permanent residence.

24. Which geographical feature affects population distribution in the Himalayan region?

  1. Mountains
  2. Plains
  3. Plateaus
  4. Coastlines

Answer: Mountains

Explanation: Mountains affect population due to rugged terrain and limited accessibility.

25. What is the term for the movement of people from rural to urban areas?

  1. Urbanization
  2. Ruralization
  3. Migration
  4. Emigration

Answer: Urbanization

Explanation: Urbanization refers to the movement of people from rural to urban areas, often driven by economic opportunities.
