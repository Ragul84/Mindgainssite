Physical India MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Physical India questions to revise the UPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. The world's largest ocean is:

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Pacific Ocean

Explanation: The Pacific Ocean (165 million sq km) is the world's largest ocean. It contains the Mariana Trench — the deepest point on Earth (11,034 m).

27. The Suez Canal connects:

  1. Red Sea and Mediterranean Sea
  2. Red Sea and Indian Ocean
  3. Persian Gulf and Red Sea
  4. Caspian Sea and Black Sea

Answer: Red Sea and Mediterranean Sea

Explanation: The Suez Canal (193 km, Egypt) connects the Red Sea to the Mediterranean Sea. Opened in 1869, it allows ships to travel between Europe and Asia without going around Africa, reducing the journey by ~7,000 km.

28. The Panama Canal connects:

  1. Atlantic Ocean and Pacific Ocean
  2. Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea
  3. South Atlantic and North Atlantic
  4. Caribbean Sea and Amazon River

Answer: Atlantic Ocean and Pacific Ocean

Explanation: The Panama Canal (82 km, Panama) connects the Atlantic (via Caribbean Sea) and Pacific Ocean. Opened in 1914, ships avoid going around South America (Cape Horn), saving about 12,000 km.

29. Chilika Lake is located in:

  1. Odisha
  2. Andhra Pradesh
  3. Tamil Nadu
  4. West Bengal

Answer: Odisha

Explanation: Chilika Lake (1,100 sq km) in Odisha is India's largest coastal lagoon and Asia's largest brackish water lake. It is a Ramsar wetland site famous for migratory birds and Irrawaddy dolphins.

30. The Coromandel Coast runs along the:

  1. Southeastern coast of India (Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh)
  2. Southwestern coast
  3. Western coast
  4. Northeastern coast

Answer: Southeastern coast of India (Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh)

Explanation: The Coromandel Coast is the southeastern coast of India — from Kanyakumari (Tamil Nadu) to Orissa Mahanadi delta, covering Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh coastline along the Bay of Bengal.

31. The state with the largest area in India is:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Madhya Pradesh
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Uttar Pradesh

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: Rajasthan (342,239 sq km) is the largest state by area in India, followed by Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra. UP is the most populous state.

32. The Brahmaputra river valley in Assam forms:

  1. A long, narrow alluvial plain between Himalayas and Shillong Plateau
  2. A wide delta
  3. A plateau region
  4. A high-altitude valley

Answer: A long, narrow alluvial plain between Himalayas and Shillong Plateau

Explanation: The Brahmaputra valley in Assam is a narrow alluvial plain (~80 km wide, 700 km long) between the Himalayas to the north and the Shillong Plateau/Meghalaya hills to the south.

33. India's first railway line was between:

  1. Bombay (Mumbai) and Thane (1853)
  2. Calcutta and Howrah
  3. Delhi and Agra
  4. Madras (Chennai) and Arcot

Answer: Bombay (Mumbai) and Thane (1853)

Explanation: India's first passenger train ran on 16 April 1853 between Bombay (Bori Bunder) and Thane (34 km), operated by Great Indian Peninsula Railway. It had 3 locomotives: Sahib, Sindh, and Sultan.

34. India's first Metro rail was established in:

  1. Kolkata (1984)
  2. Delhi (2002)
  3. Mumbai (2014)
  4. Chennai (2015)

Answer: Kolkata (1984)

Explanation: Kolkata Metro was India's first metro rail system, inaugurated on 24 October 1984 (though partial opening was in 1983). Delhi Metro (2002) is currently the largest metro network in India.

35. The Western Ghats are a biodiversity hotspot. How many biodiversity hotspots are there globally?

  1. 36 hotspots
  2. 25 hotspots
  3. 50 hotspots
  4. 18 hotspots

Answer: 36 hotspots

Explanation: There are 36 global biodiversity hotspots as recognized by Conservation International. India has 4 hotspots: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma region, and Sundaland (includes Andaman & Nicobar).

36. The Kyoto Protocol (1997) was related to:

  1. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions
  2. Nuclear disarmament
  3. Protection of wetlands
  4. Ozone layer protection

Answer: Reducing greenhouse gas emissions

Explanation: The Kyoto Protocol (1997, entered force 2005) was the first binding international agreement on greenhouse gas emission reductions. It set targets for developed countries. USA never ratified it.

37. 'Biodiversity hotspot' criteria requires the area to have:

  1. 1,500+ endemic plant species and lost 70%+ of original habitat
  2. Any rare species
  3. 100+ endemic animal species
  4. Dense forest cover

Answer: 1,500+ endemic plant species and lost 70%+ of original habitat

Explanation: A biodiversity hotspot must meet two criteria: (1) contain at least 1,500 species of endemic vascular plants, and (2) have lost at least 70% of its original habitat. Western Ghats meets both.

38. The easternmost point of India is:

  1. Kibithu, Arunachal Pradesh
  2. Imphal, Manipur
  3. Kohima, Nagaland
  4. Agartala, Tripura

Answer: Kibithu, Arunachal Pradesh

Explanation: Kibithu in Anjaw district, Arunachal Pradesh (97.5°E) is the easternmost village/point of India. India's easternmost extent is about 97°25'E longitude.

39. The westernmost point of India is:

  1. Sir Creek area, Gujarat
  2. Lakshadweep Islands
  3. Dwaraka, Gujarat
  4. Kutch, Gujarat

Answer: Sir Creek area, Gujarat

Explanation: The westernmost point of India's mainland is the Sir Creek area/western Rann of Kutch, Gujarat (approximately 68°7'E). India's westernmost extent is about 68°7'E longitude.

40. The McMahon Line is the boundary between:

  1. India and China (Arunachal Pradesh area)
  2. India and Pakistan
  3. India and Nepal
  4. India and Bangladesh

Answer: India and China (Arunachal Pradesh area)

Explanation: The McMahon Line (1914, Simla Convention) is the disputed boundary between India and China (Tibet) in the northeastern Himalayas. China does not recognize this line; it claims Arunachal Pradesh as 'South Tibet'.

41. Which is the only river that flows from the Deccan Plateau into the Arabian Sea?

  1. Narmada
  2. Tapti (Tapi)
  3. Both Narmada and Tapti
  4. Mahi

Answer: Both Narmada and Tapti

Explanation: Narmada and Tapti (Tapi) are the only two major rivers that flow west from the Deccan Plateau/interior of India and drain into the Arabian Sea. All other major Peninsular rivers flow east into the Bay of Bengal.

42. Banihal Pass is the route to:

  1. Kashmir Valley from Jammu (J&K)
  2. Ladakh from Kashmir
  3. Himachal Pradesh from Punjab
  4. Uttarakhand from UP

Answer: Kashmir Valley from Jammu (J&K)

Explanation: Banihal Pass (2,832 m) in J&K connects Jammu with the Kashmir Valley. The Banihal Tunnel (Jawahar Tunnel, 2.5 km) provides an all-weather road link. The Banihal-Qazigund rail tunnel is the longest in India.

43. Lipulekh Pass is used for:

  1. Kailash Mansarovar Yatra — border between India (Uttarakhand) and China (Tibet)
  2. India-Nepal trade
  3. India-Bhutan border
  4. Ladakh access

Answer: Kailash Mansarovar Yatra — border between India (Uttarakhand) and China (Tibet)

Explanation: Lipulekh Pass (5,334 m) in Uttarakhand (Pithoragarh district) connects India with Tibet (China). It is used for the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra. It is also disputed between India and Nepal.

44. The Zoji La Pass connects:

  1. Kashmir Valley and Ladakh
  2. Jammu and Kashmir
  3. Himachal Pradesh and Ladakh
  4. Kargil and Srinagar

Answer: Kashmir Valley and Ladakh

Explanation: Zoji La Pass (3,528 m) in J&K connects the Kashmir Valley (Srinagar) with Ladakh (Kargil, Leh). It is one of the highest motorable passes in India. The Zoji La Tunnel is under construction to provide all-weather connectivity.

45. India's first Special Economic Zone (SEZ) was established at:

  1. Kandla (Gujarat)
  2. NOIDA
  3. Mumbai
  4. Chennai

Answer: Kandla (Gujarat)

Explanation: India's first SEZ was established at Kandla (Gandhidham) in Gujarat in 1965, making it one of the world's first SEZs. The SEZ policy was formalized in 2000 and the SEZ Act was passed in 2005.

46. Yellow Revolution in India is associated with:

  1. Oilseed production (edible oils)
  2. Wheat production
  3. Pulses
  4. Sugar

Answer: Oilseed production (edible oils)

Explanation: Yellow Revolution refers to the rapid increase in oilseed production in India (primarily sunflower, mustard, groundnut, soybean). The Technology Mission on Oilseeds (1986) launched by PM Rajiv Gandhi triggered this.

47. White Revolution in India is associated with:

  1. Milk production (Operation Flood)
  2. Sugar production
  3. Cotton production
  4. Salt production

Answer: Milk production (Operation Flood)

Explanation: White Revolution (Operation Flood, 1970-1996) under Dr. Verghese Kurien (Father of White Revolution) transformed India from a milk-deficient country to the world's largest milk producer through cooperative dairy development (AMUL model).

48. Operation Flood (White Revolution) was implemented by:

  1. National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under Dr. Verghese Kurien
  2. Food Corporation of India
  3. Ministry of Agriculture
  4. NABARD

Answer: National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under Dr. Verghese Kurien

Explanation: Operation Flood was implemented by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) under Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India'). AMUL (Anand Milk Union Limited) was the model cooperative.

49. India's largest tea-producing state is:

  1. Assam
  2. West Bengal
  3. Tamil Nadu
  4. Kerala

Answer: Assam

Explanation: Assam is India's largest tea-producing state (about 53% of India's tea). Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling), and Tamil Nadu (Nilgiris) are the three major tea-growing regions in India.

50. Tobacco production in India is mainly in:

  1. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana
  2. Gujarat
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Karnataka

Answer: Andhra Pradesh and Telangana

Explanation: Andhra Pradesh (especially Guntur) and Telangana produce about 50%+ of India's tobacco (Virginia/flue-cured variety). India is the world's third-largest tobacco producer after China and Brazil.
