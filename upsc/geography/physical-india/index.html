Physical India MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Physical India questions to revise the UPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?

  1. 6
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 9

Answer: 8

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.

2. Which soil is most suitable for cotton cultivation in India?

  1. Alluvial soil
  2. Red soil
  3. Black (Regur) soil
  4. Laterite soil

Answer: Black (Regur) soil

Explanation: Black soil (Regur soil) is most suitable for cotton cultivation as it has good moisture retention. The Deccan Plateau is famous for this type of soil.

3. The Western Ghats are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. How many hotspot regions are in the Western Ghats?

  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 8
  4. 12

Answer: 8

Explanation: The Western Ghats has 8 hotspot clusters recognised by UNESCO (2012) as outstanding examples of biological diversity.

4. The latitude that divides India roughly into northern and southern halves passes through:

  1. Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)
  2. 30°N latitude
  3. Standard Meridian (82.5°E)
  4. Equator (0°)

Answer: Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N)

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N) passes through the middle of India, dividing it into tropical south and subtropical north.

5. Which is the highest waterfall in India?

  1. Jog Falls
  2. Dudhsagar Falls
  3. Nohkalikai Falls
  4. Kunchikal Falls

Answer: Kunchikal Falls

Explanation: Kunchikal Falls in Karnataka (455 m) is the highest waterfall in India. Nohkalikai (340 m) in Meghalaya is often confused for this, but Kunchikal is higher.

6. The concept of 'Rain Shadow' region is associated with which type of rainfall?

  1. Cyclonic rainfall
  2. Convectional rainfall
  3. Orographic (relief) rainfall
  4. Frontal rainfall

Answer: Orographic (relief) rainfall

Explanation: In orographic rainfall, moisture-laden winds rise over a mountain range, cool, and precipitate on the windward side. The leeward side remains dry — this is the 'rain shadow' region. E.g., Shillong is on the windward side; the Deccan Plateau is in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats.

7. The 'Hot Spot' biodiversity theory was developed by which scientist?

  1. Charles Darwin
  2. Norman Myers
  3. E.O. Wilson
  4. Gregor Mendel

Answer: Norman Myers

Explanation: The concept of biodiversity hotspots was developed by British ecologist Norman Myers in 1988. Hotspots are areas with exceptional concentrations of endemic species under threat.

8. The 'Deccan Traps' are formed by:

  1. Folding of land
  2. Faulting and rifting
  3. Massive volcanic eruptions (flood basalts)
  4. River deposition over millions of years

Answer: Massive volcanic eruptions (flood basalts)

Explanation: The Deccan Traps are formed by massive flood basalt eruptions that occurred at the end of the Cretaceous period (about 66 million years ago), covering much of the Deccan Plateau.

9. The 'Roaring Forties', 'Furious Fifties', and 'Screaming Sixties' are associated with:

  1. Monsoon winds
  2. Polar easterlies
  3. Strong westerly winds in southern hemisphere
  4. Tropical cyclones

Answer: Strong westerly winds in southern hemisphere

Explanation: These are strong westerly winds that blow in the southern hemisphere between 40°-70°S latitude. They are unimpeded by land masses and are very strong.

10. The highest peak in India is:

  1. Mt. Everest
  2. Kanchenjunga
  3. K2 (Godwin-Austen)
  4. Nanga Parbat

Answer: K2 (Godwin-Austen)

Explanation: K2 (Godwin-Austen, 8611m) in the Karakoram Range is the highest peak in India (in the Pakistan-India disputed territory of Gilgit-Baltistan). Within undisputed India, Kanchenjunga (8586m) on the Sikkim-Nepal border is the highest. Mt. Everest is in Nepal.

11. The driest region of India is:

  1. Rajasthan Thar Desert (but Tamil Nadu's Ramanathapuram gets ~350mm)
  2. Leh, Ladakh — gets <100mm annual rainfall
  3. Rajasthan (Barmer area)
  4. Gujarat (Kutch area)

Answer: Leh, Ladakh — gets <100mm annual rainfall

Explanation: Leh (Ladakh) receives less than 100mm of precipitation annually, making it the driest inhabited region of India. The Thar Desert in Rajasthan gets 100–250mm. The Himalayan rain shadow effect keeps Ladakh extremely dry.

12. The Godavari is called the 'Dakshin Ganga' because:

  1. It is the largest river in Peninsular India
  2. It flows south
  3. It is sacred like Ganga
  4. It originates from the Himalayas

Answer: It is the largest river in Peninsular India

Explanation: Godavari is called Dakshin Ganga (Ganga of the South) because it is the largest and most sacred river in Peninsular India, originating from Trimbak near Nashik, Maharashtra, flowing 1,465 km to the Bay of Bengal.

13. The highest peak within India's undisputed territory is:

  1. Kangchenjunga (Sikkim)
  2. Nanda Devi (Uttarakhand)
  3. K2 (Karakoram)
  4. Kamet (Uttarakhand)

Answer: Kangchenjunga (Sikkim)

Explanation: Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) on the Sikkim-Nepal border is the highest peak within India's undisputed territory. K2 (8,611 m) is higher but lies in POK (disputed). World's third-highest mountain.

14. The Western Ghats are also known as:

  1. Sahyadri
  2. Eastern Ghats
  3. Vindhya
  4. Aravalli

Answer: Sahyadri

Explanation: The Western Ghats are also known as the Sahyadri. They run parallel to the western coast of India for about 1,600 km, from Gujarat to Kanyakumari. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

15. The highest peak in the Western Ghats is:

  1. Anamudi (2,695 m)
  2. Doddabetta (2,637 m)
  3. Kudremukh (1,892 m)
  4. Brahmagiri (1,608 m)

Answer: Anamudi (2,695 m)

Explanation: Anamudi (2,695 m) in the Anamalai Hills of Kerala is the highest peak in the Western Ghats and also the highest peak in South India outside the Himalayas.

16. The Thar Desert is primarily located in:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Gujarat
  3. Haryana
  4. Punjab

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: The Thar Desert (Great Indian Desert) is primarily in western Rajasthan (India), extending into parts of Pakistan. In India, it also covers small areas of Gujarat, Haryana, and Punjab.

17. The Deccan Plateau is bounded in the north by:

  1. Vindhya and Satpura Ranges
  2. Aravalli Range
  3. Eastern Ghats
  4. Himalayas

Answer: Vindhya and Satpura Ranges

Explanation: The Deccan Plateau is bounded by the Vindhya and Satpura Ranges in the north, the Western Ghats in the west, and the Eastern Ghats in the east.

18. The Aravalli Range runs through:

  1. Rajasthan and Gujarat (to Delhi)
  2. Only Rajasthan
  3. Rajasthan and MP
  4. Punjab and Rajasthan

Answer: Rajasthan and Gujarat (to Delhi)

Explanation: The Aravalli Range (about 800 km) runs from Gujarat (Palanpur) through Rajasthan to Delhi-Haryana border. It is one of the oldest fold mountain ranges in the world. Mount Abu (1,722 m) is the highest peak.

19. The Eastern Ghats are not continuous because:

  1. Major rivers cut across them as they flow to Bay of Bengal
  2. They were formed differently
  3. Glaciers eroded them
  4. They are much older than Western Ghats

Answer: Major rivers cut across them as they flow to Bay of Bengal

Explanation: Major rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Pennar) cut across the Eastern Ghats as they flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal, making them discontinuous. Western Ghats are not cut by major rivers.

20. Mawsynram (Meghalaya) is famous for:

  1. Highest rainfall in the world
  2. Highest mountain
  3. Largest lake
  4. Most cyclones

Answer: Highest rainfall in the world

Explanation: Mawsynram in Meghalaya receives about 11,872 mm annually — the highest in the world. Cherrapunji (Sohra), nearby, also holds many rainfall records. Both are on the Shillong Plateau facing the Bay of Bengal.

21. Project Tiger was launched in India in:

  1. 1973
  2. 1972
  3. 1980
  4. 1968

Answer: 1973

Explanation: Project Tiger was launched on 1 April 1973 by PM Indira Gandhi. Jim Corbett National Park was the first tiger reserve. India currently has 53 tiger reserves covering about 75,000 sq km.

22. The first National Park in India is:

  1. Jim Corbett National Park (1936)
  2. Gir National Park (1975)
  3. Kaziranga National Park (1974)
  4. Bandipur National Park (1974)

Answer: Jim Corbett National Park (1936)

Explanation: Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand) was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park — India's and Asia's first national park. Renamed Jim Corbett in 1957 and became the first Project Tiger reserve in 1973.

23. Black cotton soil (Regur) is most suitable for growing:

  1. Cotton and wheat
  2. Rice
  3. Jute
  4. Tea

Answer: Cotton and wheat

Explanation: Black cotton soil (Regur) — found in the Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, Karnataka) — is highly fertile for cotton. It retains moisture well, has high clay content, and swells when wet.

24. Alluvial soil is most productive for:

  1. Paddy, wheat, sugarcane, and jute
  2. Cotton
  3. Tea
  4. Millets

Answer: Paddy, wheat, sugarcane, and jute

Explanation: Alluvial soil (found in the Indo-Gangetic plains, river deltas, coastal plains) is the most fertile soil in India. It supports paddy, wheat, sugarcane, jute, and vegetables. It is renewed by river flooding.

25. India is the world's largest producer of:

  1. Milk and pulses
  2. Wheat
  3. Rice (all varieties)
  4. Sugar

Answer: Milk and pulses

Explanation: India is the world's largest producer of milk (Operation Flood, since 1998), pulses (about 24% of world production), and spices. India is also the second-largest producer of rice and wheat.
