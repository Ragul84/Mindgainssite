Natural Resources MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Natural Resources questions to revise the UPSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The standard meridian of India is:

  1. 72°30'E
  2. 80°30'E
  3. 82°30'E
  4. 90°30'E

Answer: 82°30'E

Explanation: The standard meridian of India is 82°30'E (passing through Mirzapur, UP), which is used to determine Indian Standard Time (IST = UTC+5:30).

2. India has the world's largest reserves of:

  1. Coal
  2. Iron ore
  3. Thorium (in monazite sand, coastal Kerala and Tamil Nadu)
  4. Petroleum

Answer: Thorium (in monazite sand, coastal Kerala and Tamil Nadu)

Explanation: India has the world's largest known reserves of thorium — found in monazite sands on the Kerala and Tamil Nadu coasts (Chavara, Kerala; Manavalakurichi, Tamil Nadu). India also has 4th largest coal reserves globally.

3. The 'Oil and Natural Gas Corporation' (ONGC) produces most of India's petroleum from:

  1. Assam oilfields only
  2. Mumbai High (offshore, Arabian Sea) — about 60% of production
  3. Rajasthan (Barmer) fields
  4. Gujarat coast

Answer: Mumbai High (offshore, Arabian Sea) — about 60% of production

Explanation: Mumbai High (Bombay High) offshore oilfields in the Arabian Sea produce about 60% of India's total crude oil. Other producing areas: Gujarat (Ankleshwar, Kalol), Assam (Digboi — oldest oilfield in Asia, 1889), Rajasthan (Barmer/Mangala).

4. The Silent Valley National Park (Kerala) is famous for:

  1. Last undisturbed tropical evergreen forest and lion-tailed macaques
  2. Tigers
  3. Rhinoceros
  4. Snow leopards

Answer: Last undisturbed tropical evergreen forest and lion-tailed macaques

Explanation: Silent Valley National Park in Palakkad district, Kerala, in the Nilgiri Hills is famous for being the last undisturbed tropical moist evergreen forest in South India, home to the endangered lion-tailed macaque.

5. Gir National Park (Gujarat) is the only habitat in the world for:

  1. Asiatic Lion
  2. Bengal Tiger
  3. Indian Leopard
  4. Snow Leopard

Answer: Asiatic Lion

Explanation: Gir Forest National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary in Gujarat is the world's only natural habitat of the Asiatic Lion. The lion population has grown from ~200 in the 1970s to over 670 now.

6. The Kolar Gold Fields (KGF) are in:

  1. Karnataka
  2. Tamil Nadu
  3. Andhra Pradesh
  4. Kerala

Answer: Karnataka

Explanation: Kolar Gold Fields (KGF) are in Kolar district, Karnataka. Once the world's deepest gold mines, they produced gold for over 120 years before being closed in 2001 by the government due to non-viability.

7. Which state has the largest reserves of iron ore in India?

  1. Odisha
  2. Jharkhand
  3. Chhattisgarh
  4. Karnataka

Answer: Odisha

Explanation: Odisha has the largest reserves of iron ore in India (about 33% of total). Major mines: Barbil-Sundergarh region. Karnataka (Bellary-Hospet) and Chhattisgarh-Jharkhand are also major producers.

8. Singareni Coalfields are in:

  1. Telangana
  2. Chhattisgarh
  3. Jharkhand
  4. Odisha

Answer: Telangana

Explanation: Singareni Collieries Company Limited (SCCL) operates coal mines in Telangana (formerly Andhra Pradesh). Singareni is the only government-owned coal company owned jointly by the State (51%) and Centre (49%).

9. Which state is the leading producer of petroleum (crude oil) in India?

  1. Gujarat (offshore and onshore)
  2. Assam
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: Gujarat (offshore and onshore)

Explanation: Gujarat is the leading producer of crude oil in India, with major offshore fields in the Arabian Sea (Bombay High, operated by ONGC) and onshore fields in Ankleshwar. Rajasthan (Barmer) has emerged as a major onshore producer.

10. The Montreal Protocol (1987) aimed to phase out:

  1. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) that deplete the ozone layer
  2. Carbon dioxide emissions
  3. Plastic pollution
  4. Nuclear waste

Answer: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) that deplete the ozone layer

Explanation: The Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer (1987) is a landmark treaty phasing out CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) and other ozone-depleting substances. It is considered the most successful environmental treaty.

11. According to the 2011 census, India's population was approximately:

  1. 1.21 billion (121 crore)
  2. 1.35 billion
  3. 1 billion
  4. 1.15 billion

Answer: 1.21 billion (121 crore)

Explanation: India's population was 1.21 billion (121 crore) as per the 2011 census. India is projected to overtake China as the world's most populous country — which happened around 2023.

12. India's Silicon Valley is:

  1. Bengaluru (Bangalore)
  2. Hyderabad
  3. Chennai
  4. Mumbai

Answer: Bengaluru (Bangalore)

Explanation: Bengaluru (Bangalore) is called India's Silicon Valley due to concentration of IT companies — Infosys, Wipro, HAL, ISRO are headquartered here. It is also called the 'Pub Capital', 'Garden City', 'Startup Capital of India'.

13. Evergreen Revolution aims at:

  1. Sustainable increase in agricultural productivity without environmental damage
  2. Growing only green vegetables
  3. Forest conservation
  4. Using solar energy in agriculture

Answer: Sustainable increase in agricultural productivity without environmental damage

Explanation: Evergreen Revolution (concept by M.S. Swaminathan) aims at sustainable increase in agricultural productivity without ecological harm — combining the productivity of the Green Revolution with environmental sustainability.

14. OPEC (Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries) headquarters is in:

  1. Vienna, Austria
  2. Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
  3. Dubai, UAE
  4. Geneva, Switzerland

Answer: Vienna, Austria

Explanation: OPEC is headquartered in Vienna, Austria. Founded in Baghdad in 1960, it currently has 13 member countries. Saudi Arabia is the de facto leader. OPEC controls about 40% of world oil production.

15. The G20 is a group of:

  1. 19 major economies + the European Union
  2. 20 countries only
  3. Richest 20 countries
  4. G7 + 13 developing countries

Answer: 19 major economies + the European Union

Explanation: G20 consists of 19 major economies (Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Turkey, UK, USA) plus the European Union.

16. The 'continental shelf' is:

  1. The submerged extension of a continent under the shallow sea (up to 200 m depth)
  2. The edge of a continent
  3. A mountain range under the sea
  4. The abyssal plain

Answer: The submerged extension of a continent under the shallow sea (up to 200 m depth)

Explanation: The continental shelf is the gently sloping submerged extension of a continent from the shoreline to a depth of about 200 m. Beyond it is the steeper continental slope, then the ocean floor/abyssal plain.

17. The Strait of Malacca connects:

  1. Indian Ocean and South China Sea (between Malaysia and Indonesia)
  2. Pacific and Indian Ocean
  3. China Sea and Japan Sea
  4. Bay of Bengal and South China Sea

Answer: Indian Ocean and South China Sea (between Malaysia and Indonesia)

Explanation: The Strait of Malacca (between Malaysia/Singapore and Sumatra/Indonesia) is one of the world's most important shipping lanes, connecting the Indian Ocean with the South China Sea and Pacific Ocean.

18. The 'Chipko Movement' was related to:

  1. Forest conservation (hugging trees to prevent felling) in Uttarakhand
  2. Water conservation
  3. Wildlife protection
  4. Anti-pollution

Answer: Forest conservation (hugging trees to prevent felling) in Uttarakhand

Explanation: The Chipko Movement (1973) in Uttarakhand was a nonviolent protest where villagers (led by Sundarlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt) hugged trees to prevent logging. It influenced India's forest policy.

19. The Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) is headquartered in:

  1. Pune (Maharashtra)
  2. New Delhi
  3. Mumbai
  4. Chennai

Answer: Pune (Maharashtra)

Explanation: IMD (India Meteorological Department) is headquartered in Pune, Maharashtra (Shivajinagar). It was established in 1875 and is the principal government agency for weather forecasting and seismology.

20. The Konkan Railway runs along:

  1. Maharashtra, Goa, and Karnataka coasts (Mumbai to Mangalore)
  2. Malabar Coast, Kerala
  3. East Coast from Chennai to Odisha
  4. Rajasthan and Gujarat

Answer: Maharashtra, Goa, and Karnataka coasts (Mumbai to Mangalore)

Explanation: Konkan Railway (760 km) was built 1990-1998 along the western coast through Maharashtra, Goa, and Karnataka, connecting Roha (near Mumbai) to Mangalore. It passes through 2,000+ bridges and 91 tunnels.

21. The capital of Brazil is:

  1. Brasília
  2. São Paulo
  3. Rio de Janeiro
  4. Salvador

Answer: Brasília

Explanation: Brasília is the capital of Brazil — a planned capital built in just 41 months and inaugurated in 1960. Before Brasília, Rio de Janeiro was the capital. São Paulo is Brazil's largest city and economic center.

22. The capital of Japan is:

  1. Tokyo
  2. Osaka
  3. Kyoto
  4. Hiroshima

Answer: Tokyo

Explanation: Tokyo is the capital and largest city of Japan (population ~14 million city, ~38 million metro area — world's largest metropolitan area). Before 1868, Kyoto was Japan's capital.

23. Which is the only state in India with two capitals (summer and winter)?

  1. Himachal Pradesh (Shimla in summer, Dharamsala or Shimla all year; J&K — Srinagar/Jammu)
  2. Uttarakhand
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Tamil Nadu

Answer: Himachal Pradesh (Shimla in summer, Dharamsala or Shimla all year; J&K — Srinagar/Jammu)

Explanation: J&K had two capitals — Srinagar (summer) and Jammu (winter) — before becoming a UT. After J&K reorganization (2019), both Srinagar and Jammu serve as capitals of the J&K UT. Himachal Pradesh's capital is only Shimla.

24. Chandigarh is the capital of:

  1. Punjab and Haryana both (and a Union Territory itself)
  2. Only Punjab
  3. Only Haryana
  4. Himachal Pradesh

Answer: Punjab and Haryana both (and a Union Territory itself)

Explanation: Chandigarh is a Union Territory that serves as the shared capital of both Punjab and Haryana. Designed by Le Corbusier, it was established after Partition (Lahore went to Pakistan) as Punjab's new capital.

25. What is 'soil erosion' primarily caused by?

  1. Deforestation, overgrazing, and poor farming practices — along with wind and water
  2. Only natural rainfall
  3. Only wind
  4. Only human construction

Answer: Deforestation, overgrazing, and poor farming practices — along with wind and water

Explanation: Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by wind, water, or human activities. Major causes: deforestation (tree roots hold soil), overgrazing, poor agricultural practices (no crop rotation, monoculture), and construction.
