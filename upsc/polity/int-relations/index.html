Int Relations MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Int Relations questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 407 questions.

1. Which article of the Constitution defines India as a Union of States?

  1. Article 1
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 3
  4. Article 4

Answer: Article 1

Explanation: Article 1 states that 'India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.'

2. The Constitution describes India as:

  1. Union of States
  2. Federation of States
  3. Confederation
  4. Republic of India

Answer: Union of States

Explanation: Article 1 describes India as a 'Union of States' — not federation. This implies the union is indestructible and states cannot secede. India is not a true federation.

3. Article 17 abolishes:

  1. Untouchability
  2. Bonded labor
  3. Child labor
  4. Discrimination based on religion

Answer: Untouchability

Explanation: Article 17 abolishes untouchability and its practice in any form is an offense punishable by law. The Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 and SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 implement this.

4. Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of:

  1. Religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth
  2. Religion, race, caste, sex only
  3. Religion only
  4. Caste only

Answer: Religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth

Explanation: Article 15 prohibits discrimination by the State on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, OR place of birth. However, special provisions for women, children, SCs, STs, OBCs are allowed.

5. Cut motions on Demands for Grants are of how many types?

  1. 3 types
  2. 2 types
  3. 4 types
  4. 5 types

Answer: 3 types

Explanation: Three types of Cut Motions: (1) Disapproval of Policy Cut (reduce to ₹1), (2) Economy Cut (reduce by specific amount), (3) Token Cut (reduce by ₹100 to ventilate grievance).

6. Lok Adalats (People's Courts) are established under:

  1. Legal Services Authorities Act 1987
  2. Arbitration Act
  3. CPC 1908
  4. Code of Criminal Procedure

Answer: Legal Services Authorities Act 1987

Explanation: Lok Adalats are established under the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 to provide alternative dispute resolution. Awards made by Lok Adalat are deemed decrees and final, not appealable.

7. The Planning Commission was replaced by:

  1. NITI Aayog (2015)
  2. Economic Advisory Council
  3. Finance Commission
  4. Planning Board

Answer: NITI Aayog (2015)

Explanation: NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015. Unlike Planning Commission, NITI Aayog is an advisory body without power to allocate funds.

8. National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established in:

  1. 2010
  2. 2005
  3. 2015
  4. 2012

Answer: 2010

Explanation: National Green Tribunal Act 2010 established the NGT for effective and speedy disposal of cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests.

9. Jammu & Kashmir was reorganized into two Union Territories in:

  1. 2019 (via J&K Reorganization Act)
  2. 2017
  3. 2021
  4. 2020

Answer: 2019 (via J&K Reorganization Act)

Explanation: J&K Reorganization Act 2019 bifurcated the state of J&K into: (1) J&K UT (with legislature), (2) Ladakh UT (without legislature), effective 31 October 2019. Article 370 was also abrogated.

10. Article 371 deals with:

  1. Special provisions for certain states (like NE states)
  2. Panchayati Raj
  3. Minority rights
  4. Language policies

Answer: Special provisions for certain states (like NE states)

Explanation: Articles 371-371J provide special provisions for certain states: 371A (Nagaland), 371B (Assam), 371C (Manipur), 371D-F (Andhra Pradesh/Telangana), 371G (Mizoram), 371H (Arunachal), 371I (Goa), 371J (Karnataka).

11. Which Article prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour?

  1. Article 23
  2. Article 24
  3. Article 17
  4. Article 19

Answer: Article 23

Explanation: Article 23 prohibits trafficking in human beings, begar (forced labour) and other similar forms of forced labour. Any contravention is an offence punishable by law.

12. Under which Articles are protection against arbitrary arrest and detention provided?

  1. Article 22
  2. Article 21
  3. Article 19
  4. Article 20

Answer: Article 22

Explanation: Article 22 provides protection against arbitrary arrest: right to be informed of grounds, right to consult a lawyer, production before magistrate within 24 hours, and preventive detention limits.

13. How many times has the National Emergency (Art 352) been proclaimed in India?

  1. 3 times
  2. 2 times
  3. 4 times
  4. 1 time

Answer: 3 times

Explanation: National Emergency was proclaimed 3 times: (1) 1962 — Chinese aggression; (2) 1971 — Pakistan war; (3) 1975 — Internal disturbance (the controversial Emergency under Indira Gandhi).

14. NITI Aayog replaced which body in 2015?

  1. Planning Commission
  2. Finance Commission
  3. National Development Council
  4. Inter-State Council

Answer: Planning Commission

Explanation: NITI (National Institution for Transforming India) Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015. Unlike Planning Commission, NITI Aayog is a policy think-tank and does not allocate funds directly to States.

15. India follows the principle of:

  1. Single citizenship
  2. Dual citizenship like USA
  3. Multiple citizenship
  4. Optional citizenship

Answer: Single citizenship

Explanation: India provides for single citizenship — a person can be a citizen of India only, not of any State. This is unlike the USA where citizens have both national and state citizenship.

16. Article 1 of the Indian Constitution describes India as:

  1. A Union of States
  2. A Federation of States
  3. A Confederation
  4. A Republic of States

Answer: A Union of States

Explanation: Article 1 describes India as a 'Union of States' — not a federation. This term was deliberately chosen to indicate that States have no right to secede from the Union (unlike a federation or confederation).

17. The first case of PIL in India was filed by:

  1. Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)
  2. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
  3. Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
  4. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1987)

Answer: Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)

Explanation: Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979) is considered the first PIL in India, filed by advocate Kapila Hingorani for undertrial prisoners. Justice P.N. Bhagwati was instrumental in developing PIL jurisprudence.

18. The National Commission for Women was established in:

  1. 1992
  2. 1990
  3. 2000
  4. 1985

Answer: 1992

Explanation: The National Commission for Women was established under the National Commission for Women Act, 1990 and became operational from 31 January 1992. It reviews constitutional safeguards for women.

19. Article 17 of the Constitution abolishes:

  1. Untouchability
  2. Titles
  3. Forced labour
  4. Child labour

Answer: Untouchability

Explanation: Article 17 abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form. Enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability shall be an offence. The Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 implements this.

20. The National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established in:

  1. 2010
  2. 2005
  3. 2008
  4. 2012

Answer: 2010

Explanation: The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 established the NGT for effective and speedy disposal of cases relating to environmental protection. It has original, appellate, and settlement jurisdiction in environmental matters.

21. The Gram Sabha consists of:

  1. All persons registered as voters in the Panchayat area
  2. Only elected Panchayat members
  3. All adult residents (whether registered voters or not)
  4. All taxpayers in the village

Answer: All persons registered as voters in the Panchayat area

Explanation: The Gram Sabha (Art 243A) consists of all persons registered in the electoral rolls of a village — i.e., all adult voters of that village. It is the foundation of democratic decentralization and can exercise powers as the State Legislature decides.

22. The maximum number of members in a State Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) cannot exceed:

  1. One-third of the total members of the State Legislative Assembly
  2. One-fourth of the total members of the State Assembly
  3. 50 members
  4. 100 members

Answer: One-third of the total members of the State Legislative Assembly

Explanation: Article 171 states that the total number of members in a Vidhan Parishad shall not exceed one-third of the total members of the Vidhan Sabha, and shall not be less than 40 members.

23. Which States currently have a Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)?

  1. Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh
  2. All States have legislative councils
  3. Only 4 states — UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka
  4. Only 3 States — Maharashtra, UP, Karnataka

Answer: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh

Explanation: Currently 6 States have Vidhan Parishad: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Bihar, and Uttar Pradesh. Tamil Nadu abolished its Vidhan Parishad in 1986.

24. The principle 'One Vote, One Value' in Indian elections means:

  1. Each vote has equal value — no plural voting
  2. Only one person can vote per family
  3. Property-based voting is allowed
  4. Only citizens can vote

Answer: Each vote has equal value — no plural voting

Explanation: The principle of 'One Person, One Vote, One Value' ensures universal adult franchise — each citizen above 18 years has one vote of equal value, regardless of wealth, gender, religion, or caste.

25. The 'Doctrine of Colourable Legislation' relates to:

  1. Judicial review of emergency provisions
  2. Legislative competence — Centre vs States
  3. Parliamentary privileges
  4. Financial emergency powers

Answer: Legislative competence — Centre vs States

Explanation: The Doctrine of Colourable Legislation means that what the legislature cannot do directly, it cannot do indirectly. It is used to examine if a legislature has transgressed its constitutional powers under the legislative lists.
