26. The 'Doctrine of Severability' (or Separability) in constitutional law states that:

  1. Courts can sever an unconstitutional amendment
  2. Valid part of an unconstitutional Act can be saved if separable
  3. States can sever from the Union
  4. High Courts are severed from Supreme Court jurisdiction

Answer: Valid part of an unconstitutional Act can be saved if separable

Explanation: The Doctrine of Severability means that if an unconstitutional portion of a law can be separated from the valid portion, only the unconstitutional part is struck down; the rest remains valid.

27. The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) was established in:

  1. 1990
  2. 1993 (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993)
  3. 1988
  4. 2000

Answer: 1993 (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993)

Explanation: NHRC was established on 12 October 1993 under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 (amended 2006). Its Chairperson is a retired Chief Justice of India. It investigates human rights violations, studies treaties, and recommends measures for protection.

28. India follows which type of citizenship?

  1. Single citizenship (no dual citizenship for states)
  2. Dual citizenship (state + national)
  3. Plural citizenship
  4. Federal citizenship with state citizenship

Answer: Single citizenship (no dual citizenship for states)

Explanation: India provides for single citizenship — every citizen is a citizen of India only (unlike USA where citizens have both national and state citizenship). This promotes national unity.

29. Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) established:

  1. 50% ceiling on total reservations
  2. Right to reservation
  3. Abolition of creamy layer
  4. Reservation in promotions

Answer: 50% ceiling on total reservations

Explanation: Indra Sawhney case (Mandal Commission case, 1992): SC upheld OBC reservation but: (a) 50% ceiling on reservations, (b) Excluded creamy layer from OBC reservation, (c) No reservation in promotions.

30. The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) was set up under:

  1. Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993
  2. Human Rights Act, 1990
  3. Constitutional Amendment
  4. NHRC Act, 1989

Answer: Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993

Explanation: NHRC was established under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. Its Chairperson must be a retired Chief Justice of India. The current Act was amended in 2019.

31. Which Article deals with the official language of the Union?

  1. Article 343
  2. Article 350
  3. Article 351
  4. Article 348

Answer: Article 343

Explanation: Article 343 states Hindi in Devanagari script shall be the official language of the Union. English was also continued as an official language by the Official Languages Act, 1963.

32. Under the 'Emergency Provisions', how many National Emergencies have been declared in India?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 4

Answer: 3

Explanation: Three National Emergencies have been declared: 1962 (Chinese aggression), 1971 (Pakistani aggression), and 1975-1977 (internal disturbance — the only 'internal' Emergency).

33. Which article of the Constitution protects persons from double jeopardy?

  1. Article 20(1)
  2. Article 20(2)
  3. Article 20(3)
  4. Article 22

Answer: Article 20(2)

Explanation: Article 20(2) provides that no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once — protection against double jeopardy.

34. The official language of the Union of India is:

  1. Hindi in Devanagari script
  2. English
  3. Both Hindi and English
  4. Sanskrit

Answer: Hindi in Devanagari script

Explanation: Article 343: Hindi in Devanagari script is the official language of the Union. English continues to be used for official purposes under Article 343(2). India has no 'national language'.

35. The official language Hindi was to replace English by:

  1. 1965 (but not implemented)
  2. 1950
  3. 1952
  4. 1960

Answer: 1965 (but not implemented)

Explanation: Article 343(2) provided that English would continue for 15 years until 1965. But due to protests (especially in Tamil Nadu), the Official Languages Act 1963 made English continue indefinitely.

36. Which states in India have a bicameral legislature (two houses)?

  1. UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, J&K
  2. All states
  3. Only Kerala and West Bengal
  4. Only southern states

Answer: UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, J&K

Explanation: States with Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad): UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar. J&K had bicameral legislature before Art 370 abrogation.

37. The Right to Information Act was passed in:

  1. 2005
  2. 2000
  3. 2003
  4. 2007

Answer: 2005

Explanation: The Right to Information (RTI) Act 2005 was passed to provide citizens access to information held by public authorities, making governance more transparent and accountable.

38. The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act was passed in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2011
  3. 2015
  4. 2010

Answer: 2013

Explanation: The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013 established the Lokpal at the Union level and Lokayuktas at the state level for investigation of corruption charges against public servants.

39. Statement 1: Article 51 of the Indian Constitution deals with the promotion of international peace and security. Statement 2: The Parliament has the power to make laws on any matter related to international relations, including treaties and agreements, under Article 246 of the Constitution.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Both statements are correct as Article 51 promotes international peace and security, and Article 246 gives Parliament the power to make laws on international relations.

40. Statement 1: The Supreme Court's judgment in the Kesavananda Bharati case established the doctrine of basic structure. Statement 2: This doctrine limits the power of Parliament to amend the Constitution in matters related to international relations.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Both statements are correct as the Kesavananda Bharati case established the basic structure doctrine, limiting Parliament's power to amend the Constitution.

41. Statement 1: India is a signatory to the Geneva Conventions. Statement 2: The Parliament of India has enacted laws to implement the provisions of these Conventions.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Both statements are correct as India is a signatory to the Geneva Conventions and has enacted laws to implement their provisions.

42. Statement 1: The President of India has the power to negotiate and sign treaties and agreements with foreign countries. Statement 2: The President's power to negotiate and sign treaties is subject to the approval of the Parliament.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Both statements are correct as the President has the power to negotiate and sign treaties, which is subject to Parliament's approval.

43. Statement 1: The Indian Constitution provides for the establishment of diplomatic relations with foreign countries. Statement 2: The Constitution also provides for the appointment of diplomatic agents to represent India in foreign countries.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Both statements are correct as the Constitution provides for diplomatic relations and the appointment of diplomatic agents.

44. Statement 1: The Parliament of India has the power to declare war or make peace with foreign countries. Statement 2: The power to declare war or make peace is vested in the President of India.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: A

Explanation: Only Statement 1 is correct as Parliament has the power to declare war or make peace.

45. Statement 1: The Supreme Court of India has the power to adjudicate disputes related to international relations. Statement 2: The Supreme Court's power is limited to interpreting the Constitution and does not extend to international relations.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: A

Explanation: Only Statement 1 is correct as the Supreme Court has the power to adjudicate disputes related to international relations.

46. Statement 1: India is a member of the United Nations. Statement 2: India's membership in the United Nations is subject to the approval of the Parliament.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: A

Explanation: Only Statement 1 is correct as India is a member of the United Nations, but its membership is not subject to Parliament's approval.

47. Statement 1: The Indian Constitution provides for the protection of foreign nationals in India. Statement 2: The Constitution also provides for the extradition of foreign nationals who have committed crimes in India.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Both statements are correct as the Constitution provides for the protection of foreign nationals and their extradition.

48. Statement 1: The Government of India has the power to impose economic sanctions on foreign countries. Statement 2: The power to impose economic sanctions is vested in the Parliament of India.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: A

Explanation: Only Statement 1 is correct as the Government has the power to impose economic sanctions, not Parliament.

49. Consider the following statements regarding the relationship between the Executive and the Legislature in the context of international relations: 1. The President has the power to negotiate and sign treaties without the approval of the Parliament. 2. The Parliament has the power to ratify treaties, but this power is not absolute and can be limited by the Executive. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: Article 253 of the Constitution gives Parliament the power to make laws for implementing international treaties, while the Executive negotiates and signs them.

50. Regarding the role of the Supreme Court in international relations, consider the following: 1. The Supreme Court has the power to review international agreements. 2. The Supreme Court can declare a law passed by Parliament as unconstitutional if it violates international law. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: The Supreme Court has held that it can review international agreements and declare laws unconstitutional if they violate international law or the Constitution.
