UPSC Polity practice

Fundamental Rights MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Fundamental Rights questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 420 questions.

1. Which writ is issued by a court to compel a public authority to perform its legal duty?

  1. Habeas Corpus
  2. Mandamus
  3. Certiorari
  4. Quo Warranto

Answer: Mandamus

Explanation: Mandamus (meaning 'We command') is issued to a public authority, lower court, or corporation to perform a public or statutory duty.

2. Which writ is used to produce a detained person before a court?

  1. Mandamus
  2. Certiorari
  3. Habeas Corpus
  4. Prohibition

Answer: Habeas Corpus

Explanation: Habeas Corpus (meaning 'You may have the body') is issued to free a person from illegal detention.

3. Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees:

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right against Exploitation
  3. Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion

Answer: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty

Explanation: Article 21 states: 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.'

4. The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by which amendment?

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 46th Amendment
  4. 52nd Amendment

Answer: 44th Amendment

Explanation: The 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) removed the Right to Property from Fundamental Rights and made it a legal right under Article 300A.

5. Which constitutional body is known as the 'watchdog of public finance'?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Comptroller and Auditor General
  3. Finance Commission
  4. SEBI

Answer: Comptroller and Auditor General

Explanation: The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG), under Article 148, audits all government accounts and is called the watchdog of public finance.

6. How many Fundamental Duties are listed in the Indian Constitution?

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 11
  4. 12

Answer: 11

Explanation: There are 11 Fundamental Duties. Initially 10 were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976); the 11th (related to education for children) was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

7. Which article of the Constitution provides for the right against self-incrimination?

  1. Article 20(1)
  2. Article 20(2)
  3. Article 20(3)
  4. Article 22

Answer: Article 20(3)

Explanation: Article 20(3) provides that no person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.

8. Which article guarantees freedom of speech and expression in India?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 17
  3. Article 19(1)(a)
  4. Article 21

Answer: Article 19(1)(a)

Explanation: Article 19(1)(a) guarantees all citizens the right to freedom of speech and expression.

9. Article 19 guarantees freedom to all CITIZENS. Which of these is NOT in Article 19?

  1. Freedom of speech and expression
  2. Freedom to assemble peacefully
  3. Freedom to reside and settle anywhere in India
  4. Right to life and personal liberty

Answer: Right to life and personal liberty

Explanation: Article 19 gives citizens 6 freedoms: speech/expression, peaceful assembly (without arms), forming associations/unions, free movement, residence/settlement, and practising any profession/occupation. Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) is separate.

10. 'Habeas Corpus' literally means:

  1. We command
  2. You may have the body — a writ ordering the production of a detained person before court to determine if detention is lawful
  3. A question of right — challenging a person's claim to public office
  4. It is prohibited — to stop lower court/tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction

Answer: You may have the body — a writ ordering the production of a detained person before court to determine if detention is lawful

Explanation: Habeas Corpus ('you may have the body') is a writ ordering the production of a detained person before a court to determine if the detention is lawful. It protects against arbitrary arrest and detention.

11. The writ 'Mandamus' is issued to:

  1. Lower courts to stop exceeding jurisdiction
  2. A public official or body to perform a legal duty they have neglected or refused to perform
  3. A person to justify why they hold a public office
  4. Produce a detained person before court

Answer: A public official or body to perform a legal duty they have neglected or refused to perform

Explanation: Mandamus ('we command') is a writ directing a public official, lower court, government body, or corporation to perform a mandatory public duty that it has failed or refused to perform. Cannot be issued against a private person.

12. Equal pay for equal work for both men and women is under:

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 15
  3. Article 39(d) — DPSP
  4. Article 16

Answer: Article 39(d) — DPSP

Explanation: Article 39(d) DPSP: 'equal pay for equal work for both men and women.' Though not a Fundamental Right, the Supreme Court has interpreted Article 39(d) in conjunction with Article 14 and 16 to enforce equal pay.

13. Question Hour in Parliament is held:

  1. On Wednesdays only
  2. During the first hour of every sitting day (11 AM-12 noon)
  3. Once a month
  4. After passing of budget only

Answer: During the first hour of every sitting day (11 AM-12 noon)

Explanation: Question Hour is the first hour (11 AM-12 noon) of each Parliamentary sitting. Members can ask oral questions (starred — answered orally, supplementaries can follow) and written questions (unstarred — written answers). Zero Hour immediately follows.

14. The President of India can declare National Emergency under:

  1. Article 352 — if national security is threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion
  2. Article 356
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 368

Answer: Article 352 — if national security is threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion

Explanation: Article 352 allows the President to proclaim National Emergency if the security of India is threatened by war, external aggression, or 'armed rebellion' (changed from 'internal disturbance' by 44th Amendment). Cabinet must advise in writing. Requires 2/3 special majority in Parliament.

15. The 44th Amendment Act (1978) restricted the use of National Emergency by:

  1. Requiring approval of state legislatures
  2. Requiring a written recommendation by the Cabinet + 2/3 majority of total membership in each House (changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion')
  3. Abolishing the provision
  4. Giving it only to Parliament

Answer: Requiring a written recommendation by the Cabinet + 2/3 majority of total membership in each House (changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion')

Explanation: The 44th Amendment (1978, post-Emergency) changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion' (harder standard), required Cabinet's written recommendation, required approval by special majority (2/3 of total membership), and gave Lok Sabha power to revoke Emergency by simple majority.

16. India is described as:

  1. A Federation
  2. A Unitary State
  3. A Union of States (quasi-federal — strong centre, federal features)
  4. A Confederation

Answer: A Union of States (quasi-federal — strong centre, federal features)

Explanation: Article 1 describes India as 'a Union of States'. India has federal features (two levels of government, written constitution, division of powers, independent judiciary) but also strong unitary features (single citizenship, integrated judiciary, emergency powers). K.C. Wheare called it 'quasi-federal'.

17. The concept of Fundamental Rights in Indian Constitution is borrowed from:

  1. US Constitution
  2. British Constitution
  3. Irish Constitution
  4. Canadian Constitution

Answer: US Constitution

Explanation: Fundamental Rights (Part III) were largely inspired by the Bill of Rights in the US Constitution (First 10 Amendments). The judicial review mechanism also came from the USA.

18. Right to Education (RTE) is a Fundamental Right under which Article?

  1. Article 21A
  2. Article 21
  3. Article 29
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 21A

Explanation: Article 21A (inserted by 86th Amendment, 2002) makes free and compulsory education for children 6-14 years a Fundamental Right. The RTE Act 2009 gives effect to this right.

19. Article 19 guarantees freedom of:

  1. Speech, assembly, movement, profession, and more (6 freedoms)
  2. Religion
  3. Equality
  4. Education

Answer: Speech, assembly, movement, profession, and more (6 freedoms)

Explanation: Article 19 guarantees 6 freedoms: (a) Speech & Expression, (b) Peaceful Assembly, (c) Form Associations, (d) Move freely, (e) Reside anywhere, (g) Practice any profession/trade.

20. The Right to Property was removed as a Fundamental Right by:

  1. 44th Amendment (1978)
  2. 42nd Amendment (1976)
  3. 25th Amendment
  4. 1st Amendment

Answer: 44th Amendment (1978)

Explanation: 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights (Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31). It became a legal right under Article 300A.

21. Freedom of religion is guaranteed by Articles:

  1. 25-28
  2. 19-22
  3. 14-18
  4. 23-24

Answer: 25-28

Explanation: Articles 25-28 deal with Freedom of Religion: Art. 25 (right to profess, practice, propagate), Art. 26 (manage religious affairs), Art. 27 (no tax for religion), Art. 28 (no religious instruction in state schools).

22. Which Fundamental Right is available to both citizens and non-citizens?

  1. Right to Equality (Article 14)
  2. Right to vote
  3. Right to property
  4. Right to contest elections

Answer: Right to Equality (Article 14)

Explanation: Article 14 (Equality before law) applies to 'any person' — both citizens and non-citizens. Similarly, Articles 20, 21, 21A, 22, 25-28 apply to all persons, not just citizens.

23. Articles 23 and 24 deal with:

  1. Right against Exploitation
  2. Cultural and Educational Rights
  3. Right to Freedom
  4. Right to Religion

Answer: Right against Exploitation

Explanation: Articles 23-24 (Right against Exploitation): Art. 23 prohibits human trafficking and forced labor (begar). Art. 24 prohibits employment of children below 14 years in factories/mines/hazardous work.

24. Which Fundamental Right cannot be suspended even during Emergency?

  1. Articles 20 and 21
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 14
  4. Article 32

Answer: Articles 20 and 21

Explanation: Under Article 359, during Emergency, rights under Article 19 can be suspended, but Articles 20 (protection against conviction for offenses) and 21 (right to life) CANNOT be suspended even during Emergency.

25. The 'Golden Triangle' of Fundamental Rights refers to:

  1. Articles 14, 19, 21
  2. Articles 14, 15, 16
  3. Articles 19, 20, 21
  4. Articles 32, 226, 136

Answer: Articles 14, 19, 21

Explanation: Articles 14 (equality), 19 (freedoms), and 21 (life and personal liberty) are called the 'Golden Triangle' as they are most frequently invoked and mutually reinforce each other.
