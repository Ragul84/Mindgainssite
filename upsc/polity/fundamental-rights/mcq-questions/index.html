Fundamental Rights MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Fundamental Rights questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 420 questions.

26. Article 48A (Protection of environment) was added by:

  1. 42nd Amendment (1976)
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 52nd Amendment
  4. 61st Amendment

Answer: 42nd Amendment (1976)

Explanation: Article 48A (protection of environment, forests, and wildlife) was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment 1976. Article 51A(g) imposes corresponding duty on citizens.

27. The Minerva Mills case (1980) established that:

  1. Parliament cannot destroy Fundamental Rights by amending DPSPs
  2. DPSPs override Fundamental Rights
  3. Parliament has unlimited amendment power
  4. Preamble is not part of Constitution

Answer: Parliament cannot destroy Fundamental Rights by amending DPSPs

Explanation: Minerva Mills case (1980): SC held that Parliament cannot destroy the balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs — the 42nd Amendment's attempt to give DPSPs absolute primacy was unconstitutional.

28. Fundamental Duties are contained in:

  1. Article 51A (Part IVA)
  2. Article 51 (Part IV)
  3. Article 19 (Part III)
  4. Article 52 (Part V)

Answer: Article 51A (Part IVA)

Explanation: Fundamental Duties are in Article 51A (Part IVA), added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) based on Swaran Singh Committee recommendations. 11th duty added by 86th Amendment (2002).

29. Originally Fundamental Duties were:

  1. 10 in number
  2. 11 in number
  3. 8 in number
  4. 12 in number

Answer: 10 in number

Explanation: Originally 10 Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). The 11th Fundamental Duty (provide education to children aged 6-14) was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

30. Fundamental Duties were added on the recommendation of:

  1. Swaran Singh Committee
  2. Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
  3. Sarkaria Commission
  4. Shah Commission

Answer: Swaran Singh Committee

Explanation: Swaran Singh Committee (1976) recommended adding Fundamental Duties to the Constitution. The 42nd Amendment implemented 10 of these duties.

31. Which Parliamentary procedure is associated with 'starred questions'?

  1. Questions requiring oral answers
  2. Questions requiring written answers
  3. Questions about budget
  4. Questions about international affairs

Answer: Questions requiring oral answers

Explanation: Starred questions (marked with *) require oral answers and are followed by supplementary questions. Unstarred questions require written answers and no supplementary questions are allowed.

32. The President's term of office is:

  1. 5 years
  2. 6 years
  3. 4 years
  4. Until death

Answer: 5 years

Explanation: The President holds office for 5 years (Article 56). Can be re-elected any number of times. Can resign by writing to Vice President.

33. The concept of 'due process of law' in India was elaborated in:

  1. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
  2. Kesavananda Bharati case
  3. Golaknath case
  4. A.K. Gopalan case

Answer: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)

Explanation: Maneka Gandhi case (1978): SC interpreted Article 21 broadly — procedure for depriving life/liberty must be fair, just, and reasonable (not mere 'procedure established by law' as literally stated).

34. Which case established that Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right?

  1. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)
  2. Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
  3. Maneka Gandhi (1978)
  4. Vishaka v. Rajasthan (1997)

Answer: Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)

Explanation: Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017): 9-judge bench unanimously held that Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right under Article 21.

35. Article 13 of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void
  2. Right to equality
  3. Rights of minorities
  4. Abolition of untouchability

Answer: Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void

Explanation: Article 13: Any law that takes away or abridges Fundamental Rights is void to the extent of inconsistency. It also prohibits the State from making such laws — foundation of judicial review.

36. The 'Right to be forgotten' relates to:

  1. Right to Privacy (Article 21)
  2. Right to Information
  3. Right to Expression
  4. Freedom of Press

Answer: Right to Privacy (Article 21)

Explanation: 'Right to be forgotten' (right to erasure of personal data from internet) flows from Right to Privacy under Article 21, as elaborated in the Puttaswamy judgment (2017).

37. 'Stare Decisis' means:

  1. Following previous judicial decisions (precedent)
  2. State security
  3. Stay of proceedings
  4. State decision

Answer: Following previous judicial decisions (precedent)

Explanation: Stare decisis (Latin: 'stand by what has been decided') is the doctrine of precedent — courts follow earlier decisions (especially of higher courts) in similar cases.

38. 'Letter Jurisdiction' of High Courts is under:

  1. Article 226
  2. Article 227
  3. Article 228
  4. Article 225

Answer: Article 226

Explanation: Article 226 gives High Courts power to issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo Warranto) for enforcement of Fundamental Rights and other legal rights.

39. The Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) was established by:

  1. CVC Act 2003
  2. Executive order in 1964 and later CVC Act 2003
  3. Constitutional Amendment
  4. Parliament Act 1990

Answer: CVC Act 2003

Explanation: CVC was established by executive order in 1964 (on recommendation of Santhanam Committee on Corruption Prevention). It was given statutory status by the CVC Act 2003.

40. Which writ is known as 'Bulwark of personal liberty'?

  1. Habeas Corpus
  2. Mandamus
  3. Certiorari
  4. Quo Warranto

Answer: Habeas Corpus

Explanation: Habeas Corpus (Latin: 'to have the body') is called the great writ/bulwark of personal liberty. It directs the detaining authority to produce the detained person before court and justify detention.

41. Writ of Prohibition is issued to:

  1. Prohibit a lower court from exceeding jurisdiction
  2. Command performance of duty
  3. Quash court orders
  4. Challenge illegal detention

Answer: Prohibit a lower court from exceeding jurisdiction

Explanation: Writ of Prohibition is issued to prevent a lower court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction or usurping jurisdiction it does not have. It is issued before judgment.

42. Can High Courts issue all 5 writs?

  1. Yes, under Article 226 (and for FRs under Article 226)
  2. No, only SC can issue all 5
  3. HC can issue only Habeas Corpus
  4. HC can issue writs only for FRs

Answer: Yes, under Article 226 (and for FRs under Article 226)

Explanation: Article 226 gives High Courts power to issue all 5 writs — not just for enforcement of Fundamental Rights but also for 'any other purpose'. SC under Article 32 issues writs only for FR enforcement.

43. Which committee drafted the Fundamental Rights provisions?

  1. Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee under J.B. Kripalani
  2. Drafting Committee under B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Union Powers Committee under Nehru
  4. Minorities Sub-Committee under Sardar Patel

Answer: Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee under J.B. Kripalani

Explanation: The Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee was chaired by J.B. Kripalani. Sardar Patel chaired the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights and Minorities.

44. Article 21 of the Constitution guarantees:

  1. Protection of life and personal liberty
  2. Right to equality
  3. Right to freedom of speech
  4. Right to religion

Answer: Protection of life and personal liberty

Explanation: Article 21 states no person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Post-Maneka Gandhi (1978), this procedure must be fair and reasonable.

45. The Right to Education (Article 21A) applies to children in the age group:

  1. 6-14 years
  2. 5-18 years
  3. 6-18 years
  4. 3-14 years

Answer: 6-14 years

Explanation: Article 21A (inserted by 86th Amendment, 2002) provides free and compulsory education to children in the age of 6-14 years. The RTE Act 2009 implemented this right.

46. Which Article prohibits employment of children below 14 years in hazardous work?

  1. Article 24
  2. Article 23
  3. Article 39(f)
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 24

Explanation: Article 24 prohibits employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines or other hazardous employment. The Child Labour (Prohibition & Regulation) Act implements this.

47. Freedom of press in India is derived from which Article?

  1. Article 19(1)(a)
  2. Article 19(1)(b)
  3. Article 19(1)(g)
  4. Article 14

Answer: Article 19(1)(a)

Explanation: Freedom of the press is implicitly included in Article 19(1)(a) — Freedom of speech and expression. The Supreme Court has held press freedom is a part of this fundamental right.

48. Under which Article can Parliament restrict fundamental rights during a National Emergency?

  1. Article 358 and 359
  2. Article 352
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 356

Answer: Article 358 and 359

Explanation: Art 358 suspends Art 19 during external emergency automatically. Art 359 allows President to suspend enforcement of other FRs (except Arts 20 & 21) by order during any emergency.

49. 'Double Jeopardy' protection in India is guaranteed under:

  1. Article 20(2)
  2. Article 20(1)
  3. Article 20(3)
  4. Article 21

Answer: Article 20(2)

Explanation: Article 20(2) protects against double jeopardy — no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once. This is a fundamental right available to all persons.

50. Protection against self-incrimination is guaranteed by:

  1. Article 20(3)
  2. Article 20(2)
  3. Article 20(1)
  4. Article 22

Answer: Article 20(3)

Explanation: Article 20(3) states no person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself. This is the right against self-incrimination (nemo tenetur seipsum accusare).
