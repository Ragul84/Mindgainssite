Judiciary MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Judiciary questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 407 questions.

1. Which article provides for the establishment of the Supreme Court?

  1. Article 120
  2. Article 124
  3. Article 214
  4. Article 226

Answer: Article 124

Explanation: Article 124 of the Constitution establishes the Supreme Court of India.

2. The concept of 'Judicial Review' in India means:

  1. The judiciary reviews the budget
  2. The judiciary can declare laws unconstitutional
  3. The President reviews judicial decisions
  4. Parliament reviews court judgements

Answer: The judiciary can declare laws unconstitutional

Explanation: Judicial Review gives the Supreme Court and High Courts the power to examine legislation and executive actions and declare them void if they violate the Constitution.

3. The principle of 'Judicial Review' allows courts to:

  1. Write new laws
  2. Examine and strike down laws/executive actions that violate the Constitution
  3. Review election results
  4. Advise Parliament on legislation

Answer: Examine and strike down laws/executive actions that violate the Constitution

Explanation: Judicial Review (borrowed from the USA) allows Indian courts (especially SC and HCs) to examine the constitutional validity of legislation and executive actions, and strike them down if they violate the Constitution. Articles 13, 32, 226 provide for this.

4. The original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court is laid down in:

  1. Article 131
  2. Article 132
  3. Article 133
  4. Article 136

Answer: Article 131

Explanation: Article 131: Original jurisdiction of SC — disputes between states, or between Centre and states. Article 132-134: Appellate jurisdiction. Article 136: Special Leave Petition (SLP).

5. The Collegium system for appointment of judges was established in:

  1. Three Judges Cases (1982, 1993, 1998)
  2. Constitution originally
  3. Parliament Act 1950
  4. 42nd Amendment

Answer: Three Judges Cases (1982, 1993, 1998)

Explanation: The Collegium system emerged from three Supreme Court cases: First Judges Case (1982), Second Judges Case (1993) — established collegium, Third Judges Case (1998) — clarified composition.

6. Which Union Territory has its own Legislature and High Court?

  1. Delhi (NCT) and Puducherry
  2. All UTs
  3. Only Delhi
  4. Only Puducherry

Answer: Delhi (NCT) and Puducherry

Explanation: Delhi (National Capital Territory) and Puducherry are the only UTs with their own Legislative Assemblies (and thus elected governments). J&K is now a UT with Legislature after 2019.

7. The Supreme Court of India was established on:

  1. 28 January 1950
  2. 26 January 1950
  3. 26 November 1949
  4. 15 August 1947

Answer: 28 January 1950

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India was established on 28 January 1950, two days after the Constitution came into force (26 January 1950). It replaced the Federal Court of India.

8. The original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court (Art 131) covers disputes between:

  1. Government of India and one or more States
  2. Citizens and the Union
  3. Two citizens of different states
  4. High Courts

Answer: Government of India and one or more States

Explanation: Article 131 gives the SC exclusive original jurisdiction in disputes between: (a) GoI and one or more States; (b) GoI and States on one side and States on other; (c) Two or more States.

9. 'Curative Petition' in the Supreme Court was recognized in which case?

  1. Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)
  2. Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
  3. ADM Jabalpur (1976)
  4. Puttaswamy (2017)

Answer: Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)

Explanation: The concept of a Curative Petition was established in Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002). It is the last constitutional remedy after the SC dismisses a review petition.

10. High Court judges retire at the age of:

  1. 62 years
  2. 65 years
  3. 60 years
  4. 58 years

Answer: 62 years

Explanation: High Court judges (Art 217) retire at 62 years of age. Supreme Court judges (Art 124) retire at 65 years. This is different from the 7th Pay Commission recommendation of 65 for HCs.

11. The ADM Jabalpur case (1976) was overruled by the Supreme Court in:

  1. K.S. Puttaswamy case (2017)
  2. Maneka Gandhi case (1978)
  3. Minerva Mills (1980)
  4. Indra Sawhney (1992)

Answer: K.S. Puttaswamy case (2017)

Explanation: ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla (1976) — where the SC infamously held that right to life can be suspended during Emergency — was explicitly overruled by the 9-judge bench in K.S. Puttaswamy (2017).

12. 'NOTA' (None of the Above) option was introduced in Indian elections in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2014
  3. 2009
  4. 2015

Answer: 2013

Explanation: The Supreme Court in PUCL v. Union of India (2013) directed the Election Commission to provide NOTA option. NOTA was first used in 5 State Assembly elections in November 2013.

13. The Special Leave Petition (SLP) in the Supreme Court is filed under:

  1. Article 136
  2. Article 131
  3. Article 139
  4. Article 143

Answer: Article 136

Explanation: Article 136 empowers the Supreme Court to grant special leave to appeal from any judgment or order of any court or tribunal (except military tribunals). This is a discretionary power.

14. How many High Courts are there in India currently?

  1. 25
  2. 24
  3. 21
  4. 18

Answer: 25

Explanation: India currently has 25 High Courts. The latest ones established include the High Court for Telangana (2019) and High Courts for Jammu, Srinagar (after J&K reorganization). Some HCs have benches in multiple cities.

15. The concept of 'Judicial Activism' in India refers to:

  1. Courts actively using their power to protect rights and check government excesses
  2. Courts acting faster in disposing cases
  3. Judges giving political statements
  4. Courts making new laws

Answer: Courts actively using their power to protect rights and check government excesses

Explanation: Judicial Activism refers to the tendency of judges to actively protect citizens' rights through expansive interpretations of constitutional provisions, PILs, and suo motu actions — going beyond traditional passive adjudication.

16. India's Constitution is described as 'federal in form but unitary in spirit' because:

  1. It has a strong Centre with residual powers and emergency provisions to override States
  2. It has only one citizenship
  3. States have no independent judiciary
  4. Parliament alone makes all laws

Answer: It has a strong Centre with residual powers and emergency provisions to override States

Explanation: India's Constitution has federal features (division of powers, bicameral legislature, independent judiciary) but unitary features dominate: single citizenship, integrated judiciary, strong Centre, emergency provisions that can override States.

17. Which article deals with the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court in disputes between the Centre and States?

  1. Article 131
  2. Article 133
  3. Article 136
  4. Article 142

Answer: Article 131

Explanation: Article 131 confers original jurisdiction on the Supreme Court in disputes between the Government of India and one or more States, or between two or more States.

18. The 'Basic Structure' doctrine was evolved by the Supreme Court in:

  1. 1948
  2. 1960
  3. 1973
  4. 1980

Answer: 1973

Explanation: The Basic Structure doctrine was established in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) — a 13-judge bench decision by a 7:6 majority.

19. The concept of 'Constitutional Morality' was highlighted in which recent Supreme Court case?

  1. Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)
  2. Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)
  3. Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018)
  4. Both (b) and (c)

Answer: Both (b) and (c)

Explanation: Both Navtej Singh Johar (decriminalising Section 377) and Joseph Shine (striking down adultery law) relied heavily on Constitutional Morality — adherence to constitutional values, not social morality.

20. The 'Collegium System' for appointment of judges is based on which landmark judgements?

  1. First Judges Case (1981), Second Judges Case (1993), Third Judges Case (1998)
  2. Golaknath (1967), Kesavananda (1973), Indira Gandhi (1975)
  3. ADM Jabalpur (1976), Maneka Gandhi (1978), Minerva Mills (1980)
  4. Vishaka (1997), Samatha (1997), PUCL (2003)

Answer: First Judges Case (1981), Second Judges Case (1993), Third Judges Case (1998)

Explanation: The collegium system evolved through: First Judges Case (1981) — executive primacy; Second Judges Case (1993) — CJI's opinion has primacy; Third Judges Case (1998) — CJI to consult 4 senior judges.

21. The retirement age of a Supreme Court judge is:

  1. 65 years
  2. 60 years
  3. 62 years
  4. 70 years

Answer: 65 years

Explanation: Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years (Article 124). High Court judges retire at 62 years. This was the only age limit originally — fixed at 65 by the Constitution.

22. 'Public Interest Litigation' (PIL) was developed by:

  1. Supreme Court of India
  2. Parliament
  3. President
  4. Law Commission

Answer: Supreme Court of India

Explanation: PIL was developed by the Supreme Court of India in the 1980s, especially through Justices V.R. Krishna Iyer and P.N. Bhagwati, allowing any citizen to approach courts for public interest.

23. Who is the custodian of the Constitution?

  1. Supreme Court of India
  2. President of India
  3. Parliament of India
  4. Attorney General of India

Answer: Supreme Court of India

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India is the custodian and final interpreter of the Constitution. It has the power of judicial review to strike down any law or executive action that violates the Constitution.

24. The tenure of the Chief Election Commissioner is:

  1. 6 years or till age 65, whichever is earlier
  2. 5 years or till age 65
  3. 6 years or till age 70
  4. Fixed 5 years

Answer: 6 years or till age 65, whichever is earlier

Explanation: The Chief Election Commissioner holds office for 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier. The same applies to Election Commissioners. They can be removed only like a Supreme Court judge.

25. The Indra Sawhney case (1992) ruled that total reservation cannot exceed:

  1. 50%
  2. 33%
  3. 49%
  4. 60%

Answer: 50%

Explanation: In Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (Mandal case, 1992), the Supreme Court upheld OBC reservation but set the ceiling at 50% for all reservations combined (with exceptions for extraordinary situations).
