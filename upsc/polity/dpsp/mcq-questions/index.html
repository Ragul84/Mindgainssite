26. Which Article of Directive Principles directs the State to secure equal pay for equal work for both men and women?

  1. Article 39(d)
  2. Article 39(a)
  3. Article 41
  4. Article 43

Answer: Article 39(d)

Explanation: Article 39(d) directs the State to ensure that there is equal pay for equal work for both men and women. The Supreme Court has derived right to equal pay as a right under Art 14/16 applying DPSP.

27. Article 44 of the Constitution directs the State to secure:

  1. Uniform Civil Code for citizens
  2. Equal pay for equal work
  3. Free legal aid
  4. Village panchayats

Answer: Uniform Civil Code for citizens

Explanation: Article 44 directs the State to endeavor to secure a Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for citizens throughout India. This remains one of the most politically sensitive DPSPs yet to be implemented.

28. Article 48 of the Directive Principles deals with:

  1. Agriculture and animal husbandry (prohibition of cow slaughter)
  2. Environmental protection
  3. Village panchayats
  4. Legal aid

Answer: Agriculture and animal husbandry (prohibition of cow slaughter)

Explanation: Article 48 directs the State to organize agriculture and animal husbandry on modern scientific lines and prohibit slaughter of cows and calves and other milch and draught cattle. Art 48A (42nd Amendment) adds environment protection.

29. Which DPSP article relates to promotion of cottage industries in rural areas?

  1. Article 43
  2. Article 41
  3. Article 39
  4. Article 45

Answer: Article 43

Explanation: Article 43 directs the State to secure to all workers a living wage, decent standard of life and to promote cottage industries on individual or cooperative basis in rural areas.

30. The Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) 2019 provides citizenship to persecuted minorities from:

  1. Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan
  2. Pakistan and Bangladesh only
  3. All neighboring countries
  4. All Muslim-majority countries

Answer: Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan

Explanation: The CAA 2019 provides a path to Indian citizenship for Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, and Christians from Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan who entered India before 31 December 2014.

31. Article 368 of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution
  2. Emergency provisions
  3. Fundamental Rights
  4. Directive Principles

Answer: Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution

Explanation: Article 368 (Part XX) empowers Parliament to amend the Constitution. It provides three methods: simple majority, special majority (2/3rd + majority of total membership), and special majority with ratification by at least half the State Legislatures.

32. Under Article 239AA, the NCT of Delhi's legislature cannot make laws on:

  1. Police, public order, and land
  2. Education
  3. Health
  4. Housing

Answer: Police, public order, and land

Explanation: Art 239AA (inserted by 69th Amendment, 1991) gives Delhi's legislature power on all matters EXCEPT three: Police, Public Order, and Land. These remain with the Central Government. This was upheld by the Supreme Court.

33. The three-tier Panchayati Raj structure recommended by the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee consists of:

  1. Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad
  2. Gram Panchayat, Block Panchayat, District Board
  3. Village Council, Taluk Board, District Council
  4. Gram Sabha, Gram Panchayat, District Panchayat

Answer: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad

Explanation: The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) recommended a 3-tier structure: Gram Panchayat (village level), Panchayat Samiti (block level), and Zila Parishad (district level). This was formally adopted by the 73rd Amendment as the model.

34. The concept of 'Welfare State' in the Indian Constitution is reflected in:

  1. Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV)
  2. Fundamental Rights (Part III)
  3. Preamble only
  4. Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A)

Answer: Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV)

Explanation: The Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts 36-51) embody the concept of a Welfare State — they direct the State to promote welfare of the people by securing social, economic, and political justice.

35. Which DPSP directs the State to secure a Uniform Civil Code for all citizens?

  1. Article 40
  2. Article 44
  3. Article 45
  4. Article 48

Answer: Article 44

Explanation: Article 44 directs the State to endeavour to secure a Uniform Civil Code for all citizens throughout India.

36. Which constitutional amendment introduced the Goods and Services Tax (GST)?

  1. 99th Amendment
  2. 100th Amendment
  3. 101st Amendment
  4. 102nd Amendment

Answer: 101st Amendment

Explanation: The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act (2016) introduced GST in India by amending Article 246A and others.

37. Which article gives the Supreme Court power to transfer cases from one High Court to another?

  1. Article 136
  2. Article 139A
  3. Article 141
  4. Article 144

Answer: Article 139A

Explanation: Article 139A (added by 46th Amendment) empowers the Supreme Court to transfer cases involving the same or substantially similar questions of law from one High Court to another or to itself.

38. Article 368 of the Constitution deals with the power of Parliament to:

  1. Dissolve the Lok Sabha
  2. Amend the Constitution
  3. Impeach the President
  4. Remove the Chief Justice

Answer: Amend the Constitution

Explanation: Article 368 provides the procedure for amendment of the Constitution. Some provisions require simple majority, others a special majority, and some also require ratification by State legislatures.

39. The National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) was struck down by the Supreme Court because:

  1. It violated Article 14
  2. It violated the basic structure — independence of judiciary
  3. It was not passed with required majority
  4. The President did not give assent

Answer: It violated the basic structure — independence of judiciary

Explanation: In Supreme Court Advocates on Record Association v. Union of India (2015), the NJAC Act and 99th Amendment were struck down as they violated the 'independence of the judiciary', a basic structure element.

40. The State Election Commission (for local body elections) was established by:

  1. 73rd and 74th Amendments (Articles 243K and 243ZA)
  2. An ordinary state law
  3. The Election Commission of India
  4. Parliament

Answer: 73rd and 74th Amendments (Articles 243K and 243ZA)

Explanation: The 73rd and 74th Amendments made it mandatory for states to establish State Election Commissions (Article 243K for Panchayats, 243ZA for Municipalities) to superintend, direct, and control elections to local bodies.

41. The procedure for amending the Constitution is in:

  1. Article 360
  2. Article 368
  3. Article 356
  4. Article 370

Answer: Article 368

Explanation: Article 368 deals with the power of Parliament to amend the Constitution. There are three methods: simple majority (certain provisions), special majority (2/3 of members present and voting + majority of total membership), and special majority + ratification by half the state legislatures.

42. The President acts on the aid and advice of:

  1. Council of Ministers headed by PM
  2. Prime Minister alone
  3. Parliament
  4. Council of State

Answer: Council of Ministers headed by PM

Explanation: Article 74: Council of Ministers with PM as head shall aid and advise the President, who shall act in accordance with such advice (after 42nd and 44th Amendments, this is binding).

43. The Pocket Veto (keeping a bill indefinitely) can be exercised by:

  1. President when no time limit is specified for bill sent for assent
  2. President for all bills
  3. Vice President
  4. Only for Money Bills

Answer: President when no time limit is specified for bill sent for assent

Explanation: For ordinary bills (not Money Bills or Constitutional Amendments), there is no time limit for Presidential assent — the President can keep it indefinitely (pocket veto), as was done by Zail Singh in 1986.

44. Under Article 352, National Emergency can be proclaimed on grounds of:

  1. War, External Aggression, or Armed Rebellion
  2. Natural Disaster only
  3. Financial crisis only
  4. Constitutional breakdown only

Answer: War, External Aggression, or Armed Rebellion

Explanation: National Emergency (Article 352) can be proclaimed on grounds of: (i) War, (ii) External Aggression, or (iii) Armed Rebellion (formerly 'internal disturbance', changed by 44th Amendment).

45. Financial Emergency (Article 360) has been invoked in India:

  1. Never
  2. Once (1975)
  3. Twice
  4. Three times

Answer: Never

Explanation: Financial Emergency under Article 360 has NEVER been proclaimed in India since independence. It can be proclaimed when the financial stability or credit of India or any state is threatened.

46. The 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) changed the term 'Internal Disturbance' in Article 352 to:

  1. Armed Rebellion
  2. Civil War
  3. Insurgency
  4. Revolution

Answer: Armed Rebellion

Explanation: 44th Amendment (1978) changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion' in Article 352, making it harder to impose National Emergency on minor internal disturbances.

47. 'Gram Sabha' is a body consisting of:

  1. All registered voters in a village
  2. Elected members of Gram Panchayat
  3. Appointed officials
  4. Village heads only

Answer: All registered voters in a village

Explanation: Gram Sabha is a body consisting of all persons registered in the electoral rolls of the village — the primary democratic unit at grassroots level under the 73rd Amendment.

48. Which subject is in the Concurrent List?

  1. Education (Entry 25)
  2. Defence
  3. Police
  4. Agriculture

Answer: Education (Entry 25)

Explanation: Education (Entry 25 of Concurrent List) was moved from State List to Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment (1976). Both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate on it.

49. How many types of majority are needed for Constitutional Amendments?

  1. Three types
  2. Two types
  3. One type
  4. Four types

Answer: Three types

Explanation: Constitutional amendments require: (1) Simple majority (for some provisions like Article 5), (2) Special majority: 2/3rd present and voting + majority of total membership (most amendments), (3) Special majority + ratification by half the states.

50. The 101st Constitutional Amendment (2016) introduced:

  1. Goods and Services Tax (GST)
  2. Right to Privacy
  3. Section 377 repeal
  4. Lokpal

Answer: Goods and Services Tax (GST)

Explanation: 101st Constitutional Amendment (September 2016) introduced the Goods and Services Tax (GST) — a comprehensive indirect tax replacing multiple central and state taxes.
