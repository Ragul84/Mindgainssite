UPSC Polity practice

Parliament MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Parliament questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The President of India is elected by:

  1. All citizens of India
  2. Members of Parliament only
  3. Elected members of Parliament and State Legislatures
  4. Governors of all States

Answer: Elected members of Parliament and State Legislatures

Explanation: The President is elected by an Electoral College consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of all State Legislative Assemblies.

2. Which article empowers the Parliament to create new States or alter the boundaries of existing States?

  1. Article 1
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 3
  4. Article 4

Answer: Article 3

Explanation: Article 3 empowers Parliament to form new States by separation, union, or merger of existing States, or by altering boundaries, names, or areas.

3. What is the quorum required to conduct business in Lok Sabha?

  1. 1/5th of total members
  2. 1/10th of total members
  3. 1/3rd of total members
  4. Half the members

Answer: 1/10th of total members

Explanation: The quorum to constitute a meeting of either House is 1/10th of total membership.

4. How many members can the President nominate to the Rajya Sabha?

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 10
  4. 12

Answer: 12

Explanation: The President nominates 12 members to Rajya Sabha who have distinguished themselves in literature, science, art, or social service.

5. A 'No Confidence Motion' can be moved against:

  1. The President
  2. The Council of Ministers (the Cabinet) in the Lok Sabha
  3. Individual ministers only
  4. The Speaker

Answer: The Council of Ministers (the Cabinet) in the Lok Sabha

Explanation: A No Confidence Motion (or Motion of No Confidence) is moved in the Lok Sabha against the Council of Ministers. If passed, the Prime Minister and Cabinet must resign. Requires support of at least 50 Lok Sabha members to be admitted.

6. The Vice-President of India is elected by:

  1. Members of Lok Sabha only
  2. Members of Electoral College (same as President)
  3. Members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) — not state legislatures
  4. Members of Rajya Sabha only

Answer: Members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) — not state legislatures

Explanation: The Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament (Article 66) — Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha. State legislators are NOT part of this electoral process (unlike President's election).

7. President's Rule (State Emergency) is imposed under:

  1. Article 352
  2. Article 356 (if state government cannot function according to Constitution — on Governor's report or otherwise)
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 361

Answer: Article 356 (if state government cannot function according to Constitution — on Governor's report or otherwise)

Explanation: Article 356 (President's Rule/State Emergency): The President can take over state government if state constitutional machinery fails. Proclaimed on Governor's report (or other information). Parliament must approve every 6 months. Maximum: 3 years.

8. A judge of the Supreme Court can be removed by:

  1. President on the recommendation of PM
  2. An address of Parliament (both Houses) by special majority presented to the President (impeachment)
  3. Chief Justice of India
  4. Directly by the President

Answer: An address of Parliament (both Houses) by special majority presented to the President (impeachment)

Explanation: A Supreme Court judge can only be removed by the President on an address of Parliament — both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha must pass the resolution by a special majority (majority of total membership + 2/3 of members present and voting). This is called impeachment.

9. The Parliamentary form of government in India is borrowed from:

  1. British Constitution
  2. US Constitution
  3. Australian Constitution
  4. French Constitution

Answer: British Constitution

Explanation: Parliamentary system (Cabinet government, Prime Minister's role, collective responsibility) is borrowed from the British (Westminster) model. Also known as Westminster Model.

10. The maximum gap between two sessions of Parliament is:

  1. 6 months
  2. 3 months
  3. 1 year
  4. 2 months

Answer: 6 months

Explanation: The Constitution requires that Parliament must meet at least twice a year and the gap between two sessions must not exceed 6 months (Article 85).

11. Money Bills can be introduced only in:

  1. Lok Sabha
  2. Rajya Sabha
  3. Both Houses
  4. Either House

Answer: Lok Sabha

Explanation: Money Bills (Article 110) can be introduced only in Lok Sabha (Article 109). Rajya Sabha can only delay it for 14 days but cannot amend or reject it.

12. The Rajya Sabha is a:

  1. Permanent House (never dissolved)
  2. Temporary House
  3. House elected every 5 years
  4. House with fixed 250 members

Answer: Permanent House (never dissolved)

Explanation: Rajya Sabha is a permanent body — it is never dissolved as a whole. One-third of its members retire every two years (6-year term for each member).

13. The Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected by:

  1. Members of Lok Sabha
  2. President of India
  3. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  4. Council of Ministers

Answer: Members of Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected by the members of Lok Sabha from among themselves (Article 93). Similarly, the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha is elected by its members.

14. Zero Hour in Parliament refers to:

  1. Questions asked without prior notice
  2. 12:00 noon parliamentary business
  3. Period after oath taking
  4. Vote on account discussion

Answer: Questions asked without prior notice

Explanation: Zero Hour (informal institution) begins at noon (12:00) after Question Hour, allowing members to raise urgent public matters without prior notice to the Speaker.

15. A joint sitting of Parliament is presided over by:

  1. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  2. President of India
  3. Vice President
  4. Prime Minister

Answer: Speaker of Lok Sabha

Explanation: Joint sitting of both Houses (Article 108) in case of deadlock is presided over by the Speaker of Lok Sabha (or Deputy Speaker in Speaker's absence).

16. The Budget is presented by the:

  1. Finance Minister in Lok Sabha
  2. Prime Minister
  3. President
  4. Planning Commission

Answer: Finance Minister in Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Union Budget (Annual Financial Statement) is presented by the Finance Minister in the Lok Sabha (traditionally on the last working day of February, now changed to February 1).

17. Quorum in the Lok Sabha is:

  1. 1/10th of total membership
  2. 1/2 of total membership
  3. Simple majority
  4. 2/3rd majority

Answer: 1/10th of total membership

Explanation: Quorum for Lok Sabha (Article 100): 1/10th of total membership = 55 members (out of 543). Similarly for Rajya Sabha: 1/10th = 25 members (out of 250).

18. Minimum age to become President of India:

  1. 35 years
  2. 25 years
  3. 30 years
  4. 40 years

Answer: 35 years

Explanation: To be eligible for President: Must be a citizen of India, minimum 35 years of age, qualified to be a member of Lok Sabha (Article 58).

19. Which article gives Parliament the power to create new states?

  1. Article 3
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 4
  4. Article 5

Answer: Article 3

Explanation: Article 3 empowers Parliament to form new states, increase/decrease areas, alter boundaries or change names of existing states. Such bills require Presidential recommendation.

20. The minimum number of members required to sign a notice of No-Confidence Motion is:

  1. 50 members
  2. 100 members
  3. 25 members
  4. Simple majority

Answer: 50 members

Explanation: A No-Confidence Motion can be introduced in Lok Sabha if at least 50 members support it (notice signed by minimum 50 members). The Speaker then admits the motion.

21. Parliamentary privileges are codified in:

  1. Articles 105 and 194
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 14
  4. Not codified, in Articles 105 and 194

Answer: Not codified, in Articles 105 and 194

Explanation: Parliamentary privileges are mentioned in Articles 105 (Parliament) and 194 (State Legislatures). They are NOT fully codified — courts have ruled that privileges exist as they did in British Parliament in 1947.

22. The Speaker of Lok Sabha vacates office:

  1. When new Lok Sabha meets for the first time
  2. After 5 years
  3. On completion of term
  4. When new government is formed

Answer: When new Lok Sabha meets for the first time

Explanation: The Speaker vacates office not when Lok Sabha is dissolved, but 'immediately before the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha' (Article 94). The Speaker thus continues for the first sitting of the new House.

23. Under which article can Parliament legislate on State List subjects?

  1. Articles 249, 250, 252, 253
  2. Article 246
  3. Article 248
  4. Article 245

Answer: Articles 249, 250, 252, 253

Explanation: Parliament can legislate on State List under: Art. 249 (RS resolution for national interest), Art. 250 (during Emergency), Art. 252 (2 or more states request), Art. 253 (for treaty/agreement).

24. Rajya Sabha's special power to authorize Parliament to legislate on State subjects is under:

  1. Article 249
  2. Article 246
  3. Article 248
  4. Article 252

Answer: Article 249

Explanation: Article 249: If Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by 2/3rd majority that it is necessary in national interest, Parliament can legislate on a State List subject for 1 year (renewable).

25. Rajya Sabha can authorize creation of new All India Services under:

  1. Article 312
  2. Article 315
  3. Article 309
  4. Article 310

Answer: Article 312

Explanation: Article 312: Rajya Sabha can authorize Parliament to create new All India Services (like IAS, IPS, IFS) by 2/3rd majority resolution, if it declares such services as necessary in national interest.
