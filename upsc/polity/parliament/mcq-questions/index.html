Parliament MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Parliament questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. A political party gets National Party status if:

  1. Wins 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 states OR recognized in 4 states
  2. Wins elections in 4 states
  3. Gets 10% of votes nationally
  4. Has presence in all states

Answer: Wins 2% of Lok Sabha seats from 3 states OR recognized in 4 states

Explanation: A party is recognized as a National Party if: (a) Secures at least 6% of votes in 4 states and 4 Lok Sabha seats, OR (b) Wins at least 2% of total Lok Sabha seats from at least 3 different states.

27. The Delimitation Commission is established by:

  1. Delimitation Act passed by Parliament
  2. Constitutional provision
  3. President's order
  4. Election Commission

Answer: Delimitation Act passed by Parliament

Explanation: Delimitation Commission is established by an Act of Parliament (Delimitation Act). It determines boundaries of constituencies for Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

28. 'First Past the Post' electoral system used in India means:

  1. Candidate with most votes wins even if less than 50%
  2. Winner must get 50%+1 votes
  3. Proportional representation
  4. Second round if no majority

Answer: Candidate with most votes wins even if less than 50%

Explanation: First Past the Post (FPTP) or Simple Plurality System: The candidate with the highest number of votes wins, even if they have less than 50% of votes. Used for Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha elections.

29. Election to Rajya Sabha is conducted through:

  1. Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote
  2. Direct election by people
  3. Appointment by President
  4. Nomination by state legislatures

Answer: Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote

Explanation: Rajya Sabha members are elected by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies through Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV).

30. VVPATs (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trails) were introduced in India in:

  1. 2013 (Nagaland state election)
  2. 2004
  3. 2009
  4. 2014

Answer: 2013 (Nagaland state election)

Explanation: VVPATs were first used in India in the Nagaland state election in 2013. They were used in all Lok Sabha constituencies from 2019 elections.

31. Article 370 of the Constitution dealt with:

  1. Special status of Jammu & Kashmir
  2. Scheduled Tribes
  3. Anti-Defection
  4. Amendments

Answer: Special status of Jammu & Kashmir

Explanation: Article 370 (now abrogated by Presidential Order in August 2019 ratified by Parliament) gave special autonomous status to J&K, limiting Parliament's legislative powers over J&K.

32. 'Privilege Motion' in Parliament is moved against:

  1. Ministers/members for breach of parliamentary privilege
  2. Citizens
  3. Judges
  4. Civil servants

Answer: Ministers/members for breach of parliamentary privilege

Explanation: Privilege Motion is moved when a member believes that a minister or another member has committed a breach of parliamentary privilege (e.g., misleading the House, contempt of Parliament).

33. Which feature was borrowed from the Australian Constitution?

  1. Concurrent List and joint sitting of Parliament
  2. Parliamentary system
  3. Judicial Review
  4. Fundamental Rights

Answer: Concurrent List and joint sitting of Parliament

Explanation: From Australia: Concurrent List (Concurrent Legislative List), joint sitting of Parliament (Art 108), and the term 'Commonwealth'. The Parliamentary system came from Britain.

34. The concept of 'Rule of Law' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from:

  1. Britain (UK)
  2. USA
  3. France
  4. USSR

Answer: Britain (UK)

Explanation: Rule of Law, Parliamentary system, Cabinet government, bicameral Parliament, and the Speaker's role were all borrowed from the British Constitution (Westminster model).

35. The Rajya Sabha cannot be dissolved because:

  1. It is a permanent body
  2. The Constitution prohibits dissolution
  3. The President lacks power
  4. It represents states

Answer: It is a permanent body

Explanation: Rajya Sabha is a permanent body — it cannot be dissolved. One-third of its members retire every two years. Its members are elected for 6-year terms by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies.

36. The presiding officer of a Joint Sitting of Parliament is:

  1. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  2. Chairman of Rajya Sabha
  3. President of India
  4. Senior-most MP

Answer: Speaker of Lok Sabha

Explanation: Joint sittings (Art 108) are presided over by the Speaker of Lok Sabha, or in his absence, the Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha, or if he is also absent, the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.

37. Who can summon and prorogue sessions of Parliament?

  1. President on the advice of Cabinet
  2. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  3. Prime Minister directly
  4. Vice President

Answer: President on the advice of Cabinet

Explanation: The President summons each House of Parliament (Art 85), prorogues the Houses, and dissolves the Lok Sabha — always on the advice of the Council of Ministers (Cabinet).

38. The maximum gap allowed between two sessions of Parliament is:

  1. 6 months
  2. 3 months
  3. 1 year
  4. 4 months

Answer: 6 months

Explanation: Article 85(1) requires that the gap between two sessions of Parliament shall not exceed 6 months. This means Parliament must meet at least twice a year (Budget session and Monsoon/Winter session).

39. Which Parliamentary Committee is called the 'watchdog of public finance'?

  1. Public Accounts Committee
  2. Estimates Committee
  3. Finance Committee
  4. Standing Finance Committee

Answer: Public Accounts Committee

Explanation: The Public Accounts Committee (PAC) examines the appropriation accounts and audit reports of the CAG. It verifies whether money was spent for the purpose it was appropriated. PAC has 22 members.

40. The Estimates Committee of Parliament has how many members?

  1. 30 members from Lok Sabha
  2. 22 members from both Houses
  3. 45 members from Lok Sabha
  4. 15 members from each House

Answer: 30 members from Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Estimates Committee has 30 members, all from Lok Sabha. It examines the estimates in the budget and suggests economies in expenditure. Rajya Sabha has no representation.

41. The term 'prorogation' of Parliament means:

  1. Termination of a session without dissolution
  2. Dissolution of Lok Sabha
  3. Adjournment of a sitting
  4. End of Parliament's term

Answer: Termination of a session without dissolution

Explanation: Prorogation terminates a session of Parliament. Bills pending at prorogation lapse (except those before joint committees or passed by one House). Adjournment only terminates a sitting for a shorter period.

42. Which Article deals with the impeachment of the President?

  1. Article 61
  2. Article 56
  3. Article 52
  4. Article 75

Answer: Article 61

Explanation: Article 61 deals with impeachment of the President. It can be initiated in either House of Parliament for 'violation of the Constitution'. It requires a 2/3rd majority in both Houses separately.

43. The Vice President of India can be removed from office by:

  1. A resolution of Rajya Sabha agreed to by Lok Sabha
  2. Impeachment by 2/3rd of both Houses
  3. Presidential order
  4. Supreme Court judgment

Answer: A resolution of Rajya Sabha agreed to by Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Vice President can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha (passed by a majority of its members) agreed to by the Lok Sabha. No specific grounds are needed, unlike the President's impeachment.

44. Which Article requires ministers to be members of Parliament within 6 months?

  1. Article 75(5)
  2. Article 75(3)
  3. Article 74
  4. Article 88

Answer: Article 75(5)

Explanation: Article 75(5) states a minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament for 6 consecutive months shall cease to be a minister.

45. During a National Emergency, the term of Lok Sabha can be extended by Parliament for:

  1. 1 year at a time
  2. 6 months at a time
  3. 2 years at a time
  4. Indefinitely

Answer: 1 year at a time

Explanation: During a National Emergency, Parliament can extend the term of Lok Sabha by one year at a time for any length of time, but not beyond 6 months after the Emergency ceases.

46. A member of Lok Sabha can be disqualified if he/she is absent for how long without permission?

  1. 60 consecutive days
  2. 30 days
  3. 90 days
  4. 45 days

Answer: 60 consecutive days

Explanation: Article 101(4) states a member of either House of Parliament shall be disqualified if he/she is absent from all meetings of the House for a period of 60 days without permission.

47. The Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected by:

  1. Members of Lok Sabha
  2. Members of both Houses
  3. President of India
  4. Prime Minister

Answer: Members of Lok Sabha

Explanation: The Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected by the members of Lok Sabha from among themselves (Art 93). The Speaker can be removed by a resolution of the House by a majority of all the then members.

48. A bill is a 'Money Bill' if it deals with:

  1. Imposition of taxes and appropriation from Consolidated Fund
  2. Only tax matters
  3. Only expenditure matters
  4. Any financial matter

Answer: Imposition of taxes and appropriation from Consolidated Fund

Explanation: Article 110 defines a Money Bill as dealing with imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, regulation of taxes; regulation of public debt; custody of Consolidated Fund; appropriation from Consolidated Fund, etc.

49. The Budget is presented in Parliament by:

  1. Finance Minister
  2. Prime Minister
  3. President
  4. Chief Economic Advisor

Answer: Finance Minister

Explanation: The Union Budget (Annual Financial Statement under Art 112) is presented by the Finance Minister in Lok Sabha. The President causes it to be laid before both Houses.

50. 'Cut Motions' in Parliament can be moved only on:

  1. Demands for Grants in Budget
  2. Any ordinary bill
  3. Constitutional amendments
  4. Matters of urgent public importance

Answer: Demands for Grants in Budget

Explanation: Cut Motions are moved in Lok Sabha on Demands for Grants as part of the Budget process. There are 3 types: Policy Cut, Economy Cut, and Token Cut. They express disapproval of government policy.
