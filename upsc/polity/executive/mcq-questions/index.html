Executive MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Executive questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. Which Article deals with the composition of the UPSC?

  1. Article 316
  2. Article 315
  3. Article 317
  4. Article 320

Answer: Article 316

Explanation: Article 316 deals with the appointment and term of office of UPSC members. The Chairman and members are appointed by the President. They can serve for 6 years or until age 65, whichever is earlier.

27. Which article gives the President the power to grant pardon?

  1. Article 71
  2. Article 72
  3. Article 73
  4. Article 74

Answer: Article 72

Explanation: Article 72 empowers the President to grant pardon, reprieve, respite, or remission of punishment to persons convicted of offences.

28. Article 356 (President's Rule) was interpreted in which landmark case to require 'objective satisfaction'?

  1. Kesavananda Bharati case
  2. S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)
  3. Minerva Mills case
  4. Berubari case

Answer: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

Explanation: In S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994), the Supreme Court held that imposition of President's Rule is subject to judicial review and the President must have 'objective satisfaction' based on relevant material.

29. The Finance Commission of India is constituted by:

  1. Parliament through a law
  2. President of India every 5 years (Article 280) to recommend how taxes are divided between Centre and States
  3. Prime Minister
  4. RBI and Finance Ministry jointly

Answer: President of India every 5 years (Article 280) to recommend how taxes are divided between Centre and States

Explanation: The Finance Commission (Article 280) is constituted by the President every 5 years. It recommends: how central taxes are shared with states, grants-in-aid to states, and any other matter referred. The 15th Finance Commission (2021-26) recommended 41% tax devolution to states.

30. Who administers the oath to the President?

  1. Chief Justice of India
  2. Vice President
  3. Speaker of Lok Sabha
  4. Attorney General

Answer: Chief Justice of India

Explanation: The Chief Justice of India administers the oath to the President (Article 60). In CJI's absence, the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court administers it.

31. The Governor of a state is appointed by:

  1. President
  2. State Legislature
  3. Prime Minister
  4. Council of Ministers

Answer: President

Explanation: The Governor is appointed by the President of India on the advice of the Central Government (Article 155). The Governor is the constitutional head of the state.

32. Chief Minister is appointed by:

  1. Governor
  2. President
  3. State Legislature
  4. Lok Sabha

Answer: Governor

Explanation: Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor. Conventionally, the leader of the majority party/coalition in the State Legislative Assembly is appointed CM. CM need not be a sitting MLA.

33. The Finance Commission is constituted under:

  1. Article 280
  2. Article 263
  3. Article 315
  4. Article 324

Answer: Article 280

Explanation: Article 280: Finance Commission is constituted by the President every 5 years to recommend distribution of tax revenues between Centre and States and grants-in-aid to states.

34. The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) is established by:

  1. Article 315
  2. Article 316
  3. Article 280
  4. Article 324

Answer: Article 315

Explanation: Article 315 establishes the UPSC. It conducts examinations for appointments to the services of the Union. Its chairman is appointed by the President.

35. The Chairman of NITI Aayog is:

  1. Prime Minister
  2. President
  3. Finance Minister
  4. Home Minister

Answer: Prime Minister

Explanation: The Prime Minister is the ex-officio Chairman of NITI Aayog. It also has a CEO (appointed by PM), Vice Chairman, and Governing Council (all CMs and LGs of states/UTs).

36. Which article of the Constitution talks about abolition of titles?

  1. Article 18
  2. Article 17
  3. Article 16
  4. Article 15

Answer: Article 18

Explanation: Article 18 abolishes titles: No title shall be conferred by the State. Citizens cannot accept titles from foreign governments without President's consent. Exceptions: Military/academic distinctions.

37. The Inter-State Council (Art 263) is chaired by:

  1. Prime Minister
  2. President
  3. Vice President
  4. Home Minister

Answer: Prime Minister

Explanation: The Inter-State Council is chaired by the Prime Minister and includes Chief Ministers of all States and Administrators of UTs with legislatures, plus 6 Cabinet Ministers nominated by PM.

38. The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) is a:

  1. Constitutional body under Article 315
  2. Statutory body
  3. Executive body
  4. Advisory body to State governments

Answer: Constitutional body under Article 315

Explanation: UPSC is a Constitutional body established under Article 315. It recruits civil servants for All India Services and Central Services. Its Chairperson can be removed only by Presidential order.

39. The 'Doctrine of Pleasure' in the Indian Constitution is modified in the case of:

  1. IAS officers
  2. Judges of High Courts and Supreme Court
  3. Members of Parliament
  4. State Ministers

Answer: Judges of High Courts and Supreme Court

Explanation: Article 310 provides that government servants hold office 'at the pleasure of the President.' However, this is modified for judges of Supreme Court and High Courts, CAG, UPSC members — they can only be removed through impeachment-like processes.

40. Union Territories are administered by:

  1. President through Lieutenant Governor/Administrator
  2. Governor
  3. Chief Minister
  4. Home Ministry directly

Answer: President through Lieutenant Governor/Administrator

Explanation: Union Territories are administered by the President of India (Article 239) through a Lieutenant Governor (LG) or Administrator appointed by the President.

41. The President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament and the Legislative Assemblies of the states. The election is held in accordance with the system of proportional representation by means of a single transferable vote. The President must be a citizen of India, at least 35 years old, and eligible to be a member of the Lok Sabha.

  1. Article 55
  2. Article 56
  3. Article 55 and 56
  4. Article 57

Answer: Article 55 and 56

Explanation: The election of the President is governed by Article 55, while Article 56 deals with the term of office of the President.

42. The Council of Ministers is headed by the Prime Minister, who is appointed by the President. The Prime Minister is responsible for allocating portfolios to the Ministers and advising the President on various matters. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

  1. Article 74
  2. Article 75
  3. Article 74 and 75
  4. Article 76

Answer: Article 74 and 75

Explanation: Article 74 deals with the Council of Ministers, while Article 75 deals with the appointment of the Prime Minister and other Ministers.

43. The Governor of a state is appointed by the President and holds office during the pleasure of the President. The Governor has the power to appoint the Chief Minister and other Ministers, and also has the power to summon and dissolve the Legislative Assembly.

  1. Article 153
  2. Article 154
  3. Article 155
  4. Article 156

Answer: Article 156

Explanation: Article 155 deals with the appointment of the Governor, while Article 156 deals with the term of office of the Governor.

44. The Supreme Court has the power to declare a law passed by Parliament or a state legislature as unconstitutional if it violates the provisions of the Constitution. The Supreme Court also has the power to issue writs for the enforcement of fundamental rights.

  1. Article 13
  2. Article 32
  3. Article 13 and 32
  4. Article 14

Answer: Article 13 and 32

Explanation: Article 13 deals with the power of the Supreme Court to declare laws as unconstitutional, while Article 32 deals with the power of the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of fundamental rights.

45. The President has the power to grant pardons, reprieves, and remissions of punishment to persons convicted of offenses. The President also has the power to commute sentences and suspend sentences.

  1. Article 72
  2. Article 73
  3. Article 74
  4. Article 75

Answer: Article 72

Explanation: Article 72 deals with the power of the President to grant pardons, reprieves, and remissions of punishment.

46. The Prime Minister is responsible for communicating to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers. The Prime Minister is also responsible for furnishing information to the President on any matter that the President may require.

  1. Article 78
  2. Article 79
  3. Article 78 and 79
  4. Article 80

Answer: Article 78

Explanation: Article 78 deals with the duty of the Prime Minister to communicate decisions of the Council of Ministers to the President.

47. The Governor of a state has the power to reserve a bill passed by the Legislative Assembly for the consideration of the President. The Governor also has the power to recommend the imposition of President's rule in the state.

  1. Article 200
  2. Article 201
  3. Article 200 and 201
  4. Article 202

Answer: Article 200

Explanation: Article 200 deals with the power of the Governor to reserve a bill for the consideration of the President.

48. The President has the power to appoint the Attorney General of India, who is the chief legal advisor to the Government of India. The President also has the power to appoint the Comptroller and Auditor General of India.

  1. Article 76
  2. Article 77
  3. Article 76 and 77
  4. Article 78

Answer: Article 76 and 77

Explanation: Article 76 deals with the appointment of the Attorney General, while Article 77 deals with the appointment of the Comptroller and Auditor General.

49. The Council of Ministers is responsible for aiding and advising the President in the exercise of his functions. The Council of Ministers is also responsible for formulating policies and implementing programs of the government.

  1. Article 74
  2. Article 75
  3. Article 74 and 75
  4. Article 76

Answer: Article 74 and 75

Explanation: Article 74 deals with the Council of Ministers, while Article 75 deals with the appointment of the Prime Minister and other Ministers.

50. The Supreme Court has the power to adjudicate disputes between the Centre and states, and between states. The Supreme Court also has the power to interpret the Constitution and declare laws as unconstitutional.

  1. Article 131
  2. Article 132
  3. Article 131 and 132
  4. Article 133

Answer: Article 131

Explanation: Article 131 deals with the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court to adjudicate disputes between the Centre and states.
