Constitution Features MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Constitution Features questions to revise the UPSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Who is known as the Father of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  4. Sardar Patel

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution and is called its Father.

2. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution begins with which words?

  1. We, the People of India
  2. We, the Citizens of India
  3. We, the Sovereign People
  4. We, the Democratic People

Answer: We, the People of India

Explanation: The Preamble begins with 'WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA', emphasizing popular sovereignty.

3. What does 'Secular' in the Preamble mean?

  1. India has an official religion
  2. India has no official religion
  3. All citizens must follow Hinduism
  4. Government controls religion

Answer: India has no official religion

Explanation: Secular means the State does not favour any religion. All religions are treated equally.

4. Which fundamental right is called the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution?

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Constitutional Remedies
  4. Right to Education

Answer: Right to Constitutional Remedies

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution.

5. How many schedules does the Indian Constitution have?

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 14

Answer: 12

Explanation: The Indian Constitution originally had 8 schedules; it now has 12 schedules after various amendments.

6. Which schedule of the Constitution deals with anti-defection law?

  1. Eighth Schedule
  2. Ninth Schedule
  3. Tenth Schedule
  4. Eleventh Schedule

Answer: Tenth Schedule

Explanation: The Tenth Schedule (added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985) contains provisions for disqualification on grounds of defection.

7. The Eleventh Schedule of the Constitution lists subjects relating to:

  1. Powers of Union
  2. Powers of States
  3. Panchayati Raj
  4. Municipalities

Answer: Panchayati Raj

Explanation: The Eleventh Schedule (added by 73rd Amendment) lists 29 subjects that may be entrusted to Panchayati Raj institutions.

8. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) is called:

  1. The Democratic Amendment
  2. The Mini Constitution
  3. The Social Amendment
  4. The Economic Amendment

Answer: The Mini Constitution

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment is called the 'Mini Constitution' because it made extensive changes, including adding 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble.

9. The Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with:

  1. Languages
  2. Anti-defection law
  3. Distribution of powers between Centre and States
  4. Tribal areas

Answer: Distribution of powers between Centre and States

Explanation: The Seventh Schedule contains three lists — Union List, State List, and Concurrent List — which distribute legislative powers between the Centre and States.

10. The Constitution of India originally had:

  1. 395 articles, 8 schedules
  2. 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules (1950)
  3. 448 articles, 12 schedules
  4. 300 articles, 7 schedules

Answer: 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules (1950)

Explanation: The original Constitution (1950) had 395 articles, 22 parts, and 8 schedules. After amendments, it now has 448 articles, 25 parts, and 12 schedules. It is the world's lengthiest written constitution.

11. The Preamble is considered to be:

  1. A part of the Constitution (Supreme Court's Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)
  2. Not a part of the Constitution
  3. A directive principle
  4. An ordinary statute

Answer: A part of the Constitution (Supreme Court's Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)

Explanation: Initially in Berubari Union Case (1960), the SC said Preamble is not part of the Constitution. But in Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973), the SC held that the Preamble IS a part of the Constitution and can be amended (but not the basic structure).

12. Article 32 is called the 'Heart and Soul of the Constitution' by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar because:

  1. It establishes the Supreme Court
  2. It gives the right to move Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights — right to constitutional remedies
  3. It defines fundamental duties
  4. It establishes Parliament

Answer: It gives the right to move Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights — right to constitutional remedies

Explanation: Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) allows any citizen to move the Supreme Court directly if their Fundamental Rights are violated. The SC can issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari). Ambedkar called it the 'very soul of the Constitution'.

13. The Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973) established the doctrine of:

  1. Equality of laws
  2. Basic Structure of the Constitution — Parliament cannot amend the basic structure
  3. Separation of powers
  4. Federal supremacy

Answer: Basic Structure of the Constitution — Parliament cannot amend the basic structure

Explanation: Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala (1973): 13-judge bench held (7:6) that Parliament can amend ANY part of the Constitution under Article 368, BUT cannot alter the 'basic structure' (essential features). Basic structure includes: supremacy of Constitution, democracy, republicanism, secular character, federal character, judicial review.

14. The Seventh Schedule of the Constitution contains:

  1. Forms of oaths
  2. Languages in the 8th Schedule
  3. Three lists — Union List (97 subjects), State List (66 subjects), Concurrent List (47 subjects)
  4. Names of the States

Answer: Three lists — Union List (97 subjects), State List (66 subjects), Concurrent List (47 subjects)

Explanation: The Seventh Schedule (Article 246) contains three legislative lists: Union List (97 subjects on which only Parliament can legislate), State List (66 subjects for states), and Concurrent List (47 subjects where both can legislate — in conflict, Union law prevails).

15. The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) related to:

  1. Panchayati Raj
  2. Urban local bodies (Municipalities) — added Part IXA, Articles 243P-243ZG, and 12th Schedule
  3. Elections to state assemblies
  4. Financial emergency

Answer: Urban local bodies (Municipalities) — added Part IXA, Articles 243P-243ZG, and 12th Schedule

Explanation: 74th Amendment (1992) added Part IXA and the 12th Schedule, giving constitutional status to urban local bodies (Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils, Nagar Panchayats). 12th Schedule lists 18 functions for municipalities.

16. How many subjects are listed in the 11th Schedule (Panchayati Raj functions)?

  1. 18
  2. 29
  3. 47
  4. 66

Answer: 29

Explanation: The 11th Schedule (73rd Amendment) lists 29 subjects that may be transferred to Panchayats, including agriculture, animal husbandry, fisheries, education, markets, public health, libraries, etc. The 12th Schedule (74th Amendment) has 18 subjects for Municipalities.

17. The Constitution of India was drafted by the Constituent Assembly in how many days?

  1. 2 years 11 months 18 days
  2. 3 years 5 months
  3. 2 years 6 months
  4. 1 year 11 months

Answer: 2 years 11 months 18 days

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days (December 9, 1946 to November 26, 1949) to draft the Constitution, holding 11 sessions with 165 sitting days.

18. India's Constitution is the world's longest written constitution with originally how many articles?

  1. 395 articles
  2. 442 articles
  3. 448 articles
  4. 444 articles

Answer: 395 articles

Explanation: Originally the Constitution had 395 articles in 22 parts and 8 schedules. Currently it has 448 articles (as amended) in 25 parts and 12 schedules (as of 2023).

19. The original Constitution had how many Schedules?

  1. 8
  2. 12
  3. 10
  4. 9

Answer: 8

Explanation: Original Constitution (1950) had 8 Schedules. Currently it has 12 Schedules (4 new schedules added by various amendments).

20. Which schedule lists the official languages of India?

  1. 8th Schedule
  2. 7th Schedule
  3. 9th Schedule
  4. 6th Schedule

Answer: 8th Schedule

Explanation: 8th Schedule originally listed 14 official languages. Currently it has 22 languages. Tamil was one of the original 14 languages.

21. The 9th Schedule was added by which Constitutional Amendment?

  1. 1st Amendment (1951)
  2. 42nd Amendment
  3. 24th Amendment
  4. 44th Amendment

Answer: 1st Amendment (1951)

Explanation: 9th Schedule was added by the 1st Constitutional Amendment (1951) to protect certain land reform laws from judicial review. Laws in 9th Schedule are immune from fundamental rights challenge.

22. The Preamble of India's Constitution begins with:

  1. We, the People of India
  2. We, the Citizens of India
  3. The People of India
  4. We, the Constituent Assembly

Answer: We, the People of India

Explanation: The Preamble begins with 'WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into...' — reflecting popular sovereignty.

23. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by:

  1. 42nd Amendment (1976)
  2. 44th Amendment (1978)
  3. 25th Amendment
  4. 1st Amendment (1951)

Answer: 42nd Amendment (1976)

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble during the Emergency period under Indira Gandhi.

24. The Preamble to the Constitution was amended by:

  1. Only once (42nd Amendment)
  2. Twice
  3. Never
  4. Three times

Answer: Only once (42nd Amendment)

Explanation: The Preamble has been amended only once — by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act 1976 — which added 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity'.

25. 'Justice — Social, Economic, and Political' in the Preamble was inspired by:

  1. Russian Revolution
  2. French Revolution
  3. American Revolution
  4. British Democracy

Answer: Russian Revolution

Explanation: The concept of Social, Economic, and Political Justice in the Preamble was inspired by the Russian Revolution (1917) and the ideals of socialism.
