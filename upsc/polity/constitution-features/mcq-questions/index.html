26. The Preamble declares India as a:

  1. Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic
  2. Sovereign, Democratic, Federal, Secular Republic
  3. Democratic, Federal, Secular, Socialist Republic
  4. Federal, Secular, Socialist, Democratic Republic

Answer: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic

Explanation: Preamble declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic (SSSDR). Sovereign=independent; Socialist=social justice; Secular=no state religion; Democratic=elected government; Republic=no hereditary head.

27. Is the Preamble a part of the Constitution?

  1. Yes, per Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)
  2. No, per Berubari Case (1960)
  3. Not settled by Supreme Court
  4. Only after 42nd Amendment

Answer: Yes, per Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)

Explanation: Initially in Berubari case (1960) SC said Preamble is not part of Constitution. Later in Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) SC ruled Preamble IS part of the Constitution and can be amended.

28. Article 32 is called the 'soul of the Constitution' by:

  1. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Sardar Patel
  4. Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'heart and soul of the Constitution'. It allows citizens to directly approach the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights.

29. The S.R. Bommai case (1994) significantly restricted:

  1. Misuse of Article 356 (President's Rule)
  2. Right to property
  3. Emergency powers
  4. Inter-state disputes

Answer: Misuse of Article 356 (President's Rule)

Explanation: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994): 9-judge bench held that: (a) President's Rule is subject to judicial review, (b) Floor test must be held before dismissing state governments, (c) Secularism is Basic Structure.

30. Inter-State River Water Disputes are adjudicated by:

  1. Tribunals under Article 262 and Inter-State Water Disputes Act
  2. Supreme Court directly
  3. Central Water Commission
  4. State Governments jointly

Answer: Tribunals under Article 262 and Inter-State Water Disputes Act

Explanation: Article 262 empowers Parliament to provide for adjudication of disputes relating to use, distribution, or control of inter-state rivers. Parliament enacted the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956.

31. Fifth Schedule of the Constitution deals with:

  1. Administration of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes
  2. Official languages
  3. Anti-defection
  4. Emoluments of constitutional functionaries

Answer: Administration of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes

Explanation: 5th Schedule: Administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in states (most states). 6th Schedule: Administration of Tribal Areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram.

32. The PESA Act (1996) extended Panchayati Raj to:

  1. Scheduled (Tribal) Areas of 5th Schedule
  2. All urban areas
  3. Himalayan states
  4. All UTs

Answer: Scheduled (Tribal) Areas of 5th Schedule

Explanation: Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act 1996 (PESA): Extended modified Panchayati Raj provisions to Scheduled Tribal Areas in 10 states with provisions to protect tribal customs and traditions.

33. The Constituent Assembly of India was set up under which plan?

  1. Cabinet Mission Plan 1946
  2. Cripps Mission 1942
  3. Mountbatten Plan 1947
  4. Simon Commission 1927

Answer: Cabinet Mission Plan 1946

Explanation: The Cabinet Mission Plan (May 1946) provided for the formation of the Constituent Assembly. Elections to the Constituent Assembly were held in July-August 1946.

34. How many sessions did the Constituent Assembly hold before adopting the Constitution?

  1. 11 sessions
  2. 5 sessions
  3. 7 sessions
  4. 15 sessions

Answer: 11 sessions

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly held 11 sessions over 2 years, 11 months and 18 days (9 December 1946 – 26 November 1949) before adopting the Constitution.

35. The Objectives Resolution was moved in the Constituent Assembly by:

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Rajendra Prasad
  4. Sardar Patel

Answer: Jawaharlal Nehru

Explanation: Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946, which outlined the aims and objectives of the Constitution. It was adopted on 22 January 1947.

36. The total number of members who signed the Constitution on 24 January 1950 was:

  1. 284
  2. 389
  3. 299
  4. 308

Answer: 284

Explanation: 284 members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution on 24 January 1950. The total strength of the Constituent Assembly was 389 (including 4 seats for Chief Commissioners' provinces).

37. Which schedule of the Constitution lists the powers and functions of Gram Panchayats?

  1. 11th Schedule
  2. 7th Schedule
  3. 8th Schedule
  4. 10th Schedule

Answer: 11th Schedule

Explanation: The 11th Schedule (added by 73rd Amendment, 1992) contains 29 subjects/functions of Gram Panchayats, like agriculture, rural housing, drinking water, education, etc.

38. The 12th Schedule of the Constitution relates to:

  1. Municipalities (urban local bodies)
  2. Panchayati Raj
  3. Languages
  4. Anti-defection

Answer: Municipalities (urban local bodies)

Explanation: The 12th Schedule (added by 74th Amendment, 1992) contains 18 functions of Municipalities/Urban Local Bodies, like urban planning, regulation of land use, public health, etc.

39. Which Fundamental Right was described as the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' by Ambedkar?

  1. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32)
  2. Right to Equality (Art 14)
  3. Right to Freedom (Art 19)
  4. Right to Life (Art 21)

Answer: Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32)

Explanation: Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' as it gives the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

40. The 1st Constitutional Amendment (1951) was mainly related to:

  1. Adding 9th Schedule to protect land reform laws
  2. Adding Fundamental Duties
  3. Adding Directive Principles
  4. Reorganizing States

Answer: Adding 9th Schedule to protect land reform laws

Explanation: The 1st Amendment (1951) added the 9th Schedule to protect land reform laws from judicial review, added Art 15(4) for OBC/SC/ST reservations, and restricted right to property and trade.

41. The 42nd Amendment (1976) is called:

  1. Mini Constitution
  2. Fundamental Amendment
  3. Emergency Amendment
  4. Peoples' Amendment

Answer: Mini Constitution

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment (1976), passed during the Emergency, is called the 'Mini Constitution' as it made sweeping changes — added 'Socialist', 'Secular', 'Integrity' to Preamble, added Fundamental Duties, curtailed judicial review, etc.

42. The 52nd Amendment (1985) relates to:

  1. Anti-Defection Law
  2. OBC Reservations
  3. Panchayati Raj
  4. Voting age reduction

Answer: Anti-Defection Law

Explanation: The 52nd Amendment (1985) introduced the Anti-Defection Law by adding the 10th Schedule to the Constitution. It was passed during Rajiv Gandhi's tenure to curb political defections.

43. The 100th Constitutional Amendment (2015) relates to:

  1. Exchange of territories with Bangladesh
  2. GST
  3. Cooperative Societies
  4. Lokpal

Answer: Exchange of territories with Bangladesh

Explanation: The 100th Amendment (2015) enabled implementation of the Land Boundary Agreement with Bangladesh — territories were exchanged, and the First Schedule was amended to update India's boundary.

44. The 'Doctrine of Basic Structure' means:

  1. Parliament cannot amend certain fundamental features of the Constitution
  2. Courts cannot review constitutional amendments
  3. All constitutional amendments require 2/3rd majority
  4. State governments have basic powers that Parliament cannot take away

Answer: Parliament cannot amend certain fundamental features of the Constitution

Explanation: The Basic Structure Doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) limits Parliament's power to amend the Constitution. Certain features (supremacy of Constitution, republican form, separation of powers, federalism, secular character, judicial review, FRs) form the basic structure and cannot be amended.

45. The S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) case relates to:

  1. Imposition of President's Rule under Art 356
  2. Judicial review of constitutional amendments
  3. OBC reservations
  4. Anti-defection law

Answer: Imposition of President's Rule under Art 356

Explanation: S.R. Bommai (1994) — 9-judge bench — held that President's Rule is subject to judicial review, the floor test must be the only way to determine majority, and secularism is part of Basic Structure.

46. The Maneka Gandhi case (1978) expanded the meaning of Article 21 by holding:

  1. 'Procedure established by law' must be fair, just, and reasonable
  2. Article 21 applies only to citizens
  3. Personal liberty does not include freedom of travel
  4. Article 21 cannot be read with Article 19

Answer: 'Procedure established by law' must be fair, just, and reasonable

Explanation: Before Maneka Gandhi, 'procedure established by law' meant any procedure enacted by legislature (Gopalan case, 1950). After Maneka Gandhi, the procedure must be fair, just, and reasonable — linking Art 21 with Art 14 and 19.

47. The directive to provide free legal aid to ensure equal justice is under:

  1. Article 39A
  2. Article 39
  3. Article 41
  4. Article 43A

Answer: Article 39A

Explanation: Article 39A (inserted by 42nd Amendment, 1976) directs the State to ensure equal justice and free legal aid to citizens, especially the poor. Under this, the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 was enacted.

48. Citizenship in India is dealt with in:

  1. Part II (Articles 5-11)
  2. Part III (Articles 12-35)
  3. Part IV (Articles 36-51)
  4. Part I (Articles 1-4)

Answer: Part II (Articles 5-11)

Explanation: Part II of the Constitution (Articles 5-11) deals with citizenship at the time of commencement of the Constitution. Parliament enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955 to provide for acquisition and loss of citizenship.

49. The Model Code of Conduct comes into effect from:

  1. Date of announcement of elections by Election Commission
  2. Date of filing of nominations
  3. Date of election notification
  4. Date of voting

Answer: Date of announcement of elections by Election Commission

Explanation: The Model Code of Conduct comes into force from the date of announcement of election schedule by the Election Commission. It remains in force till the completion of the election process.

50. The Prevention of Atrocities Act (SC/ST Act) was enacted in:

  1. 1989
  2. 1955
  3. 1978
  4. 2000

Answer: 1989

Explanation: The Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 was enacted to prevent crimes against SCs and STs and provide special courts for speedy trial. It was amended in 2015 to strengthen it.
