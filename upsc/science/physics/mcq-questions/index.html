26. The green pigment in plants responsible for photosynthesis is:

  1. Chlorophyll
  2. Carotene
  3. Xanthophyll
  4. Anthocyanin

Answer: Chlorophyll

Explanation: Chlorophyll (mainly chlorophyll-a and chlorophyll-b) absorbs red and blue light for photosynthesis. Carotene (orange) and xanthophyll (yellow) are accessory pigments. Anthocyanin gives red/purple color to flowers/fruits.

27. Birds belong to the class:

  1. Aves
  2. Mammalia
  3. Reptilia
  4. Amphibia

Answer: Aves

Explanation: Birds belong to Class Aves. They are warm-blooded vertebrates with feathers, beaks, hollow bones (for flight), and lay eggs with hard shells. About 10,000 species exist. Adapted for flight (wings, streamlined body).

28. Adrenaline (epinephrine) is produced by:

  1. Adrenal medulla (inner part of adrenal gland)
  2. Pituitary gland
  3. Thyroid gland
  4. Pancreas

Answer: Adrenal medulla (inner part of adrenal gland)

Explanation: Adrenaline (epinephrine) is produced by the adrenal medulla (inner part of adrenal glands, above kidneys). It is the 'fight or flight' hormone — increases heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose during stress.

29. The hormone responsible for 'fight or flight' response is:

  1. Adrenaline (epinephrine)
  2. Insulin
  3. Thyroxine
  4. Oxytocin

Answer: Adrenaline (epinephrine)

Explanation: Adrenaline (epinephrine) triggers the fight-or-flight response — dilates pupils, increases heart rate, raises blood pressure, diverts blood to muscles, releases glucose from glycogen stores.

30. Which phenomenon explains why the sky is blue?

  1. Rayleigh scattering of sunlight by air molecules (shorter wavelengths scatter more)
  2. Reflection of ocean blue color
  3. Absorption by ozone
  4. Refraction of light

Answer: Rayleigh scattering of sunlight by air molecules (shorter wavelengths scatter more)

Explanation: Rayleigh scattering: air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) much more than longer wavelengths (red). The sky appears blue because scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions. At sunset, light travels farther, and blue is scattered away, leaving red/orange.

31. A mirage is caused by:

  1. Total internal reflection of light in hot air layers near the ground
  2. Reflection of sky off water
  3. Refraction by clouds
  4. Dispersion of light

Answer: Total internal reflection of light in hot air layers near the ground

Explanation: Mirage is caused by TIR — hot air near the ground is less dense (lower refractive index) than cooler air above. Light from the sky bends back upward as it tries to enter the low-density layer, creating an illusion of water (the sky reflection).

32. The phenomenon of splitting of white light into its component colours is called:

  1. Dispersion
  2. Refraction
  3. Reflection
  4. Diffraction

Answer: Dispersion

Explanation: Dispersion: white light splits into its component colours (VIBGYOR) when it passes through a prism because different colours have different refractive indices. Violet refracts most; red least.

33. Scattering of light by particles larger than wavelength of light is:

  1. Tyndall effect
  2. Rayleigh scattering
  3. Compton scattering
  4. Mie scattering

Answer: Tyndall effect

Explanation: Tyndall Effect: scattering of light by colloidal particles (particle size ~ wavelength of light). Makes a beam of light visible in a colloid (like dust in a sunbeam through a window). Named after John Tyndall.

34. The principle of optical fiber is:

  1. Total Internal Reflection (TIR) of light through a glass/plastic fiber
  2. Refraction of light
  3. Absorption of light
  4. Interference of light

Answer: Total Internal Reflection (TIR) of light through a glass/plastic fiber

Explanation: Optical fibers work by TIR — light traveling through a dense glass core hits the glass-cladding interface at angles beyond the critical angle and is totally reflected, staying in the fiber over long distances. Used in communications.

35. Which mirror is used in the rear-view mirror of vehicles?

  1. Convex mirror (gives a wider field of view)
  2. Concave mirror
  3. Plane mirror
  4. Parabolic mirror

Answer: Convex mirror (gives a wider field of view)

Explanation: Convex mirrors are used as rear-view/wing mirrors because they produce an erect, diminished virtual image with a wide field of view. Concave mirrors are used as shaving/makeup mirrors and in searchlights.

36. Which mirror is used as a reflector in headlights and searchlights?

  1. Concave mirror (converges light into a parallel beam)
  2. Convex mirror
  3. Plane mirror
  4. Both concave and convex

Answer: Concave mirror (converges light into a parallel beam)

Explanation: Concave (converging) mirrors are used in headlights, searchlights, solar concentrators, and telescopes. When a light source is placed at the focal point, the reflected rays form a parallel (collimated) beam.

37. The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of:

  1. Speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in that medium (n = c/v)
  2. Wavelength in vacuum to wavelength in medium
  3. Frequency in medium to frequency in vacuum
  4. Density of medium to density of vacuum

Answer: Speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in that medium (n = c/v)

Explanation: Refractive index (n) = c/v (speed of light in vacuum / speed in medium). Higher n = slower light, more bending. Glass n ≈ 1.5, Water n ≈ 1.33, Diamond n ≈ 2.42 (hence diamond's high reflectance).

38. The household electricity supply in India is:

  1. 230 V AC at 50 Hz
  2. 110 V AC at 60 Hz
  3. 220 V DC
  4. 415 V AC at 50 Hz

Answer: 230 V AC at 50 Hz

Explanation: India uses 230 V AC at 50 Hz for household supply. USA uses 110-120 V at 60 Hz. Three-phase supply for industries is 415 V. The frequency (50 Hz) means current changes direction 50 times per second.

39. The unit of electrical energy consumed is:

  1. Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — also called a 'unit'
  2. Joule only
  3. Watt
  4. Volt

Answer: Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — also called a 'unit'

Explanation: Electrical energy consumed = Power (kW) × Time (hours). 1 kWh = 1 'unit' of electricity on the electricity bill. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules. A 1000 W (1 kW) appliance running for 1 hour uses 1 unit.

40. The noble gases (Group 18) are unreactive because:

  1. They have completely filled outermost electron shells (8 electrons — stable octet)
  2. They are radioactive
  3. They have no protons
  4. They are liquids at room temperature

Answer: They have completely filled outermost electron shells (8 electrons — stable octet)

Explanation: Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn) have completely filled outermost electron shells (He=2, others=8), making them extremely stable and unreactive. They are called 'inert gases'. Used in lighting (neon, argon).

41. X-rays were discovered by:

  1. Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen (1895)
  2. Marie Curie
  3. Ernest Rutherford
  4. Henri Becquerel

Answer: Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen (1895)

Explanation: X-rays were discovered by Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen on 8 November 1895. He won the first Nobel Prize in Physics (1901) for this discovery. X-rays penetrate soft tissue but are absorbed by dense materials like bone.

42. Ultrasound imaging uses:

  1. High-frequency sound waves (>20,000 Hz) to image internal organs
  2. X-rays
  3. Magnetic fields
  4. Radioactive tracers

Answer: High-frequency sound waves (>20,000 Hz) to image internal organs

Explanation: Ultrasound imaging (sonography) uses high-frequency sound waves. A transducer sends sound waves into the body; echoes returning from organs are converted into images. Safe (no radiation) — used for fetal imaging, abdominal organs.

43. The nearest star to Earth (after the Sun) is:

  1. Proxima Centauri (4.24 light years away)
  2. Alpha Centauri A
  3. Sirius
  4. Barnard's Star

Answer: Proxima Centauri (4.24 light years away)

Explanation: Proxima Centauri is the nearest star to Earth at 4.24 light years (about 40 trillion km). It is part of the Alpha Centauri triple star system. It has at least one exoplanet (Proxima b) in the habitable zone.

44. A 'light year' is:

  1. The distance light travels in one year (about 9.46 × 10¹² km)
  2. The time for light to reach the Moon
  3. The brightness of a star
  4. The age of the universe in years

Answer: The distance light travels in one year (about 9.46 × 10¹² km)

Explanation: A light year is the distance light travels in one year: approximately 9.46 trillion km (9.46 × 10¹² km). It is a unit of distance, not time. Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth.

45. The Big Bang theory states that the universe began:

  1. About 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state that rapidly expanded
  2. 5 billion years ago
  3. Always existed (steady state)
  4. Was created by a star explosion

Answer: About 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state that rapidly expanded

Explanation: Big Bang Theory: the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense singularity that rapidly expanded (Big Bang). Evidence: cosmic microwave background radiation (CMB), redshift of galaxies, abundance of light elements.

46. Black holes are formed when:

  1. Massive stars collapse after exhausting their nuclear fuel (core collapse → extreme gravity from which light cannot escape)
  2. Stars explode
  3. Two galaxies merge
  4. Planets are destroyed

Answer: Massive stars collapse after exhausting their nuclear fuel (core collapse → extreme gravity from which light cannot escape)

Explanation: Black holes form when massive stars (>20 solar masses) exhaust nuclear fuel and their cores collapse under gravity. The gravity is so strong that even light cannot escape (escape velocity > c). Detected through gravitational effects.

47. The correct order of electromagnetic spectrum from lowest to highest frequency is:

  1. Radio waves → Microwaves → IR → Visible → UV → X-rays → Gamma rays
  2. Gamma → X-ray → UV → Visible → IR → Microwave → Radio
  3. X-ray → UV → Visible → IR → Radio
  4. Radio → Visible → UV → X-ray

Answer: Radio waves → Microwaves → IR → Visible → UV → X-rays → Gamma rays

Explanation: Electromagnetic spectrum by increasing frequency (and energy): Radio < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible light < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Gamma rays. All travel at speed of light (3×10⁸ m/s) in vacuum.

48. The hormone that regulates the sleep-wake cycle is:

  1. Melatonin (secreted by the pineal gland — more at night, less during day)
  2. Cortisol
  3. Serotonin
  4. Dopamine

Answer: Melatonin (secreted by the pineal gland — more at night, less during day)

Explanation: Melatonin is secreted by the pineal gland in the brain. Its production increases in darkness (night) and decreases in light (day), regulating the circadian rhythm (24-hour sleep-wake cycle). Jet lag disrupts melatonin production.

49. The escape velocity from Earth's surface is approximately:

  1. 11.2 km/s (about 40,000 km/h) — minimum speed to escape Earth's gravitational field
  2. 7.9 km/s
  3. 3 km/s
  4. 30 km/s

Answer: 11.2 km/s (about 40,000 km/h) — minimum speed to escape Earth's gravitational field

Explanation: Escape velocity from Earth = √(2gR) ≈ 11.2 km/s. This is the minimum speed needed for an object to escape Earth's gravity without further propulsion. Orbital velocity (first cosmic velocity) ≈ 7.9 km/s for low Earth orbit.

50. Newton's Law of Gravitation states that gravitational force is:

  1. Directly proportional to product of masses and inversely proportional to square of distance (F = Gm₁m₂/r²)
  2. Equal to mass times acceleration
  3. Directly proportional to distance
  4. Inversely proportional to mass

Answer: Directly proportional to product of masses and inversely proportional to square of distance (F = Gm₁m₂/r²)

Explanation: Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation: F = Gm₁m₂/r², where G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² is the gravitational constant. The force is attractive, acts between all masses, and decreases with square of distance.
