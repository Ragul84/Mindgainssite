Energy MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Energy questions to revise the UPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The largest planet in the Solar System is:

  1. Jupiter
  2. Saturn
  3. Uranus
  4. Neptune

Answer: Jupiter

Explanation: Jupiter is the largest planet — 1,321 Earths could fit inside it. It has a Great Red Spot (giant storm), 95 known moons (including Ganymede — the largest moon in the solar system), and a strong magnetic field.

2. The specific heat capacity of water (4186 J/kg/K) is important because:

  1. Water can absorb/release large amounts of heat with minimal temperature change — moderates Earth's climate and makes water ideal as a coolant
  2. Water is a good conductor of electricity
  3. Water has high density
  4. Water is transparent

Answer: Water can absorb/release large amounts of heat with minimal temperature change — moderates Earth's climate and makes water ideal as a coolant

Explanation: Water's high specific heat (4186 J/kg/K — among highest of all common substances): coastal areas have milder climate than inland (ocean as heat reservoir), used as coolant in car radiators and nuclear reactors, and is why the human body (mostly water) maintains stable temperature.

3. The Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC) is located at:

  1. Trombay, Mumbai (Maharashtra) — India's premier nuclear research centre, established 1954
  2. Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu
  3. Hyderabad
  4. Bengaluru

Answer: Trombay, Mumbai (Maharashtra) — India's premier nuclear research centre, established 1954

Explanation: BARC (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, est. 1954 by Dr. Homi Bhabha as Atomic Energy Establishment Trombay/AEET, renamed 1967): India's flagship nuclear research centre. Developed Dhruva research reactor, nuclear weapons program, and peaceful nuclear applications. HQ at Trombay, Mumbai.

4. The unit of radioactivity is:

  1. Becquerel (Bq) — 1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second; older unit: Curie (1 Ci = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq)
  2. Gray
  3. Sievert
  4. Roentgen

Answer: Becquerel (Bq) — 1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second; older unit: Curie (1 Ci = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq)

Explanation: Radioactivity units: Becquerel (Bq) = 1 nuclear decay/second (SI unit). Curie (Ci) = 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq (older unit). Gray (Gy) = absorbed dose of radiation (1 J/kg). Sievert (Sv) = effective dose (accounts for radiation type's biological harm). Dose in Sieverts matters for radiation protection.

5. The half-life of a radioactive substance is:

  1. Time for exactly half of the radioactive atoms to decay — constant for each isotope (C-14: 5730 years, U-235: 703 million years)
  2. Time for all atoms to decay
  3. Time for substance to double
  4. Temperature-dependent

Answer: Time for exactly half of the radioactive atoms to decay — constant for each isotope (C-14: 5730 years, U-235: 703 million years)

Explanation: Half-life (t½): time for half the radioactive nuclei to decay. It is constant for each radioisotope regardless of amount or conditions. After n half-lives, amount remaining = (½)ⁿ × initial amount. Used in carbon dating (C-14, t½=5,730 y), nuclear medicine (Tc-99m, t½=6 h), and nuclear fuel assessment.

6. The largest planet in our solar system is:

  1. Jupiter — a gas giant (90% H₂, 10% He) with 79 known moons including Ganymede (solar system's largest moon)
  2. Saturn
  3. Uranus
  4. Neptune

Answer: Jupiter — a gas giant (90% H₂, 10% He) with 79 known moons including Ganymede (solar system's largest moon)

Explanation: Jupiter is the largest planet: diameter 142,984 km (11× Earth's), mass 318× Earth's. It is a gas giant with a Great Red Spot (giant storm, 350+ years old). Moons: Io (most volcanically active body), Europa (possible subsurface ocean), Ganymede (largest moon), Callisto.

7. Which planet is known as the 'Red Planet'?

  1. Mars — reddish color due to iron oxide (rust) on its surface; has the largest volcano (Olympus Mons) and deepest canyon (Valles Marineris) in the solar system
  2. Venus (hottest planet)
  3. Jupiter
  4. Saturn

Answer: Mars — reddish color due to iron oxide (rust) on its surface; has the largest volcano (Olympus Mons) and deepest canyon (Valles Marineris) in the solar system

Explanation: Mars: 4th planet from Sun, reddish appearance from iron oxide (Fe₂O₃) on surface. Features: Olympus Mons (21 km high, largest volcano in solar system), Valles Marineris (4,000 km long canyon). 2 tiny moons: Phobos and Deimos. India's Mangalyaan (MOM) reached Mars in 2014 — India's first interplanetary mission, at a record low cost.

8. The process of nuclear energy generation in the Sun is:

  1. Thermonuclear fusion of hydrogen to helium
  2. Nuclear fission
  3. Chemical combustion
  4. Radioactive decay

Answer: Thermonuclear fusion of hydrogen to helium

Explanation: The Sun generates energy through thermonuclear fusion — hydrogen nuclei (protons) fuse under extreme temperature (~15 million K) and pressure to form helium, releasing enormous energy (Einstein's E=mc²).

9. The hottest planet in our Solar System is:

  1. Venus (464°C average surface temperature due to greenhouse effect)
  2. Mercury (closest to Sun)
  3. Earth
  4. Mars

Answer: Venus (464°C average surface temperature due to greenhouse effect)

Explanation: Venus (464°C average) is hotter than Mercury despite being farther from the Sun. Venus's thick CO₂ atmosphere (96.5%) creates an extreme greenhouse effect. Atmospheric pressure on Venus is 90 times Earth's.

10. Homi J. Bhabha is known as:

  1. Father of India's Nuclear Programme
  2. Father of India's Space Programme
  3. Father of India's Green Revolution
  4. Father of India's Missile Programme

Answer: Father of India's Nuclear Programme

Explanation: Dr. Homi J. Bhabha (1909-1966) is the Father of India's Nuclear Programme. He founded the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) and Atomic Energy Establishment (now BARC). First Chairman of India's Atomic Energy Commission.

11. The Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR) is located at:

  1. Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu — India's second nuclear research centre after BARC Mumbai
  2. Chennai
  3. Coimbatore
  4. Madurai

Answer: Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu — India's second nuclear research centre after BARC Mumbai

Explanation: IGCAR (Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research) is at Kalpakkam, near Chennai, Tamil Nadu. It operates the Fast Breeder Test Reactor (FBTR) and is building the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR). Kalpakkam also has the Madras Atomic Power Station (MAPS).

12. Which scientist is known as the 'Father of Indian Nuclear Programme'?

  1. Dr. Homi J. Bhabha — founded TIFR (1945) and BARC; shaped India's three-stage nuclear power programme
  2. Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam
  3. Dr. Vikram Sarabhai
  4. Dr. C.V. Raman

Answer: Dr. Homi J. Bhabha — founded TIFR (1945) and BARC; shaped India's three-stage nuclear power programme

Explanation: Homi J. Bhabha (1909-1966): Father of India's nuclear program. Founded TIFR (Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, 1945) and AEET/BARC (1954). Devised India's three-stage nuclear power programme: PHWRs → FBRs → Thorium reactors. Died in Air India Boeing 707 crash near Mont Blanc, 1966.

Explanation: The International Space Station uses solar panels to generate electricity from sunlight.
