26. Halogens (Group 17) are reactive non-metals because:

  1. They have 7 valence electrons and need just 1 more to complete their octet
  2. They have 8 valence electrons
  3. They are always liquid
  4. They have no neutrons

Answer: They have 7 valence electrons and need just 1 more to complete their octet

Explanation: Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At) have 7 valence electrons — one short of a full octet. They readily gain one electron (forming -1 ions in ionic bonds) or share electrons (covalent bonds). Fluorine is the most reactive non-metal.

27. Transition metals are characterized by:

  1. Partially filled d-orbitals — multiple oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalytic activity
  2. Only one oxidation state
  3. Being non-metals
  4. Having no metallic properties

Answer: Partially filled d-orbitals — multiple oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalytic activity

Explanation: Transition metals (Groups 3-12) have partially filled d-orbitals. Properties: multiple oxidation states (Fe can be +2 or +3), form coloured compounds (Cu is blue, Cr is green), good catalysts (Fe in Haber process, Pt in catalytic converters).

28. Which element is most abundant in the human body (by mass)?

  1. Oxygen (~65%)
  2. Carbon (~18%)
  3. Hydrogen (~10%)
  4. Nitrogen (~3%)

Answer: Oxygen (~65%)

Explanation: Human body composition (by mass): Oxygen ~65%, Carbon ~18%, Hydrogen ~10%, Nitrogen ~3%, Calcium ~1.5%, Phosphorus ~1%. Oxygen is mainly in water (H₂O) and organic molecules.

29. Hydrogen is unique because:

  1. It can act as both a metal (alkali metal-like) and non-metal (halogen-like) — placed separately in periodic table
  2. It has only 1 neutron
  3. It is always a gas
  4. It is always radioactive

Answer: It can act as both a metal (alkali metal-like) and non-metal (halogen-like) — placed separately in periodic table

Explanation: Hydrogen (H) is unique: 1 electron, 1 proton. It can lose its electron (like alkali metals, forming H⁺) or gain one (like halogens, forming H⁻). Future clean fuel source (fuel cells).

30. Oxidation is:

  1. Loss of electrons (or gain of oxygen, or loss of hydrogen)
  2. Gain of electrons
  3. Loss of protons
  4. Gain of neutrons

Answer: Loss of electrons (or gain of oxygen, or loss of hydrogen)

Explanation: Oxidation: loss of electrons (or gain of oxygen, or loss of hydrogen). Reduction: gain of electrons (or loss of oxygen, or gain of hydrogen). Redox reactions involve simultaneous oxidation and reduction. OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

31. A catalyst is a substance that:

  1. Increases reaction rate without being consumed (remains unchanged after reaction)
  2. Is consumed in the reaction
  3. Decreases temperature
  4. Increases the amount of product

Answer: Increases reaction rate without being consumed (remains unchanged after reaction)

Explanation: A catalyst speeds up a chemical reaction by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy, without being consumed in the process. Biological catalysts are called enzymes.

32. Bleaching powder is:

  1. Calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)Cl) — a mixture of CaOCl₂
  2. Calcium carbonate
  3. Sodium hypochlorite
  4. Chlorine gas

Answer: Calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)Cl) — a mixture of CaOCl₂

Explanation: Bleaching powder (Chloride of lime) is Ca(OCl)Cl — made by passing chlorine over slaked lime. It releases chlorine on contact with water/acids, which bleaches cotton fabrics and disinfects water. Used in swimming pools.

33. Deficiency of Vitamin B3 (Niacin) causes:

  1. Pellagra (4Ds: Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia, Death)
  2. Beriberi
  3. Rickets
  4. Anaemia

Answer: Pellagra (4Ds: Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia, Death)

Explanation: Vitamin B3 (Niacin) deficiency causes Pellagra — characterized by the '4Ds': Dermatitis (skin rash), Diarrhoea, Dementia, and Death if untreated. Common in maize-based diets (maize lacks available niacin).

34. Decomposers in an ecosystem are:

  1. Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Secondary consumers
  4. Producers

Answer: Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients

Explanation: Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead organisms and waste products into simpler inorganic substances (minerals, CO₂, water), recycling nutrients back into the soil/ecosystem. Essential for nutrient cycling.

35. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is based on:

  1. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) — hydrogen atoms align in magnetic field and emit radio waves when disturbed
  2. X-ray absorption
  3. Sound waves
  4. Radioactive tracers

Answer: Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) — hydrogen atoms align in magnetic field and emit radio waves when disturbed

Explanation: MRI uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves. Hydrogen protons in the body align with the magnetic field; when radio pulses disturb them, they emit signals that are used to create detailed images of soft tissues.

36. In a chemical reaction, the law of conservation of mass states:

  1. Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products (Lavoisier, 1774)
  2. Mass increases in exothermic reactions
  3. Mass decreases in endothermic reactions
  4. Mass is not conserved in reactions

Answer: Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products (Lavoisier, 1774)

Explanation: Antoine Lavoisier (1774) established: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products. This is why chemical equations must be balanced.

37. The primary cause of acid rain is:

  1. Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from burning fossil fuels, reacting with water to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Methane
  4. Ammonia

Answer: Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from burning fossil fuels, reacting with water to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃

Explanation: Acid rain is caused by SO₂ and NOₓ emissions (from power plants, vehicles, industries) reacting with atmospheric water to form sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃). Normal rain pH ≈ 5.6; acid rain pH < 5.6. Damages buildings, forests, lakes.

38. The greenhouse gas with the highest global warming potential (per molecule) over 100 years is:

  1. SF₆ (sulfur hexafluoride) — ~23,500 times CO₂; or among common gases: N₂O (~265×), CH₄ (~28×), CO₂ (1×)
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Water vapor
  4. Ozone

Answer: SF₆ (sulfur hexafluoride) — ~23,500 times CO₂; or among common gases: N₂O (~265×), CH₄ (~28×), CO₂ (1×)

Explanation: Among common greenhouse gases: CO₂ (baseline 1×), CH₄ (methane, ~28× CO₂), N₂O (nitrous oxide, ~265×), HFCs/CFCs (hundreds to thousands×). SF₆ has the highest GWP (~23,500×). CO₂ is most abundant though.

39. The atomic number of an element represents:

  1. Number of protons in the nucleus (also equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom)
  2. Mass of the atom
  3. Number of neutrons
  4. Number of electrons only

Answer: Number of protons in the nucleus (also equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom)

Explanation: Atomic number (Z) = number of protons in the nucleus. It uniquely identifies an element. For a neutral atom, protons = electrons. Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons. Neutrons = A - Z.

40. Carbon can form so many compounds because:

  1. It has 4 valence electrons and can form 4 covalent bonds, chain (catenation), and ring structures with itself and many elements
  2. It is the most abundant element
  3. It is a metal
  4. It has 12 atomic mass

Answer: It has 4 valence electrons and can form 4 covalent bonds, chain (catenation), and ring structures with itself and many elements

Explanation: Carbon's unique properties enabling millions of organic compounds: (1) tetravalency — 4 covalent bonds possible, (2) catenation — forms long chains/rings of C-C bonds, (3) forms bonds with H, O, N, S, halogens. Branch of chemistry studying carbon compounds is organic chemistry.

41. The pH of pure water at 25°C is:

  1. 7 (neutral — [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ mol/L)
  2. 0
  3. 14
  4. 5

Answer: 7 (neutral — [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ mol/L)

Explanation: Pure water is neutral with pH = 7 at 25°C. pH = -log[H⁺]. In water: H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻; [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ M. pH < 7 = acidic; pH > 7 = basic/alkaline. pH 0 = strongest acid; pH 14 = strongest base.

42. Charles Darwin's theory of evolution is based on:

  1. Natural selection — organisms with favorable variations survive and reproduce more (survival of the fittest)
  2. Inheritance of acquired characters (Lamarck)
  3. Divine creation
  4. Random mutation alone

Answer: Natural selection — organisms with favorable variations survive and reproduce more (survival of the fittest)

Explanation: Darwin's Natural Selection (1859, 'On the Origin of Species'): (1) variation exists within populations, (2) variations are heritable, (3) organisms produce more offspring than can survive, (4) individuals with favorable variations survive and reproduce more → evolution.

43. The role of decomposers in an ecosystem is:

  1. Break down dead organic matter into simpler inorganic compounds, returning nutrients to the soil (nutrient cycling)
  2. Fix nitrogen from atmosphere
  3. Produce food by photosynthesis
  4. Consume plants

Answer: Break down dead organic matter into simpler inorganic compounds, returning nutrients to the soil (nutrient cycling)

Explanation: Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down dead organisms and waste into simpler inorganic materials (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus) that are returned to soil and atmosphere. This is essential for nutrient cycling — without decomposers, nutrients would be locked in dead matter.

44. The element with the highest electronegativity is:

  1. Fluorine (F) — most electronegative element (Pauling scale: 3.98); top of halogen group
  2. Oxygen
  3. Chlorine
  4. Nitrogen

Answer: Fluorine (F) — most electronegative element (Pauling scale: 3.98); top of halogen group

Explanation: Electronegativity increases across a period and up a group. Fluorine (Group 17, Period 2) has the highest electronegativity (3.98 on Pauling scale). It attracts electrons most strongly, explaining why HF is a weak acid despite F being in Group 17 (strong H-F bond).

45. Noble gases (Group 18) are chemically inert because:

  1. They have completely filled valence electron shells (8 electrons — stable octet, except He with 2)
  2. They are metals
  3. They are radioactive
  4. They react violently

Answer: They have completely filled valence electron shells (8 electrons — stable octet, except He with 2)

Explanation: Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn) have completely filled outermost shells (He: 2 electrons; others: 8 electrons), giving them maximum stability. They have very high ionization energies and don't normally form compounds. Kr and Xe can form compounds with F under extreme conditions.

46. The Periodic Table was arranged by Mendeleev based on:

  1. Atomic mass and periodic repetition of properties (1869) — gaps left for undiscovered elements predicted correctly
  2. Atomic number (modern periodic table by Moseley, 1913)
  3. Electron configuration
  4. Number of neutrons

Answer: Atomic mass and periodic repetition of properties (1869) — gaps left for undiscovered elements predicted correctly

Explanation: Mendeleev (1869) arranged elements by atomic mass, noticing properties repeat periodically. He left gaps for undiscovered elements (gallium, germanium) and correctly predicted their properties. The modern periodic table (Moseley, 1913) arranges by atomic number (protons).

47. The main nitrogenous waste in human urine is:

  1. Urea (CO(NH₂)₂) — produced in the liver by the ornithine cycle from ammonia (from amino acid breakdown)
  2. Uric acid (mainly in birds and reptiles)
  3. Ammonia (mainly in aquatic invertebrates)
  4. Creatinine only

Answer: Urea (CO(NH₂)₂) — produced in the liver by the ornithine cycle from ammonia (from amino acid breakdown)

Explanation: Humans excrete urea as the primary nitrogenous waste (ureotelic). Urea is produced in the liver's urea cycle from ammonia (toxic). Uric acid is the main waste in birds, reptiles, and insects (uricotelic — saves water). Ammonia is excreted by fish (ammonotelic — requires lots of water).

48. The double helix structure of DNA was proposed in:

  1. 1953 by Watson and Crick (using X-ray data from Rosalind Franklin and Raymond Gosling)
  2. 1869 by Mendel
  3. 1900
  4. 1930 by Morgan

Answer: 1953 by Watson and Crick (using X-ray data from Rosalind Franklin and Raymond Gosling)

Explanation: Watson and Crick (1953) proposed DNA double helix using Franklin's X-ray crystallography data (Photo 51). DNA = two antiparallel strands of nucleotides (sugar + phosphate + base) held by hydrogen bonds. A-T, G-C base pairing. Nobel Prize 1962 (Watson, Crick, Wilkins — Franklin died 1958).

49. Bronze is an alloy of:

  1. Copper (Cu) and Tin (Sn) — harder than either pure metal; used since the Bronze Age (3300–1200 BCE)
  2. Copper and Zinc (that's Brass)
  3. Iron and Carbon
  4. Aluminum and Copper

Answer: Copper (Cu) and Tin (Sn) — harder than either pure metal; used since the Bronze Age (3300–1200 BCE)

Explanation: Bronze: Cu + Sn (typically ~90% Cu, ~10% Sn). Harder than pure copper, corrosion-resistant. Bronze Age saw its use for weapons, tools, and ornaments. Brass = Cu + Zn (zinc). Bell metal = Cu + Sn (high Sn content for bells). German silver/nickel silver = Cu + Zn + Ni (no actual silver).

50. E-waste (electronic waste) is a concern because:

  1. Electronics contain toxic materials (lead, mercury, cadmium, arsenic, beryllium) that pollute soil and water when improperly disposed; also valuable recoverable metals (gold, silver, copper)
  2. Electronics are not recyclable
  3. E-waste produces ozone
  4. E-waste generates greenhouse gases directly

Answer: Electronics contain toxic materials (lead, mercury, cadmium, arsenic, beryllium) that pollute soil and water when improperly disposed; also valuable recoverable metals (gold, silver, copper)

Explanation: E-waste from discarded computers, phones, and batteries contains: toxic metals (Pb in solder, Hg in switches/LCDs, Cd in batteries, Be in circuit boards, As in semiconductors). When burned or landfilled unsafely, these leach into soil and water. Also contains valuable Au, Ag, Cu that can be recovered (urban mining).
