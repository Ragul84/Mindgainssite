UPSC Science practice

Chemistry MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Chemistry questions to revise the UPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Electromagnets are used in:

  1. Electric bells, cranes, MRI machines, electric motors
  2. Only loudspeakers
  3. Only motors
  4. Only generators

Answer: Electric bells, cranes, MRI machines, electric motors

Explanation: Electromagnets (coil of wire wound on iron core carrying current) are used in: electric bells, cranes for lifting scrap metal, MRI machines, electric motors, generators, relays, loudspeakers, and maglev trains.

2. The atomic number of an element is:

  1. The number of protons in the nucleus
  2. The number of neutrons
  3. The total number of protons and neutrons
  4. The number of electrons in the outermost shell

Answer: The number of protons in the nucleus

Explanation: Atomic number (Z) = number of protons in the nucleus. It uniquely identifies an element. For a neutral atom, atomic number = number of electrons. Mass number = protons + neutrons.

3. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with:

  1. Same atomic number but different mass numbers (different neutrons)
  2. Same mass number but different atomic number
  3. Same protons and neutrons but different electrons
  4. Different atomic numbers

Answer: Same atomic number but different mass numbers (different neutrons)

Explanation: Isotopes have the same number of protons (same element) but different numbers of neutrons, hence different mass numbers. Example: Carbon-12, Carbon-13, Carbon-14 all have 6 protons but 6, 7, 8 neutrons respectively.

4. The modern periodic table arranges elements in order of:

  1. Increasing atomic number (number of protons)
  2. Increasing atomic mass
  3. Increasing number of electrons
  4. Alphabetical order

Answer: Increasing atomic number (number of protons)

Explanation: The modern periodic table (Moseley's arrangement, 1913) arranges elements in increasing order of atomic number (not atomic mass as Mendeleev did). Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties.

5. The valence electrons are electrons in:

  1. The outermost shell (determines chemical reactivity and bonding)
  2. The innermost shell
  3. The second shell
  4. The nucleus

Answer: The outermost shell (determines chemical reactivity and bonding)

Explanation: Valence electrons are in the outermost electron shell (valence shell) of an atom. They determine an element's chemical properties and how it forms bonds. Elements with 8 valence electrons (noble gases) are highly stable.

6. Chemical bonds form because:

  1. Atoms share or transfer electrons to achieve stable electron configuration (usually 8 valence electrons — octet rule)
  2. Atoms lose neutrons
  3. Protons attract protons
  4. Electrons repel electrons

Answer: Atoms share or transfer electrons to achieve stable electron configuration (usually 8 valence electrons — octet rule)

Explanation: Atoms form chemical bonds (covalent, ionic, metallic) to achieve stable electron configurations. The octet rule says most atoms are stable with 8 valence electrons (like noble gases). Hydrogen needs 2 (duet rule).

7. Ionic bonds form between:

  1. Metals and non-metals (transfer of electrons)
  2. Two non-metals
  3. Two metals
  4. Non-metals and noble gases

Answer: Metals and non-metals (transfer of electrons)

Explanation: Ionic bonds form when one atom (usually metal) transfers electrons to another (usually non-metal), forming positive (cation) and negative (anion) ions that attract each other. Example: NaCl (table salt) — Na⁺ + Cl⁻.

8. Covalent bonds form between:

  1. Non-metals sharing electrons
  2. Metal and non-metal
  3. Two metals
  4. Ions

Answer: Non-metals sharing electrons

Explanation: Covalent bonds form when two non-metal atoms share pairs of electrons. Example: H₂O (water) — oxygen shares electrons with two hydrogens. Many organic compounds have covalent bonds.

9. PH less than 7 indicates:

  1. Acidic solution
  2. Basic (alkaline) solution
  3. Neutral solution
  4. Saturated solution

Answer: Acidic solution

Explanation: pH scale: 0-7 = acidic (H⁺ > OH⁻), pH 7 = neutral (pure water), pH 7-14 = basic/alkaline (OH⁻ > H⁺). Strong acids have pH near 0; strong bases near 14. Lemon juice pH~2; blood pH~7.4.

10. The chemical formula of common salt (Sodium Chloride) is:

  1. NaCl
  2. NaOH
  3. Na₂CO₃
  4. HCl

Answer: NaCl

Explanation: Common salt is Sodium Chloride, formula NaCl. NaOH is Sodium Hydroxide (caustic soda). Na₂CO₃ is Sodium Carbonate (washing soda). HCl is Hydrochloric acid.

11. Baking soda (Sodium Bicarbonate) has the formula:

  1. NaHCO₃
  2. Na₂CO₃
  3. NaOH
  4. NaCl

Answer: NaHCO₃

Explanation: Baking soda is Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃). It is used in cooking (raising agent), fire extinguishers, and antacids. Na₂CO₃ is Washing soda (Sodium Carbonate). NaOH is Caustic soda.

12. The chemical name of vinegar is:

  1. Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) — dilute solution (5%)
  2. Citric acid
  3. Lactic acid
  4. Formic acid

Answer: Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) — dilute solution (5%)

Explanation: Vinegar is a dilute solution (5%) of acetic acid (CH₃COOH / ethanoic acid). It is produced by fermentation of ethanol. Used as a food preservative and condiment.

13. The acid present in the human stomach is:

  1. Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
  2. Sulfuric acid
  3. Nitric acid
  4. Acetic acid

Answer: Hydrochloric acid (HCl)

Explanation: The stomach produces Hydrochloric acid (HCl) to aid digestion. The pH of stomach acid is 1.5-3.5. The stomach lining is protected by mucus. Excess acid causes acidity/heartburn.

14. The most abundant element in the Earth's crust is:

  1. Oxygen (about 46%)
  2. Silicon
  3. Aluminum
  4. Iron

Answer: Oxygen (about 46%)

Explanation: Oxygen is the most abundant element in Earth's crust by mass (~46%), followed by Silicon (~28%), Aluminum (~8%), and Iron (~5%). In Earth's atmosphere, Nitrogen (78%) is most abundant.

15. The chemical symbol of Gold is:

  1. Au (from Latin 'Aurum')
  2. Go
  3. Gd
  4. Gl

Answer: Au (from Latin 'Aurum')

Explanation: Gold's symbol Au comes from Latin 'Aurum'. Other examples: Fe (Iron, from Latin 'Ferrum'), Cu (Copper, 'Cuprum'), Ag (Silver, 'Argentum'), Pb (Lead, 'Plumbum'), Hg (Mercury, 'Hydrargyrum').

16. The chemical formula of water is:

  1. H₂O
  2. HO₂
  3. H₂O₂
  4. H₃O

Answer: H₂O

Explanation: Water is H₂O — two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom by covalent bonds. H₂O₂ is Hydrogen Peroxide (a bleaching agent). Water has a bent/V-shaped molecular geometry.

17. DNA is found mainly in:

  1. Nucleus (also in mitochondria and chloroplasts)
  2. Ribosome
  3. Cell membrane
  4. Cytoplasm (all of it)

Answer: Nucleus (also in mitochondria and chloroplasts)

Explanation: DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) is mainly in the nucleus (chromosomes). Small amounts are also found in mitochondria (mtDNA) and chloroplasts (cpDNA). DNA carries the genetic information of an organism.

18. Heat transfer by direct contact of particles is called:

  1. Conduction
  2. Convection
  3. Radiation
  4. Advection

Answer: Conduction

Explanation: Conduction is heat transfer through direct contact — heat flows from hot to cold through a material without the material itself moving. Metals are good conductors; wood and air are poor conductors (insulators).

19. Nuclear fission is the process of:

  1. Splitting a heavy nucleus into smaller fragments, releasing energy
  2. Combining light nuclei to form heavier ones
  3. Emission of alpha particles
  4. Beta decay

Answer: Splitting a heavy nucleus into smaller fragments, releasing energy

Explanation: Nuclear fission: a heavy nucleus (e.g., U-235) splits into smaller fragments when bombarded with neutrons, releasing enormous energy and more neutrons (chain reaction). Basis for nuclear power plants and atomic bombs.

20. A lever is a simple machine based on the principle of:

  1. Moments (torque) — force × distance from fulcrum
  2. Friction
  3. Gravity
  4. Pressure

Answer: Moments (torque) — force × distance from fulcrum

Explanation: A lever consists of a rigid bar pivoted at a fulcrum. Mechanical advantage = effort arm / load arm. Three classes of levers based on position of fulcrum, effort, and load. Examples: seesaw (1st), wheelbarrow (2nd), tweezers (3rd).

21. Stainless steel is an alloy of:

  1. Iron, Chromium (10-20%), and Nickel
  2. Iron and Carbon only
  3. Iron and Aluminum
  4. Iron and Zinc

Answer: Iron, Chromium (10-20%), and Nickel

Explanation: Stainless steel = Iron + Chromium (10-20%) + Nickel (0-35%) + small amounts of other metals. Chromium forms a thin oxide layer that prevents rusting. Used in cutlery, medical equipment, building materials.

22. The process of digestion begins in:

  1. Mouth (mechanical and chemical digestion begin here)
  2. Stomach
  3. Small intestine
  4. Esophagus

Answer: Mouth (mechanical and chemical digestion begin here)

Explanation: Digestion begins in the mouth: teeth mechanically break food, saliva (amylase enzyme) starts chemical digestion of starch. Food travels: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine.

23. The main function of the kidney is:

  1. Filtration of blood and excretion of urine (removing metabolic waste)
  2. Producing blood cells
  3. Digesting fats
  4. Producing hormones only

Answer: Filtration of blood and excretion of urine (removing metabolic waste)

Explanation: Kidneys filter blood, removing waste products (urea, uric acid, creatinine) and excess water/salts to form urine. Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons. They also regulate blood pressure (renin) and pH.

24. Which gland is called the 'master gland'?

  1. Pituitary gland
  2. Thyroid gland
  3. Adrenal gland
  4. Pancreas

Answer: Pituitary gland

Explanation: Pituitary gland (at the base of the brain) is the 'master gland' — it secretes hormones that control other endocrine glands (thyroid, adrenal, reproductive glands). Controlled by the hypothalamus.

25. The alkali metals (Group 1) are highly reactive because:

  1. They have only 1 valence electron (easily lost to form +1 ions)
  2. They are radioactive
  3. They have many electrons
  4. They are non-metals

Answer: They have only 1 valence electron (easily lost to form +1 ions)

Explanation: Alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr) each have 1 valence electron that is easily lost. They react vigorously with water (sodium + water → NaOH + H₂ gas). Reactivity increases down the group.
