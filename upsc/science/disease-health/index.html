Disease Health MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Disease Health questions to revise the UPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Carbohydrates provide how many calories per gram?

  1. 4 kcal/gram
  2. 9 kcal/gram
  3. 7 kcal/gram
  4. 2 kcal/gram

Answer: 4 kcal/gram

Explanation: Carbohydrates: 4 kcal/g. Proteins: 4 kcal/g. Fats (lipids): 9 kcal/g (most energy-dense). Alcohol: 7 kcal/g. Dietary fiber provides ~2 kcal/g (partially fermented by gut bacteria).

2. Eutrophication of water bodies is caused by:

  1. Excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates from fertilizers/sewage) causing algal bloom, oxygen depletion, and fish death
  2. Excess salt
  3. Industrial metal discharge
  4. Oil spills

Answer: Excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates from fertilizers/sewage) causing algal bloom, oxygen depletion, and fish death

Explanation: Eutrophication: excess nutrients (N, P) from agricultural runoff and sewage promote algal bloom. When algae die, bacterial decomposition consumes dissolved oxygen (hypoxia), creating 'dead zones' where fish cannot survive.

3. The process of nitrogen fixation is carried out by:

  1. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium in legume root nodules, Azotobacter free-living in soil, Cyanobacteria in water/soil) — convert N₂ gas to NH₃
  2. Plants directly
  3. Lightning (some, but minor)
  4. Only Haber Process industrially

Answer: Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Rhizobium in legume root nodules, Azotobacter free-living in soil, Cyanobacteria in water/soil) — convert N₂ gas to NH₃

Explanation: Biological nitrogen fixation: bacteria reduce N₂ → NH₃ using enzyme nitrogenase. Rhizobium (symbiotic with legumes: peas, beans, soy, peanuts). Azotobacter (free-living aerobic). Cyanobacteria (e.g., Anabaena). Haber-Bosch process (industrial, 400-500°C, 150-200 atm, Fe catalyst): produces artificial fertilizers.

4. Dengue fever is caused by:

  1. Dengue virus (DENV 1-4), transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes
  2. Plasmodium parasite
  3. Bacteria
  4. Anopheles mosquito

Answer: Dengue virus (DENV 1-4), transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes

Explanation: Dengue is caused by the Dengue virus (4 serotypes — DENV 1-4), transmitted by Aedes aegypti mosquito (day-biting mosquito, breeds in clean stagnant water). Symptoms: high fever, joint/muscle pain (breakbone fever).

5. Tuberculosis (TB) is caused by:

  1. Mycobacterium tuberculosis (bacteria)
  2. Virus
  3. Fungus
  4. Plasmodium parasite

Answer: Mycobacterium tuberculosis (bacteria)

Explanation: Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacteria. It primarily affects the lungs (pulmonary TB). Spread by airborne droplets from infected person's cough/sneeze. Treated with DOTS (antibiotics for 6 months).

6. Eutrophication in water bodies is caused by:

  1. Excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) causing algal blooms that deplete oxygen
  2. Oil spills
  3. Acid rain
  4. Heavy metal pollution

Answer: Excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) causing algal blooms that deplete oxygen

Explanation: Eutrophication: excess nutrients (N, P) from agricultural runoff and sewage cause rapid algal bloom growth. When algae die and decompose, bacteria consume oxygen, creating hypoxic (oxygen-depleted) 'dead zones'.

7. Antibiotics were first discovered by:

  1. Alexander Fleming (penicillin, 1928)
  2. Louis Pasteur
  3. Robert Koch
  4. Joseph Lister

Answer: Alexander Fleming (penicillin, 1928)

Explanation: Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928 when he noticed mold (Penicillium notatum) killing bacteria in his Petri dish. Howard Florey and Ernst Boris Chain later developed it as a medicine. Nobel Prize 1945.

8. The Polio vaccine was developed by:

  1. Jonas Salk (inactivated, 1955) and Albert Sabin (oral, 1961)
  2. Louis Pasteur
  3. Alexander Fleming
  4. Edward Jenner

Answer: Jonas Salk (inactivated, 1955) and Albert Sabin (oral, 1961)

Explanation: Jonas Salk developed the inactivated polio vaccine (IPV, injected, 1955). Albert Sabin developed the oral polio vaccine (OPV, 1961) used in India's Pulse Polio campaign. Edward Jenner created smallpox vaccine (1796).

9. What technology has enabled precise editing of genes to combat genetic diseases?

  1. CRISPR

  3. Stem cells
  4. RNA interference

Explanation: CRISPR technology allows for precise editing of genes, which can be used to combat genetic diseases.

19. What technology enables precise editing of genes to combat genetic diseases?

  1. CRISPR
  2. Gene therapy
  3. Stem cells
  4. Cloning

Answer: CRISPR

Explanation: CRISPR technology allows for precise editing of genes, showing promise in treating genetic diseases. It has revolutionized the field of genetics and biotechnology.

20. Which of the following vaccines is used to prevent COVID-19?
