Biology MCQs with answers

26. Antibodies are produced by:

  1. B-lymphocytes (B-cells) in response to antigens
  2. T-lymphocytes
  3. Red blood cells
  4. Platelets

Answer: B-lymphocytes (B-cells) in response to antigens

Explanation: B-lymphocytes (B-cells) produce antibodies (immunoglobulins) when stimulated by antigens. Antibodies are Y-shaped proteins that specifically bind to antigens and neutralize pathogens. T-cells coordinate the immune response.

27. Vaccines work by:

  1. Stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies without causing full disease (immunological memory)
  2. Killing bacteria directly
  3. Providing ready-made antibodies
  4. Reducing body temperature

Answer: Stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies without causing full disease (immunological memory)

Explanation: Vaccines contain weakened/killed pathogens, toxoids, or specific antigens. They stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies and memory cells. On future exposure to the real pathogen, the immune system responds rapidly.

28. HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks which cells?

  1. CD4+ T-helper cells (T-lymphocytes) — destroying the immune system's coordination
  2. Red blood cells
  3. Platelets
  4. Liver cells

Answer: CD4+ T-helper cells (T-lymphocytes) — destroying the immune system's coordination

Explanation: HIV specifically targets CD4+ T-helper lymphocytes (T-cells), which coordinate immune responses. As T-cell count drops, the immune system weakens, leading to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) — susceptibility to opportunistic infections.

29. The hormone insulin is produced by the pancreas and its function is:

  1. Lower blood glucose levels by facilitating glucose uptake into cells (deficiency → Type 1 diabetes)
  2. Increase blood glucose levels
  3. Regulate calcium levels
  4. Control growth

Answer: Lower blood glucose levels by facilitating glucose uptake into cells (deficiency → Type 1 diabetes)

Explanation: Insulin (produced by beta cells of islets of Langerhans in pancreas) lowers blood glucose by helping cells absorb glucose. Deficiency of insulin or insulin resistance leads to diabetes mellitus. Glucagon (alpha cells) raises blood glucose.

30. The human heart has how many chambers?

  1. Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle
  2. Two chambers
  3. Three chambers
  4. Five chambers

Answer: Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle

Explanation: The human heart has 4 chambers: right atrium and right ventricle (receive deoxygenated blood from body, pump to lungs) and left atrium and left ventricle (receive oxygenated blood from lungs, pump to body). Left ventricle has the thickest wall.

31. The blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart is:

  1. Pulmonary vein (the only vein that carries oxygenated blood)
  2. Pulmonary artery
  3. Aorta
  4. Vena cava

Answer: Pulmonary vein (the only vein that carries oxygenated blood)

Explanation: Pulmonary veins (4 of them, 2 from each lung) carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. Note: arteries generally carry oxygenated blood EXCEPT pulmonary artery. Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs.

32. The process by which a fertilized egg develops into an organism is called:

  1. Embryogenesis — cell division (cleavage), differentiation, and organogenesis
  2. Meiosis
  3. Mitosis
  4. Metamorphosis

Answer: Embryogenesis — cell division (cleavage), differentiation, and organogenesis

Explanation: Embryogenesis: after fertilization, the zygote undergoes cleavage (rapid mitotic divisions) → blastula → gastrulation → organogenesis. In humans, this process takes about 9 months. The embryo becomes a fetus after 8 weeks.

33. Xylem and phloem are plant tissues that transport:

  1. Xylem: water and minerals (upward from roots); Phloem: sugars/food (bidirectionally)
  2. Xylem: food; Phloem: water
  3. Only water
  4. Only food

Answer: Xylem: water and minerals (upward from roots); Phloem: sugars/food (bidirectionally)

Explanation: Xylem (dead tissue) transports water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves (upward). Phloem (living tissue) transports photosynthetic products (mainly sucrose) from leaves to other parts — up or down (bidirectional transport).

34. Biomagnification refers to:

  1. Increasing concentration of toxic substances (DDT, mercury) at each higher trophic level in a food chain
  2. Reduction in biodiversity
  3. Increase in plant growth
  4. Decomposition process

Answer: Increasing concentration of toxic substances (DDT, mercury) at each higher trophic level in a food chain

Explanation: Biomagnification (bioaccumulation): non-biodegradable toxins (DDT, PCBs, mercury, lead) accumulate in fatty tissues and become more concentrated at each trophic level. Top predators (eagles, polar bears, humans) have the highest concentrations.

35. Solar cells (photovoltaic cells) convert:

  1. Solar (light) energy directly into electrical energy using the photoelectric effect in semiconductor (silicon) material
  2. Heat energy to electricity
  3. Chemical energy to electricity
  4. Kinetic energy to electricity

Answer: Solar (light) energy directly into electrical energy using the photoelectric effect in semiconductor (silicon) material

Explanation: Solar cells (PV cells) use silicon p-n junctions. When photons hit the cell, they excite electrons across the band gap, generating a potential difference (voltage). Multiple cells in series form solar panels. Efficiency of commercial cells: ~15-22%.

36. DNA replication occurs during which phase of the cell cycle?

  1. S phase (Synthesis phase) of interphase — before cell division (mitosis or meiosis)
  2. G1 phase
  3. G2 phase
  4. M phase (mitosis)

Answer: S phase (Synthesis phase) of interphase — before cell division (mitosis or meiosis)

Explanation: The cell cycle: Interphase (G1 → S → G2) + M phase (mitosis/meiosis). DNA replication occurs in S phase. G1: cell growth; S: DNA synthesis; G2: preparation for division; M phase: cell divides. Cancer cells have uncontrolled cell cycle.

37. Mendel's Law of Segregation states that:

  1. Two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation — each gamete carries only one allele for each trait
  2. Traits blend together
  3. Dominant allele always shows
  4. All genes are inherited together

Answer: Two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation — each gamete carries only one allele for each trait

Explanation: Mendel's First Law: alleles (two copies of each gene in diploid organisms) segregate (separate) during gamete (sex cell) formation. Each gamete gets only one allele. At fertilization, offspring inherit one allele from each parent. Verified by Punnett square.

38. Viruses differ from bacteria in that viruses:

  1. Are not living cells — they are non-cellular; lack own metabolism; can only replicate inside host cells; are much smaller
  2. Are cells with nucleus
  3. Can grow outside host
  4. Have their own ribosomes for protein synthesis

Answer: Are not living cells — they are non-cellular; lack own metabolism; can only replicate inside host cells; are much smaller

Explanation: Viruses are non-cellular (acellular) — just genetic material (DNA or RNA) enclosed in a protein coat (capsid). They cannot replicate or metabolize independently — they hijack host cell machinery. Viruses are ~10-300 nm (smaller than bacteria, 0.5-5 µm). Antibiotics don't work on viruses.

39. The antibiotic penicillin works by:

  1. Inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis (bacteria lyse due to osmotic pressure without the wall) — does NOT affect human cells
  2. Killing viruses
  3. Disrupting DNA replication
  4. Blocking protein synthesis directly

Answer: Inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis (bacteria lyse due to osmotic pressure without the wall) — does NOT affect human cells

Explanation: Penicillin (and other beta-lactam antibiotics) inhibit enzymes needed to form cross-links in the bacterial cell wall (peptidoglycan). Without the wall, bacteria burst from osmotic pressure. Human cells have no cell wall, so penicillin is safe. Alexander Fleming discovered it in 1928.

40. Fungi are different from plants because fungi:

  1. Cannot perform photosynthesis; have cell walls of chitin (not cellulose); are heterotrophs (absorb food externally via secreted enzymes)
  2. Are photosynthetic
  3. Have roots, stems, leaves
  4. Are prokaryotes

Answer: Cannot perform photosynthesis; have cell walls of chitin (not cellulose); are heterotrophs (absorb food externally via secreted enzymes)

Explanation: Fungi are eukaryotes but NOT plants: (1) no chlorophyll, cannot photosynthesize, (2) cell walls contain chitin (like insect exoskeleton), not cellulose, (3) heterotrophic — secrete digestive enzymes externally and absorb nutrients (saprotrophic, parasitic, or mutualistic).

41. The term 'biodiversity hotspot' was coined by:

  1. Norman Myers (1988) — areas with high species richness and endemism under significant threat of habitat loss
  2. Charles Darwin
  3. E.O. Wilson
  4. Humboldt

Answer: Norman Myers (1988) — areas with high species richness and endemism under significant threat of habitat loss

Explanation: Norman Myers coined 'biodiversity hotspot' (1988). Criteria: ≥1,500 endemic vascular plant species AND ≥70% of original habitat already destroyed. There are 36 global hotspots. India has 4: Western Ghats-Sri Lanka, Himalaya, Indo-Burma, and Sundaland (Andaman-Nicobar).

42. The water cycle (hydrological cycle) involves:

  1. Evaporation (water→vapor), condensation (vapor→clouds), precipitation (rain/snow), surface runoff, and infiltration → continuous recycling
  2. Only evaporation
  3. Only precipitation
  4. Only river flow

Answer: Evaporation (water→vapor), condensation (vapor→clouds), precipitation (rain/snow), surface runoff, and infiltration → continuous recycling

Explanation: The water cycle: solar energy evaporates water from oceans/land → water vapor rises and condenses into clouds → precipitation (rain, snow) → some runs off (rivers), some infiltrates (groundwater), some transpires through plants → evaporates again. Continuous cycle driven by solar energy.

43. Cancer is caused by:

  1. Uncontrolled division of abnormal cells (due to mutations in genes controlling cell cycle — oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes)
  2. Bacterial infection
  3. Viral infection only
  4. Nutritional deficiency

Answer: Uncontrolled division of abnormal cells (due to mutations in genes controlling cell cycle — oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes)

Explanation: Cancer: mutations in proto-oncogenes (become oncogenes promoting growth) or tumor suppressor genes (p53, Rb — normally prevent uncontrolled division) lead to uncontrolled cell growth. Causes: tobacco, UV radiation, some viruses (HPV, HBV), carcinogens, heredity.

44. The disease malaria is transmitted by:

  1. Female Anopheles mosquito — the Plasmodium parasite (P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. malariae, P. ovale) develops in the mosquito
  2. Male Anopheles mosquito
  3. Aedes aegypti mosquito
  4. Culex mosquito

Answer: Female Anopheles mosquito — the Plasmodium parasite (P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. malariae, P. ovale) develops in the mosquito

Explanation: Malaria: caused by Plasmodium protozoa (most severe: P. falciparum). Vector: female Anopheles mosquito (males don't bite — they feed on nectar). The parasite develops in the mosquito's gut (sporogony) and is injected into human blood during biting.

45. Type 2 diabetes is characterized by:

  1. Insulin resistance — cells do not respond adequately to insulin (often linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and poor diet) — most common type (90% of cases)
  2. Complete absence of insulin-producing cells (autoimmune) — that is Type 1
  3. Bacterial infection of pancreas
  4. Genetic disorder only

Answer: Insulin resistance — cells do not respond adequately to insulin (often linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and poor diet) — most common type (90% of cases)

Explanation: Type 2 diabetes: cells develop insulin resistance (don't respond to insulin), so glucose cannot enter cells efficiently. Initially pancreas compensates by making more insulin; eventually it can't keep up. Risk factors: obesity, physical inactivity, poor diet, family history, age. Managed by diet, exercise, oral medications, sometimes insulin.

46. The five-kingdom classification system was proposed by:

  1. Robert Whittaker (1969) — Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia
  2. Carolus Linnaeus (two-kingdom: Plants and Animals)
  3. Carl Woese (three-domain: Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya)
  4. Ernst Haeckel

Answer: Robert Whittaker (1969) — Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia

Explanation: Whittaker (1969) proposed 5 kingdoms: Monera (bacteria/prokaryotes), Protista (unicellular eukaryotes), Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Carl Woese (1990) proposed 3 domains: Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya (most modern classification). Linnaeus (1758) established binomial nomenclature.

47. Binomial nomenclature (scientific naming of organisms) uses:

  1. Genus name (capitalized) + species name (lowercase) in Latin/Greek, italicized — e.g., Homo sapiens, Panthera tigris
  2. Common name only
  3. Only one Latin name
  4. Kingdom and phylum names

Answer: Genus name (capitalized) + species name (lowercase) in Latin/Greek, italicized — e.g., Homo sapiens, Panthera tigris

Explanation: Carl Linnaeus (1758) established binomial nomenclature: each organism gets a two-part Latin name — genus (capitalized) + specific epithet (lowercase). Example: Homo sapiens (humans), Panthera tigris (tiger), Oryza sativa (rice), Mangifera indica (mango). Universally accepted system.

48. The scientific name of humans (Homo sapiens) means:

  1. 'Wise man' in Latin (Homo = man, sapiens = wise/knowing) — classified under family Hominidae, order Primates
  2. 'Modern man'
  3. 'Tool user'
  4. 'Upright walker'

Answer: 'Wise man' in Latin (Homo = man, sapiens = wise/knowing) — classified under family Hominidae, order Primates

Explanation: Homo sapiens: genus Homo (man) + species sapiens (wise/knowing). Taxonomy: Kingdom Animalia → Phylum Chordata → Class Mammalia → Order Primates → Family Hominidae → Genus Homo → Species H. sapiens. Modern humans emerged ~300,000 years ago.

49. The exchange of gases in the lungs occurs in:

  1. Alveoli (tiny air sacs — ~700 million in lungs, total surface area ~70 m²) — O₂ enters blood, CO₂ exits
  2. Bronchi
  3. Trachea
  4. Diaphragm

Answer: Alveoli (tiny air sacs — ~700 million in lungs, total surface area ~70 m²) — O₂ enters blood, CO₂ exits

Explanation: Alveoli are microscopic air sacs surrounded by capillaries. O₂ diffuses from alveolar air into blood; CO₂ diffuses from blood into alveoli for exhalation. Large surface area (~70 m², size of a tennis court) and thin walls (1 cell thick) enable efficient gas exchange.

50. Carbon monoxide is toxic because it:

  1. Binds to hemoglobin with 200× greater affinity than O₂, forming carboxyhemoglobin (HbCO) — prevents O₂ transport
  2. Destroys red blood cells
  3. Paralyzes the diaphragm
  4. Acidifies blood

Answer: Binds to hemoglobin with 200× greater affinity than O₂, forming carboxyhemoglobin (HbCO) — prevents O₂ transport

Explanation: CO binds to hemoglobin at the same site as O₂ but with 200-250× higher affinity, forming carboxyhemoglobin (HbCO). This reduces blood's O₂-carrying capacity. Symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, death. CO is colorless and odorless — hence 'silent killer'.
