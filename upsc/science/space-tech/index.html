Space Tech MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Space Tech questions to revise the UPSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle states that:

  1. Energy and mass are interconvertible
  2. One cannot simultaneously know the exact position and momentum of a particle
  3. Light travels at a constant speed in all mediums
  4. Electrons occupy specific energy levels

Answer: One cannot simultaneously know the exact position and momentum of a particle

Explanation: Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle (1927): It is impossible to simultaneously determine the exact position (Δx) and momentum (Δp) of a particle: Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2.

2. Superconductivity is the phenomenon where:

  1. Electrical resistance becomes infinite at low temperatures
  2. Electrical resistance becomes zero below a critical temperature
  3. Materials become magnetic at high temperatures
  4. Electrons flow at the speed of light

Answer: Electrical resistance becomes zero below a critical temperature

Explanation: Superconductivity occurs when certain materials are cooled below their critical temperature, causing electrical resistance to drop to exactly zero, allowing current to flow without energy loss.

3. The unit of pressure is:

  1. Pascal (Pa)
  2. Newton (N)
  3. Joule (J)
  4. Watt (W)

Answer: Pascal (Pa)

Explanation: The SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa). 1 Pa = 1 N/m². Atmospheric pressure at sea level is about 101,325 Pa (1 atm). Named after Blaise Pascal.

4. Bernoulli's Principle states that:

  1. In a fluid, increased velocity causes decreased pressure
  2. Pressure increases with depth
  3. Buoyancy equals weight of displaced fluid
  4. Gases expand when heated

Answer: In a fluid, increased velocity causes decreased pressure

Explanation: Bernoulli's Principle: In a streamlined fluid flow, as velocity increases, pressure decreases. This explains lift in airplane wings, the Venturi effect, and why shower curtains are pulled inward.

5. Ohm's Law states that:

  1. Current (I) = Voltage (V) / Resistance (R)
  2. Voltage = Current × Power
  3. Resistance = Current × Voltage
  4. Power = Resistance × Current

Answer: Current (I) = Voltage (V) / Resistance (R)

Explanation: Ohm's Law: V = I × R (Voltage = Current × Resistance). At constant temperature, current is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance. Named after Georg Simon Ohm.

6. The SI unit of electric current is:

  1. Ampere (A)
  2. Volt (V)
  3. Ohm (Ω)
  4. Watt (W)

Answer: Ampere (A)

Explanation: The SI unit of electric current is Ampere (A). 1 Ampere = 1 Coulomb per second. Named after André-Marie Ampère. Current is measured with an ammeter (connected in series).

7. Electric power is given by:

  1. P = V × I (Voltage × Current)
  2. P = V / I
  3. P = I / V
  4. P = R × I

Answer: P = V × I (Voltage × Current)

Explanation: Electric Power (P) = Voltage (V) × Current (I) = I²R = V²/R. The SI unit of power is Watt (W). 1 Watt = 1 Joule per second. A 60W bulb consumes 60 Joules per second.

8. A fuse wire in an electrical circuit is made of:

  1. Alloy with low melting point (tin-lead alloy)
  2. Copper
  3. Aluminium
  4. Iron

Answer: Alloy with low melting point (tin-lead alloy)

Explanation: Fuse wire is made of an alloy with a low melting point (typically tin-lead alloy). When current exceeds safe limits, the fuse wire melts (fuses), breaking the circuit and preventing damage. It is the weakest link in the circuit.

9. The most abundant gas in the Earth's atmosphere is:

  1. Nitrogen (about 78%)
  2. Oxygen (21%)
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Argon

Answer: Nitrogen (about 78%)

Explanation: Earth's atmosphere composition: Nitrogen (N₂) ~78%, Oxygen (O₂) ~21%, Argon ~0.9%, Carbon dioxide ~0.04%, and trace amounts of other gases. The 1% (besides N₂ and O₂) is mainly Argon.

10. Which gas is responsible for global warming?

  1. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — main greenhouse gas, also methane, N₂O, ozone
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Oxygen
  4. Argon

Answer: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — main greenhouse gas, also methane, N₂O, ozone

Explanation: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the primary greenhouse gas causing global warming/climate change. Methane (CH₄), Nitrous oxide (N₂O), and CFCs are other significant greenhouse gases. CO₂ traps outgoing infrared radiation.

11. The hardest natural substance is:

  1. Diamond (pure carbon in cubic structure)
  2. Corundum (Sapphire/Ruby)
  3. Topaz
  4. Quartz

Answer: Diamond (pure carbon in cubic structure)

Explanation: Diamond (10 on Mohs scale) is the hardest natural substance — carbon atoms arranged in a strong 3D covalent lattice (tetrahedral arrangement). Graphite is also pure carbon but arranged in layers (soft, used as lubricant).

12. Rusting of iron is an example of:

  1. Oxidation (iron reacts with oxygen and water to form iron oxide)
  2. Reduction
  3. Neutralization
  4. Fermentation

Answer: Oxidation (iron reacts with oxygen and water to form iron oxide)

Explanation: Rusting is oxidation: 4Fe + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃ → 2Fe₂O₃·3H₂O (hydrated iron oxide). Prevention: painting, galvanizing (zinc coating), oiling, stainless steel (iron + chromium alloy).

13. The SI unit of temperature is:

  1. Kelvin (K)
  2. Celsius (°C)
  3. Fahrenheit (°F)
  4. Rankine (R)

Answer: Kelvin (K)

Explanation: The SI unit of temperature is Kelvin (K). 0 K = absolute zero (−273.15°C), the lowest possible temperature. Conversion: K = °C + 273. Water freezes at 273 K (0°C) and boils at 373 K (100°C).

14. Heat transfer in fluids (liquids and gases) by bulk movement is called:

  1. Convection
  2. Conduction
  3. Radiation
  4. Diffusion

Answer: Convection

Explanation: Convection is heat transfer in fluids (liquids/gases) through the bulk movement of the fluid itself. Hot fluid (less dense) rises, cool fluid (denser) sinks, creating convection currents. Ocean currents and atmospheric circulation are examples.

15. Heat transfer through electromagnetic waves (without a medium) is:

  1. Radiation
  2. Conduction
  3. Convection
  4. Induction

Answer: Radiation

Explanation: Radiation is heat transfer through electromagnetic waves (infrared radiation). It does not need a medium — heat from the sun reaches Earth through vacuum. All objects above 0 K emit thermal radiation.

16. The First Law of Thermodynamics states:

  1. Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted (Conservation of Energy)
  2. Entropy of a system always increases
  3. Heat flows from cold to hot
  4. Energy is quantized

Answer: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted (Conservation of Energy)

Explanation: First Law of Thermodynamics is the law of conservation of energy: the total energy of an isolated system is constant. Energy can change forms (kinetic to potential, heat to work) but cannot be created or destroyed.

17. The Second Law of Thermodynamics states:

  1. Entropy of an isolated system always increases (natural processes are irreversible)
  2. Energy is conserved
  3. Heat flows from hot to cold
  4. All processes are reversible

Answer: Entropy of an isolated system always increases (natural processes are irreversible)

Explanation: Second Law: Entropy (disorder) of the universe always increases in natural processes. Heat spontaneously flows from hot to cold (not vice versa). No heat engine can be 100% efficient — some energy always becomes unusable.

18. The specific heat capacity of water is:

  1. 4,186 J/kg/K (4.186 kJ/kg/K) — highest among common substances
  2. 1,000 J/kg/K
  3. 400 J/kg/K
  4. 100 J/kg/K

Answer: 4,186 J/kg/K (4.186 kJ/kg/K) — highest among common substances

Explanation: Water has specific heat capacity of 4,186 J/kg/K — one of the highest among common substances. This means water heats up and cools down slowly, moderating Earth's climate and making it ideal as a coolant.

19. Radioactivity was discovered by:

  1. Henri Becquerel (1896)
  2. Marie Curie
  3. Ernest Rutherford
  4. Albert Einstein

Answer: Henri Becquerel (1896)

Explanation: Radioactivity was discovered by Henri Becquerel in 1896 when he found that uranium salts emitted penetrating radiation. Marie Curie and Pierre Curie subsequently discovered polonium and radium (1898).

20. Alpha particles are:

  1. Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2)
  2. Electrons
  3. Electromagnetic radiation
  4. Neutrons

Answer: Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2)

Explanation: Alpha particles (α) are helium-4 nuclei with 2 protons and 2 neutrons (charge +2). They have the greatest ionizing power but least penetrating power — stopped by a sheet of paper or a few cm of air.

21. Beta particles are:

  1. High-energy electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺) emitted from nuclei
  2. Helium nuclei
  3. Electromagnetic radiation
  4. Neutrons

Answer: High-energy electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺) emitted from nuclei

Explanation: Beta particles (β) are high-speed electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺) emitted during radioactive decay. They have moderate ionizing and penetrating power — stopped by a few mm of aluminum.

22. Gamma rays are:

  1. High-energy electromagnetic radiation — most penetrating type of nuclear radiation
  2. Alpha particles
  3. Beta particles
  4. X-rays from electrons

Answer: High-energy electromagnetic radiation — most penetrating type of nuclear radiation

Explanation: Gamma rays (γ) are high-energy electromagnetic radiation emitted from the nucleus during radioactive decay. They have the least ionizing power but greatest penetrating power — need several cm of lead or concrete to stop.

23. A transformer is used to:

  1. Change (increase or decrease) alternating current (AC) voltage
  2. Convert AC to DC
  3. Convert DC to AC
  4. Store electrical energy

Answer: Change (increase or decrease) alternating current (AC) voltage

Explanation: A transformer changes AC voltage using electromagnetic induction. Step-up transformers increase voltage (reduce current); step-down transformers decrease voltage (increase current). V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ (turns ratio).

24. The simplest hydrocarbon (alkane) is:

  1. Methane (CH₄)
  2. Ethane (C₂H₆)
  3. Propane (C₃H₈)
  4. Butane (C₄H₁₀)

Answer: Methane (CH₄)

Explanation: Methane (CH₄) is the simplest alkane (saturated hydrocarbon). It is the main component of natural gas. Propane and butane are used in LPG. Methane is also a potent greenhouse gas (cattle produce it).

25. LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas) mainly consists of:

  1. Propane and Butane
  2. Methane
  3. Ethane
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Propane and Butane

Explanation: LPG mainly consists of propane (C₃H₈) and butane (C₄H₁₀). LPG is stored in cylinders under pressure as liquid. It is used for cooking and heating. CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) is mainly methane.
