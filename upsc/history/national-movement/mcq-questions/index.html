National Movement MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked National Movement questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. Charaka Samhita deals with:

  1. Medicine (Ayurveda)
  2. Astronomy
  3. Grammar
  4. Music

Answer: Medicine (Ayurveda)

Explanation: Charaka Samhita by Charaka is one of the foundational texts of Ayurveda (Indian medicine) covering internal medicine, surgery, pharmacology, and medical ethics.

27. The famous astronomer who stated Earth rotates on its axis was:

  1. Aryabhata
  2. Brahmagupta
  3. Varahamihira
  4. Bhaskara

Answer: Aryabhata

Explanation: Aryabhata (476-550 CE) stated that the Earth rotates on its own axis, causing apparent movement of stars — a revolutionary concept 1000 years before Copernicus.

28. The 'Periplus of the Erythraean Sea' was written by:

  1. An anonymous Greek merchant
  2. Megasthenes
  3. Fa-Hien
  4. Ptolemy

Answer: An anonymous Greek merchant

Explanation: 'Periplus of the Erythraean Sea' (1st century CE) written by an anonymous Greek merchant describes ancient trade routes and ports along the Indian Ocean, including Indian ports.

29. The Satavahana dynasty ruled mainly over:

  1. Deccan
  2. North India
  3. South India
  4. Bengal

Answer: Deccan

Explanation: Satavahanas (1st century BCE – 3rd century CE) were the first major dynasty of the Deccan, ruling from Pratishthana (Paithan). They were great patrons of Buddhism and trade.

30. The Rashtrakutas were the successors of:

  1. Chalukyas of Badami
  2. Pallavas
  3. Gangas
  4. Pandyas

Answer: Chalukyas of Badami

Explanation: Dantidurga overthrew the Chalukya ruler Kirtivarman II and established the Rashtrakuta dynasty in 753 CE, making them the major power in the Deccan.

31. Guru Nanak Dev founded:

  1. Sikhism
  2. Brahmo Samaj
  3. Arya Samaj
  4. Prarthana Samaj

Answer: Sikhism

Explanation: Guru Nanak Dev (1469-1539 CE) founded Sikhism, emphasizing one God, equality of all humans, truthful living, service to humanity, and opposition to caste discrimination.

32. The Chishti Silsila of Sufi order in India was founded by:

  1. Moinuddin Chishti
  2. Nizamuddin Auliya
  3. Khwaja Bakhtiyar Kaki
  4. Qutb-ud-din Bakhtiyar Kaki

Answer: Moinuddin Chishti

Explanation: Moinuddin Chishti (1142-1236 CE) brought the Chishti order to India, establishing his khanqah at Ajmer. He and his followers emphasized love of God and service.

33. The Wood's Education Despatch (1854) is considered:

  1. Magna Carta of Indian Education
  2. Beginning of railways
  3. First census
  4. First election

Answer: Magna Carta of Indian Education

Explanation: Wood's Education Despatch (1854) by Charles Wood is called the 'Magna Carta of Indian Education' — it introduced English as medium of instruction, established universities, and created a department of education.

34. The first railway line in India ran between:

  1. Bombay and Thane
  2. Calcutta and Delhi
  3. Madras and Arcot
  4. Bombay and Calcutta

Answer: Bombay and Thane

Explanation: India's first railway line ran between Bombay (Bori Bunder) and Thane on 16 April 1853, a distance of 34 km. The train pulled by three engines carried 400 passengers.

35. The Simon Commission (1927) was boycotted because:

  1. It had no Indian members
  2. It recommended partition
  3. It was pro-Pakistan
  4. It denied Indian independence

Answer: It had no Indian members

Explanation: The Simon Commission (1927) was boycotted by all Indian political parties as it had no Indian members — all 7 members were British MPs. 'Simon Go Back' became the protest slogan.

36. Gopal Krishna Gokhale founded the:

  1. Servants of India Society
  2. Indian National Congress
  3. Hindu Mahasabha
  4. Brahmo Samaj

Answer: Servants of India Society

Explanation: Gopal Krishna Gokhale founded the Servants of India Society (1905) to train people for selfless public service. He was Gandhi's political mentor.

37. The Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) introduced:

  1. Separate electorates for Muslims
  2. Dyarchy
  3. Provincial autonomy
  4. Universal adult franchise

Answer: Separate electorates for Muslims

Explanation: Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909) introduced separate electorates for Muslims — Muslims would vote only for Muslim candidates — a communal step that sowed seeds of partition.

38. The Lucknow Pact (1916) was signed between:

  1. INC and Muslim League
  2. INC and British
  3. Muslim League and British
  4. INC and Hindu Mahasabha

Answer: INC and Muslim League

Explanation: Lucknow Pact (1916) was signed between INC and Muslim League (proposed by M.A. Jinnah), agreeing on a common scheme for constitutional reforms and Hindu-Muslim unity.

39. Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) proposed:

  1. Union of India with provinces and princely states
  2. Direct independence
  3. Partition
  4. Federal structure only for provinces

Answer: Union of India with provinces and princely states

Explanation: Cabinet Mission Plan (1946): proposed a three-tier federal structure — Union, provincial groups, and provinces — to keep India united. Congress accepted it but Muslim League initially rejected.

40. The Indian Independence Act was passed on:

  1. 18 July 1947
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 26 January 1947
  4. 14 August 1947

Answer: 18 July 1947

Explanation: The Indian Independence Act was passed by British Parliament on 18 July 1947, partitioning British India into two dominions — India and Pakistan — effective 15 August 1947.

41. Lord Mountbatten announced the date of partition as:

  1. 3 June 1947 plan
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 14 August 1947
  4. 18 July 1947

Answer: 3 June 1947 plan

Explanation: The Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan) of 3 June 1947 announced the partition of British India into India and Pakistan. 15 August was set as the transfer of power date.

42. Who gave the slogan 'Jai Hind'?

  1. Subhas Chandra Bose
  2. Bhagat Singh
  3. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  4. Gandhi

Answer: Subhas Chandra Bose

Explanation: 'Jai Hind' (Victory to India) was given as a rallying cry by Subhas Chandra Bose, head of the Indian National Army (INA). It became India's national salutation.

43. The Ghadar Party was founded by:

  1. Sohan Singh Bhakna
  2. Lala Hardayal
  3. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  4. V.D. Savarkar

Answer: Sohan Singh Bhakna

Explanation: Ghadar Party was founded in 1913 at San Francisco by Sohan Singh Bhakna (president) and Lala Hardayal (Secretary) among Indian immigrants to organize a mutiny against British rule.

44. The first session of Indian National Congress was presided over by:

  1. W.C. Bonnerjee
  2. Dadabhai Naoroji
  3. A.O. Hume
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: W.C. Bonnerjee

Explanation: Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee) presided over the first session of the Indian National Congress in Bombay (December 1885).

45. Chandrashekhar Azad died at:

  1. Alfred Park, Allahabad
  2. Delhi
  3. Lahore
  4. Lucknow

Answer: Alfred Park, Allahabad

Explanation: Chandrashekhar Azad died at Alfred Park (now Chandrashekhar Azad Park), Allahabad on 27 February 1931, when surrounded by police — he shot himself to avoid capture, living up to his vow never to be taken alive.

46. Lala Lajpat Rai died after police lathi charge during protests against:

  1. Simon Commission
  2. Rowlatt Act
  3. Jallianwala Bagh massacre
  4. Non-Cooperation Movement

Answer: Simon Commission

Explanation: Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab Kesari) died on 17 November 1928 due to injuries sustained during police lathi charge on protesters against the Simon Commission in Lahore.

47. Aurobindo Ghosh was associated with:

  1. Extremist nationalism and Pondicherry ashram
  2. Moderate politics
  3. Brahmo Samaj
  4. INA

Answer: Extremist nationalism and Pondicherry ashram

Explanation: Sri Aurobindo Ghosh was an extremist nationalist, editor of 'Yugantar', involved in Alipore Bomb Case (1908). After release, moved to Pondicherry where he founded his spiritual ashram.

48. 'Swaraj Party' was formed by:

  1. C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru
  2. Gandhi and Tilak
  3. Congress and Muslim League
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru

Explanation: Swaraj Party was formed in 1923 by C.R. Das (Deshbandhu) and Motilal Nehru, after disagreements within Congress on entering legislatures to 'wreck' the government from within.

49. The Government of India Act 1935 introduced:

  1. Provincial autonomy and dyarchy at center
  2. Partition of India
  3. Indian independence
  4. Adult franchise

Answer: Provincial autonomy and dyarchy at center

Explanation: Government of India Act 1935 introduced provincial autonomy (provinces got self-government), dyarchy at the Center, and established the framework for a federal structure — largely formed the basis of India's Constitution.

50. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of:

  1. Drafting Committee of Constitution
  2. Constituent Assembly
  3. Planning Commission
  4. Election Commission

Answer: Drafting Committee of Constitution

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly (1947-1949), drafting India's Constitution. He is called 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.
