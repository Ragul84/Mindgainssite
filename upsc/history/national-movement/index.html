National Movement MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked National Movement questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The 'Quit India Movement' was launched on:

  1. 8 August 1940
  2. 8 August 1942
  3. 15 August 1942
  4. 26 January 1942

Answer: 8 August 1942

Explanation: The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi on 8 August 1942 at the Bombay session of the All India Congress Committee with the slogan 'Do or Die'.

2. Who was the last Viceroy of British India?

  1. Lord Wavell
  2. Lord Curzon
  3. Lord Mountbatten
  4. Lord Irwin

Answer: Lord Mountbatten

Explanation: Lord Louis Mountbatten was the last Viceroy of British India (1947). He oversaw the partition of India and transfer of power on 15 August 1947.

3. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was annulled in:

  1. 1907
  2. 1910
  3. 1911
  4. 1919

Answer: 1911

Explanation: The Partition of Bengal (1905) by Lord Curzon was annulled in 1911 under Lord Hardinge following massive protests. The capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

4. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on:

  1. 13 April 1918
  2. 13 April 1919
  3. 13 April 1920
  4. 15 August 1919

Answer: 13 April 1919

Explanation: On 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi Day), British Brigadier General Reginald Dyer ordered troops to fire on a peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. Hundreds were killed.

5. The 'Lucknow Pact' (1916) was an agreement between:

  1. Congress and Muslim League
  2. Congress and British Government
  3. Muslim League and British Government
  4. Congress and Maratha leaders

Answer: Congress and Muslim League

Explanation: The Lucknow Pact (1916) was an agreement between the Indian National Congress (led by Tilak) and the All India Muslim League (led by Jinnah) for joint action against British rule and for separate electorates.

6. The 'Cabinet Mission Plan' (1946) proposed:

  1. A Constituent Assembly with separate groups for Hindu-majority and Muslim-majority provinces
  2. Immediate independence and partition
  3. A federal structure with provinces and a centre
  4. Formation of Pakistan

Answer: A Constituent Assembly with separate groups for Hindu-majority and Muslim-majority provinces

Explanation: The Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) proposed a three-tier federal structure: a Union of India at the top, groups of provinces in the middle, and individual provinces at the bottom. It aimed to keep India united while giving autonomy to Muslim-majority areas.

7. The 'Hunter Commission' (1882) recommended:

  1. Better treatment of political prisoners
  2. Reforms in Indian education system
  3. Annulment of Partition of Bengal
  4. Establishment of Indian Civil Services

Answer: Reforms in Indian education system

Explanation: The Hunter Commission (1882) under William Hunter reviewed the state of education in British India. It recommended the strengthening of primary and secondary education.

8. The 'Indian Independence Act 1947' was passed by the British Parliament on:

  1. 3 June 1947
  2. 4 July 1947
  3. 18 July 1947
  4. 15 August 1947

Answer: 18 July 1947

Explanation: The Indian Independence Act 1947 received Royal Assent on 18 July 1947. It provided for the partition of British India into two independent dominions — India and Pakistan.

9. The 'Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms' of 1919 introduced which concept in Indian administration?

  1. Provincial Autonomy
  2. Dyarchy (dual government) in provinces
  3. Complete responsible government
  4. Federal system

Answer: Dyarchy (dual government) in provinces

Explanation: The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act 1919) introduced 'Dyarchy' in provinces — dividing subjects into 'transferred' (under Indian ministers) and 'reserved' (under the Governor) categories.

10. The concept of 'Ahimsa' (non-violence) as a political tool was first applied by Gandhi in:

  1. South Africa (Satyagraha, 1906–1914)
  2. India, Champaran Satyagraha (1917)
  3. India, Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)
  4. India, Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)

Answer: South Africa (Satyagraha, 1906–1914)

Explanation: Gandhi first applied non-violent resistance (Satyagraha) in South Africa (1906–1914) while fighting for the rights of Indian immigrants. This experience shaped his philosophy for India's independence struggle.

11. The 'Champaran Satyagraha' (1917) was related to:

  1. Salt tax
  2. Indigo cultivation under Tinkathia system
  3. Land revenue policy
  4. Partition of Bengal

Answer: Indigo cultivation under Tinkathia system

Explanation: The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) was Gandhi's first Satyagraha in India. It was against the Tinkathia system — forcing peasants to cultivate indigo on 3/20th of their land and sell it to British planters at fixed prices.

12. The Purusha Sukta, which describes the creation of the four varnas, is in:

  1. Atharvaveda
  2. Rigveda (10th mandala)
  3. Upanishads
  4. Manusmriti

Answer: Rigveda (10th mandala)

Explanation: The Purusha Sukta (Rigveda, 10th Mandala, Hymn 90) describes the cosmic being Purusha's sacrifice: Brahmins from his mouth, Kshatriyas from arms, Vaishyas from thighs, Shudras from feet.

13. The 'Four Noble Truths' (Chatur Arya Satya) of Buddhism state:

  1. The world is eternal and perfect
  2. Life involves suffering (dukkha); suffering has causes (desire); suffering can end; there is a path to end it (Noble Eightfold Path)
  3. Karma alone determines rebirth
  4. Meditation is the only path to liberation

Answer: Life involves suffering (dukkha); suffering has causes (desire); suffering can end; there is a path to end it (Noble Eightfold Path)

Explanation: The Four Noble Truths: 1) Dukkha (life is suffering), 2) Samudaya (suffering arises from desire/craving — tanha), 3) Nirodha (suffering can be ended), 4) Magga (the Noble Eightfold Path leads to liberation).

14. Hinayana and Mahayana are two branches of Buddhism. Mahayana believes:

  1. Only monks can attain Nirvana
  2. Both monks and laypeople can achieve salvation; the Bodhisattva ideal — staying in the world to help others attain liberation
  3. Strict individual meditation alone leads to liberation
  4. The Buddha was only a human teacher

Answer: Both monks and laypeople can achieve salvation; the Bodhisattva ideal — staying in the world to help others attain liberation

Explanation: Mahayana ('Greater Vehicle'): emphasises Bodhisattva ideal — beings who delay their own Nirvana to save others. Spread to China, Japan, Korea, Tibet. Hinayana ('Lesser Vehicle', now called Theravada) spread to Sri Lanka, Burma, Thailand.

15. Gayatri Mantra is found in:

  1. Rigveda
  2. Samaveda
  3. Yajurveda
  4. Atharvaveda

Answer: Rigveda

Explanation: Gayatri Mantra (dedicated to solar deity Savitri) is found in the Rigveda (3.62.10). It is the most sacred mantra in Hinduism.

16. The word 'Arya' in Sanskrit means:

  1. Noble/Respectable
  2. Warrior
  3. Learned
  4. Foreigner

Answer: Noble/Respectable

Explanation: 'Arya' in Sanskrit means noble, honorable, or respectable. It was a cultural/social term, not a racial one.

17. Mahavira attained 'Kaivalya' (enlightenment) at:

  1. Jrimbhikagrama
  2. Vaishali
  3. Pawa
  4. Rajgir

Answer: Jrimbhikagrama

Explanation: Mahavira attained Kaivalya (perfect knowledge) at Jrimbhikagrama on the banks of Rijupalika river after 12.5 years of intense meditation.

18. Theravada Buddhism is also called:

  1. Hinayana
  2. Mahayana
  3. Vajrayana
  4. Tantrayana

Answer: Hinayana

Explanation: Theravada (Way of the Elders) Buddhism is also called Hinayana (Lesser Vehicle) by Mahayana Buddhists. It emphasizes personal salvation and the original Pali Canon.

19. The Pali Canon (Tripitaka) consists of:

  1. Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma Pitakas
  2. Three Buddhist councils
  3. Three stages of meditation
  4. Three categories of monks

Answer: Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma Pitakas

Explanation: Pali Canon (Tripitaka = Three Baskets): Vinaya Pitaka (monastic rules), Sutta Pitaka (discourses), Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical analysis).

20. The doctrine of 'Bodhisattva' is central to:

  1. Mahayana Buddhism
  2. Theravada Buddhism
  3. Jainism
  4. Advaita Vedanta

Answer: Mahayana Buddhism

Explanation: Bodhisattva (enlightenment being) concept is central to Mahayana Buddhism — beings who delay their own nirvana to help all sentient beings attain liberation.

21. The Arthashastra deals with:

  1. State administration and economics
  2. Astronomy
  3. Medicine
  4. Grammar

Answer: State administration and economics

Explanation: Arthashastra (Science of Polity) by Kautilya covers statecraft, economic policy, military strategy, welfare state, espionage, law, and diplomacy.

22. The Kanishka was the greatest ruler of the:

  1. Kushan Empire
  2. Gupta Empire
  3. Sunga dynasty
  4. Satavahana dynasty

Answer: Kushan Empire

Explanation: Kanishka I was the greatest ruler of the Kushan Empire (1st-2nd century CE), patronized Buddhism, convened the 4th Buddhist Council at Kundalvana (Kashmir).

23. Aryabhata wrote the famous work:

  1. Aryabhatiya
  2. Arthashastra
  3. Ashtadhyayi
  4. Amarakosa

Answer: Aryabhatiya

Explanation: Aryabhata (476-550 CE) wrote Aryabhatiya — covering arithmetic, algebra, plane geometry, spherical trigonometry. He correctly calculated value of π and explained solar/lunar eclipses.

24. Who authored 'Ashtadhyayi'?

  1. Panini
  2. Patanjali
  3. Bhartrhari
  4. Kalidasa

Answer: Panini

Explanation: Panini's Ashtadhyayi (4000 sutras) is the foundational text of Sanskrit grammar, one of the greatest intellectual achievements of ancient civilization.

25. The founder of the Pala dynasty of Bengal was:

  1. Gopala
  2. Dharmapala
  3. Devapala
  4. Mahipala

Answer: Gopala

Explanation: Gopala (750-770 CE) founded the Pala dynasty in Bengal. The Palas were great patrons of Buddhism and built the famous Vikramashila University.
