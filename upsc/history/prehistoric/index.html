Prehistoric MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Prehistoric questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The ancient city of Mohenjo-daro is located in present-day:

  1. India
  2. Pakistan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. Nepal

Answer: Pakistan

Explanation: Mohenjo-daro is located in Sindh province of present-day Pakistan. It was one of the major centres of the Indus Valley Civilization.

2. The Indus Valley Civilization is also known as:

  1. Vedic Civilization
  2. Harappan Civilization
  3. Aryan Civilization
  4. Dravidian Civilization

Answer: Harappan Civilization

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilization is also called Harappan Civilization, named after Harappa — one of its major sites (in present-day Pakistan), excavated in 1921.

3. The 'Wavell Plan' (1945) was presented at which conference?

  1. Round Table Conference
  2. Shimla Conference
  3. Lahore Conference
  4. London Conference

Answer: Shimla Conference

Explanation: The Wavell Plan (1945) was presented at the Shimla Conference. It proposed that the Executive Council of the Viceroy would have equal representation of Hindus and Muslims.

4. The standard unit of weight used by the Harappans was:

  1. Based on the decimal system
  2. Based on the binary system (doubling: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64) and then decimal
  3. Identical to Mesopotamian weights
  4. There was no standard weight

Answer: Based on the binary system (doubling: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64) and then decimal

Explanation: Harappan weights followed a binary system up to 64, then decimal: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64,160,200,320,640. Cuboid chert weights were found across sites, suggesting standardised trade.

5. The decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation (c. 1900 BCE) was most likely caused by:

  1. Aryan invasions alone
  2. A combination of factors: climate change, flooding, river course changes, and possible epidemic
  3. Nuclear radiation (fringe theory)
  4. Complete drying up of all rivers

Answer: A combination of factors: climate change, flooding, river course changes, and possible epidemic

Explanation: Most modern scholars believe the IVC decline was multicausal: climate change causing drought, shifts in monsoon patterns, flooding, river course changes (Ghaggar-Hakra drying up), and possible trade collapse. The 'Aryan invasion' theory alone is now rejected.

6. The Indus Valley people traded with:

  1. China and Japan
  2. Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) — seals found in Ur and other Mesopotamian sites
  3. Egypt and Greece
  4. No external trade evidence

Answer: Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) — seals found in Ur and other Mesopotamian sites

Explanation: IVC traded with Mesopotamia (Sumer, Akkad). Harappan seals, weights, and carnelian beads found at Ur. Mesopotamian texts mention 'Meluhha' — believed to refer to the Indus region.

7. The Rigveda mentions which river most frequently?

  1. Ganga
  2. Indus (Sindhu)
  3. Saraswati
  4. Yamuna

Answer: Saraswati

Explanation: The Saraswati River is the most praised river in the Rigveda, described as the greatest of rivers. The Sindhu (Indus) is also frequently mentioned. The Ganga appears only twice in the Rigveda.

8. The Harappan city known for its large dockyard is:

  1. Lothal
  2. Mohenjo-daro
  3. Harappa
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Lothal

Explanation: Lothal in Gujarat had a well-planned dockyard (170m×37m), indicating maritime trade with Mesopotamia. It is the only Harappan site with a dockyard.

9. Great Bath was found at which Harappan site?

  1. Mohenjo-daro
  2. Harappa
  3. Lothal
  4. Dholavira

Answer: Mohenjo-daro

Explanation: The Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro (11.88m×7.01m) is the most striking feature of the Indus Valley Civilization, used for ritualistic bathing.

10. The Indus script is:

  1. Undeciphered
  2. Brahmi-based
  3. Sanskrit-derived
  4. Dravidian

Answer: Undeciphered

Explanation: The Indus script (Harappan script) remains undeciphered despite many attempts. It contains over 400 signs and was written right-to-left.

11. Which Harappan site is located in Gujarat and is known for its water conservation systems?

  1. Dholavira
  2. Lothal
  3. Surkotada
  4. Rangpur

Answer: Dholavira

Explanation: Dholavira in Gujarat is known for its elaborate water conservation system with large reservoirs, stepwells, and dams — unique among all Harappan sites.

12. Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) culture preceded which period in India?

  1. Vedic period
  2. Gupta period
  3. Kushan period
  4. Mauryan period

Answer: Vedic period

Explanation: OCP culture (2000-1500 BCE) in the Ganga plains preceded the Vedic period and marks the transition from the Harappan civilization to early Vedic settlements.

13. Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) was co-founded by:

  1. Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad
  2. Gandhi and Nehru
  3. Subhas Bose and Tilak
  4. VOC and Subramania Bharati

Answer: Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad

Explanation: HSRA was reorganized by Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev, and others in 1928. They believed in socialist revolution through direct action against British imperialism.

14. The first cotton textile mill in India was established at:

  1. Bombay (1854)
  2. Calcutta (1830)
  3. Madras (1870)
  4. Ahmedabad (1860)

Answer: Bombay (1854)

Explanation: India's first modern cotton textile mill was established in Bombay in 1854 by Cowasjee Nanabhoy Davar. This marked the beginning of modern industrialization in India.

15. The word 'India' is derived from the river:

  1. Indus (Sindhu)
  2. Ganga
  3. Brahmaputra
  4. Kaveri

Answer: Indus (Sindhu)

Explanation: The name 'India' derives from the river Indus (Sanskrit: Sindhu). Ancient Persians and Greeks called the land east of the Indus as 'Hind' or 'India'.

16. The term 'Second Urbanization' in Indian history refers to:

  1. Rise of cities along Ganga around 600 BCE
  2. Harappan Civilization
  3. Mughal city building
  4. British colonial cities

Answer: Rise of cities along Ganga around 600 BCE

Explanation: Second Urbanization (c. 600-300 BCE) refers to the rise of large cities (Pataliputra, Vaishali, Kashi, Ujjain) in the Gangetic plains, distinct from the first urbanization of the Indus Valley.

17. Firuz Shah Tughlaq is known for:

  1. Military conquests
  2. Welfare works — canals (including the 200km Rajasthan canal), hospitals (shifakhana), madrasas, rest houses, orchards; banned torture
  3. Introducing market reforms
  4. Inviting Timur to India

Answer: Welfare works — canals (including the 200km Rajasthan canal), hospitals (shifakhana), madrasas, rest houses, orchards; banned torture

Explanation: Firuz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388) built extensive infrastructure: 150 wells, 100 caravanserais, 30 reservoirs/canals, 100 hospitals, 150 bridges. Banned many cruel punishments. But also discriminated against Hindus.

18. The Indus Valley script has approximately how many signs?

  1. 400+
  2. 26
  3. 50-100
  4. 1000+

Answer: 400+

Explanation: The Indus Valley script has over 400 distinct signs, suggesting it is a logosyllabic system (like Egyptian hieroglyphics). It remains one of the world's great undeciphered writing systems.

19. Aurangzeb's policy towards Hindus differed from Akbar's because:

  1. He was more generous with Hindus
  2. He reimposed jizya (1679), destroyed some Hindu temples, banned music, and applied strict Sharia — more orthodox Islamic policies
  3. He gave more Hindus positions in administration
  4. He merged Hindu and Muslim festivals

Answer: He reimposed jizya (1679), destroyed some Hindu temples, banned music, and applied strict Sharia — more orthodox Islamic policies

Explanation: Aurangzeb (1658–1707) reversed Akbar's tolerant policies: reimposed jizya (1679), destroyed some temples (Kashi Vishwanath, Mathura's Keshav Deo), banned music and dance, executed Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675). This led to Rajput and Sikh revolts.

20. The Swadeshi Movement (1905) involved:

  1. Promoting foreign goods
  2. Boycott of British goods and promotion of Indian-made goods — also included national education and Swadesh sanghas
  3. Only political speeches
  4. Violent uprisings only

Answer: Boycott of British goods and promotion of Indian-made goods — also included national education and Swadesh sanghas

Explanation: The Swadeshi Movement arose after the Partition of Bengal (1905). It involved: burning foreign cloth, boycotting British goods, promoting Indian industries (Swadeshi), national schools, and Swadesh Sanghas. Leaders: Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai (Lal-Bal-Pal), Aurobindo.

21. The Industrial Revolution first began in:

  1. England
  2. France
  3. Germany
  4. USA

Answer: England

Explanation: The Industrial Revolution began in Britain (England) in the 18th century (1760s-1840s) with mechanization of textile industry, steam engine, iron production, and railways.

22. What was the primary source of subsistence for the people of the Mesolithic period in India?

  1. Hunting
  2. Gathering
  3. Pastoralism
  4. Agriculture

Answer: Hunting

Explanation: The Mesolithic period in India was characterized by a primary source of subsistence as hunting. This period saw the use of microliths.

23. Which of the following prehistoric sites is known for its ash mounds?

  1. Paiyampalli
  2. Brahmagiri
  3. Maski
  4. Kodekal

Answer: Paiyampalli

Explanation: Paiyampalli is a prehistoric site known for its ash mounds, which were a result of burning cattle dung.

24. What was the name of the tool used by the Paleolithic man for hunting and other purposes?

  1. Handaxe
  2. Scraper
  3. Hammerstone
  4. Chopper

Answer: Handaxe

Explanation: The handaxe was a tool used by the Paleolithic man for hunting and other purposes, characterized by its distinctive shape.

25. In which state is the prehistoric site of Bhimbetka located?

  1. Madhya Pradesh
  2. Gujarat
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Maharashtra

Answer: Madhya Pradesh

Explanation: Bhimbetka is a prehistoric site located in Madhya Pradesh, known for its rock shelters and paintings.
