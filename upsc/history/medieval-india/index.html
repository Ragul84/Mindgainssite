Medieval India MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Medieval India questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between:

  1. Akbar and Hemu
  2. Babur and Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Humayun and Sher Shah Suri
  4. Baber and Rana Sanga

Answer: Babur and Ibrahim Lodi

Explanation: The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur (founder of Mughal Empire) and Ibrahim Lodi (last ruler of Delhi Sultanate). Babur's victory established Mughal rule in India.

2. Tipu Sultan was the ruler of which kingdom?

  1. Hyderabad
  2. Mysore
  3. Maratha Empire
  4. Vijayanagara Empire

Answer: Mysore

Explanation: Tipu Sultan (1750–1799) was the Sultan of Mysore. He was called the 'Tiger of Mysore' for his fierce resistance against British expansion. He died in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War.

3. Which battle in 1565 CE decisively ended the Vijayanagara Empire?

  1. Battle of Talikota
  2. Battle of Panipat (Third)
  3. Battle of Tarain
  4. Battle of Haldighat

Answer: Battle of Talikota

Explanation: The Battle of Talikota (1565) was fought between the Vijayanagara Empire and the Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Bidar, Ahmadnagar, Golconda). The decisive defeat led to the sack of the Vijayanagara capital.

4. The 'Third Battle of Panipat' (1761) was fought between:

  1. Mughals and Marathas
  2. Ahmed Shah Durrani (Abdali) and Marathas
  3. British and Marathas
  4. Mughals and Rohillas

Answer: Ahmed Shah Durrani (Abdali) and Marathas

Explanation: The Third Battle of Panipat (14 Jan 1761) between Ahmad Shah Durrani (Afghan king) and the Maratha Confederacy resulted in Maratha defeat, ending their ambition of becoming the dominant power in India.

5. Bal Gangadhar Tilak gave the slogan:

  1. Swarajya is my birthright
  2. Jai Hind
  3. Vande Mataram
  4. Dilli Chalo

Answer: Swarajya is my birthright

Explanation: 'Swarajya is my birthright and I shall have it' was the famous slogan of Bal Gangadhar Tilak, the fiery nationalist who also started the Ganapati and Shivaji festivals for national awakening.

6. Hampi was the capital of:

  1. Vijayanagara Empire
  2. Chalukyas
  3. Rashtrakutas
  4. Kakatiya Kingdom

Answer: Vijayanagara Empire

Explanation: Hampi (Karnataka) was the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire. It is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site with magnificent ruins including Virupaksha Temple, Vittala Temple, and palace structures.

7. Lord Hastings was Governor-General during the:

  1. Anglo-Maratha wars
  2. Sepoy Mutiny
  3. Partition of Bengal
  4. Anglo-Mysore wars

Answer: Anglo-Maratha wars

Explanation: Lord Hastings (Warren Hastings, 1772-1785) was the first Governor-General. Lord Francis Rawdon-Hastings (1813-1823) fought the Third Anglo-Maratha War. Warren Hastings fought the First Anglo-Maratha War.

8. Sher Shah Suri introduced the currency called:

  1. Rupee
  2. Tanka
  3. Dam
  4. Mohur

Answer: Rupee

Explanation: Sher Shah Suri (1540-1545) introduced the silver rupee (rupiya) weighing 178 grains. He also introduced copper paisa and gold mohur — currency reforms that continued under Mughals.

9. Who was the author of 'Baburnama'?

  1. Babur
  2. Akbar
  3. Abul Fazl
  4. Gulbadan Begum

Answer: Babur

Explanation: Baburnama (Tuzuk-i-Baburi) was written by Babur himself in Chagatai Turkish — it is the first autobiography in Islamic literary tradition, describing his life, campaigns, and observations of India.

10. Which city was called 'City of Victory' after the Battle of Panipat 1526?

  1. None — it was already Agra
  2. Panipat
  3. Lahore
  4. Delhi (called Shahjahanabad later)

Answer: None — it was already Agra

Explanation: After the First Battle of Panipat, Babur captured Agra and made it his capital. Delhi was also used. The battle didn't create a new city; Babur ruled from existing cities.

11. The Tabaqat-i-Nasiri was written by:

  1. Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani
  2. Alberuni
  3. Ibn Battuta
  4. Amir Khusrau

Answer: Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani

Explanation: Tabaqat-i-Nasiri by Minhaj-i-Siraj Juzjani (1260 CE) is a valuable source of history of early Delhi Sultans, covering rulers up to Balban's period.

12. The Battle of Khanwa (1527) was between:

  1. Babur and Rana Sanga
  2. Babur and Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Humayun and Sher Shah
  4. Akbar and Hemu

Answer: Babur and Rana Sanga

Explanation: Battle of Khanwa (17 March 1527): Babur defeated Rana Sanga (Rana Sangram Singh) of Mewar, who had formed a coalition of Rajput chiefs to challenge Babur.

13. Akbar defeated Hemu in:

  1. Second Battle of Panipat (1556)
  2. First Battle of Panipat (1526)
  3. Battle of Khanwa (1527)
  4. Battle of Haldighati (1576)

Answer: Second Battle of Panipat (1556)

Explanation: Second Battle of Panipat (5 November 1556): Akbar's general Bairam Khan defeated and killed Hemu (the last Hindu emperor to sit on Delhi's throne briefly), securing Mughal rule.

14. The treaty that ended the Third Anglo-Mysore War was:

  1. Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
  2. Treaty of Mangalore (1784)
  3. Treaty of Srirangapatna (1799)
  4. Treaty of Mysore (1799)

Answer: Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)

Explanation: Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) ended the Third Anglo-Mysore War — Tipu Sultan surrendered half his territory and paid large indemnity. The Fourth War (1799) ended with Tipu's death.

15. Tipu Sultan died defending:

  1. Srirangapatna Fort
  2. Mysore Palace
  3. Bangalore Fort
  4. Chitradurga

Answer: Srirangapatna Fort

Explanation: Tipu Sultan (Sultan of Mysore) died on 4 May 1799 defending his capital Srirangapatna against British forces. His death ended the kingdom of Mysore.

16. The Mughal emperor who was also called 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint):

  1. Aurangzeb
  2. Akbar
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Aurangzeb

Explanation: Aurangzeb was called 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint) for his piety — he fasted regularly, never drank alcohol, lived simply, and supplemented his income by copying Qurans and making prayer caps.

17. The battle that finally ended Maratha power was:

  1. Third Battle of Panipat (1761)
  2. Battle of Assaye (1803)
  3. Battle of Korgaon (1818)
  4. Battle of Kirkee (1817)

Answer: Third Battle of Panipat (1761)

Explanation: Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817-18) battles of Kirkee, Korgaon, and Sitabaldi ended Maratha power. The Peshwai was abolished, Peshwa Baji Rao II exiled, making British supreme in India.

18. In Indian history, 'Second Battle of Tarain (1192)' established:

  1. Permanent Muslim rule in North India
  2. Maratha dominance
  3. Rajput supremacy
  4. British East India Company dominance

Answer: Permanent Muslim rule in North India

Explanation: Second Battle of Tarain (1192) — Muhammad Ghori's defeat of Prithviraj Chauhan — established permanent Muslim rule in North India, beginning the Delhi Sultanate period.

19. The Bahmani Kingdom was founded by:

  1. Alauddin Bahman Shah
  2. Mahmud Gawan
  3. Firuz Shah Bahmani
  4. Ibrahim Adil Shah

Answer: Alauddin Bahman Shah

Explanation: Alauddin Bahman Shah (Hassan Gangu, 1347-1358) founded the Bahmani Kingdom after revolting against Muhammad bin Tughluq of Delhi. It split into 5 Deccan Sultanates after 1518.

20. Akbar's 'Sulh-i-Kul' policy means:

  1. Universal peace and tolerance
  2. Military alliance
  3. Royal decree
  4. Religious conversion

Answer: Universal peace and tolerance

Explanation: Sulh-i-Kul (Peace with all/Absolute peace) was Akbar's policy of universal tolerance — treating all religions equally, respecting all faiths without discrimination.

21. Iltutmish is considered the true founder of the Delhi Sultanate because:

  1. He was the first Sultan
  2. He consolidated the Sultanate, got recognition from Caliph of Baghdad, completed Qutb Minar, and introduced iqta system
  3. He defeated Prithviraj Chauhan
  4. He invaded South India

Answer: He consolidated the Sultanate, got recognition from Caliph of Baghdad, completed Qutb Minar, and introduced iqta system

Explanation: Iltutmish (1210–1236) got recognition from the Abbasid Caliph (Islamic legitimacy), introduced the iqta system (land revenue assignments for military service), completed the Qutb Minar (started by Aibak), and introduced silver tanka and copper jital coins.

22. The Battle of Talikota (1565) resulted in:

  1. Vijayanagara victory over the Deccan Sultanates
  2. Destruction of the Vijayanagara Empire — the capital Hampi was sacked and ruined
  3. Mughal invasion of South India
  4. Alliance between Vijayanagara and Bijapur

Answer: Destruction of the Vijayanagara Empire — the capital Hampi was sacked and ruined

Explanation: The Battle of Talikota (Rakshasatangadi, 1565) was fought between Vijayanagara (Aliya Rama Raya) and the allied Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Bidar, Golconda). Rama Raya was killed, Hampi was devastated and never recovered.

23. Portuguese traveller Domingo Paes visited Vijayanagara during the reign of:

  1. Deva Raya II
  2. Krishna Deva Raya
  3. Achyuta Raya
  4. Sadashiva Raya

Answer: Krishna Deva Raya

Explanation: Domingo Paes (Portuguese traveller, c. 1520–1522) visited during Krishna Deva Raya's reign and described Vijayanagara as 'the best provided city in the world' with a population of 500,000. Other visitors: Abdur Razzak (1443), Nuniz (1535–36).

24. Sher Shah Suri is famous for:

  1. Building the Taj Mahal
  2. Introducing the Grand Trunk Road, administrative reforms, land revenue system (zabt), and silver rupee
  3. Defeating Akbar
  4. Commissioning Akbarnama

Answer: Introducing the Grand Trunk Road, administrative reforms, land revenue system (zabt), and silver rupee

Explanation: Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545) defeated Humayun, reorganised the empire: Grand Trunk Road (Peshawar to Bengal), silver rupee (precursor to modern rupee), land revenue based on measurement (zabt), relay postal system, and built Sher Shah mosque.

25. The founder of the Slave Dynasty was:

  1. Qutb-ud-din Aibak
  2. Iltutmish
  3. Balban
  4. Razia Sultana

Answer: Qutb-ud-din Aibak

Explanation: Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206-1210 CE) founded the Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty — the first Muslim dynasty in India. He was a slave of Muhammad Ghori who rose to become a sultan.
