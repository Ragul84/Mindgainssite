Medieval India MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Medieval India questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. The first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate was:

  1. Razia Sultana
  2. Chand Bibi
  3. Nur Jahan
  4. Jahanara

Answer: Razia Sultana

Explanation: Razia Sultana (1236-1240 CE), daughter of Iltutmish, was the first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate. She discarded purdah and held court openly.

27. The 'iqta' system in Delhi Sultanate was similar to:

  1. Feudal land grants for military service
  2. Tax farming
  3. Permanent settlement
  4. Zamindari system

Answer: Feudal land grants for military service

Explanation: Iqta was a grant of land revenue (not land itself) to military officers (iqtadars) in lieu of salary, in return for maintaining troops and providing military service.

28. The Vijayanagara Empire was founded by:

  1. Harihara and Bukka
  2. Devaraya I
  3. Krishnadevaraya
  4. Saluva Narasimha

Answer: Harihara and Bukka

Explanation: Harihara I and Bukka I (brothers, disciples of Vidyaranya), formerly officers of the Kakatiyas, founded the Vijayanagara Empire in 1336 CE on the banks of Tungabhadra.

29. The greatest ruler of Vijayanagara Empire was:

  1. Krishnadevaraya
  2. Devaraya II
  3. Harihara II
  4. Saluva Narasimha

Answer: Krishnadevaraya

Explanation: Krishnadevaraya (1509-1529 CE) was the greatest ruler — a brilliant military commander, poet (wrote Amuktamalyada in Telugu), and generous patron of arts. Known as Kannada Rajya Rama Ramana.

30. Abdur Razzaq, the Persian ambassador, visited which Vijayanagara king's court?

  1. Devaraya II
  2. Krishnadevaraya
  3. Harihara I
  4. Bukka I

Answer: Devaraya II

Explanation: Abdur Razzaq (Persian envoy, 1443) visited the court of Devaraya II and gave a detailed description of Vijayanagara city and the empire's grandeur.

31. Battle of Talikota (1565) ended:

  1. Vijayanagara Empire
  2. Mughal dominance
  3. Delhi Sultanate
  4. Bahmani Kingdom

Answer: Vijayanagara Empire

Explanation: Battle of Talikota (Jan 1565): Vijayanagara defeated by the combined forces of Bijapur, Bidar, Ahmadnagar, and Golconda. The city of Vijayanagara was sacked and destroyed.

32. Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat in:

  1. 1526
  2. 1556
  3. 1761
  4. 1527

Answer: 1526

Explanation: First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526): Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi using the Tulughma strategy and artillery, establishing the Mughal Empire in India.

33. The Mughal emperor famous for building Taj Mahal:

  1. Shah Jahan
  2. Akbar
  3. Jahangir
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Shah Jahan

Explanation: Shah Jahan (1628-1658) built the Taj Mahal at Agra (1632-1643) as a mausoleum for his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

34. Aurangzeb reimposed the 'Jizya' tax which had been abolished by:

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Babur
  4. Shah Jahan

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar abolished the discriminatory Jizya (poll tax on non-Muslims) in 1564 CE as part of his policy of religious tolerance. Aurangzeb reimposed it in 1679 CE.

35. Todar Mal was the Finance Minister of:

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Jahangir

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Raja Todar Mal was Akbar's revenue minister who introduced the Zabti (measurement) system of land revenue — measuring land and fixing revenue based on soil type and cultivation.

36. The 'Mansabdari system' was a Mughal system of:

  1. Military ranking and revenue
  2. Religious administration
  3. Land grants
  4. Educational hierarchy

Answer: Military ranking and revenue

Explanation: Mansabdari system (introduced by Akbar) graded military and civil officials (mansabdars) by numerical ranks (mansabs) determining salary and cavalry obligation.

37. Shivaji's administrative divisions were called:

  1. Swarajya
  2. Khalsa
  3. Pargana
  4. Talukas

Answer: Swarajya

Explanation: Shivaji's kingdom was called Swarajya (self-rule). It was divided into prants (provinces), with the capital at Raigad (Raigarh Fort).

38. The Battle of Haldighati (1576) was fought between:

  1. Maharana Pratap and Akbar's army
  2. Prithviraj and Ghori
  3. Shivaji and Aurangzeb
  4. Rana Sanga and Babur

Answer: Maharana Pratap and Akbar's army

Explanation: Battle of Haldighati (18 June 1576) was fought between Maharana Pratap of Mewar and the Mughal forces led by Man Singh I on Akbar's behalf. Pratap was defeated but never surrendered.

39. The Battle of Buxar (1764) was significant because:

  1. British defeated combined armies of three powers
  2. First British victory in India
  3. British acquired Bengal
  4. First use of firearms in India

Answer: British defeated combined armies of three powers

Explanation: Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764): British under Hector Munro defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula (Awadh), and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II — establishing British dominance.

40. The 'Ain-i-Akbari' was written by:

  1. Abul Fazl
  2. Birbal
  3. Todar Mal
  4. Tansen

Answer: Abul Fazl

Explanation: Ain-i-Akbari (Institutes of Akbar) was written by Abul Fazl, Akbar's court historian. It is the third volume of Akbarnama, providing detailed accounts of Akbar's administration, policies, and the Mughal court.

41. Akbar's 'Ain-i-Akbari' and 'Akbarnama' were written by:

  1. Birbal
  2. Abul Fazl
  3. Faizi
  4. Tansen

Answer: Abul Fazl

Explanation: Abul Fazl (1551–1602), a minister and historian, wrote Akbarnama (in 3 volumes, including the Ain-i-Akbari). The Ain-i-Akbari is a detailed account of Akbar's administration, court, culture, and statistics of the empire.

42. The Mughal land revenue system during Akbar's reign was called:

  1. Iqta
  2. Zamindari
  3. Dahsala or Zabti system (introduced by Todar Mal)
  4. Mahalwari

Answer: Dahsala or Zabti system (introduced by Todar Mal)

Explanation: Raja Todar Mal introduced the Dahsala (ten-year average) system: land was measured (zabt), soil quality classified into 4 types, and revenue fixed for 10 years based on average produce. It replaced earlier arbitrary systems.

43. The medieval term 'amil' refers to

  1. Revenue Officer
  2. Military Commander
  3. Administrative Head
  4. Judicial Magistrate

Answer: Revenue Officer

Explanation: Amil was a revenue officer in medieval India, responsible for collecting taxes and revenue.

44. The Bhakti movement in medieval India emphasized

  1. Devotion to a personal deity
  2. Rituals and sacrifices
  3. Asceticism and renunciation
  4. Social and cultural reform

Answer: Devotion to a personal deity

Explanation: The Bhakti movement focused on personal devotion to a deity, promoting a direct relationship between the individual and God.

45. The muqaddam was a medieval Indian term for

  1. Village headman
  2. Revenue collector
  3. Military officer
  4. Administrative official

Answer: Village headman

Explanation: Muqaddam referred to the headman of a village in medieval India, responsible for local administration and revenue collection.

46. The Sufi philosopher who founded the Chishti order in India was

  1. Khawaja Moinuddin Chishti
  2. Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya
  3. Shaikh Salim Chishti
  4. Baba Farid

Answer: Khawaja Moinuddin Chishti

Explanation: Khawaja Moinuddin Chishti introduced the Chishti order of Sufism to India, emphasizing love, tolerance, and devotion.

47. The Vijayanagara kingdom was founded by

  1. Harihara and Bukka
  2. Krishna Deva Raya
  3. Ashoka
  4. Samudragupta

Answer: Harihara and Bukka

Explanation: Harihara and Bukka, two brothers, established the Vijayanagara kingdom in southern India in the 14th century.

48. The architectural feature of the Qutub Minar is

  1. Minaret
  2. Dome
  3. Arch
  4. Pillar

Answer: Minaret

Explanation: The Qutub Minar is a prominent example of a minaret, a tall, slender tower in Islamic architecture.

49. The Treaty of Purandar was signed between

  1. Aurangzeb and Shivaji
  2. Aurangzeb and Sambhaji
  3. Shivaji and Mirza Raja Jai Singh
  4. Shivaji and Afzal Khan

Answer: Aurangzeb and Shivaji

Explanation: The Treaty of Purandar was a peace agreement between the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb and the Maratha king Shivaji in 1665.

50. The medieval Indian saint who preached the doctrine of 'Soham' was

  1. Kabir
  2. Nanak
  3. Vallabhacharya
  4. Chaitanya

Answer: Chaitanya

Explanation: Chaitanya, a prominent Vaishnavite saint, emphasized the doctrine of 'Soham', or the identity of the individual self with the divine.
