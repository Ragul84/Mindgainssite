World History MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked World History questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Cuba Crisis (1962) was between:

  1. USA and USSR
  2. USA and Cuba
  3. USA and NATO
  4. Cuba and USSR

Answer: USA and USSR

Explanation: Cuban Missile Crisis (October 1962): 13-day confrontation between USA and USSR over Soviet missiles in Cuba. The closest the Cold War came to full-scale nuclear war, resolved diplomatically.

2. The First World War was also known as:

  1. The Great War
  2. War of Nations
  3. War of Empires
  4. The European War

Answer: The Great War

Explanation: World War I (1914-1918) was contemporaneously called 'The Great War' or 'The War to End All Wars'. The name 'World War I' became common only after the Second World War began.

3. The French Revolution began in:

  1. 1789
  2. 1799
  3. 1776
  4. 1804

Answer: 1789

Explanation: The French Revolution began in 1789 with the storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789). It overthrew the monarchy and established principles of Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.

4. World War I began in:

  1. 1914
  2. 1939
  3. 1918
  4. 1904

Answer: 1914

Explanation: World War I began in 1914 following the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria (June 28, 1914). It ended in 1918 with the Armistice of November 11.

5. The Berlin Wall fell in:

  1. 1989
  2. 1991
  3. 1985
  4. 1987

Answer: 1989

Explanation: The Berlin Wall (built 1961) fell on 9 November 1989, leading to German reunification in 1990 and symbolizing the end of the Cold War.

6. The concept of 'muqaddam' is associated with which administrative position in medieval India?

  1. Village Headman
  2. Revenue Officer
  3. Military Commander
  4. Provincial Governor

Answer: Village Headman

Explanation: Muqaddam refers to the village headman in medieval India, responsible for local administration and revenue collection. This position was crucial in the rural administrative structure.

7. The 'Bhakti movement' in medieval India emphasized which of the following?

  1. Devotion to a personal deity
  2. Ritualistic worship
  3. Asceticism
  4. Social hierarchy

Answer: Devotion to a personal deity

Explanation: The Bhakti movement focused on personal devotion to a deity, promoting a direct relationship between the individual and the divine. It rejected ritualism and social hierarchies.

8. The 'Regulating Act of 1773' was significant in the history of British India because it?

  1. Established the East India Company's monopoly
  2. Created the office of the Governor-General
  3. Introduced the concept of territorial revenue
  4. Abolished the Mughal Empire

Answer: Created the office of the Governor-General

Explanation: The Regulating Act of 1773 created the office of the Governor-General of India, marking a significant step in the British administration of India. It aimed to regulate the affairs of the East India Company.

9. The 'Swadeshi movement' during the Indian freedom struggle was characterized by?

  1. Boycott of foreign goods
  2. Non-violent resistance
  3. Armed rebellion
  4. Social reform

Answer: Boycott of foreign goods

Explanation: The Swadeshi movement involved the boycott of foreign goods, especially British, to promote Indian industries and self-reliance. It was a key aspect of the Indian independence movement.

10. The architectural feature of the 'chaitya' is commonly found in which type of ancient Indian structure?

  1. Stupas
  2. Temples
  3. Monasteries
  4. Caves

Answer: Caves

Explanation: Chaityas are typically found in ancient Indian cave structures, serving as prayer halls or shrines. These caves often feature intricate carvings and sculptures.

11. The 'Arya Samaj' was a socio-religious reform association founded by?

  1. Dayananda Saraswati
  2. Ram Mohan Roy
  3. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  4. Keshab Chandra Sen

Answer: Dayananda Saraswati

Explanation: The Arya Samaj was founded by Dayananda Saraswati, aiming to reform Hindu society and promote Vedic values. It emphasized education and social reform.

12. The term 'amil' refers to which administrative position in medieval India?

  1. Revenue collector
  2. Military officer
  3. Judicial magistrate
  4. Provincial administrator

Answer: Revenue collector

Explanation: An amil was a revenue collector or officer responsible for tax collection in medieval India, playing a crucial role in the administrative and fiscal systems.

13. The 'Pitts India Act of 1784' was enacted to?

  1. Regulate the East India Company's affairs
  2. Establish British parliamentary control over India
  3. Promote Indian trade and commerce
  4. Abolish the East India Company's charter

Answer: Establish British parliamentary control over India

Explanation: The Pitts India Act of 1784 aimed to establish British parliamentary control over the East India Company's affairs in India, marking a significant step in British governance of the subcontinent.

14. The 'Khilafat movement' was closely associated with which of the following?

  1. Indian independence
  2. Muslim rights
  3. Hindu-Muslim unity
  4. Turkish sovereignty

Answer: Hindu-Muslim unity

Explanation: The Khilafat movement was linked to Hindu-Muslim unity in the context of Indian independence, as it sought to preserve the Ottoman Caliphate and promote cooperation between Hindus and Muslims in India.

15. The 'Rowlatt Act of 1919' was significant because it?

  1. Granted Indian independence
  2. Introduced diarchy in provinces
  3. Allowed for the detention of political activists
  4. Established the Federal Court of India

Answer: Allowed for the detention of political activists

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act of 1919 enabled the British to detain political activists without trial, leading to widespread protests and contributing to the Non-Cooperation Movement in India.

16. The medieval administrative term 'amil' refers to

  1. Revenue Officer
  2. Military Commander
  3. Village Headman
  4. Judge

Answer: Revenue Officer

Explanation: Amil was a revenue officer in medieval India, responsible for collecting taxes and revenue from a particular area.

17. The socio-religious reform association 'Brahmo Samaj' was founded by

  1. Raja Ram Mohan Roy
  2. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  3. Swami Vivekananda
  4. Dayananda Saraswati

Answer: Raja Ram Mohan Roy

Explanation: Brahmo Samaj was founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828 to reform Hindu society.

18. The architectural feature 'minaret' is characteristic of

  1. Hindu Temples
  2. Buddhist Stupas
  3. Jain Basadis
  4. Mosques

Answer: Mosques

Explanation: Minarets are tall, slender towers that are typically found in mosques, used for calling the faithful to prayer.

19. The 'Regulating Act of 1773' was enacted to

  1. Establish British rule in India
  2. Regulate the East India Company
  3. Promote Indian trade and commerce
  4. Abolish the East India Company

Answer: Regulate the East India Company

Explanation: The Regulating Act of 1773 was enacted to regulate the affairs of the East India Company in India.

20. The 'Khilafat Movement' was launched in

  1. 1919
  2. 1920
  3. 1921
  4. 1922

Answer: 1920

Explanation: The Khilafat Movement was launched in 1920 to support the Ottoman Caliphate and promote Hindu-Muslim unity.

21. The 'muqaddam' was a medieval administrative term for

  1. Village Headman
  2. Revenue Officer
  3. Military Commander
  4. Judge

Answer: Village Headman

Explanation: Muqaddam was a medieval administrative term for a village headman, responsible for collecting taxes and maintaining law and order.

22. The 'Treaty of Versailles' was signed in

  1. 1918
  2. 1919
  3. 1920
  4. 1921

Answer: 1919

Explanation: The Treaty of Versailles was signed in 1919, marking the end of World War I.

23. The 'Swadeshi Movement' was launched in

  1. 1905
  2. 1906
  3. 1907
  4. 1908

Answer: 1905

Explanation: The Swadeshi Movement was launched in 1905 to promote Indian industries and boycott British goods.

24. The 'Non-Cooperation Movement' was launched by

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Subhas Chandra Bose
  4. Bhagat Singh

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920 to protest British rule in India.

25. The 'Sarvodaya Movement' was inspired by

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Subhas Chandra Bose
  4. Bhagat Singh

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: The Sarvodaya Movement was inspired by Mahatma Gandhi's ideals of non-violence and social upliftment.
