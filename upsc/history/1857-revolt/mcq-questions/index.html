1857 Revolt MCQs with answers

26. Which architectural feature was a symbol of the revolt?

  1. Red Fort
  2. India Gate
  3. Taj Mahal
  4. Qutub Minar

Answer: Red Fort

Explanation: The Red Fort was a symbol of the revolt, as it was the seat of the Mughal Empire and the center of the revolt in Delhi.

27. What was the name of the act that led to the annexation of Indian states?

  1. Doctrine of Lapse
  2. Subsidiary Alliance
  3. Permanent Settlement
  4. Regulating Act

Answer: Doctrine of Lapse

Explanation: The Doctrine of Lapse was a British policy that led to the annexation of Indian states.

28. Which of the following was a key factor in the spread of the revolt?

  1. Telegraph
  2. Railway
  3. Press
  4. Sepoy network

Answer: Sepoy network

Explanation: The sepoy network played a crucial role in the spread of the revolt across India.

29. Who was the British commander-in-chief during the revolt?

  1. Lord Canning
  2. Sir Colin Campbell
  3. Sir Hugh Rose
  4. Sir James Outram

Answer: Sir Colin Campbell

Explanation: Sir Colin Campbell was the British commander-in-chief during the revolt.

30. What was the outcome of the revolt?

  1. Indian victory
  2. British victory
  3. Treaty of alliance
  4. Partition of India

Answer: British victory

Explanation: The revolt ultimately ended in British victory and the consolidation of British power in India.

31. Which of the following was a consequence of the revolt?

  1. Abolition of East India Company
  2. Introduction of direct rule
  3. Establishment of Indian National Congress
  4. Promulgation of Queen's Proclamation

Answer: Introduction of direct rule

Explanation: The revolt led to the introduction of direct British rule in India.

32. Which of the following was a key feature of the revolt?

  1. National unity
  2. Regionalism
  3. Social reform
  4. Military modernization

Answer: National unity

Explanation: The revolt was marked by a sense of national unity among the Indian population.

33. Which act led to the annexation of Indian states by the British East India Company?

  1. Doctrine of Lapse
  2. Subsidiary Alliance
  3. Permanent Settlement
  4. Regulating Act

Answer: Doctrine of Lapse

Explanation: The Doctrine of Lapse was a policy that allowed the British East India Company to annex Indian states.

34. Who was the founder of the Brahmo Samaj, a socio-religious reform association?

  1. Raja Ram Mohan Roy
  2. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  3. Swami Vivekananda
  4. Dayananda Saraswati

Answer: Raja Ram Mohan Roy

Explanation: Raja Ram Mohan Roy was the founder of the Brahmo Samaj, a socio-religious reform association.

35. The British government passed the Act for the Good Government of India in?

  1. 1857
  2. 1858
  3. 1861
  4. 1870

Answer: 1858

Explanation: The British government passed the Act for the Good Government of India in 1858.

36. The revolt of 1857 started from?

  1. Barrackpore
  2. Meerut
  3. Kanpur
  4. Delhi

Answer: Meerut

Explanation: The revolt of 1857 started from Meerut.

37. The concept of subsidiary alliance was introduced by?

  1. Lord Wellesley
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Curzon
  4. Lord Dalhousie

Answer: Lord Wellesley

Explanation: The concept of subsidiary alliance was introduced by Lord Wellesley.

38. The Indian Rebellion of 1857 ended with the defeat of?

  1. Tantia Tope
  2. Rani Lakshmibai
  3. Bahadur Shah II
  4. Kunwar Singh

Answer: Bahadur Shah II

Explanation: The Indian Rebellion of 1857 ended with the defeat of Bahadur Shah II.

39. What was the primary reason behind the 1857 revolt?

  1. Economic grievances
  2. Social reforms
  3. Political interference
  4. Military discontent

Answer: Military discontent

Explanation: The 1857 revolt was sparked by the introduction of the Enfield rifle, which used greased cartridges that had to be bitten open before loading, offending both Hindu and Muslim soldiers. This led to widespread military discontent.

40. What was the name of the British Act that led to the annexation of Indian states?

  1. Doctrine of Lapse
  2. Subsidiary Alliance
  3. Permanent Settlement
  4. Regulating Act

Answer: Doctrine of Lapse

Explanation: The Doctrine of Lapse was a British policy that allowed them to annex Indian states if the ruler died without a male heir, leading to widespread resentment among Indian princes.

41. Which of the following was a key factor in the failure of the 1857 revolt?

  1. Lack of coordination
  2. Inadequate resources
  3. Divided leadership
  4. British military superiority

Answer: Lack of coordination

Explanation: The lack of coordination and unified leadership among the rebel groups was a key factor in the failure of the 1857 revolt.

42. Which of the following was a consequence of the 1857 revolt?

  1. Introduction of democratic reforms
  2. Abolition of the East India Company
  3. Establishment of the British Raj
  4. All of the above

Answer: Establishment of the British Raj

Explanation: The 1857 revolt led to the establishment of the British Raj in India, with the British government taking direct control of the country.

43. What was the name of the British general who suppressed the revolt in Delhi?

  1. Sir Colin Campbell
  2. Sir Henry Lawrence
  3. Sir John Nicholson
  4. Sir James Outram

Answer: Sir John Nicholson

Explanation: Sir John Nicholson was the British general who played a key role in the suppression of the revolt in Delhi.

44. Which of the following was a factor that contributed to the spread of the 1857 revolt?

  1. Economic grievances
  2. Social reforms
  3. Telegraph lines
  4. Railway network

Answer: Economic grievances

Explanation: Economic grievances, such as the introduction of the Enfield rifle and the exploitation of Indian resources, contributed to the spread of the 1857 revolt.

45. The Indian Rebellion of 1857 was sparked by the introduction of the Enfield rifle, which used greased cartridges made from what?

  1. Beef fat
  2. Pig fat
  3. Cow fat
  4. Lard

Answer: Beef fat

Explanation: The Enfield rifle used greased cartridges made from beef and pig fat, offending both Hindus and Muslims.

46. Who was the leader of the rebellion in Delhi?

  1. Bahadur Shah II
  2. Nana Saheb
  3. Tantia Tope
  4. Rani Lakshmibai

Answer: Bahadur Shah II

Explanation: Bahadur Shah II was the last Mughal emperor and the leader of the rebellion in Delhi.

47. The rebellion of 1857 led to the end of which administrative system in India?

  1. Mughal
  2. British East India Company
  3. Peshwa
  4. Sultanate

Answer: British East India Company

Explanation: The rebellion marked the end of the British East India Company's rule in India.

48. Which of the following was a major cause of the rebellion of 1857?

  1. Economic exploitation
  2. Social reform
  3. Cultural imperialism
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: The rebellion was caused by a combination of economic, social, and cultural factors.

49. Who was the British commander-in-chief during the rebellion of 1857?

  1. Lord Canning
  2. Lord Dalhousie
  3. Sir Colin Campbell
  4. Sir Hugh Rose

Answer: Sir Colin Campbell

Explanation: Sir Colin Campbell was the British commander-in-chief during the rebellion.

50. The rebellion of 1857 started with the mutiny of sepoys in which city?

  1. Delhi
  2. Meerut
  3. Kanpur
  4. Lucknow

Answer: Meerut

Explanation: The rebellion started with the mutiny of sepoys in Meerut.
