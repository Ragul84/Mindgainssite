1857 Revolt MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked 1857 Revolt questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The Revolt of 1857 began at:

  1. Delhi
  2. Lucknow
  3. Meerut
  4. Kanpur

Answer: Meerut

Explanation: The Revolt of 1857 began on 10 May 1857 at Meerut (Uttar Pradesh) when Indian sepoys refused to use the greased cartridges and revolted.

2. Mangal Pandey was a sepoy in:

  1. 34th Bengal Native Infantry
  2. Bengal Lancers
  3. Bombay Fusiliers
  4. Madras Infantry

Answer: 34th Bengal Native Infantry

Explanation: Mangal Pandey was a sepoy in the 34th Bengal Native Infantry who fired at a British sergeant-major on 29 March 1857, becoming the first martyr of the 1857 revolt.

3. Indian National Army's women's regiment was named:

  1. Rani of Jhansi Regiment
  2. Bose's Women Corps
  3. Azad Women Brigade
  4. Praja Women's Force

Answer: Rani of Jhansi Regiment

Explanation: INA's women's regiment was the Rani of Jhansi Regiment, named after Rani Lakshmi Bai, commanded by Captain Lakshmi Sahgal (Dr. Lakshmi). About 1,500 women enlisted.

4. The 1857 revolt was called 'First War of Indian Independence' by:

  1. V.D. Savarkar
  2. Karl Marx
  3. Sir Syed Ahmed Khan
  4. R.C. Majumdar

Answer: V.D. Savarkar

Explanation: V.D. Savarkar in his book '1857: The Indian War of Independence' (1909) called the 1857 revolt the First War of Independence, as it was organized resistance to British rule.

5. The Vellore Mutiny (1806) was the first major armed uprising against the British in South India. It was triggered by:

  1. Land revenue policies
  2. New military regulations (turbans, earrings, tilaks banned) — seen as attempts to Christianise soldiers
  3. Salt tax
  4. Forced labour

Answer: New military regulations (turbans, earrings, tilaks banned) — seen as attempts to Christianise soldiers

Explanation: The Vellore Mutiny (10 July 1806) was triggered by new uniform regulations that banned turbans, facial marks, and earrings for sepoys (soldiers), which they saw as forced Christianisation. The mutiny was suppressed; over 100 sepoys were executed.

6. The 1857 revolt was sparked by the introduction of the Enfield rifle, which used greased cartridges made from what?

  1. Beef fat
  2. Pig fat
  3. Cow fat
  4. Lard

Answer: Beef fat

Explanation: The Enfield rifle used greased cartridges made from beef and pig fat, offending both Hindus and Muslims.

7. Who was the leader of the revolt in Meerut?

  1. Mangal Pandey
  2. Bahadur Shah II
  3. Kunwar Singh
  4. Bakht Khan

Answer: Bakht Khan

Explanation: Bakht Khan led the revolt in Meerut, playing a significant role in the 1857 uprising.

8. The revolt of 1857 was also known as the?

  1. Indian Rebellion
  2. Sepoy Mutiny
  3. War of Independence
  4. Great Revolt

Answer: Sepoy Mutiny

Explanation: The revolt of 1857 is also known as the Sepoy Mutiny, due to the involvement of Indian sepoys.

9. The Doctrine of Lapse, which contributed to the discontent leading to the 1857 revolt, was introduced by?

  1. Lord Dalhousie
  2. Lord Canning
  3. Lord Cornwallis
  4. Lord Curzon

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: The Doctrine of Lapse was introduced by Lord Dalhousie, leading to the annexation of Indian states and contributing to the revolt.

10. The Rani of which state played a key role in the revolt of 1857?

  1. Jhansi
  2. Kanpur
  3. Lucknow
  4. Delhi

Answer: Jhansi

Explanation: The Rani of Jhansi, Laxmi Bai, played a key role in the revolt of 1857, leading the resistance against British forces.

11. The Indian Independence Act that led to the eventual dissolution of the East India Company was passed in which year?

  1. 1857
  2. 1858
  3. 1860
  4. 1861

Answer: 1858

Explanation: The Indian Independence Act was passed in 1858, leading to the dissolution of the East India Company.

12. Who was the last Mughal emperor and played a symbolic role in the 1857 revolt?

  1. Akbar II
  2. Bahadur Shah II
  3. Shah Alam II
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Bahadur Shah II

Explanation: Bahadur Shah II was the last Mughal emperor and played a symbolic role in the 1857 revolt, being proclaimed the emperor of India by the rebels.

13. The revolt of 1857 began on which date?

  1. 10 May 1857
  2. 11 May 1857
  3. 15 August 1857
  4. 26 January 1857

Answer: 10 May 1857

Explanation: The revolt of 1857 began on 10 May 1857, in Meerut, marking the start of the uprising against British rule.

14. Which of the following was a major consequence of the 1857 revolt?

  1. Abolition of the East India Company
  2. Introduction of the British Raj
  3. Establishment of the Indian National Congress
  4. Partition of India

Answer: Introduction of the British Raj

Explanation: The 1857 revolt led to the introduction of the British Raj, with the British government taking direct control of India.

15. The 1857 revolt was ultimately put down by the British, with the help of which group?

  1. Sikhs
  2. Gurkhas
  3. Rajputs
  4. Punjabi troops

Answer: Sikhs

Explanation: The 1857 revolt was ultimately put down by the British, with the help of the Sikhs, who remained loyal to the British.

16. What was the primary reason for the 1857 revolt?

  1. Economic grievances
  2. Social reforms
  3. Political power
  4. Foreign rule

Answer: Economic grievances

Explanation: The revolt was sparked by economic grievances, including the introduction of the Enfield rifle and the exploitation of Indian resources.

17. Who was the leader of the revolt in Delhi?

  1. Bahadur Shah II
  2. Nana Saheb
  3. Tantia Tope
  4. Rani Lakshmibai

Answer: Bahadur Shah II

Explanation: Bahadur Shah II was the last Mughal emperor and the leader of the revolt in Delhi.

18. What was the name of the British East India Company's revenue collector?

  1. Amil
  2. Muqaddam
  3. Tehsildar
  4. Zamindar

Answer: Amil

Explanation: The amil was the revenue collector of the British East India Company.

19. Which act played a significant role in the 1857 revolt?

  1. Indian Councils Act
  2. Government of India Act
  3. Pitts India Act
  4. Regulating Act

Answer: Government of India Act

Explanation: The Government of India Act of 1858 was passed after the revolt, but the revolt was sparked by the policies of the British East India Company, which was regulated by the Indian Councils Act and the Government of India Act.

20. Who was the leader of the revolt in Kanpur?

  1. Nana Saheb
  2. Tantia Tope
  3. Rani Lakshmibai
  4. Begum Hazrat Mahal

Answer: Nana Saheb

Explanation: Nana Saheb was the leader of the revolt in Kanpur.

21. What was the name of the philosophical idea that inspired the revolt?

  1. Swadeshi
  2. Swaraj
  3. Nationalism
  4. Indianism

Answer: Swaraj

Explanation: The idea of Swaraj, or self-rule, inspired the revolt.

22. Which socio-religious reform association supported the revolt?

  1. Brahmo Samaj
  2. Arya Samaj
  3. Ramakrishna Mission
  4. Wahabi Movement

Answer: Wahabi Movement

Explanation: The Wahabi Movement supported the revolt, as it sought to overthrow British rule and establish an Islamic state.

23. What was the outcome of the 1857 revolt?

  1. British victory
  2. Indian independence
  3. Partition of India
  4. Establishment of the Mughal Empire

Answer: British victory

Explanation: The revolt ended in British victory and the establishment of direct British rule in India.

24. Who was the leader of the revolt in Jhansi?

  1. Rani Lakshmibai
  2. Nana Saheb
  3. Tantia Tope
  4. Begum Hazrat Mahal

Answer: Rani Lakshmibai

Explanation: Rani Lakshmibai was the leader of the revolt in Jhansi.

25. What was the name of the treaty that marked the end of the Mughal Empire?

  1. Treaty of Allahabad
  2. Treaty of Versailles
  3. Treaty of Nanjing
  4. Treaty of Lahore

Answer: Treaty of Allahabad

Explanation: The Treaty of Allahabad marked the end of the Mughal Empire and the beginning of British rule in India.
