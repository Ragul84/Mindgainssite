British Conquest MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked British Conquest questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between:

  1. British and Marathas
  2. British East India Company and Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal)
  3. British and Tipu Sultan
  4. British and Mughal Emperor

Answer: British East India Company and Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal)

Explanation: The Battle of Plassey (1757) was fought between the British East India Company and Siraj-ud-Daulah, Nawab of Bengal. British victory laid the foundation of British rule in India.

2. The Subsidiary Alliance system was introduced by:

  1. Lord Clive
  2. Warren Hastings
  3. Lord Wellesley
  4. Lord Cornwallis

Answer: Lord Wellesley

Explanation: Lord Wellesley introduced the Subsidiary Alliance system (1798) to bring Indian states under British control without direct annexation. Under this, Indian rulers had to maintain British troops at their cost.

3. The 'Doctrine of Lapse' was used by which Governor-General to annex Indian states?

  1. Lord Dalhousie
  2. Lord Wellesley
  3. Lord Cornwallis
  4. Lord Curzon

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) used the Doctrine of Lapse to annex states without a natural male heir, including Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, and Awadh.

4. The 'Permanent Settlement' of 1793 was introduced by:

  1. Lord Wellesley
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Dalhousie
  4. Lord Clive

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement of Bengal in 1793. It fixed the land revenue to be paid to the British permanently by zamindars.

5. The Doctrine of Lapse was introduced by:

  1. Dalhousie
  2. Hastings
  3. Wellesley
  4. Cornwallis

Answer: Dalhousie

Explanation: Doctrine of Lapse (Governor-General Lord Dalhousie, 1848-1856): states without natural heirs would be annexed by the British. Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, etc. were annexed.

6. After the 1857 revolt, India came under:

  1. Crown Rule
  2. Company Rule
  3. Mughal Rule
  4. Parliament Rule

Answer: Crown Rule

Explanation: After the 1857 revolt, the Government of India Act 1858 transferred power from the East India Company to the British Crown. Queen Victoria was proclaimed Empress of India in 1876.

7. Permanent Settlement was introduced by:

  1. Lord Cornwallis
  2. Lord Dalhousie
  3. Lord Hastings
  4. Lord Wellesley

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: Permanent Settlement of Bengal (1793) by Lord Cornwallis fixed land revenue permanently with zamindars, who became permanent owners of land if they paid fixed revenue.

8. 'Sati' was abolished by law under:

  1. Lord Bentinck
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Dalhousie
  4. Lord Hastings

Answer: Lord Bentinck

Explanation: Lord William Bentinck abolished the practice of Sati (widow self-immolation) by Regulation XVII in 1829, largely influenced by Raja Ram Mohan Roy's campaign.

9. Who was known as 'Maker of Modern India'?

  1. Lord Dalhousie
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Bentinck
  4. Lord Ripon

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: Lord Dalhousie (1848-1856) is called 'Maker of Modern India' for introducing railways, telegraph, postal reforms, engineering colleges, while also annexing many territories.

10. The Permanent Settlement (1793) of land revenue was introduced by:

  1. Robert Clive
  2. Lord Cornwallis
  3. Lord Wellesley
  4. Lord Dalhousie

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement in Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa (1793). It fixed land revenue permanently with zamindars who became hereditary owners. Peasants (ryots) had no land rights — caused widespread impoverishment.

11. The Doctrine of Lapse (by Lord Dalhousie) stated:

  1. States with public debt would be taken over
  2. If an Indian ruler died without a natural heir, the state would be annexed by the British (no adoption allowed)
  3. All states must pay tribute
  4. Any state that refused alliance would be conquered

Answer: If an Indian ruler died without a natural heir, the state would be annexed by the British (no adoption allowed)

Explanation: Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie, 1848–1856): Princely states without natural heirs could not adopt successors — they would lapse to the Company. States annexed: Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854), Awadh (1856, on different grounds).

12. The British conquest of India was facilitated by the

  1. Treaty of Allahabad
  2. Battle of Plassey
  3. Regulating Act
  4. Subsidiary Alliance

Answer: Battle of Plassey

Explanation: The Battle of Plassey marked the beginning of British conquest in India. It was a decisive victory for the British East India Company.

13. The term 'amil' refers to a

  1. Revenue collector
  2. Military officer
  3. Administrative officer
  4. Judicial officer

Answer: Revenue collector

Explanation: Amil was a revenue collector or administrator under the Mughal and British regimes in India.

14. The Rowlatt Act was passed in the year

  1. 1914
  2. 1917
  3. 1919
  4. 1920

Answer: 1919

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act was a repressive law passed by the British in 1919 to curb nationalist activities.

15. The socio-religious reform association 'Arya Samaj' was founded by

  1. Dayananda Saraswati
  2. Rammohan Roy
  3. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  4. Keshab Chandra Sen

Answer: Dayananda Saraswati

Explanation: Arya Samaj was founded by Dayananda Saraswati in 1875 to reform Hindu society.

16. The 'Regulating Act' of 1773 was passed to

  1. Establish British rule in India
  2. Regulate the affairs of the East India Company
  3. Abolish the East India Company
  4. Promote trade with India

Answer: Regulate the affairs of the East India Company

Explanation: The Regulating Act was passed to regulate the affairs of the East India Company and limit its powers.

17. The 'muqaddam' was a

  1. Village headman
  2. Revenue collector
  3. Military officer
  4. Judicial officer

Answer: Village headman

Explanation: Muqaddam was a village headman or leader under the Mughal and British regimes in India.

18. The 'Indian National Congress' was founded in the year

  1. 1884
  2. 1885
  3. 1886
  4. 1887

Answer: 1885

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885 by A.O. Hume and others.

19. The 'Government of India Act' of 1858 was passed to

  1. Establish British rule in India
  2. Abolish the East India Company
  3. Promote trade with India
  4. Regulate the affairs of the East India Company

Answer: Abolish the East India Company

Explanation: The Government of India Act was passed to abolish the East India Company and establish direct British rule in India.

20. The administrative term 'amil' refers to a

  1. Revenue collector
  2. Military officer
  3. Judge
  4. Village headman

Answer: Revenue collector

Explanation: Amil was a revenue collector or administrator under the Mughal Empire and later during the British period.

21. The socio-religious reform association 'Brahmo Samaj' was founded by

  1. Rammohan Roy
  2. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  3. Dayananda Saraswati
  4. Keshab Chandra Sen

Answer: Rammohan Roy

Explanation: Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828 to reform Hindu society.

22. The British government in India was established by the

  1. Government of India Act 1858
  2. Indian Councils Act 1892
  3. Regulating Act 1773
  4. Pitt's India Act 1784

Answer: Government of India Act 1858

Explanation: The Government of India Act 1858 marked the beginning of direct British rule in India.

23. The 'Regulating Act' of 1773 was enacted to

  1. Establish British rule in India
  2. Regulate the affairs of the East India Company
  3. Abolish the Mughal Empire
  4. Promote trade with India

Answer: Regulate the affairs of the East India Company

Explanation: The Regulating Act aimed to regulate the affairs of the East India Company and limit its powers.

24. The 'Treaty of Allahabad' was signed in

  1. 1765
  2. 1773
  3. 1784
  4. 1793

Answer: 1765

Explanation: The Treaty of Allahabad was signed in 1765 between the East India Company and the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II.

25. The British conquest of India was marked by the expansion of

  1. Trade and commerce
  2. Missionary activities
  3. Education and social reform
  4. Territorial control

Answer: Territorial control

Explanation: The British conquest of India was characterized by the expansion of territorial control through various means.
