British Conquest MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked British Conquest questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. The 'Pitt's India Act' of 1784 was enacted to

  1. Regulate the East India Company
  2. Establish British rule in India
  3. Promote trade with India
  4. Abolish the Mughal Empire

Answer: Regulate the East India Company

Explanation: Pitt's India Act aimed to regulate the East India Company and limit its powers.

27. What was the primary reason for the British conquest of India in the 18th century?

  1. Economic interests
  2. Religious conversion
  3. Strategic location
  4. Cultural exchange

Answer: Economic interests

Explanation: The British East India Company's economic interests drove the conquest of India. Economic interests included exploiting India's resources and markets.

28. Which act marked the beginning of British rule in India?

  1. Regulating Act 1773
  2. Pitt's India Act 1784
  3. Charter Act 1813
  4. Government of India Act 1858

Answer: Regulating Act 1773

Explanation: The Regulating Act 1773 was the first attempt to regulate the affairs of the British East India Company in India, marking the beginning of British rule.

29. Who was the key figure in the Indian Rebellion of 1857 against British rule?

  1. Mangal Pandey
  2. Bahadur Shah II
  3. Rani Lakshmibai
  4. Tantia Tope

Answer: Bahadur Shah II

Explanation: Bahadur Shah II was the last Mughal emperor and a key figure in the Indian Rebellion of 1857, symbolizing the resistance against British rule.

30. What was the name of the British administrative term for a revenue collector in India?

  1. Amil
  2. Muqaddam
  3. Tehsildar
  4. Zamindar

Answer: Amil

Explanation: Amil was a British administrative term for a revenue collector in India, responsible for collecting taxes and revenue.

31. Which socio-religious reform association was founded by Rammohan Roy in 1828?

  1. Brahmo Samaj
  2. Arya Samaj
  3. Ramakrishna Mission
  4. Theosophical Society

Answer: Brahmo Samaj

Explanation: Brahmo Samaj was a socio-religious reform association founded by Rammohan Roy in 1828, aiming to reform Hindu society and promote social and religious reform.

32. What was the name of the treaty that marked the end of the First Anglo-Maratha War in 1782?

  1. Treaty of Salbai
  2. Treaty of Purandhar
  3. Treaty of Bassein
  4. Treaty of Surat

Answer: Treaty of Salbai

Explanation: The Treaty of Salbai marked the end of the First Anglo-Maratha War in 1782, establishing a temporary peace between the British and the Marathas.

33. Which architectural feature is characteristic of British colonial architecture in India?

  1. Mughal arches
  2. Gothic spires
  3. Greek columns
  4. Roman domes

Answer: Gothic spires

Explanation: Gothic spires are a characteristic feature of British colonial architecture in India, reflecting the British attempt to impose their cultural and architectural styles.

34. Who was the leader of the Indian National Congress during the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920-1922?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Subhas Chandra Bose
  4. Lala Lajpat Rai

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi was the leader of the Indian National Congress during the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920-1922, advocating for non-violent resistance against British rule.

35. What was the name of the British policy of exploiting India's resources and economy for British interests?

  1. Drain of wealth
  2. Exploitation theory
  3. Imperialism
  4. Colonialism

Answer: Drain of wealth

Explanation: The drain of wealth refers to the British policy of exploiting India's resources and economy for British interests, leading to the depletion of India's wealth and resources.

36. Which phase of the freedom struggle in India is characterized by the use of non-violent resistance and civil disobedience?

  1. Non-Cooperation Movement
  2. Salt March
  3. Quit India Movement
  4. Indian National Army

Answer: Non-Cooperation Movement

Explanation: The Non-Cooperation Movement phase of the freedom struggle in India is characterized by the use of non-violent resistance and civil disobedience, led by Mahatma Gandhi.

37. The Indian Rebellion of 1857 was sparked by the introduction of the

  1. Enfield Rifle
  2. Brown Bess
  3. Lee-Metford
  4. Snider-Enfield

Answer: Enfield Rifle

Explanation: The Indian Rebellion of 1857 began as a mutiny by sepoys over the introduction of the Enfield Rifle. The rifle used greased cartridges that had to be bitten open.

38. The Permanent Settlement of 1793 was implemented by

  1. Lord Cornwallis
  2. Lord Wellesley
  3. Lord Hastings
  4. Lord Dalhousie

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: The Permanent Settlement was a revenue system implemented by Lord Cornwallis. It fixed revenue collection at a permanent rate.

39. The British East India Company's monopoly on Indian trade was abolished by the

  1. Regulating Act
  2. Pitt's India Act
  3. Charter Act of 1813
  4. Government of India Act

Answer: Charter Act of 1813

Explanation: The Charter Act of 1813 abolished the British East India Company's monopoly on Indian trade. It opened up Indian trade to other British traders.

40. The Rowlatt Act of 1919 was opposed by

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Subhas Chandra Bose
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act was a repressive law that was opposed by Indian nationalists. Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, and Subhas Chandra Bose were among those who opposed it.

41. The Indian National Congress was founded in

  1. 1885
  2. 1905
  3. 1915
  4. 1925

Answer: 1885

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. It was founded by A.O. Hume with the aim of promoting Indian rights.

42. The term 'muqaddam' refers to a

  1. Village headman
  2. Revenue collector
  3. Military commander
  4. Administrative officer

Answer: Village headman

Explanation: The term 'muqaddam' refers to a village headman in the Mughal administration. Muqaddams were responsible for collecting revenue and maintaining law and order.

43. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurred in

  1. 1915
  2. 1919
  3. 1925
  4. 1930

Answer: 1919

Explanation: The Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurred in 1919. It was a brutal suppression of a peaceful protest by British troops.

44. The Quit India Movement was launched in

  1. 1940
  2. 1942
  3. 1945
  4. 1947

Answer: 1942

Explanation: The Quit India Movement was launched in 1942. It was a major movement in the Indian freedom struggle.

45. The Regulating Act of 1773 was passed to regulate the affairs of the

  1. British East India Company
  2. British Government
  3. Indian Princes
  4. Indian Nobles

Answer: British East India Company

Explanation: The Regulating Act was passed to regulate the affairs of the British East India Company. It was the first attempt to regulate the company's affairs.

46. The Pitt's India Act of 1784 was passed to

  1. Regulate the British East India Company
  2. Abolish the British East India Company
  3. Increase the power of the British East India Company
  4. Reduce the power of the British East India Company

Answer: Regulate the British East India Company

Explanation: The Pitt's India Act was passed to regulate the British East India Company. It established the Board of Control to oversee the company's affairs.

47. The Government of India Act of 1935 was passed to

  1. Introduce federalism in India
  2. Abolish federalism in India
  3. Increase the power of the British Government
  4. Reduce the power of the British Government

Answer: Introduce federalism in India

Explanation: The Government of India Act was passed to introduce federalism in India. It established a federal system of government in India.

48. The term 'Divide and Rule' is associated with

  1. Warren Hastings
  2. Lord Curzon
  3. Lord Dalhousie
  4. Lord Cornwallis

Answer: Lord Curzon

Explanation: Lord Curzon is often associated with the policy of Divide and Rule.

49. The Rowlatt Act was passed in

  1. 1919
  2. 1921
  3. 1935
  4. 1947

Answer: 1919

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act was passed in 1919, extending wartime restrictions on civil liberties.

50. The Government of India Act of 1935

  1. Introduced dyarchy
  2. Established provincial autonomy
  3. Abolished the zamindari system
  4. Separated Burma from India

Answer: Established provincial autonomy

Explanation: The Act established provincial autonomy and provided for a federal structure.
