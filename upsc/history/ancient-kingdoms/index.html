Ancient Kingdoms MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Ancient Kingdoms questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Who was the first ruler of the Maurya Empire?

  1. Ashoka
  2. Chandragupta Maurya
  3. Bindusara
  4. Bimbisara

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire around 321 BCE with the help of Chanakya (Kautilya).

2. Which Chola king sent a naval expedition to Southeast Asia?

  1. Rajaraja I
  2. Rajendra Chola I
  3. Kulottunga I
  4. Parantaka I

Answer: Rajendra Chola I

Explanation: Rajendra Chola I (1014–1044 CE) sent a famous naval expedition to Southeast Asia (Srivijaya Empire in Sumatra and Malay Peninsula) around 1025 CE.

3. Who wrote 'Arthashastra', a treatise on statecraft and economic policy?

  1. Kalidasa
  2. Chanakya (Kautilya)
  3. Patanjali
  4. Vatsyayana

Answer: Chanakya (Kautilya)

Explanation: Arthashastra was written by Chanakya (Kautilya), the chief minister of Chandragupta Maurya. It is an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy.

4. Harshavardhana's empire was described by the Chinese pilgrim:

  1. Fa Hien
  2. Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)
  3. I-tsing
  4. Sung Yun

Answer: Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)

Explanation: Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) visited India during Harsha's reign (7th century CE) and described Harsha's empire and character in detail in his 'Si-Yu-Ki' (Record of Western Countries).

5. The 'Hathigumpha inscription' of Kharavela provides information about which dynasty?

  1. Satavahana dynasty
  2. Chedi dynasty of Kalinga
  3. Maurya dynasty
  4. Gupta dynasty

Answer: Chedi dynasty of Kalinga

Explanation: The Hathigumpha (Elephant Cave) inscription near Bhubaneswar describes the achievements of Kharavela, the greatest king of the Chedi dynasty of Kalinga (1st century BCE).

6. The Varna system in early Vedic society was based on:

  1. Birth
  2. Occupation (karma) — it was not hereditary initially
  3. Colour of skin
  4. Wealth

Answer: Occupation (karma) — it was not hereditary initially

Explanation: In the Rigvedic period, the varna system was based on occupation, not birth. The four varnas were Brahmin (priest), Kshatriya (warrior), Vaishya (trader), Shudra (servant). Hereditary rigidity came in the Later Vedic period.

7. The Upanishads deal with:

  1. Rituals and sacrifices
  2. Philosophical and metaphysical ideas (Brahman, Atman, Maya, Karma)
  3. Military strategy
  4. Agricultural practices

Answer: Philosophical and metaphysical ideas (Brahman, Atman, Maya, Karma)

Explanation: The Upanishads (c. 800–200 BCE) are philosophical texts exploring the nature of ultimate reality (Brahman), individual soul (Atman), and their identity. They form the basis of Vedanta philosophy.

8. The founder of Jainism is considered to be:

  1. Mahavira
  2. Parshvanatha
  3. Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)
  4. Neminatha

Answer: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)

Explanation: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha) is considered the first Tirthankara and founder of Jainism. Mahavira (599–527 BCE) was the 24th and last Tirthankara who revived and systematised Jainism.

9. The first sermon of the Buddha is called:

  1. Mahaparinibbana
  2. Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Turning of the Wheel of Dharma)
  3. Tripitaka
  4. Jataka

Answer: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Turning of the Wheel of Dharma)

Explanation: The Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Setting the Wheel of Dharma in Motion) was the first sermon delivered at Sarnath (Deer Park/Isipatana) to the five ascetics. This event is called 'Dharma-chakra-pravartana'.

10. The Buddhist councils were held to:

  1. Elect new kings
  2. Compile, recite, and standardise Buddhist scriptures
  3. Discuss political matters
  4. Spread Buddhism outside India

Answer: Compile, recite, and standardise Buddhist scriptures

Explanation: Four major Buddhist Councils: 1st at Rajgriha (483 BCE), 2nd at Vaishali (383 BCE), 3rd at Pataliputra (250 BCE, Ashoka's reign), 4th at Kundalvana, Kashmir (1st century CE, Kanishka's reign).

11. The Greek ambassador Megasthenes visited the court of:

  1. Ashoka
  2. Bindusara
  3. Chandragupta Maurya
  4. Chandragupta II

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Megasthenes (c. 350–290 BCE) was the Greek ambassador sent by Seleucus I Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya. His account 'Indica' (mostly lost, known through fragments) described Mauryan India.

12. Ashoka's edicts are found in which scripts?

  1. Only in Sanskrit
  2. Brahmi (majority), Kharosthi (northwest), Aramaic and Greek (west)
  3. Only in Pali
  4. Only in Tamil Brahmi

Answer: Brahmi (majority), Kharosthi (northwest), Aramaic and Greek (west)

Explanation: Ashoka's edicts are inscribed in: Brahmi script (most of India), Kharosthi (northwest — modern Pakistan/Afghanistan), Aramaic (Afghanistan), and Greek (Kandahar). Content is in Pali, Prakrit, Aramaic, and Greek.

13. The famous rock-cut architecture of the Mauryan period includes:

  1. Ajanta Caves
  2. Ellora Caves
  3. Barabar Caves (Bihar) — earliest rock-cut caves in India
  4. Elephanta Caves

Answer: Barabar Caves (Bihar) — earliest rock-cut caves in India

Explanation: The Barabar Caves (Bihar) are the earliest surviving rock-cut caves in India, built during Ashoka's reign (c. 3rd century BCE) for the Ajivika sect. They have highly polished interiors (Mauryan polish).

14. The Gandhara art style was a blend of:

  1. Indian and Chinese art
  2. Greek/Hellenistic and Indian (Buddhist) artistic traditions
  3. Persian and Indian art
  4. Roman and Indian art exclusively

Answer: Greek/Hellenistic and Indian (Buddhist) artistic traditions

Explanation: Gandhara art (1st–5th century CE) blended Hellenistic/Greek realism with Buddhist iconography. It emerged in the Gandhara region (Afghanistan/Pakistan). First human images of the Buddha were made here.

15. The concept of 'Ahimsa' was first emphasized in:

  1. Jainism
  2. Buddhism
  3. Vedic texts
  4. Bhagavad Gita

Answer: Jainism

Explanation: Ahimsa (non-violence) was most strongly and systematically emphasized in Jainism, especially by Mahavira. It became central to Jain philosophy.

16. Sabha and Samiti in Vedic period were:

  1. Tribal assemblies
  2. Religious centers
  3. Military units
  4. Tax collection bodies

Answer: Tribal assemblies

Explanation: Sabha and Samiti were tribal assemblies in the Vedic period. Sabha was a smaller, exclusive council of elders; Samiti was a larger, more democratic gathering.

17. Iron was first used in India during:

  1. Later Vedic period
  2. Mauryan period
  3. Gupta period
  4. Early Vedic period

Answer: Later Vedic period

Explanation: Iron came into widespread use in India during the Later Vedic period (1000-600 BCE), transforming agriculture, warfare, and craftsmanship.

18. Purushmedha refers to:

  1. Human sacrifice in Vedic rituals
  2. A Vedic prayer
  3. Marriage ceremony
  4. Battle formation

Answer: Human sacrifice in Vedic rituals

Explanation: Purushmedha was a ritual human sacrifice described in Vedic texts, though scholars debate whether it was actually practiced or purely symbolic.

19. The first Tirthankara of Jainism is:

  1. Rishabhadeva
  2. Mahavira
  3. Parsvanatha
  4. Neminatha

Answer: Rishabhadeva

Explanation: Rishabhadeva (Adinatha) is the first Tirthankara (ford-maker) of Jainism. There are 24 Tirthankaras in total.

20. The principle of 'Anekantavada' is associated with:

  1. Jainism
  2. Buddhism
  3. Vedanta
  4. Lokayata

Answer: Jainism

Explanation: Anekantavada (many-sidedness of truth) is a Jain doctrine stating that truth and reality are complex, and no single perspective is complete.

21. Jains were divided into Digambara and Shvetambara at a council held at:

  1. Pataliputra
  2. Vaishali
  3. Rajgir
  4. Pava

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: The split between Digambara (sky-clad, don't wear clothes) and Shvetambara (white-robed) Jains occurred at a council at Pataliputra around 300 BCE.

22. Triratna of Jainism refers to:

  1. Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct
  2. Three Tirthankaras
  3. Three sacred texts
  4. Three monasteries

Answer: Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct

Explanation: Jain Triratna (Three Jewels): Right Faith (Samyak Darshan), Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana), Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra) — the path to liberation.

23. The First Buddhist Council was held at:

  1. Rajgriha
  2. Vaishali
  3. Pataliputra
  4. Kundalvana

Answer: Rajgriha

Explanation: First Buddhist Council (483 BCE) at Rajgriha (Sattapanni Cave), immediately after Buddha's death, presided over by Mahakassapa, compiled Vinaya Pitaka and Sutta Pitaka.

24. 'Dhammachakkapavattana Sutta' refers to:

  1. Buddha's first sermon
  2. Buddha's last words
  3. Buddhist monastery rules
  4. Jataka tales

Answer: Buddha's first sermon

Explanation: Dhammachakkapavattana Sutta is the record of Buddha's first sermon at Sarnath (Deer Park) to his five former companions — the 'Setting in Motion of the Wheel of Dharma'.

25. Bodhgaya is associated with:

  1. Buddha's enlightenment
  2. Buddha's birth
  3. Buddha's first sermon
  4. Buddha's death

Answer: Buddha's enlightenment

Explanation: Bodhgaya (Bihar) is where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree and became the Buddha. It is the holiest Buddhist pilgrimage site.
