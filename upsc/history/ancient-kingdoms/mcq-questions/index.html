Ancient Kingdoms MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Ancient Kingdoms questions to revise the UPSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. Nalanda University was established during:

  1. Gupta period
  2. Mauryan period
  3. Kushan period
  4. Pala period

Answer: Gupta period

Explanation: Nalanda University was established during the Gupta period (5th century CE, under Kumaragupta I) and flourished until it was destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khilji in 1193 CE.

27. Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nanda dynasty with the help of:

  1. Chanakya
  2. Seleucus
  3. Bindusara
  4. Ashoka

Answer: Chanakya

Explanation: Chanakya (Kautilya), the brilliant political strategist and author of Arthashastra, helped Chandragupta Maurya overthrow the last Nanda king (Dhana Nanda) around 321 BCE.

28. Which Ashokan edict describes his conversion to Buddhism?

  1. Bhabru edict
  2. Rock Edict XIII
  3. Rock Edict II
  4. Pillar Edict VII

Answer: Bhabru edict

Explanation: The Bhabru (Calcutta-Bairat) edict mentions Ashoka's conversion to Buddhism and his faith in the Triple Jewel (Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha).

29. Kalinga was captured by Ashoka in:

  1. 261 BCE
  2. 273 BCE
  3. 232 BCE
  4. 269 BCE

Answer: 261 BCE

Explanation: Ashoka conquered Kalinga (modern Odisha) in 261 BCE (8th year of his reign), 13 years after becoming king. The massive bloodshed transformed him.

30. The term 'Dhamma' used by Ashoka was derived from:

  1. Pali
  2. Sanskrit
  3. Prakrit
  4. Tamil

Answer: Pali

Explanation: Dhamma is the Pali equivalent of Sanskrit 'Dharma'. Ashoka used Pali and Prakrit languages in his edicts to make them accessible to common people.

31. The Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador at the court of:

  1. Chandragupta Maurya
  2. Ashoka
  3. Bindusara
  4. Kanishka

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Megasthenes was sent as ambassador of Seleucus I Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya. His book 'Indica' gives valuable information about Mauryan India.

32. The Mauryan Empire declined after Ashoka mainly because of:

  1. Weak successors and economic strain
  2. Foreign invasions
  3. Natural disasters
  4. Religious conflicts

Answer: Weak successors and economic strain

Explanation: After Ashoka's death (232 BCE), weak successors, heavy expenditure on Buddhist welfare, and weakening of military led to rapid decline. Pusyamitra Sunga killed last Mauryan king in 185 BCE.

33. Ashokan pillars are made of:

  1. Polished sandstone
  2. Marble
  3. Granite
  4. Limestone

Answer: Polished sandstone

Explanation: Ashokan pillars are made of a single piece of highly polished Chunar sandstone. The polish is so fine that they look like metal — technique still not fully understood.

34. Chandragupta II was also known as:

  1. Vikramaditya
  2. Samudragupta
  3. Skandagupta
  4. Ramagupta

Answer: Vikramaditya

Explanation: Chandragupta II (375-415 CE) was known as Vikramaditya (Sun of Valor). His reign is considered the golden age of Gupta empire — Kalidasa, Varahamihira flourished in his court.

35. 'Allahabad Pillar Inscription' (Prayag Prashasti) eulogizes:

  1. Samudragupta
  2. Chandragupta I
  3. Ashoka
  4. Kanishka

Answer: Samudragupta

Explanation: Prayag Prashasti (composed by court poet Harisena) on the Allahabad Pillar describes the military conquests and character of Samudragupta — called 'Napoleon of India'.

36. The decimal system of numeration was developed in:

  1. Gupta period
  2. Mauryan period
  3. Vedic period
  4. Colonial period

Answer: Gupta period

Explanation: The decimal system with place value notation (including zero) was developed in India during the Gupta period, later transmitted to Arabs and then to Europe.

37. The Huna invasion of India weakened the:

  1. Gupta Empire
  2. Mauryan Empire
  3. Vijayanagara Empire
  4. Delhi Sultanate

Answer: Gupta Empire

Explanation: The Huna (Hephthalite) invasions in the late 5th and 6th centuries significantly weakened the Gupta Empire, contributing to its decline.

38. Ellora caves contain temples of:

  1. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism
  2. Only Hinduism
  3. Only Buddhism
  4. Islam and Hinduism

Answer: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism

Explanation: Ellora Caves (Maharashtra) contain 34 caves — 12 Buddhist (caves 1-12), 17 Hindu (caves 13-29), 5 Jain (caves 30-34). Shows religious coexistence during 6th-11th centuries CE.

39. Fa-Hien (Faxian) visited India during whose reign?

  1. Chandragupta II
  2. Ashoka
  3. Kanishka
  4. Samudragupta

Answer: Chandragupta II

Explanation: Fa-Hien (399-414 CE), the Chinese Buddhist pilgrim, visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) to collect Buddhist scriptures and study Buddhism.

40. The Constituent Assembly adopted the National Flag on:

  1. 22 July 1947
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 26 November 1949

Answer: 22 July 1947

Explanation: The National Flag of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 July 1947, three weeks before independence. The horizontal tricolor with Ashoka Chakra replaced the INC flag.

41. India's first nuclear test was conducted at:

  1. Pokhran, Rajasthan
  2. Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre
  3. Sriharikota
  4. Bhabha Atomic Research Centre

Answer: Pokhran, Rajasthan

Explanation: India conducted its first nuclear test (Smiling Buddha) at Pokhran, Rajasthan on 18 May 1974. Second nuclear tests (Pokhran-II/Operation Shakti) were conducted in May 1998.

42. 'Gandharva' art style is known for depicting:

  1. Buddhist images with Hellenistic influence
  2. Pure Indian Buddhist art
  3. Dravidian temple style
  4. Mughal miniature painting

Answer: Buddhist images with Hellenistic influence

Explanation: Gandhara art (1st-5th century CE, NW India and Afghanistan) shows a fusion of Hellenistic/Roman and Indian Buddhist art — the first realistic images of the Buddha in human form.

43. Mathura art school is known for:

  1. Indigenous Indian Buddhist images in red sandstone
  2. Indo-Greek style
  3. Mughal style
  4. Dravidian style

Answer: Indigenous Indian Buddhist images in red sandstone

Explanation: Mathura school (1st-12th century CE) produced purely Indian Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu sculptures in red sandstone — without Hellenistic influence, unlike Gandhara school.

44. Who wrote 'Mudrarakshasa'?

  1. Vishakhadatta
  2. Kalidasa
  3. Bana
  4. Harsha

Answer: Vishakhadatta

Explanation: Mudrarakshasa by Vishakhadatta is a Sanskrit play depicting the political intrigue after Chandragupta Maurya's rise to power, with Chanakya as the main character.

45. Kalidasa's famous works include:

  1. Meghaduta, Abhijnana Shakuntalam, Raghuvamsa
  2. Arthashastra, Mudrarakshasa
  3. Ashtadhyayi, Raghuvamsa
  4. Amarakosa, Nitisara

Answer: Meghaduta, Abhijnana Shakuntalam, Raghuvamsa

Explanation: Kalidasa (Gupta period) wrote: Plays — Abhijnana Shakuntalam, Vikramorvasiyam, Malavikagnimitram; Epic poems — Raghuvamsa, Kumarasambhavam; Lyric poems — Meghaduta, Ritusamhara.

46. The Nagara style of temple architecture is mainly found in:

  1. North India
  2. South India
  3. Both equally
  4. East India only

Answer: North India

Explanation: Nagara style (North Indian/Indo-Aryan) has a curvilinear tower (shikhara) over the sanctum. Dravidian style (South Indian) has a pyramidal tower (vimana). Vesara is a mix of both.

47. 'Janpad' in ancient India referred to:

  1. Territorial states in Vedic period
  2. Village assembly
  3. Tax collection unit
  4. Military camp

Answer: Territorial states in Vedic period

Explanation: Janapada (jana=tribe/people, pada=foot/territory) refers to the territorial states that developed in the Later Vedic period. 16 great Mahajanapadas existed around 600 BCE.

48. Harshavardhana's Chinese visitor was:

  1. Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)
  2. Fa-Hien
  3. I-tsing
  4. Marco Polo

Answer: Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)

Explanation: Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang), the Chinese Buddhist pilgrim, visited India during Harsha's reign (630-644 CE). His 'Si-Yu-Ki' gives valuable information about 7th century India.

49. The Banabhatta was the court poet of:

  1. Harshavardhana
  2. Chandragupta II
  3. Ashoka
  4. Samudragupta

Answer: Harshavardhana

Explanation: Banabhatta was the court poet of King Harshavardhana (606-647 CE) and wrote Harshacharita (biography of Harsha) and Kadambari (prose romance).

50. Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) was established by:

  1. Akbar
  2. Babur
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar established Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri in 1575 CE for religious debates among scholars of different faiths — precursor to his Din-i-Ilahi.
