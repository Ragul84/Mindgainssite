Env Laws MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Env Laws questions to revise the UPSC Environment syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Under the Environment Protection Act, 1986, which authority is empowered to issue directions for closure, prohibition, or regulation of any industry?

  1. Central Government
  2. State Government
  3. National Green Tribunal
  4. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change

Answer: Central Government

Explanation: Section 5 of the EPA empowers the Central Government to issue such directions.

2. Which of the following is a statutory body established under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972?

  1. Central Zoo Authority
  2. National Biodiversity Authority
  3. Central Pollution Control Board
  4. National Green Tribunal

Answer: Central Zoo Authority

Explanation: The Central Zoo Authority is a statutory body under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.

3. Under the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, prior approval of the Central Government is required for:

  1. Diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes
  2. Assignment of forest land to any private person
  3. Both (a) and (b)
  4. None of the above

Answer: Both (a) and (b)

Explanation: Section 2 of the Act requires central approval for both diversion and assignment of forest land.

4. The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 has jurisdiction over civil cases involving:

  1. Substantial questions relating to environment
  2. Compensation for environmental damage
  3. Both (a) and (b)
  4. Only (a)

Answer: Both (a) and (b)

Explanation: NGT handles substantial environmental questions and can award compensation and relief.

5. Under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010, the limitation period for filing an application for seeking relief or compensation is:

  1. 6 months
  2. 1 year
  3. 2 years
  4. 3 years

Answer: 6 months

Explanation: Section 14 prescribes 6 months (extendable up to 60 days upon showing sufficient cause).

6. The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 recognizes:

  1. Individual forest rights only
  2. Community forest rights only
  3. Both individual and community forest rights
  4. Rights to mining in forests

Answer: Both individual and community forest rights

Explanation: The Act recognizes both individual rights over forest land (Section 3(1)) and community rights (Section 3(2)).

7. Under the Environment Impact Assessment (EIA) Notification, 2006, projects are classified into:

  1. Category A and Category B
  2. Category I, II, III
  3. Red, Orange, Green categories
  4. Major and Minor projects

Answer: Category A and Category B

Explanation: EIA 2006 classifies projects as Category A (central clearance) and Category B (state clearance).

8. Under the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, which of the following is a function of the Central Pollution Control Board?

  1. Advise the Central Government on air pollution matters
  2. Establish ambient air quality standards
  3. Both (a) and (b)
  4. Only (a)

Answer: Both (a) and (b)

Explanation: CPCB advises the government and sets air quality standards under the Act.

9. Consider the following statements regarding the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification, 2019: 1. It classifies coastal areas into four categories. 2. CRZ-I includes ecologically sensitive areas. Which of the above statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: CRZ 2019 classifies coastal areas into CRZ-I, II, III, IV, with CRZ-I being ecologically sensitive.

10. The rule of 'absolute liability' for hazardous substances was established by the Supreme Court in which case?

  1. M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak case)
  2. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India
  3. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India
  4. Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of U.P.

Answer: M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak case)

Explanation: The Oleum Gas Leak case (1987) set the precedent for absolute liability for hazardous industries.

11. Consider the following statements regarding the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972: 1. It provides for the constitution of the National Board for Wildlife. 2. Hunting of wild animals is completely prohibited under all circumstances. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Statement 1 is correct; the Act establishes the National Board for Wildlife. Statement 2 is incorrect because hunting is permitted in certain cases, such as for vermin species or under special permits.

12. Under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, which of the following is the maximum period of imprisonment for non-compliance with its provisions?

  1. 2 years
  2. 5 years
  3. 7 years
  4. 10 years

Answer: 5 years

Explanation: Section 15 of the Act provides for imprisonment up to 5 years, or fine, or both, for violation of its provisions.

13. Consider the following statements about the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification, 2019: 1. CRZ-III areas are divided into two sub-categories: CRZ-IIIA and CRZ-IIIB. 2. No construction is allowed in CRZ-I areas except for projects of national security. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Statement 1 is correct: CRZ-III (rural areas) is subdivided into IIIA (population density >2161 per sq km) and IIIB (lower density). Statement 2 is incorrect because certain eco-tourism facilities are also allowed in CRZ-I areas.

14. The concept of 'Public Trust Doctrine' in Indian environmental law was significantly applied by the Supreme Court in which of the following cases?

  1. M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997)
  2. Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (1996)
  3. Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996)
  4. T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India (1997)

Answer: M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997)

Explanation: The Public Trust Doctrine was applied in M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997) holding that the state holds natural resources in trust for the public.

15. Under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, which of the following authorities is responsible for granting approval for accessing biological resources for commercial purposes?

  1. National Biodiversity Authority
  2. State Biodiversity Board
  3. Biodiversity Management Committee
  4. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change

Answer: National Biodiversity Authority

Explanation: Section 3 of the Act requires prior approval of the National Biodiversity Authority for obtaining biological resources for commercial use.

16. Consider the following statements regarding the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980: 1. It requires prior approval of the central government for diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes. 2. The Act applies to all forests irrespective of ownership. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Statement 1 is correct; Section 2 mandates central approval. Statement 2 is correct as the Supreme Court in the Godavarman case held that the Act covers all forests as per dictionary meaning.

17. Which of the following is NOT a function of the National Green Tribunal (NGT) under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010?

  1. Adjudicating disputes related to environmental clearance
  2. Awarding compensation for environmental damage
  3. Creating new environmental legislation
  4. Hearing appeals against orders of pollution control boards

Answer: Creating new environmental legislation

Explanation: The NGT adjudicates disputes, awards compensation, and hears appeals, but does not have legislative powers to create new laws.

18. Consider the following statements about the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) Notification, 2006: 1. Projects are categorized as Category A (central appraisal) and Category B (state appraisal). 2. Public hearing is mandatory for all Category A projects. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Statement 1 is correct; Category A projects require central clearance, Category B state clearance. Statement 2 is incorrect; public hearing is required for both categories unless exempted under certain conditions.

19. The concept of 'Sustainable Development' was explicitly incorporated into Indian environmental jurisprudence through which of the following principles?

  1. Polluter Pays Principle
  2. Precautionary Principle
  3. Inter-Generational Equity
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: The Supreme Court in Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum case (1996) held that sustainable development includes Polluter Pays, Precautionary Principle, and Inter-Generational Equity.

20. Under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, which body is constituted at the central level for prevention and control of water pollution?

  1. Central Pollution Control Board
  2. National Green Tribunal
  3. Ministry of Water Resources
  4. Central Water Commission

Answer: Central Pollution Control Board

Explanation: The Act establishes the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) at the national level with powers to advise and coordinate state boards.

21. Consider the following statements regarding the Environment Protection Act (EPA), 1986: 1. It is an umbrella legislation that empowers the central government to take measures to protect and improve the environment. 2. It was enacted in response to the Bhopal Gas Tragedy. 3. It provides for the establishment of the National Green Tribunal. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 2 only

Explanation: The EPA, 1986 was enacted after the Bhopal Gas Tragedy. It does not establish the NGT; the NGT was created by the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.

22. Under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, which of the following is/are included in Schedule I? 1. Blackbuck 2. Indian Wild Ass 3. Wild Dog Select the correct answer using the code below:

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: All three—Blackbuck, Indian Wild Ass, and Wild Dog (Dhole)—are listed under Schedule I of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, which provides absolute protection.

23. The 'Precautionary Principle' and 'Polluter Pays Principle' are recognized as part of environmental law in India primarily through:

  1. The Environment Protection Act, 1986
  2. The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010
  3. Judicial interpretations by the Supreme Court
  4. The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

Answer: Judicial interpretations by the Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court in several judgments, notably Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India, embedded these principles into Indian environmental jurisprudence.

24. Consider the following statements about the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification, 2019: 1. It classifies the coastal area into four zones. 2. CRZ-III areas are rural coastal areas with high population density. 3. No construction is allowed in CRZ-I areas except for projects related to defence or security. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 1 and 3 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: CRZ-I includes ecologically sensitive areas where only limited activities are permitted. CRZ-III is for relatively undisturbed rural areas, not high density. Statement 1 is correct; statement 2 is incorrect.

25. Which of the following is NOT a function of the National Green Tribunal (NGT)?

  1. To handle disputes arising from the implementation of the Forest Conservation Act
  2. To impose penalties on violators of environmental laws
  3. To recommend amendments to environmental legislations to the government
  4. To provide relief and compensation to victims of environmental damage

Answer: To recommend amendments to environmental legislations to the government

Explanation: The NGT is a quasi-judicial body for dispute resolution and compensation, but it does not have the power to recommend legislative amendments; that is the role of the government or Parliament.
