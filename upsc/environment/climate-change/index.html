Climate Change MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Climate Change questions to revise the UPSC Environment syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Consider the following statements regarding greenhouse gases: 1. Methane has a higher global warming potential than carbon dioxide over a 100-year period. 2. Carbon dioxide remains in the atmosphere for a longer duration than methane. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Methane has a GWP ~28 times that of CO2 over 100 years, but its atmospheric lifetime is about 12 years, much shorter than CO2's centuries-long persistence.

2. Which of the following is a primary outcome of the Paris Agreement? 1. Legally binding emission reduction targets for all parties. 2. Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) submitted every five years. 3. A common carbon tax applied uniformly across countries. Select the correct answer using the code given below:

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: NDCs are the core mechanism, but emission targets are not legally binding; no common carbon tax exists under the Paris Agreement.

3. Consider the following statements about black carbon: 1. It is a short-lived climate pollutant. 2. It contributes to global warming by absorbing sunlight. 3. It is the primary component of soot from incomplete combustion. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 3 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 2 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: Black carbon is a short-lived climate forcer, absorbs solar radiation, and is emitted from incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and biomass.

4. Ocean acidification is primarily caused by: 1. Increased absorption of carbon dioxide by seawater. 2. Release of sulfur dioxide from volcanic eruptions. 3. Decomposition of marine organic matter. Select the correct answer using the code given below:

  1. 1 only
  2. 1 and 2 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: The primary cause is the absorption of excess atmospheric CO2, which reacts with seawater to form carbonic acid, lowering pH.

5. Which of the following are considered major carbon sinks? 1. Tropical rainforests. 2. Oceans. 3. Peatlands. Select the correct answer using the code given below:

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: Tropical rainforests, oceans, and peatlands all sequester significant amounts of carbon, making them vital carbon sinks.

6. Consider the following statements about climate feedback loops: 1. Ice-albedo feedback is a positive feedback loop. 2. Water vapor feedback amplifies initial warming. 3. The carbon cycle feedback can release more CO2 from soils as temperatures rise. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: All three are positive feedbacks: ice-albedo reduces reflectivity, water vapor is a strong greenhouse gas, and warming soils release stored carbon.

7. The Kyoto Protocol differs from the Paris Agreement primarily because: 1. The Kyoto Protocol imposed legally binding emission reduction targets on developed countries only. 2. The Paris Agreement applies to all nations but with voluntary contributions. 3. The Kyoto Protocol included all greenhouse gases, while the Paris Agreement only covers CO2. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 2 only

Explanation: Kyoto had binding targets for Annex I parties; Paris uses voluntary NDCs for all parties, but covers multiple GHGs, not just CO2.

8. Consider the following statements regarding carbon credits: 1. One carbon credit typically represents one tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent reduced or removed. 2. They can be traded in voluntary or compliance carbon markets. 3. The Clean Development Mechanism under the Kyoto Protocol generated carbon credits. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: All statements are correct: carbon credits represent 1 tCO2e, are traded in markets, and CDM was a Kyoto mechanism for crediting emission reductions.

9. Which of the following best describes 'climate adaptation'? 1. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions to limit future warming. 2. Adjusting systems and practices to cope with the impacts of climate change. 3. Geoengineering techniques to reflect sunlight. Select the correct answer using the code given below:

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1 and 3 only

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: Adaptation involves adjustments to actual or expected climate effects, while mitigation (option 1) and geoengineering (option 3) are different approaches.

10. According to the IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report, which of the following statements is/are correct? 1. Global surface temperature was 1.1°C higher in 2011–2020 than 1850–1900. 2. Human-caused emissions are extremely likely to be the main driver of observed warming. 3. The rate of sea-level rise has accelerated over recent decades. Select the correct answer using the code given below:

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: IPCC AR6 states warming of about 1.1°C, extremely likely human influence, and accelerating sea-level rise; all three are correct.

11. Consider the following statements regarding 'Carbon Footprint': 1. It measures the total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly or indirectly by an individual/organization. 2. Carbon dioxide is the only gas considered in calculating carbon footprint. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Carbon footprint includes all major greenhouse gases, not just CO2, expressed as CO2 equivalent.

12. Which of the following gases is NOT a greenhouse gas?

  1. Methane
  2. Nitrous oxide
  3. Oxygen
  4. Water vapor

Answer: Oxygen

Explanation: Oxygen is not a greenhouse gas as it does not absorb infrared radiation.

13. Consider the following statements regarding 'Global Warming Potential' (GWP): 1. It compares the heat-trapping ability of a greenhouse gas relative to CO2 over a specific time horizon. 2. Methane has a higher GWP than CO2 over 100 years. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: GWP is a relative measure; methane's GWP is about 28 times that of CO2 over 100 years.

14. The term 'Ocean Acidification' refers to:

  1. Increase in pH of ocean water
  2. Decrease in pH due to absorption of CO2
  3. Reduction in salinity
  4. Increase in temperature

Answer: Decrease in pH due to absorption of CO2

Explanation: Ocean absorbs excess atmospheric CO2 forming carbonic acid, lowering pH (more acidic).

15. Which of the following is a source of 'Black Carbon'?

  1. Coal-fired power plants
  2. Diesel engines
  3. Biomass burning
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Black carbon is produced by incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and biomass, e.g., diesel, coal, wood.

16. Consider the following statements regarding 'Carbon Sequestration': 1. It is the process of capturing and storing atmospheric CO2. 2. Geological sequestration involves injecting CO2 into deep underground rock formations. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Carbon sequestration includes both biological (e.g., forests) and geological methods.

17. The 'Paris Agreement' under UNFCCC aims to limit global temperature rise to:

  1. 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels
  2. 2°C
  3. Both (A) and (B) with emphasis on 1.5°C
  4. 3°C

Answer: Both (A) and (B) with emphasis on 1.5°C

Explanation: Paris Agreement aims to hold warming to well below 2°C and pursue efforts to limit to 1.5°C.

18. Which of the following is a 'Climate Forcing Agent' with a warming effect?

  1. Sulfate aerosols
  2. Black carbon
  3. Volcanic ash
  4. Cloud condensation nuclei

Answer: Black carbon

Explanation: Black carbon absorbs solar radiation and warms the atmosphere; sulfates and volcanic ash have cooling effects.

19. Consider the following statements regarding 'Albedo Effect': 1. It is the measure of reflectivity of a surface. 2. Melting of Arctic sea ice decreases albedo leading to further warming. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Ice has high albedo; melting exposes darker ocean which absorbs more heat, amplifying warming.

20. Which of the following international agreements is directly related to climate change?

  1. Montreal Protocol
  2. Kyoto Protocol
  3. Convention on Biological Diversity
  4. Basel Convention

Answer: Kyoto Protocol

Explanation: Kyoto Protocol is a treaty under UNFCCC that sets emission reduction targets.

21. Consider the following statements regarding climate change mitigation: 1. Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) involves capturing CO2 from point sources and storing it underground. 2. Afforestation is considered a mitigation strategy because it enhances carbon sequestration. 3. Solar Radiation Management (SRM) reduces atmospheric CO2 concentration directly. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 2 only

Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct; SRM aims to reflect sunlight, not reduce CO2, so statement 3 is incorrect.

22. With reference to the concept of 'carbon budget', consider the following statements: 1. It is the cumulative amount of CO2 emissions allowed to keep global temperature rise below a specified limit. 2. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) estimates that the remaining carbon budget for 1.5°C is larger than that for 2°C. 3. Carbon budget accounts for all greenhouse gases in CO2-equivalent terms. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 1 and 2 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1 and 3 only

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Statement 1 is correct; the budget for 1.5°C is smaller than for 2°C, so statement 2 is false. Carbon budget typically refers to CO2, not all GHGs, hence statement 3 is incorrect.

23. Which of the following is the primary driver of the recent global sea-level rise?

  1. Increased precipitation over oceans
  2. Thermal expansion of seawater
  3. Melting of sea ice
  4. Glacial isostatic adjustment

Answer: Thermal expansion of seawater

Explanation: Thermal expansion of seawater and meltwater from land-based ice are the main contributors; sea ice melt does not raise sea level (displacement).

24. Consider the following statements regarding the Paris Agreement (2015): 1. It aims to hold the increase in global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. 2. Each Party is legally bound to achieve its Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs). 3. Developed countries are required to provide climate finance to developing countries. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 3 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 2 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: Statement 1 and 3 are correct. NDCs are not legally binding (statement 2 is false).

25. The term 'climate sensitivity' refers to:

  1. The rate at which global temperatures respond to changes in solar radiation
  2. The equilibrium temperature change resulting from a doubling of atmospheric CO2 concentration
  3. The time taken for global temperature to stabilize after a pulse emission of CO2
  4. The ratio of warming in the Arctic to global average warming

Answer: The equilibrium temperature change resulting from a doubling of atmospheric CO2 concentration

Explanation: Climate sensitivity is defined as the long-term temperature change in response to a doubling of CO2 concentration.
