Pollution MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Pollution questions to revise the UPSC Environment syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Consider the following statements regarding Particulate Matter (PM): 1. PM10 particles are smaller than PM2.5. 2. PM2.5 can penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: PM2.5 are finer particles (<2.5 µm) that can enter the bloodstream; PM10 are larger (2.5-10 µm). Statement 1 is false.

2. Which of the following pollutants is primarily responsible for the formation of photochemical smog?

  1. Carbon monoxide
  2. Nitrogen oxides
  3. Sulfur dioxide
  4. Particulate matter

Answer: Nitrogen oxides

Explanation: Photochemical smog is formed by the reaction of nitrogen oxides (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in sunlight.

3. Consider the following statements about Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD): 1. High BOD indicates a high level of dissolved oxygen in water. 2. BOD is a measure of organic pollution in water bodies. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: High BOD indicates low dissolved oxygen as microbes consume oxygen to decompose organic matter. Statement 2 is correct.

4. Which of the following gases is not a primary pollutant but a secondary pollutant formed in the atmosphere?

  1. Sulfur dioxide
  2. Nitrogen dioxide
  3. Ozone
  4. Carbon monoxide

Answer: Ozone

Explanation: Ozone is a secondary pollutant formed from photochemical reactions of NOx and VOCs; others are directly emitted.

5. Consider the following statements regarding Eutrophication: 1. It is caused by enrichment of water bodies with nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus. 2. It leads to an increase in dissolved oxygen levels. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Eutrophication causes algal blooms, and subsequent decomposition depletes dissolved oxygen, not increase.

6. Which of the following is a major source of indoor air pollution in rural India?

  1. Use of biomass fuels for cooking
  2. Industrial emissions
  3. Vehicular exhaust
  4. Burning of crop residue

Answer: Use of biomass fuels for cooking

Explanation: Biomass fuels (wood, dung) burned in traditional stoves release high levels of indoor air pollutants.

7. Consider the following pairs: Pollutant - Source 1. Lead - Paint 2. Mercury - Coal combustion 3. Arsenic - Pesticides How many of the above pairs are correctly matched?

  1. Only one
  2. Only two
  3. All three
  4. None

Answer: All three

Explanation: Lead from old paints, mercury from coal burning, and arsenic from some pesticides are all well-established sources.

8. Which of the following is the most significant greenhouse gas emitted from landfills?

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Methane
  3. Nitrous oxide
  4. Ozone

Answer: Methane

Explanation: Anaerobic decomposition of organic waste in landfills generates methane, a potent greenhouse gas.

9. Consider the following statements about Acid Rain: 1. It is primarily caused by emissions of sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides. 2. The pH of acid rain is below 5.6. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Normal rainwater is slightly acidic (~pH 5.6) due to CO2; acid rain has lower pH from SOx and NOx.

10. Which of the following pollutants is responsible for the 'Blue Baby Syndrome'?

  1. Lead
  2. Arsenic
  3. Nitrate
  4. Fluoride

Answer: Nitrate

Explanation: Nitrate in drinking water causes methemoglobinemia (blue baby syndrome) by impairing oxygen transport in infants.

11. Consider the following statements regarding the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP): 1. It aims to reduce PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations by 40% by 2026 from 2017 levels. 2. It covers 132 non-attainment cities across India. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: NCAP targets 20-30% reduction by 2024, not 40% by 2026, and covers 132 cities.

12. Which of the following is a primary air pollutant?

  1. Ozone
  2. Peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN)
  3. Sulphur dioxide
  4. Formaldehyde

Answer: Sulphur dioxide

Explanation: Primary pollutants are directly emitted; SO₂ is emitted from combustion. Ozone and PAN are secondary.

13. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) is a measure of:

  1. Dissolved oxygen consumption by aquatic plants
  2. Oxygen required to decompose organic matter in water
  3. Total amount of dissolved oxygen in water
  4. Oxygen released during photosynthesis

Answer: Oxygen required to decompose organic matter in water

Explanation: BOD measures oxygen needed by microorganisms to decompose organic pollutants in water.

14. Which of the following organisms is commonly used as a bioindicator of air pollution?

  1. Earthworm
  2. Lichens
  3. Daphnia
  4. E. coli

Answer: Lichens

Explanation: Lichens are sensitive to SO₂ and other air pollutants, indicating air quality.

15. Consider the following pollutants: 1. Fly ash 2. Sulphur dioxide 3. Carbon monoxide Which of these are released from coal‑based thermal power plants?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1, 2 and 3

Explanation: Thermal power plants emit fly ash, SO₂, and CO (incomplete combustion).

16. The 'Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016' in India introduced the concept of:

  1. Ban on single‑use plastics nationwide
  2. Extended Producer Responsibility
  3. Waste‑to‑energy plants
  4. Plastic recycling targets of 50%

Answer: Extended Producer Responsibility

Explanation: The rules mandate Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for plastic waste management.

17. Which of the following is correctly matched with its associated health effect? 1. Lead – Minamata disease 2. Mercury – Itai‑itai disease 3. Cadmium – Neurological damage Select the correct answer using the codes below:

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 3 only
  4. None of the above

Answer: None of the above

Explanation: Minamata is mercury, Itai‑itai is cadmium, lead causes neurological damage. All matches are incorrect.

18. Consider the following statements about Black Carbon: 1. It is a short‑lived climate pollutant. 2. It contributes to global warming by absorbing sunlight. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Black Carbon is a short‑lived climate pollutant that absorbs solar radiation, warming the atmosphere.

19. Which of the following is a secondary pollutant?

  1. Carbon monoxide
  2. Nitrogen dioxide
  3. Tropospheric ozone
  4. Sulphur dioxide

Answer: Tropospheric ozone

Explanation: Tropospheric ozone is formed by photochemical reactions of NOx and VOCs; CO, NO₂, SO₂ are primary.

20. Eutrophication of water bodies is primarily caused by an excess of which nutrients?

  1. Nitrogen and Phosphorus
  2. Carbon and Oxygen
  3. Potassium and Calcium
  4. Iron and Manganese

Answer: Nitrogen and Phosphorus

Explanation: Excess nitrogen and phosphorus from fertilizers and sewage cause algal blooms and eutrophication.

21. Consider the following statements regarding National Clean Air Programme (NCAP): 1. It aims to reduce PM2.5 and PM10 concentration by 20-30% by 2024. 2. It covers both urban and rural areas. 3. It is a central sector scheme. Which of the statements given above are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: NCAP aims to reduce PM2.5 and PM10 by 20-30% by 2024 and is a central sector scheme, but it currently focuses only on non-attainment cities, not rural areas.

22. With reference to 'Bhopal Gas Tragedy', which of the following pollutants was primarily responsible for the disaster?

  1. Methyl isocyanate
  2. Phosgene
  3. Hydrogen cyanide
  4. Carbon monoxide

Answer: Methyl isocyanate

Explanation: The disaster involved leakage of methyl isocyanate (MIC) gas from the Union Carbide plant, leading to thousands of deaths.

23. Consider the following statements about 'Smog': 1. Photochemical smog is a mixture of ozone, PAN and aldehydes. 2. London smog is caused by high concentration of sulphur dioxide and particulate matter. 3. Both types of smog occur in cold weather conditions. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 2 only
  4. 2 and 3 only

Answer: 1 and 2 only

Explanation: Photochemical smog contains ozone, PAN, aldehydes; London smog involves SO2 and particulates. London smog occurs in cold, damp conditions, but photochemical smog requires sunlight and occurs in warmer conditions.

24. Which of the following is a major 'Stockholm Convention' Persistent Organic Pollutant (POP) banned globally for its toxicity and persistence?

  1. DDT
  2. Bisphenol A
  3. Phthalates
  4. Polyethylene

Answer: DDT

Explanation: DDT is listed under the Stockholm Convention as a POP; other options are not classic POPs covered by the convention.

25. Consider the following statements regarding 'Biochemical Oxygen Demand' (BOD): 1. High BOD indicates clean water. 2. BOD measures the amount of oxygen required by aerobic microorganisms to decompose organic matter. 3. BOD is directly related to the level of pollution in water bodies. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1 and 3 only

Answer: 2 and 3 only

Explanation: BOD measures oxygen needed for decomposition; high BOD indicates high organic pollution, not clean water. Statements 2 and 3 are correct.
