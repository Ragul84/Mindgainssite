Polity MCQs with answers

Use these 5 worked Polity questions to revise the Railway Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 25 questions.

1. The Indian Constitution was adopted on:

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 November 1949
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 1 January 1949

Answer: 26 November 1949

Explanation: The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day/Samvidhan Divas). It came into force on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months, 18 days.

2. The Constituent Assembly was chaired by:

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Dr. Rajendra Prasad
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Explanation: Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President (permanent chairman) of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the chairman of the Drafting Committee and is called the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

3. India borrowed the concept of 'Fundamental Rights' from:

  1. UK
  2. USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)
  3. USSR
  4. Australia

Answer: USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)

Explanation: India borrowed the concept of Fundamental Rights from the US Constitution (First 10 Amendments — Bill of Rights). The idea of judicial review was also borrowed from the USA.

4. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution begins with:

  1. 'We, the Parliament of India'
  2. 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'
  3. 'India is a democratic country'
  4. 'In the name of God'

Answer: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'

Explanation: The Preamble begins: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic and to secure to all its citizens JUSTICE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY, and FRATERNITY...'

5. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
  2. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  3. 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
  4. 73rd Amendment Act, 1992

Answer: 42nd Amendment Act, 1976

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) during Emergency (PM Indira Gandhi) added 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble, making India a 'Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic'.
