Railway Polity practice

Polity MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Polity questions to revise the Railway Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 25 questions.

1. The Indian Constitution was adopted on:

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 November 1949
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 1 January 1949

Answer: 26 November 1949

Explanation: The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day/Samvidhan Divas). It came into force on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months, 18 days.

2. The Constituent Assembly was chaired by:

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Dr. Rajendra Prasad
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Explanation: Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President (permanent chairman) of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the chairman of the Drafting Committee and is called the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

3. India borrowed the concept of 'Fundamental Rights' from:

  1. UK
  2. USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)
  3. USSR
  4. Australia

Answer: USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)

Explanation: India borrowed the concept of Fundamental Rights from the US Constitution (First 10 Amendments — Bill of Rights). The idea of judicial review was also borrowed from the USA.

4. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution begins with:

  1. 'We, the Parliament of India'
  2. 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'
  3. 'India is a democratic country'
  4. 'In the name of God'

Answer: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'

Explanation: The Preamble begins: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic and to secure to all its citizens JUSTICE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY, and FRATERNITY...'

5. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
  2. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  3. 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
  4. 73rd Amendment Act, 1992

Answer: 42nd Amendment Act, 1976

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) during Emergency (PM Indira Gandhi) added 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble, making India a 'Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic'.

6. Fundamental Rights are enshrined in:

  1. Part II (Articles 5–11)
  2. Part III (Articles 12–35)
  3. Part IV (Articles 36–51)
  4. Part I (Articles 1–4)

Answer: Part III (Articles 12–35)

Explanation: Fundamental Rights are in Part III (Articles 12–35) of the Indian Constitution. They are justiciable (enforceable by courts). Originally 7, now 6 (Right to Property removed by 44th Amendment 1978, made a legal right under Article 300A).

7. The six Fundamental Rights are:

  1. Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies
  2. Right to Property, Right to Education, Right to Equality, Right to Vote, Right to Work, Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Life, Right to Liberty, Right to Property, Right to Religion, Right to Speech, Right to Assembly
  4. Right to Equality, Right to Work, Right to Education, Right to Food, Right to Health, Right to Housing

Answer: Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies

Explanation: The 6 Fundamental Rights: 1) Right to Equality (Art 14-18), 2) Right to Freedom (Art 19-22), 3) Right against Exploitation (Art 23-24), 4) Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25-28), 5) Cultural and Educational Rights (Art 29-30), 6) Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32).

8. Article 14 of the Indian Constitution guarantees:

  1. Right to Freedom of Speech
  2. Right to Equality before law and Equal protection of laws
  3. Right to Life and Personal Liberty
  4. Right against Exploitation

Answer: Right to Equality before law and Equal protection of laws

Explanation: Article 14: 'The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.' It is available to citizens AND non-citizens.

9. Untouchability is abolished and its practice is forbidden under:

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 17
  3. Article 15
  4. Article 16

Answer: Article 17

Explanation: Article 17: 'Untouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability shall be an offence punishable by law.' The Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955 penalises untouchability.

10. Article 21 guarantees:

  1. Freedom of speech
  2. Protection of life and personal liberty — 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law'
  3. Right to property
  4. Right to education

Answer: Protection of life and personal liberty — 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law'

Explanation: Article 21 is one of the most fundamental rights. Through judicial interpretation, it now covers: right to privacy, right to livelihood, right to health, right to clean environment, right to speedy trial, right to dignity, etc. Article 21A guarantees free education for children 6-14 years.

11. The Right to Education (Article 21A) was added by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 86th Amendment Act, 2002
  4. 93rd Amendment

Answer: 86th Amendment Act, 2002

Explanation: The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act (2002) inserted Article 21A: 'The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years.' The Right to Education Act 2009 gives effect to this.

12. Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are in:

  1. Part III (Articles 12-35)
  2. Part IV (Articles 36-51)
  3. Part V
  4. Part VI

Answer: Part IV (Articles 36-51)

Explanation: DPSPs are in Part IV (Articles 36–51). They are non-justiciable (cannot be enforced by court), but they are 'fundamental to governance.' Inspired by Irish Constitution and the UN's Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

13. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 (Part IVA, Article 51A)
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 73rd Amendment
  4. 86th Amendment

Answer: 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 (Part IVA, Article 51A)

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment Act (1976) added Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A) with 10 duties based on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. The 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th duty — to provide opportunities for education to children aged 6-14.

14. How many Fundamental Duties are there currently?

  1. 10
  2. 11
  3. 12
  4. 8

Answer: 11

Explanation: There are currently 11 Fundamental Duties under Article 51A. Originally 10 were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). The 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th duty: for parents/guardians to provide opportunities for education to children aged 6-14.

15. The Indian Parliament consists of:

  1. Only Lok Sabha
  2. Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha
  3. President, Lok Sabha, and Rajya Sabha
  4. Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, and Supreme Court

Answer: President, Lok Sabha, and Rajya Sabha

Explanation: The Indian Parliament consists of the President + Lok Sabha (House of the People) + Rajya Sabha (Council of States). Article 79. The President is an integral part of Parliament (though not a member of either House).

16. The term of the Lok Sabha is:

  1. 4 years
  2. 5 years (can be extended during National Emergency)
  3. 6 years
  4. 3 years

Answer: 5 years (can be extended during National Emergency)

Explanation: The normal term of the Lok Sabha is 5 years from the date of its first sitting after a general election (Article 83). During a National Emergency (Article 352), Parliament can extend its term by 1 year at a time by law.

17. The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body because:

  1. Its members serve for life
  2. It is never dissolved — only 1/3 of its members retire every 2 years
  3. It has more powers than Lok Sabha
  4. Its members are elected for 10 years

Answer: It is never dissolved — only 1/3 of its members retire every 2 years

Explanation: Rajya Sabha is a permanent body (Article 83) — it is never dissolved. Each member serves a 6-year term, and 1/3 of its members retire every 2 years. Currently has 245 members (238 elected + 12 nominated by President for expertise in arts, science, literature, social service).

18. A Money Bill can only be introduced in:

  1. Rajya Sabha
  2. Lok Sabha (Article 110)
  3. Either House
  4. Joint sitting of both Houses

Answer: Lok Sabha (Article 110)

Explanation: A Money Bill (Article 110) can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha. After Lok Sabha passes it, it is sent to Rajya Sabha which can only make recommendations (not amend/reject). If no action in 14 days, it is deemed passed. Lok Sabha has final say on Money Bills.

19. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is elected by:

  1. President of India
  2. Members of Lok Sabha from among themselves
  3. Members of both Houses
  4. Prime Minister

Answer: Members of Lok Sabha from among themselves

Explanation: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is elected by the members of the Lok Sabha from among themselves (Article 93). The Speaker maintains order, presides over proceedings, and is the guardian of the powers and privileges of the House.

20. The Prime Minister is appointed by:

  1. Elected by Lok Sabha members
  2. President of India — who appoints the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha
  3. Rajya Sabha
  4. Electoral College

Answer: President of India — who appoints the leader of the majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha

Explanation: The President appoints the Prime Minister (Article 75). Conventionally, the President appoints the leader of the party/coalition that commands majority in the Lok Sabha. The PM is the head of the Council of Ministers and real executive head.

21. The Governor of a state is appointed by:

  1. State Legislature
  2. Chief Minister of the state
  3. President of India
  4. Prime Minister

Answer: President of India

Explanation: The Governor is appointed by the President of India (Article 155). The Governor is the constitutional head of the state (like President at national level) and acts as the agent/representative of the Centre in the state.

22. A State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) member must be at least:

  1. 21 years old
  2. 25 years old
  3. 30 years old
  4. 18 years old

Answer: 25 years old

Explanation: To be a member of a State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha), a person must be at least 25 years old (Article 173). To vote, the minimum age is 18 years (reduced from 21 by the 61st Amendment, 1988).

23. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act (1992) related to:

  1. Parliament elections
  2. Panchayati Raj institutions — gave constitutional status to 3-tier system
  3. Urban local bodies
  4. Financial relations between states and centre

Answer: Panchayati Raj institutions — gave constitutional status to 3-tier system

Explanation: 73rd Amendment (1992) added Part IX (Articles 243-243O) and 11th Schedule to the Constitution, giving constitutional status to Panchayati Raj. Mandated 3-tier system (Village, Intermediate, District Panchayats), elections, reservation for SC/ST/women (33%), 5-year term.

24. The minimum age for voting in India is:

  1. 21 years
  2. 18 years (changed from 21 by 61st Amendment, 1988)
  3. 20 years
  4. 25 years

Answer: 18 years (changed from 21 by 61st Amendment, 1988)

Explanation: The voting age was reduced from 21 to 18 years by the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act (1988). This was done to ensure greater youth participation in democracy. India has ~950 million eligible voters (2024).

25. UPSC (Union Public Service Commission) conducts examinations for:

  1. State government jobs
  2. All India Services and Central Services Group A & B (civil services exam, IAS, IPS, IFS, etc.)
  3. Only IAS examination
  4. Defence recruitment only

Answer: All India Services and Central Services Group A & B (civil services exam, IAS, IPS, IFS, etc.)

Explanation: UPSC (Article 315) conducts Civil Services Examination (IAS, IPS, IFS), combined defence services, engineering services, NDA, etc. for Group A and B Central Services and All India Services. State PSCs conduct state service exams.
