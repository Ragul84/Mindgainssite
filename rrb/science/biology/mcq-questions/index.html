Biology MCQs with answers

Use these 8 worked Biology questions to revise the Railway Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 8 questions.

1. Which is the largest organ in the human body?

  1. Heart
  2. Liver
  3. Skin
  4. Brain

Answer: Skin

Explanation: The skin is the largest organ of the human body. It protects the body, regulates temperature, and provides sensation.

2. The basic unit of life is the:

  1. Tissue
  2. Organ
  3. Cell
  4. Atom

Answer: Cell

Explanation: The cell is the basic structural, functional, and biological unit of all living organisms.

3. The powerhouse of the cell is:

  1. Nucleus
  2. Mitochondria
  3. Ribosome
  4. Chloroplast

Answer: Mitochondria

Explanation: Mitochondria are called the 'powerhouse of the cell' because they produce ATP (energy) through cellular respiration.

4. Which blood group is called the 'universal donor'?

  1. A+
  2. B+
  3. O-
  4. AB+

Answer: O-

Explanation: O- (O negative) is the universal donor for red blood cells as it can be given to patients of any blood group.

5. Which blood group is the 'universal recipient'?

  1. A+
  2. AB+
  3. O+
  4. B+

Answer: AB+

Explanation: AB+ (AB positive) is the universal recipient as people with this blood group can receive blood from any donor group.

6. Which hormone is produced by the pancreas to control blood sugar?

  1. Adrenaline
  2. Thyroxine
  3. Insulin
  4. Oestrogen

Answer: Insulin

Explanation: Insulin is produced by the beta cells of the pancreatic islets. It regulates blood glucose by allowing cells to take up glucose. Deficiency of insulin causes diabetes.

7. Photosynthesis takes place in which organelle?

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Nucleus
  3. Chloroplast
  4. Ribosome

Answer: Chloroplast

Explanation: Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Only plant cells and some protists have chloroplasts.

8. The number of chromosomes in a normal human body cell is:

  1. 23
  2. 44
  3. 46
  4. 48

Answer: 46

Explanation: Normal human body cells have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). Sperm and egg cells have 23 chromosomes each (haploid).
