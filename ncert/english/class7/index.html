Class7 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class7 questions to revise the NCERT English syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Choose the correct passive voice for the sentence: 'The gardener waters the plants daily.'

  1. The plants are watered daily by the gardener.
  2. The plants were watered daily by the gardener.
  3. The plants is watered daily by the gardener.
  4. The gardener is watering the plants daily.

Answer: The plants are watered daily by the gardener.

Explanation: In active voice, the structure is Subject + Verb + Object. In passive voice, it becomes Object + 'be' verb (according to tense) + Past Participle of Verb + by + Subject. Here, 'waters' is in simple present tense, so 'are watered' is used. 'The plants' is the object, and 'the gardener' is the subject.

2. Convert the following into indirect speech: She said, 'I am going to the market.'

  1. She said that she was going to the market.
  2. She said that I was going to the market.
  3. She said that she is going to the market.
  4. She said that I am going to the market.

Answer: She said that she was going to the market.

Explanation: When converting direct speech to indirect speech, the tense usually shifts backward (present continuous to past continuous), and pronouns change. 'I' becomes 'she' to match the speaker, and 'am going' becomes 'was going'.

3. Which of the following sentences shows correct subject-verb agreement?

  1. The team play well together.
  2. Each of the students have a book.
  3. Neither Rohan nor his friends is coming.
  4. The dog and the cat are playing.

Answer: The dog and the cat are playing.

Explanation: Subject-verb agreement means the verb must agree with its subject in number. 'The dog and the cat' is a compound subject (plural), so the plural verb 'are' is correct. In option A, 'team' is singular, so 'plays' should be used. In option B, 'Each' is singular, so 'has' should be used. In option C, 'Neither...nor' with a plural subject closer to the verb takes a plural verb, so 'are coming' would be correct if 'friends' was the closer subject, but 'is coming' is incorrect for 'friends'.

4. Choose the most appropriate modal verb to complete the sentence: 'You _______ submit your homework by Friday.'

  1. might
  2. could
  3. must
  4. may

Answer: must

Explanation: 'Must' is used to express strong obligation or necessity. Submitting homework by a deadline is usually a strong requirement. 'Might', 'could', and 'may' express possibility or permission, which are not suitable here.

5. Identify the tense of the verb in the sentence: 'They have been studying for three hours.'

  1. Present Perfect
  2. Present Perfect Continuous
  3. Past Perfect
  4. Past Perfect Continuous

Answer: Present Perfect Continuous

Explanation: The structure 'have/has + been + verb-ing' indicates the Present Perfect Continuous tense. This tense is used to describe an action that started in the past and is still continuing in the present.

6. Fill in the blank with the correct preposition: 'The book is _______ the table.'

  1. in
  2. on
  3. at
  4. under

Answer: on

Explanation: 'On' is used to indicate that something is physically touching and supported by a surface. The book is typically placed 'on' the table.

7. What does the idiom 'to bite the bullet' mean?

  1. To eat something quickly
  2. To face a difficult situation with courage
  3. To argue with someone
  4. To make a quick decision

Answer: To face a difficult situation with courage

Explanation: The idiom 'to bite the bullet' means to endure a difficult or unpleasant situation with courage and without complaining, often because it is unavoidable.

8. Choose the synonym for the word 'ancient'.

  1. Modern
  2. Old
  3. New
  4. Recent

Answer: Old

Explanation: A synonym is a word that has the same or nearly the same meaning as another word. 'Ancient' means belonging to the very distant past, so 'old' is its closest synonym. 'Modern', 'new', and 'recent' are antonyms.

9. Choose the correct passive voice form of the sentence: 'The students are reading books.'

  1. Books are being read by the students.
  2. Books were read by the students.
  3. Books are read by the students.
  4. The students read books.

Answer: Books are being read by the students.

Explanation: The original sentence is in the present continuous tense (are reading). To change it to passive voice, we use 'is/are/am + being + past participle'. So, 'Books are being read by the students' is the correct passive form.

10. Choose the correct verb to complete the sentence: 'Neither John nor his friends ______ present at the party.'

  1. was
  2. were
  3. is
  4. has been

Answer: were

Explanation: When 'neither...nor' connects two subjects, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. In this case, 'his friends' is plural, so the plural verb 'were' should be used. Therefore, 'Neither John nor his friends were present at the party' is correct.

11. Which modal verb best completes the sentence: 'You ______ submit your homework by Friday.'

  1. must
  2. might
  3. could
  4. may

Answer: must

Explanation: The sentence implies a strong obligation or requirement. 'Must' is used to express strong necessity or obligation. 'Might', 'could', and 'may' express possibility or permission, which do not fit the context of submitting homework by a deadline. Therefore, 'You must submit your homework by Friday' is the most appropriate.

12. Identify the tense of the verb in the sentence: 'They have been playing cricket for two hours.'

  1. Present Perfect Continuous
  2. Present Perfect
  3. Past Perfect Continuous
  4. Future Perfect Continuous

Answer: Present Perfect Continuous

Explanation: The structure 'have/has + been + verb-ing' indicates the Present Perfect Continuous tense. This tense is used to describe an action that started in the past and is still continuing in the present. Therefore, 'Present Perfect Continuous' is the correct tense.

13. Choose the correct preposition to complete the sentence: 'The cat jumped ______ the table.'

  1. on
  2. over
  3. into
  4. at

Answer: over

Explanation: The preposition 'over' is used to indicate movement from one side to another, often implying going above something. 'On' suggests being positioned on the surface, 'into' suggests entering something, and 'at' indicates a specific location. Since the cat is jumping from one side to another, 'over' is the most suitable preposition. Therefore, 'The cat jumped over the table' is correct.

14. Convert the following into indirect speech: 'He said, 'I am going to school today.''

  1. He said that he was going to school that day.
  2. He said that he is going to school today.
  3. He said that I was going to school that day.
  4. He said that he went to school today.

Answer: He said that he was going to school that day.

Explanation: When converting direct speech to indirect speech, we make several changes: the reporting verb 'said' remains, the comma and quotation marks are removed and 'that' is often added. The pronoun 'I' changes to 'he' to match the subject 'He'. The present continuous tense 'am going' changes to past continuous 'was going'. Finally, time expressions like 'today' change to 'that day'. Therefore, 'He said that he was going to school that day' is the correct indirect speech.

15. Which sentence shows correct subject-verb agreement?

  1. The dog and the cat is playing in the garden.
  2. Each of the students have submitted their assignments.
  3. Neither John nor Mary are coming to the party.
  4. The team of players is ready for the match.

Answer: The team of players is ready for the match.

Explanation: Subject-verb agreement means the verb must agree with its subject in number. In option A, 'dog and cat' is a compound subject, so the verb should be 'are'. In option B, 'Each' is singular, so the verb should be 'has'. In option C, 'Neither John nor Mary' takes a singular verb when referring to two singular subjects joined by 'nor', so it should be 'is'. In option D, 'The team' is a collective noun acting as a single unit, so the singular verb 'is' is correct.

16. Identify the sentence with the correct use of tenses:

  1. She will go to the market yesterday.
  2. They are playing football since morning.
  3. He has finished his work an hour ago.
  4. I have been studying for two hours.

Answer: I have been studying for two hours.

Explanation: Option A is incorrect because 'will go' (future tense) cannot be used with 'yesterday' (past time). Option B is incorrect because 'since morning' indicates an action that started in the past and continues, requiring the present perfect continuous tense ('have been playing'), not present continuous. Option C is incorrect because 'an hour ago' indicates a completed action in the past, requiring simple past tense ('finished'), not present perfect ('has finished'). Option D is correct because 'have been studying' (present perfect continuous) correctly indicates an action that started in the past and is still continuing, which aligns with 'for two hours'.

17. Fill in the blank with the correct preposition: 'The cat jumped _______ the table.'

  1. in
  2. on
  3. over
  4. at

Answer: over

Explanation: The preposition 'over' is used to describe movement from one side to another, often implying going above something. When a cat jumps from the floor to the top of a table, it moves 'over' the space between them to land 'on' the table. 'In' implies being inside, 'on' implies being on the surface (but not the action of jumping to it), and 'at' indicates a specific point or location. Therefore, 'over' best describes the action of jumping.

18. Convert the following into indirect speech: He said, 'I am going to school.'

  1. He said that he was going to school.
  2. He said that I was going to school.
  3. He said that he is going to school.
  4. He said that he had gone to school.

Answer: He said that he was going to school.

Explanation: When converting direct speech to indirect speech, if the reporting verb (said) is in the past tense, the tense of the reported speech changes. 'I am going' (Present Continuous) changes to 'he was going' (Past Continuous). Also, the pronoun 'I' changes to 'he' to match the subject of the reporting verb. The conjunction 'that' is used to join the reporting clause and the reported clause.

19. You ______ submit your project by Friday, otherwise, it will not be accepted.

  1. must
  2. might
  3. could
  4. may

Answer: must

Explanation: The sentence implies a strong obligation or necessity, with a consequence if the action is not performed ('otherwise, it will not be accepted'). 'Must' is used to express strong obligation, necessity, or compulsion. 'Might', 'could', and 'may' express possibility or permission, which do not fit the context of a strict deadline.

20. Identify the tense of the verb in the sentence: 'She has been studying for three hours.'

  1. Present Perfect Continuous
  2. Present Perfect
  3. Past Perfect Continuous
  4. Future Perfect

Answer: Present Perfect Continuous

Explanation: The structure 'has/have + been + verb-ing' indicates the Present Perfect Continuous tense. This tense is used to describe an action that started in the past and is still continuing in the present, often with a duration mentioned (e.g., 'for three hours').

21. The cat jumped ______ the table to catch the mouse.

  1. onto
  2. on
  3. in
  4. at

Answer: onto

Explanation: 'Onto' is used to show movement towards a surface or area. The cat was not already 'on' the table, but moved from a different position to be 'on' it. 'In' implies being inside something, and 'at' indicates a specific point or location, neither of which fits the context of movement to a surface.

22. Choose the correct passive voice form of the sentence: 'The children are playing cricket.'

  1. Cricket is being played by the children.
  2. Cricket was played by the children.
  3. Cricket is played by the children.
  4. The children have played cricket.

Answer: Cricket is being played by the children.

Explanation: The original sentence is in the present continuous tense (Subject + is/are + V-ing + Object). To change it to passive voice, the structure becomes Object + is/are + being + V3 + by + Subject. Here, 'Cricket' is the object, 'is being played' is the passive verb form, and 'by the children' is the agent.

23. Choose the correct verb to complete the sentence: 'Neither of the students ______ present today.'

  1. is
  2. are
  3. were
  4. have

Answer: is

Explanation: When 'neither of' is used, the verb always agrees with the singular form, even if the noun following 'of' is plural. 'Neither of the students' refers to 'not one student', implying a singular subject, hence 'is' is the correct verb.

24. Identify the tense of the verb in the sentence: 'They have been living in this city for ten years.'

  1. Present Perfect Continuous
  2. Present Perfect
  3. Past Perfect Continuous
  4. Future Perfect

Answer: Present Perfect Continuous

Explanation: The sentence uses the structure 'have/has + been + V-ing', which is characteristic of the Present Perfect Continuous tense. This tense indicates an action that started in the past and is still continuing in the present.

25. Choose the correct preposition to complete the sentence: 'The book is ______ the table.'

  1. on
  2. in
  3. at
  4. under

Answer: on

Explanation: 'On' is the correct preposition to indicate that something is physically resting on the surface of another object. 'In' implies being enclosed, 'at' indicates a specific point, and 'under' means below.
