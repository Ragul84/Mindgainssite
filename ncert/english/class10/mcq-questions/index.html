26. Fill in the blank with the correct determiner: There isn't _______ milk left in the fridge.

  1. many
  2. much
  3. a few
  4. several

Answer: much

Explanation: 'Milk' is an uncountable noun. 'Much' is used with uncountable nouns in negative sentences and questions. 'Many', 'a few', and 'several' are used with countable nouns.

27. Choose the sentence that correctly uses the idiom 'to bite the bullet'.

  1. He decided to bite the bullet and confess his mistake.
  2. She bit the bullet to enjoy the delicious meal.
  3. The dog bit the bullet thrown by its owner.
  4. They bit the bullet to win the race easily.

Answer: He decided to bite the bullet and confess his mistake.

Explanation: The idiom 'to bite the bullet' means to endure a difficult or unpleasant situation with courage and fortitude. Confessing a mistake, especially a difficult one, aligns with this meaning. The other options do not reflect the correct usage of the idiom.

28. Read the following passage and answer the question: 'The old house stood on a hill, overlooking the town. Its windows, like vacant eyes, stared out at the bustling streets below. A gnarled oak tree, centuries old, guarded its entrance, its branches twisted into grotesque shapes by years of wind and weather. No one had lived there for decades, and local legends spoke of strange occurrences within its crumbling walls.' What can be inferred about the house from the passage?

  1. It is a newly built house.
  2. It is a popular tourist attraction.
  3. It is likely considered eerie or haunted by the locals.
  4. It is a well-maintained property.

Answer: It is likely considered eerie or haunted by the locals.

Explanation: The description uses words like 'vacant eyes', 'gnarled oak tree', 'grotesque shapes', 'no one had lived there for decades', and 'local legends spoke of strange occurrences'. These details strongly suggest that the house is perceived as eerie or haunted by the locals, not new, popular, or well-maintained.

29. Which of the following is a correctly formed question?

  1. He is going where?
  2. Where he is going?
  3. Where is he going?
  4. Going where he is?

Answer: Where is he going?

Explanation: In English questions, especially with 'wh-' words, the auxiliary verb (or 'be' verb) typically comes before the subject. So, 'Where is he going?' follows the correct subject-verb inversion for a question. The other options are grammatically incorrect question structures.

30. Choose the correct option to complete the sentence: 'Neither the students nor the teacher ______ present at the meeting.'

  1. was
  2. were
  3. is
  4. are

Answer: was

Explanation: This question tests Subject-Verb Concord. When 'neither...nor' is used, the verb agrees with the noun or pronoun closest to it. In this case, 'teacher' is singular, so the singular verb 'was' is appropriate. 'Is' would be incorrect as the meeting has already happened (implied by 'present at the meeting').

31. Transform the following into reported speech: 'The principal said to the students, 'You must submit your projects by Friday.''

  1. The principal told the students that they must submit their projects by Friday.
  2. The principal asked the students if they must submit their projects by Friday.
  3. The principal ordered the students to submit their projects by Friday.
  4. The principal said to the students that they had to submit their projects by Friday.

Answer: The principal told the students that they must submit their projects by Friday.

Explanation: This question tests Reported Speech. When 'must' expresses an obligation that remains in the reported speech, it often stays as 'must' or can be changed to 'had to'. Option A correctly changes the pronoun 'You' to 'they' and keeps 'must' as the obligation is still present. Option B changes it to a question, which is incorrect. Option C uses 'ordered', which is too strong for a general instruction. Option D uses 'had to', which is also grammatically correct for reported speech of 'must' but 'must' is often retained when the obligation persists. However, in CBSE context, 'must' is often retained when the obligation is still valid. Both A and D are plausible, but A is a more direct and common transformation when the obligation is still current.

32. Identify the type of sentence: 'Although it was raining heavily, we decided to go for a walk.'

  1. Simple Sentence
  2. Compound Sentence
  3. Complex Sentence
  4. Compound-Complex Sentence

Answer: Complex Sentence

Explanation: This question tests Complex and Compound Sentences. A complex sentence contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. Here, 'we decided to go for a walk' is the independent clause, and 'Although it was raining heavily' is a dependent clause (subordinate clause introduced by the subordinating conjunction 'Although').

33. Choose the correct determiner to complete the sentence: 'There isn't ______ milk left in the fridge.'

  1. many
  2. much
  3. a few
  4. some

Answer: much

Explanation: This question tests Determiners. 'Milk' is an uncountable noun. 'Many' and 'a few' are used with countable nouns. 'Some' is typically used in affirmative sentences or offers/requests, not usually in negative statements like 'There isn't'. 'Much' is correctly used with uncountable nouns in negative sentences and questions.

34. Which of the following words is an antonym for 'benevolent'?

  1. Kind
  2. Generous
  3. Malicious
  4. Compassionate

Answer: Malicious

Explanation: This question tests Synonyms and Antonyms. 'Benevolent' means well-meaning and kindly. 'Kind', 'Generous', and 'Compassionate' are all synonyms or closely related in meaning. 'Malicious' means intending or intended to do harm, making it the direct opposite or antonym of 'benevolent'.

35. Read the following passage and answer the question: 'The old man, with his weathered face and calloused hands, sat on the park bench, watching the children play. A faint smile touched his lips as he remembered his own childhood days, full of laughter and carefree adventures. He sighed, a sound barely audible above the joyful shouts of the youngsters.' What can be inferred about the old man's feelings?

  1. He is feeling angry and frustrated.
  2. He is feeling nostalgic and content.
  3. He is feeling bored and indifferent.
  4. He is feeling sad and regretful.

Answer: He is feeling nostalgic and content.

Explanation: This question tests Reading Comprehension and Inference-based Qs. The passage states he had 'a faint smile' and 'remembered his own childhood days, full of laughter and carefree adventures'. While he 'sighed', it was 'barely audible above the joyful shouts', suggesting a gentle reflection rather than deep sorrow. These details collectively imply a sense of pleasant remembrance and satisfaction, hence 'nostalgic and content'.
