Class12 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class12 questions to revise the NCERT Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 390 questions.

1. Which event led to significant changes in global politics and forced international organisations to adapt?

  1. The Industrial Revolution
  2. The French Revolution
  3. The collapse of the Soviet Union
  4. The formation of the European Union

Answer: The collapse of the Soviet Union

Explanation: The text states that after the collapse of the Soviet Union, the world saw significant changes in global politics.

2. What specific challenge did international organisations face following the collapse of the Soviet Union?

  1. The decline of global trade
  2. The rise of US power
  3. The end of all treaties
  4. The disappearance of the UN

Answer: The rise of US power

Explanation: The text mentions that organisations had to adapt to new challenges, including the rise of US power.

3. Which organ of the United Nations is specifically responsible for maintaining international peace and security?

  1. The General Assembly
  2. The International Court of Justice
  3. The UN Security Council
  4. The Secretariat

Answer: The UN Security Council

Explanation: The UN Security Council is described as the crucial part responsible for maintaining peace and security.

4. Why are reforms in the UN Security Council often discussed?

  1. To dissolve the United Nations
  2. To make it more effective and representative
  3. To reduce the number of member states
  4. To eliminate the role of treaties

Answer: To make it more effective and representative

Explanation: Reforms are discussed to make the Security Council more effective and representative.

5. International organisations are established through which of the following?

  1. Military force
  2. Treaties
  3. Oral agreements
  4. Corporate mergers

Answer: Treaties

Explanation: The text defines international organisations as groups established by treaties.

6. Which country's involvement is highlighted as a key aspect of the discussion on international organisations?

  1. China
  2. Russia
  3. India
  4. France

Answer: India

Explanation: The text explicitly mentions India's involvement as a key aspect of the discussion.

7. What is one of the primary goals of the United Nations?

  1. To promote war
  2. To develop friendly relations among nations
  3. To isolate nations from each other
  4. To create a single world government

Answer: To develop friendly relations among nations

Explanation: The UN was founded to develop friendly relations among nations and maintain peace.

8. The UN serves as a center for what action regarding nations?

  1. Harmonizing the actions of nations
  2. Controlling the economies of nations
  3. Dictating national laws
  4. Dividing nations into blocks

Answer: Harmonizing the actions of nations

Explanation: The UN is founded to be a center for harmonizing the actions of nations.

9. What does the potential reform of the UN Security Council highlight?

  1. The ease of changing global laws
  2. The complexities and difficulties of the reform process
  3. The lack of interest in peace
  4. The total failure of the UN

Answer: The complexities and difficulties of the reform process

Explanation: The potential reform of the UNSC highlights the complexities and difficulties of the reform process.

10. The UN was founded to achieve international ________.

  1. Competition
  2. Cooperation
  3. Conflict
  4. Isolation

Answer: Cooperation

Explanation: The UN was founded to achieve international cooperation.

11. What happened to global politics after the collapse of the Soviet Union?

  1. It remained stagnant
  2. It saw significant changes
  3. It became less complex
  4. International organisations became irrelevant

Answer: It saw significant changes

Explanation: The text states the world saw significant changes in global politics after the collapse.

12. The rise of US power is mentioned as a challenge that required what from international organisations?

  1. Immediate dissolution
  2. Adaptation
  3. Ignoring the change
  4. Merging with the US government

Answer: Adaptation

Explanation: International organisations had to adapt to new challenges, including the rise of US power.

13. What is the nature of the process of reforming the UN Security Council?

  1. Simple and fast
  2. Complex and difficult
  3. Automatic and effortless
  4. Unnecessary

Answer: Complex and difficult

Explanation: The text describes the reform process as having complexities and difficulties.

14. The United Nations is one of the most prominent international organisations.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: The text explicitly describes the UN as one of the most prominent international organisations.

15. The rise of US power had no impact on international organisations.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: The text states that organisations had to adapt to the rise of US power.

16. The UN was founded to create conflict between nations.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: The UN was founded to maintain peace and develop friendly relations, not create conflict.

17. India's involvement in international organisations is a key aspect of the discussion.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: The text mentions India's involvement as a key aspect of the discussion.

18. What is the primary purpose of international organisations?

  1. To establish global dominance
  2. To facilitate cooperation between nations on a global scale
  3. To replace national governments
  4. To manage the economy of a single nation

Answer: To facilitate cooperation between nations on a global scale

Explanation: International organisations are established by treaties to facilitate global cooperation between nations.

19. When was the United Nations (UN) founded?

  1. Before World War I
  2. During World War I
  3. After World War II
  4. After the Cold War

Answer: After World War II

Explanation: The UN was founded after World War II to prevent future conflicts.

20. Which organ of the UN is specifically responsible for maintaining peace and security?

  1. The General Assembly
  2. The UN Security Council
  3. The International Court of Justice
  4. The Economic and Social Council

Answer: The UN Security Council

Explanation: The UN Security Council is the crucial part responsible for maintaining peace and security.

21. What is a common reason for discussing reforms in the UN Security Council?

  1. To dissolve the council
  2. To make it more effective and representative
  3. To remove all member nations
  4. To limit its power to one nation

Answer: To make it more effective and representative

Explanation: Reforms are discussed to ensure the Security Council is more representative and effective.

22. The UN serves as a platform for which of the following?

  1. Military conquest
  2. Dialogue and diplomacy among nations
  3. Establishing a global monarchy
  4. Ignoring human rights

Answer: Dialogue and diplomacy among nations

Explanation: The UN serves as a platform for dialogue and diplomacy among nations.

23. Which of these is NOT mentioned as a global issue the UN addresses?

  1. Poverty
  2. Environmental degradation
  3. Interplanetary colonization
  4. Illiteracy

Answer: Interplanetary colonization

Explanation: The text mentions poverty, environment, and illiteracy, but not interplanetary colonization.

24. What is a common criticism faced by the United Nations?

  1. It is too small
  2. Perceived ineffectiveness in certain crises
  3. Lack of any agencies
  4. Lack of interest in peace

Answer: Perceived ineffectiveness in certain crises

Explanation: The UN often faces criticism for its perceived ineffectiveness in certain crises.

25. What is one of the goals of the UN regarding relations between nations?

  1. To create competition
  2. To develop friendly relations among nations
  3. To isolate nations
  4. To encourage conflict

Answer: To develop friendly relations among nations

Explanation: One of the UN's goals is to develop friendly relations among nations.
