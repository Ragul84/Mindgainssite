26. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a democratic government?

  1. Rule of law
  2. Free and fair elections
  3. One-party rule
  4. Respect for human rights

Answer: One-party rule

Explanation: A democratic government is characterized by the rule of law, free and fair elections, and respect for human rights. One-party rule is a feature of authoritarian or totalitarian regimes, not democracies, as it restricts political competition and choice.

27. Who among the following is the head of the Indian Union government?

  1. The President
  2. The Prime Minister
  3. The Chief Justice of India
  4. The Speaker of Lok Sabha

Answer: The Prime Minister

Explanation: While the President is the head of the state and the nominal executive, the Prime Minister is the head of the government and the real executive authority in India's parliamentary system. The Prime Minister leads the Council of Ministers and is responsible for the day-to-day administration.

28. Which of the following rights is NOT explicitly mentioned in the Indian Constitution but is implied by other rights?

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom of Speech and Expression
  3. Right to Privacy
  4. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Answer: Right to Privacy

Explanation: The Right to Privacy is not explicitly mentioned as a fundamental right in the Indian Constitution. However, the Supreme Court of India has interpreted it as an intrinsic part of the Right to Life and Personal Liberty under Article 21.

29. Why is it important to have free and fair elections in a democracy?

  1. To ensure that only educated people vote
  2. To allow the ruling party to stay in power indefinitely
  3. To give people a real choice and ensure their representatives are truly chosen by them
  4. To reduce the number of political parties

Answer: To give people a real choice and ensure their representatives are truly chosen by them

Explanation: Free and fair elections are crucial in a democracy because they ensure that citizens have a genuine opportunity to choose their representatives. This prevents manipulation and ensures that the government is accountable to the people, reflecting their will.

30. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. What does 'Sovereign' imply?

  1. The government will work for the welfare of all sections of society.
  2. People have the supreme right to make decisions on internal as well as external matters.
  3. The head of the state is an elected person, not a hereditary monarch.
  4. Citizens have complete freedom to follow any religion.

Answer: People have the supreme right to make decisions on internal as well as external matters.

Explanation: The term 'Sovereign' in the Preamble means that India is an independent state, free from external control. Its people have the supreme right to make decisions on internal as well as external matters without any outside interference. Option A relates to 'Socialist', Option C to 'Republic', and Option D to 'Secular'.

31. Which of the following statements about the Election Commission of India is incorrect?

  1. It is an independent and very powerful body.
  2. It takes decisions on every aspect of conduct and control of elections.
  3. It can order the government to follow some guidelines during election period.
  4. It is appointed by the ruling party and works under its direct control.

Answer: It is appointed by the ruling party and works under its direct control.

Explanation: The Election Commission of India is an independent body, not appointed by or working under the direct control of the ruling party. Its independence is crucial for ensuring free and fair elections. Options A, B, and C correctly describe the powers and functions of the Election Commission, as discussed in the chapter 'Electoral Politics'.

32. Which institution plays a crucial role in resolving disputes between the central government and state governments in India?

  1. The Parliament
  2. The Prime Minister's Office
  3. The Supreme Court
  4. The President

Answer: The Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India has the power of judicial review and is the final interpreter of the Constitution. It resolves disputes between the Union (central) government and one or more states, or between two or more states. This function is a key aspect of the 'Working of Institutions' in India's federal structure.

33. Which of the following is NOT a fundamental right guaranteed by the Indian Constitution?

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Property
  4. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Answer: Right to Property

Explanation: The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment Act of 1978 and made a legal right under Article 300A. The other options (Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, and Right to Constitutional Remedies) are all fundamental rights enshrined in the Indian Constitution, as detailed in the 'Democratic Rights' chapter.

34. In a parliamentary democracy like India, who is the head of the government?

  1. The President
  2. The Chief Justice of India
  3. The Prime Minister
  4. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

Answer: The Prime Minister

Explanation: In India's parliamentary system, the Prime Minister is the head of the government and exercises most of the executive powers. The President is the head of the state and holds a largely ceremonial position. This distinction is fundamental to understanding the 'Working of Institutions' in India.

35. Which of the following statements best describes the concept of 'apartheid'?

  1. A system of racial discrimination and segregation enforced by the government.
  2. A form of government where power is held by a single, absolute ruler.
  3. A political ideology advocating for a classless society with common ownership of the means of production.
  4. A system of governance where citizens elect representatives to make decisions on their behalf.

Answer: A system of racial discrimination and segregation enforced by the government.

Explanation: Apartheid was a system of institutionalised racial segregation and discrimination in South Africa from 1948 to the early 1990s. It was enforced by the National Party government and involved the classification of inhabitants into racial groups, with severe restrictions and disadvantages for non-white populations. The other options describe different political systems or ideologies: monarchy/dictatorship, communism, and representative democracy, respectively.
