Class9 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class9 questions to revise the NCERT Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of a democratic government?

  1. Rulers elected by the people take all the major decisions.
  2. Free and fair electoral competition.
  3. Rule of law and respect for rights.
  4. Decisions are taken by a single, powerful leader without public consultation.

Answer: Decisions are taken by a single, powerful leader without public consultation.

Explanation: A democratic government is characterized by rulers elected by the people making major decisions, free and fair elections, and adherence to the rule of law and respect for citizens' rights. Decisions taken by a single, powerful leader without public consultation is a characteristic of an authoritarian or dictatorial regime, not a democracy.

2. The Constituent Assembly of India was elected in which year?

  1. 1945
  2. 1946
  3. 1947
  4. 1949

Answer: 1946

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly of India, which drafted the Indian Constitution, was elected in July 1946. Its first meeting was held in December 1946.

3. What is the minimum age required to be a voter in India?

  1. 16 years
  2. 18 years
  3. 21 years
  4. 25 years

Answer: 18 years

Explanation: In India, the minimum age for a citizen to be eligible to vote in elections (universal adult franchise) is 18 years. This was reduced from 21 years by the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act of 1988.

4. Which body is responsible for interpreting the Constitution and acting as the guardian of Fundamental Rights in India?

  1. The Parliament
  2. The President
  3. The Supreme Court
  4. The Election Commission

Answer: The Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India, along with the High Courts, is responsible for interpreting the Constitution and safeguarding the Fundamental Rights of citizens. It acts as the final arbiter in constitutional matters and can declare laws unconstitutional if they violate fundamental rights.

5. Which of the following is an example of a political right?

  1. Right to education
  2. Right to vote
  3. Right to property
  4. Right to health

Answer: Right to vote

Explanation: Political rights are those that allow citizens to participate in the governance of their country. The Right to Vote is a fundamental political right, enabling citizens to choose their representatives. Right to education, property, and health are generally considered social, economic, or legal rights.

6. Who among the following was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  3. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  4. Rajendra Prasad

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, which was responsible for preparing the draft of the Indian Constitution. His contribution to the making of the Constitution is immense, earning him the title 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

7. What is the term used for a specific area from which voters elect their representatives?

  1. State
  2. District
  3. Constituency
  4. Ward

Answer: Constituency

Explanation: A constituency is a specific geographical area from which voters elect their representatives to a legislative body. For Lok Sabha elections, these are called parliamentary constituencies, and for state assemblies, they are called assembly constituencies.

8. Which institution plays a crucial role in making laws, controlling the executive, and making financial decisions in India?

  1. The Judiciary
  2. The Parliament
  3. The President
  4. The Prime Minister

Answer: The Parliament

Explanation: The Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) is the supreme legislative body in India. It is responsible for making laws, holding the executive (government) accountable, approving the budget, and discussing matters of national importance.

9. What is the minimum age required for a person to be eligible to vote in India?

  1. 16 years
  2. 18 years
  3. 21 years
  4. 25 years

Answer: 18 years

Explanation: In India, the minimum age for voting (universal adult franchise) is 18 years. This was reduced from 21 years by the 61st Amendment Act of 1988.

10. Which body is responsible for conducting elections in India?

  1. Supreme Court
  2. Parliament
  3. Election Commission of India
  4. Ministry of Home Affairs

Answer: Election Commission of India

Explanation: The Election Commission of India is an independent and autonomous constitutional body responsible for administering election processes in India at national, state, and district levels. It ensures free and fair elections.

11. Who among the following is the head of the state in India?

  1. Prime Minister
  2. Chief Justice of India
  3. President
  4. Speaker of Lok Sabha

Answer: President

Explanation: In India, the President is the head of the state and the supreme commander of the armed forces. The Prime Minister is the head of the government.

12. Which of the following institutions can introduce a money bill in India?

  1. Rajya Sabha only
  2. Lok Sabha only
  3. Both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha
  4. President's office

Answer: Lok Sabha only

Explanation: A money bill can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha (House of the People). The Rajya Sabha (Council of States) can only recommend changes, but the Lok Sabha is not bound by these recommendations.

13. The 'Right to Constitutional Remedies' allows citizens to move to court if they believe that any of their fundamental rights have been violated. Which article of the Indian Constitution guarantees this right?

  1. Article 19
  2. Article 21
  3. Article 32
  4. Article 25

Answer: Article 32

Explanation: Article 32 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the 'Right to Constitutional Remedies'. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar famously called it the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution because it makes other fundamental rights enforceable. It allows individuals to seek justice directly from the Supreme Court for the enforcement of their fundamental rights.

14. Which of the following institutions plays a crucial role in resolving disputes between the Union government and state governments in India?

  1. The Parliament
  2. The Prime Minister's Office
  3. The Supreme Court
  4. The Election Commission

Answer: The Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India is the highest judicial body and has original jurisdiction to resolve disputes between the Union (central) government and one or more state governments, or between two or more state governments.

15. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  3. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  4. Rajendra Prasad

Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, which was responsible for preparing the draft of the Indian Constitution. His immense contribution earned him the title 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

16. What does 'Universal Adult Franchise' mean?

  1. Only educated adults can vote.
  2. All adults, regardless of caste, religion, gender, or economic status, have the right to vote.
  3. Only men above 21 years of age can vote.
  4. Only property owners have the right to vote.

Answer: All adults, regardless of caste, religion, gender, or economic status, have the right to vote.

Explanation: Universal Adult Franchise (or Suffrage) is the right of all adult citizens to vote in elections, irrespective of their wealth, income, gender, social status, race, ethnicity, or any other restriction, subject only to minor exceptions like mental incapacity or criminal conviction.

17. Which body is responsible for conducting free and fair elections in India?

  1. The Parliament
  2. The Supreme Court
  3. The Election Commission of India
  4. The Ministry of Home Affairs

Answer: The Election Commission of India

Explanation: The Election Commission of India is an autonomous constitutional authority responsible for administering election processes in India at national, state, and district levels. Its primary role is to ensure free and fair elections.

18. What is the primary role of the Election Commission of India?

  1. To form the government.
  2. To interpret laws passed by Parliament.
  3. To conduct free and fair elections.
  4. To appoint judges to the Supreme Court.

Answer: To conduct free and fair elections.

Explanation: The Election Commission of India is an independent and autonomous constitutional body responsible for administering election processes in India. Its primary role is to conduct free, fair, and impartial elections to the Parliament, state legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President.

19. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution declares India to be a:

  1. Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic
  2. Sovereign, Socialist, Communist, Democratic Republic
  3. Sovereign, Capitalist, Secular, Democratic Republic
  4. Sovereign, Socialist, Theocratic, Democratic Republic

Answer: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic

Explanation: The Preamble to the Indian Constitution, as amended in 1976, declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. These terms define the nature of the Indian state and its objectives.

20. Which institution plays a crucial role in making laws in India?

  1. The Judiciary
  2. The Executive
  3. The Parliament
  4. The Election Commission

Answer: The Parliament

Explanation: The Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) is the supreme legislative body in India. Its primary function is to make laws for the country. The Executive implements laws, the Judiciary interprets them, and the Election Commission conducts elections.

21. The Constituent Assembly of India was formed in which year?

  1. 1945
  2. 1946
  3. 1947
  4. 1948

Answer: 1946

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly of India was formed in 1946 to draft the Constitution of India. Its first meeting was held on December 9, 1946.

22. Who among the following is the head of the Indian state?

  1. Prime Minister
  2. President
  3. Chief Justice of India
  4. Speaker of Lok Sabha

Answer: President

Explanation: The President is the head of the Indian state and the first citizen of India. The Prime Minister is the head of the government, while the President holds a largely ceremonial but constitutionally significant position.

23. The 'Preamble' to the Indian Constitution serves what purpose?

  1. It lists the names of all the members of the Constituent Assembly.
  2. It outlines the fundamental duties of citizens.
  3. It introduces the Constitution and states its guiding principles and objectives.
  4. It describes the procedure for amending the Constitution.

Answer: It introduces the Constitution and states its guiding principles and objectives.

Explanation: The Preamble to the Indian Constitution serves as an introduction, outlining the philosophy, ideals, and objectives of the Constitution. It declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic and secures justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity for all its citizens.

24. Which of the following rights is NOT a fundamental right in the Indian Constitution?

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Property
  4. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Answer: Right to Property

Explanation: The Right to Property was originally a fundamental right but was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment Act of 1978. It is now a legal right under Article 300A of the Constitution.

25. What is the primary role of the Judiciary in a democracy?

  1. To make laws for the country.
  2. To implement the laws made by the legislature.
  3. To interpret laws, settle disputes, and administer justice.
  4. To represent the country in international forums.

Answer: To interpret laws, settle disputes, and administer justice.

Explanation: The primary role of the Judiciary in a democracy is to interpret the laws, settle disputes between individuals, between individuals and the government, and between governments, and to administer justice. The legislature makes laws, and the executive implements them.
