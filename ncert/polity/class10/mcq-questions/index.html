26. What is the primary function of a 'recognised political party' in India?

  1. To form a coalition government with other parties.
  2. To contest elections and form a government if they win a majority.
  3. To act as a pressure group influencing government policies.
  4. To provide social services and welfare programs to the public.

Answer: To contest elections and form a government if they win a majority.

Explanation: The primary function of any political party, especially a recognised one, is to contest elections, win seats, and form or be part of a government. While they may engage in other activities like forming coalitions or influencing policies, their core objective is to gain political power through electoral means. Providing social services is generally a government function or a role for NGOs.

27. Which of the following is a key challenge faced by political parties in India today?

  1. Lack of internal democracy within parties.
  2. Excessive reliance on public funding for elections.
  3. Absence of charismatic leaders.
  4. Too much transparency in party funding.

Answer: Lack of internal democracy within parties.

Explanation: A significant challenge faced by political parties in India is the lack of internal democracy. Often, power is concentrated in the hands of a few leaders, and ordinary members do not have sufficient say in decision-making or candidate selection. Dynastic succession is also a related issue. Excessive reliance on public funding is not a current challenge; rather, the issue is often opaque private funding. Transparency in funding is a desired reform, not a challenge.

28. Which of the following is generally NOT considered a positive outcome of democracy?

  1. Promotes equality among citizens.
  2. Enhances the dignity of the individual.
  3. Reduces economic inequalities among citizens.
  4. Allows room to correct mistakes.

Answer: Reduces economic inequalities among citizens.

Explanation: While democracy aims to promote equality and dignity, and provides mechanisms for correcting mistakes, it has not been universally successful in significantly reducing economic inequalities. Democracies often coexist with substantial economic disparities, and sometimes even exacerbate them due to market forces or policy choices. Therefore, reducing economic inequalities is an aspiration and a challenge for democracies, rather than a guaranteed outcome.

29. What is the main idea behind the concept of 'majoritarianism' in power sharing?

  1. Power is shared equally among all social groups.
  2. The majority community should be able to rule in whichever way it wants, disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority.
  3. Power is divided between different levels of government.
  4. Different organs of government exercise different powers.

Answer: The majority community should be able to rule in whichever way it wants, disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority.

Explanation: Majoritarianism is a belief that the majority community should be able to rule a country in whichever way it wants, by disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority. This approach often leads to conflict and alienation of minority groups, as seen in Sri Lanka. Power sharing, in contrast, aims to accommodate diverse groups.

30. Why is 'accountability' considered a key outcome of democracy?

  1. It ensures that all citizens have equal economic status.
  2. It makes the government answerable to the people.
  3. It guarantees that all political parties will win elections.
  4. It allows for the immediate removal of any elected leader.

Answer: It makes the government answerable to the people.

Explanation: Accountability in a democracy means that the government is responsible and answerable to the citizens. Through elections, public debates, and various institutions, citizens can hold their representatives and the government responsible for their actions and decisions. This ensures that the government acts in the best interests of the people, or faces consequences in the next election. It does not guarantee equal economic status or immediate removal of leaders, nor does it ensure all parties win.

31. Which of the following is an example of 'prudential' reason for power sharing?

  1. It helps to reduce the possibility of conflict between social groups.
  2. It upholds the spirit of democracy.
  3. It ensures the stability of the political order.
  4. Both A and C

Answer: Both A and C

Explanation: Prudential reasons for power sharing are based on careful calculation of gains and losses. They emphasize that power sharing brings better outcomes. Reducing conflict between social groups (A) and ensuring political stability (C) are both practical, outcome-oriented reasons, making them prudential. Upholding the spirit of democracy (B) is a moral reason.

32. Which of the following statements about the division of labour between men and women is generally true in most societies?

  1. Men are primarily responsible for household chores and childcare.
  2. Women are primarily responsible for earning income outside the home.
  3. Most of the household work is done by women, and paid work outside the home is primarily done by men.
  4. Both men and women equally share all household and outside work.

Answer: Most of the household work is done by women, and paid work outside the home is primarily done by men.

Explanation: In most societies, there is a traditional division of labour where women are expected to perform most of the domestic chores and childcare, while men are seen as the primary breadwinners working outside the home. Options A, B, and D do not accurately reflect the prevalent gender roles in many societies.

33. What is the primary function of a 'ruling party' in a democratic system?

  1. To form the government and implement policies.
  2. To criticize the government and hold it accountable.
  3. To contest elections and win seats.
  4. To represent the interests of various social groups.

Answer: To form the government and implement policies.

Explanation: The primary function of a ruling party, which is the party that wins the majority of seats in an election, is to form the government and then implement its policies and programs. Criticizing the government (B) is the role of the opposition. Contesting elections (C) and representing interests (D) are functions of all political parties, but forming the government is specific to the ruling party.

34. Which of the following is a common challenge faced by democracies in achieving their outcomes?

  1. Lack of transparency and accountability.
  2. Absence of free and fair elections.
  3. Suppression of dissent and opposition.
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: While democracies aim for transparency, accountability, free elections, and respect for dissent, they often face challenges in fully achieving these. Lack of transparency and accountability (A), issues with free and fair elections (B), and suppression of dissent (C) are all common problems that can hinder the effective functioning and outcomes of a democracy. Therefore, 'All of the above' is the correct answer.

35. Which of the following statements best describes the concept of 'horizontal power sharing' in a democracy?

  1. Power is shared among different organs of government at the same level, such as the legislature, executive, and judiciary.
  2. Power is shared among different levels of government, such as the central, state, and local governments.
  3. Power is shared among different social groups, such as linguistic or religious groups.
  4. Power is shared among different political parties that compete for power.

Answer: Power is shared among different organs of government at the same level, such as the legislature, executive, and judiciary.

Explanation: Horizontal power sharing refers to the distribution of power among different organs of government at the same level. This system ensures a 'check and balance' mechanism, preventing any one organ from exercising unlimited power. For example, the legislature makes laws, the executive implements them, and the judiciary interprets them, with each having some power to check the others. Options B, C, and D describe other forms of power sharing (vertical, community government, and coalition government, respectively).
