Class10 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class10 questions to revise the NCERT Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is an example of 'horizontal' power sharing?

  1. Power shared among different organs of government like the legislature, executive, and judiciary.
  2. Power shared among governments at different levels, such as the Union, State, and local governments.
  3. Power shared among different social groups, like linguistic or religious groups.
  4. Power shared among different political parties that compete for power.

Answer: Power shared among different organs of government like the legislature, executive, and judiciary.

Explanation: Horizontal power sharing involves the distribution of power among different organs of government at the same level, ensuring a system of checks and balances. The legislature, executive, and judiciary are examples of such organs.

2. In a 'holding together' federation, what is a common characteristic?

  1. Independent states come together on their own to form a bigger unit.
  2. A large country decides to divide its power between the constituent states and the national government.
  3. States have equal powers and are strong in relation to the federal government.
  4. The central government has very limited powers compared to the state governments.

Answer: A large country decides to divide its power between the constituent states and the national government.

Explanation: In a 'holding together' federation, a large country chooses to divide its power between the central authority and its constituent units. This often results in the central government being more powerful than the state governments, and different constituent units may have unequal powers.

3. Which of the following statements is true regarding the 'caste system' in India today?

  1. Caste has completely disappeared from Indian society due to economic development.
  2. Caste identities are still very much present and can influence electoral politics.
  3. Inter-caste marriages are now universally accepted across all regions of India.
  4. The government has abolished all forms of caste-based discrimination, making it irrelevant.

Answer: Caste identities are still very much present and can influence electoral politics.

Explanation: While economic development and urbanisation have weakened the caste system, caste identities continue to be a significant factor in Indian society, particularly in electoral politics. Political parties often appeal to caste sentiments to garner votes, and caste-based discrimination, though illegal, still persists in various forms.

4. What is the primary function of an 'opposition party' in a democracy?

  1. To form the government and implement policies.
  2. To provide constructive criticism of the government and hold it accountable.
  3. To ensure that all citizens vote for the ruling party.
  4. To prevent any new laws from being passed by the government.

Answer: To provide constructive criticism of the government and hold it accountable.

Explanation: Opposition parties play a crucial role in a democracy by scrutinising government policies, raising public issues, and holding the ruling party accountable. They offer alternative policies and ensure that the government does not become autocratic.

5. Which of the following is a key indicator of a successful outcome of democracy?

  1. High levels of economic inequality.
  2. Regular and free elections.
  3. Suppression of dissent and protests.
  4. A single-party rule for a long duration.

Answer: Regular and free elections.

Explanation: Regular and free elections are a fundamental characteristic and a key outcome of a successful democracy. They ensure that citizens have the right to choose their representatives and hold them accountable, reflecting the principle of popular sovereignty.

6. In Belgium, the power-sharing arrangement is often cited as an example of:

  1. Majoritarianism
  2. Accommodation
  3. Unitary government
  4. Dictatorship

Answer: Accommodation

Explanation: The Belgian model of power sharing is a prime example of accommodation. It involved carefully designed arrangements to share power among different linguistic groups (Dutch, French, and German-speaking) to prevent civil strife and maintain unity, unlike the majoritarian approach taken in Sri Lanka.

7. Which subject is included in the 'Concurrent List' under the Indian federal system?

  1. Defence
  2. Banking
  3. Education
  4. Railways

Answer: Education

Explanation: The Concurrent List includes subjects on which both the Union (Central) government and the State governments can make laws. Education is one such subject. Defence, Banking, and Railways are subjects under the Union List, meaning only the Central government can legislate on them.

8. What is the main challenge faced by political parties in a democracy today?

  1. Lack of public interest in politics.
  2. Dynastic succession and lack of internal democracy.
  3. Too many political parties competing for power.
  4. The absence of free and fair elections.

Answer: Dynastic succession and lack of internal democracy.

Explanation: Dynastic succession and a lack of internal democracy are significant challenges faced by many political parties. This means that power often remains concentrated within a few families or individuals, limiting opportunities for ordinary members to rise and reducing accountability within the party structure.

9. In a federal system, which level of government has the power to legislate on subjects of national importance like defence and foreign affairs?

  1. State government
  2. Local government
  3. Central government
  4. Both State and Central governments jointly

Answer: Central government

Explanation: In a federal system, subjects of national importance like defence, foreign affairs, currency, and communication are typically under the exclusive jurisdiction of the Central (or Union) government. This ensures uniformity and coherence in policies that affect the entire nation. State governments handle subjects of regional importance.

10. What is the term used to describe a society where there are no social divisions based on gender, religion, or caste?

  1. Homogeneous society
  2. Pluralistic society
  3. Secular society
  4. Utopian society

Answer: Homogeneous society

Explanation: A homogeneous society is one where there are no significant ethnic, religious, or social differences. While 'secular society' relates to religion, it doesn't encompass gender or caste. 'Pluralistic society' implies diversity, and 'utopian society' is an ideal, often unattainable, concept. The question specifically asks for a society without divisions based on these factors, which aligns with the definition of a homogeneous society.

11. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of a multi-party system?

  1. Only two major parties compete for power.
  2. Power is usually shared by various parties forming a coalition government.
  3. The government is formed by a single dominant party.
  4. Citizens do not have a choice of political parties.

Answer: Power is usually shared by various parties forming a coalition government.

Explanation: In a multi-party system, several parties compete for power, and often, no single party secures a clear majority. This frequently leads to the formation of coalition governments, where different parties come together to form a government. Option A describes a two-party system, and Option C describes a single-party dominant system or a single-party system.

12. Which of the following is NOT a challenge to democracy?

  1. Dynastic succession in political parties.
  2. Growing economic inequalities.
  3. Lack of internal democracy within political parties.
  4. Regular and fair elections.

Answer: Regular and fair elections.

Explanation: Regular and fair elections are a fundamental characteristic and a strength of democracy, ensuring accountability and representation. Dynastic succession, growing economic inequalities, and lack of internal democracy within political parties are all significant challenges that can undermine the democratic process and its effectiveness.

13. In the context of power sharing in Belgium, what was the unique arrangement adopted to avoid civil strife?

  1. The capital city, Brussels, was made a separate state with its own government.
  2. The Dutch-speaking majority was given more power than the French-speaking minority.
  3. Both Dutch and French-speaking ministers were given equal representation in the Central Government.
  4. The country was divided into two separate nations based on linguistic lines.

Answer: Both Dutch and French-speaking ministers were given equal representation in the Central Government.

Explanation: The Belgian model of power sharing is famous for its innovative approach. One key element was the provision that the number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers shall be equal in the Central Government. This ensured that no single linguistic community could make decisions unilaterally, fostering a sense of equality and preventing conflict. Option A is also part of the Belgian model, but Option C is a more direct answer to the 'unique arrangement' for the central government.

14. Which of the following subjects is included in the Concurrent List of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Defence
  2. Banking
  3. Education
  4. Railways

Answer: Education

Explanation: The Concurrent List includes subjects on which both the Union (Central) and State governments can legislate. Education is a prominent example of a subject in the Concurrent List. Defence, Banking, and Railways are subjects under the Union List, meaning only the Central government can legislate on them.

15. What is the primary goal of a political party?

  1. To provide social services to the community.
  2. To contest elections and hold power in the government.
  3. To promote cultural activities and traditions.
  4. To enforce laws and maintain order.

Answer: To contest elections and hold power in the government.

Explanation: The primary goal of a political party is to contest elections and win power in the government. While parties may engage in social work or promote cultural values, these are secondary to their main objective of gaining and exercising political power. Enforcing laws is the role of the executive and judiciary, not primarily political parties.

16. Which of the following countries successfully adopted a power-sharing arrangement that led to a stable political system, despite having diverse ethnic groups?

  1. Sri Lanka
  2. Belgium
  3. Yugoslavia
  4. Lebanon

Answer: Belgium

Explanation: Belgium successfully accommodated its diverse linguistic groups (Dutch and French speakers) by adopting a complex power-sharing model, including a community government and equal representation in the central government. This prevented civil strife and led to a stable political system. Sri Lanka, on the other hand, faced civil war due to majoritarian policies. Yugoslavia disintegrated due to ethnic tensions, and Lebanon has faced significant challenges in maintaining stability despite power-sharing attempts.

17. Which of the following is NOT a feature of a federal system of government?

  1. Two or more levels of government.
  2. Different tiers of government govern the same citizens, but each tier has its own jurisdiction in specific matters of legislation, taxation, and administration.
  3. The fundamental provisions of the constitution cannot be unilaterally changed by one level of government.
  4. A single central government has all the powers, and regional governments are subordinate to it.

Answer: A single central government has all the powers, and regional governments are subordinate to it.

Explanation: A federal system is characterized by multiple levels of government, each with its own distinct jurisdiction, and a constitution that guarantees the autonomy of these levels. The fundamental provisions of the constitution require the consent of both levels of government for amendment. Option D describes a unitary system of government, where the central government holds supreme power and regional governments, if they exist, are subordinate and derive their authority from the central government.

18. In India, subjects like defence, foreign affairs, banking, and communications are included in which list?

  1. State List
  2. Union List
  3. Concurrent List
  4. Residuary Subjects

Answer: Union List

Explanation: The Union List includes subjects of national importance such as defence, foreign affairs, banking, communications, and currency. The Union Government alone can make laws relating to these subjects. The State List contains subjects of state and local importance, while the Concurrent List includes subjects of common interest to both the Union and State governments. Residuary subjects are those not mentioned in any of the three lists, and the Union Government has the power to legislate on them.

19. What does 'patriarchy' primarily refer to in the context of gender relations?

  1. A system where women have equal rights and opportunities as men.
  2. A system where men hold primary power and predominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, social privilege, and control of property.
  3. A system where both men and women share power equally in all spheres of life.
  4. A system where women are given preferential treatment in society.

Answer: A system where men hold primary power and predominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, social privilege, and control of property.

Explanation: Patriarchy is a social system in which men hold primary power and predominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, social privilege, and control of property. It implies a male-dominated society where women are often subordinate. Options A, C, and D describe scenarios that are either contrary to patriarchy or do not accurately define it.

20. Which of the following is a characteristic of a 'national party' in India?

  1. It secures at least 6% of the total votes in a Lok Sabha election or Assembly elections in four States and wins at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha.
  2. It secures at least 3% of the total votes polled in the last Assembly election of a State.
  3. It wins at least 3 seats in the State Legislative Assembly.
  4. It is registered with the Election Commission of India.

Answer: It secures at least 6% of the total votes in a Lok Sabha election or Assembly elections in four States and wins at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha.

Explanation: To be recognized as a 'national party' by the Election Commission of India, a party must fulfill specific criteria. One key criterion is securing at least 6% of the total votes in a Lok Sabha election or Assembly elections in four States AND winning at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha. Option B and C are criteria for a 'state party'. Option D is a basic requirement for any political party, not specifically a national one.

21. What is the primary function of a 'pressure group' in a democracy?

  1. To directly contest elections and form the government.
  2. To influence government policies and decisions without directly participating in electoral politics.
  3. To provide legal aid to citizens.
  4. To manage the day-to-day administration of the country.

Answer: To influence government policies and decisions without directly participating in electoral politics.

Explanation: Pressure groups are organizations that attempt to influence public policy in their favor without directly seeking to capture political power. They represent specific interests (e.g., trade unions, business associations, environmental groups) and try to persuade the government to adopt policies beneficial to their members or cause. Political parties, unlike pressure groups, aim to contest elections and form the government. Options C and D are not the primary functions of pressure groups.

22. Which of the following is a positive outcome of democracy?

  1. It always ensures economic equality among all citizens.
  2. It promotes accountability and responsiveness of the government to the needs of the citizens.
  3. It guarantees that there will be no conflicts or disagreements in society.
  4. It leads to faster economic growth compared to other forms of government.

Answer: It promotes accountability and responsiveness of the government to the needs of the citizens.

Explanation: One of the most significant positive outcomes of democracy is its ability to promote accountability and responsiveness. Democratic governments are, in principle, accountable to the people through elections and various oversight mechanisms. Citizens can question government actions and demand responses, making the government more responsive to their needs and expectations. While democracy strives for economic equality, it doesn't always achieve it (Option A). Conflicts are inherent in diverse societies, and democracy provides mechanisms to resolve them peacefully, not eliminate them (Option C). The relationship between democracy and economic growth is complex and not always faster than other forms of government (Option D).

23. Which of the following is an example of 'vertical' power sharing?

  1. Power shared among different organs of government like the legislature, executive, and judiciary.
  2. Power shared among different levels of government like the Union, State, and local governments.
  3. Power shared among different social groups like linguistic or religious groups.
  4. Power shared among different political parties that compete for power.

Answer: Power shared among different levels of government like the Union, State, and local governments.

Explanation: Vertical power sharing refers to the distribution of power among different levels of government, such as the central, state, and local governments. This is a key feature of federalism. The other options represent horizontal power sharing (organs of government), community government (social groups), and coalition governments (political parties).

24. In a 'holding together' federation, what is a common characteristic regarding the distribution of power?

  1. Independent states come together on their own to form a bigger unit.
  2. The central government tends to be more powerful vis-à-vis the constituent units.
  3. All constituent units usually have equal powers.
  4. States retain maximum autonomy and the central government has limited powers.

Answer: The central government tends to be more powerful vis-à-vis the constituent units.

Explanation: In a 'holding together' federation, a large country decides to divide its power between the constituent states and the national government. In this arrangement, the central government often tends to be more powerful than the state governments. India, Spain, and Belgium are examples of 'holding together' federations. 'Coming together' federations involve independent states forming a larger unit, where states often have more equal powers.

25. Which of the following statements about the 'family laws' in India is correct?

  1. They are uniform for all religious communities across the country.
  2. They deal with matters related to marriage, divorce, adoption, and inheritance.
  3. They are exclusively governed by the central government.
  4. They are not subject to any constitutional provisions.

Answer: They deal with matters related to marriage, divorce, adoption, and inheritance.

Explanation: Family laws in India, also known as personal laws, deal with matters such as marriage, divorce, adoption, and inheritance. These laws are not uniform for all religious communities; different communities (Hindu, Muslim, Christian, etc.) have their own personal laws. While the central government can legislate on these matters, they are also influenced by religious customs and traditions, and are subject to constitutional provisions like fundamental rights.
