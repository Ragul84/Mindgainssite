26. Who plays a crucial role in passing a new law in India?

  1. The President only
  2. The Prime Minister only
  3. The Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha)
  4. The Supreme Court

Answer: The Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha)

Explanation: In India, the Parliament, which consists of the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States), is responsible for making laws. A bill must be passed by both houses and then receive the assent of the President to become a law.

27. Which of the following is an example of 'Confronting Marginalisation'?

  1. Ignoring the needs of minority groups
  2. Enacting laws to protect the rights of Scheduled Castes and Tribes
  3. Promoting discrimination based on religion
  4. Limiting access to education for certain communities

Answer: Enacting laws to protect the rights of Scheduled Castes and Tribes

Explanation: Confronting Marginalisation involves taking active steps to address and overcome the exclusion faced by marginalised groups. Enacting laws like the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act is a direct way to protect their rights and ensure justice, thereby confronting marginalisation.

28. Which of the following is considered a 'Public Facility'?

  1. A private car
  2. A luxury hotel
  3. Public healthcare services
  4. A personal computer

Answer: Public healthcare services

Explanation: Public facilities are essential services provided by the government for the benefit of all citizens, which everyone can access. Public healthcare services, like government hospitals and clinics, are a prime example of a public facility, unlike private goods or services.

29. What is the main purpose of 'Law and Social Justice'?

  1. To maintain the status quo in society
  2. To ensure that laws are fair and promote equality for all
  3. To benefit only the powerful sections of society
  4. To restrict individual freedoms

Answer: To ensure that laws are fair and promote equality for all

Explanation: The concept of 'Law and Social Justice' aims to ensure that laws are not only just in their formulation but also in their application, leading to a fair and equitable society. It seeks to protect the vulnerable, address inequalities, and promote the well-being of all citizens, especially those who are disadvantaged.

30. Which of the following groups is often considered 'marginalised' in India?

  1. People living in urban areas
  2. People belonging to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes
  3. People with high income
  4. People working in government jobs

Answer: People belonging to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes

Explanation: Marginalisation refers to the process by which certain groups are pushed to the fringes of society, facing social, economic, and political exclusion. Historically, and even today, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes have faced discrimination and exclusion, making them a significant marginalised group in India. The other options generally represent groups that are not typically considered marginalised.

31. In India, the state does not officially promote any one religion. This principle is known as:

  1. Democracy
  2. Secularism
  3. Socialism
  4. Federalism

Answer: Secularism

Explanation: Secularism is a fundamental principle of the Indian Constitution, meaning that the state maintains a neutral stance towards all religions and does not endorse or discriminate against any particular faith. It ensures religious freedom for all citizens. Democracy refers to a system of government by the whole population or all the eligible members of a state, typically through elected representatives. Socialism is an economic and political theory advocating that the means of production, distribution, and exchange should be owned or regulated by the community as a whole. Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units.

32. Who plays a crucial role in making laws in India by discussing bills and approving them?

  1. The President
  2. The Prime Minister
  3. The Judiciary
  4. The Parliament

Answer: The Parliament

Explanation: The Parliament, consisting of the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha, is the supreme legislative body in India. Its primary function is to make laws, which involves discussing, debating, and approving bills. The President gives assent to bills passed by Parliament to make them laws. The Prime Minister is the head of the executive, and the Judiciary interprets laws.

33. Which of the following groups is often subjected to social and economic exclusion, leading to marginalisation?

  1. People living in urban areas
  2. People with high income
  3. Adivasis (Indigenous people)
  4. Government employees

Answer: Adivasis (Indigenous people)

Explanation: Adivasis, or indigenous people, are often subjected to social and economic exclusion and marginalisation due to historical injustices, loss of land, cultural differences, and lack of access to resources and opportunities. They frequently face discrimination and are pushed to the fringes of society. The other options generally represent groups that are not typically considered marginalised in the same systemic way.

34. The right to clean drinking water, sanitation, and healthcare are examples of:

  1. Political Rights
  2. Economic Rights
  3. Public Facilities
  4. Cultural Rights

Answer: Public Facilities

Explanation: Clean drinking water, sanitation, and healthcare are considered essential public facilities that the government is responsible for providing to all citizens. These are basic necessities for a dignified life and are crucial for public health and well-being. While they can be linked to economic and social rights, the term 'Public Facilities' specifically refers to these services provided by the government.
