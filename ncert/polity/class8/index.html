Class8 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class8 questions to revise the NCERT Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is NOT a key feature of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Federalism
  2. Parliamentary Form of Government
  3. Absolute Monarchy
  4. Separation of Powers

Answer: Absolute Monarchy

Explanation: The Indian Constitution establishes a democratic republic with a parliamentary form of government, federalism, and separation of powers. Absolute Monarchy, where a single ruler holds all power, is contrary to the principles of the Indian Constitution.

2. What does 'secularism' primarily mean in the Indian context?

  1. The state promotes one particular religion.
  2. The state is hostile towards all religions.
  3. The state maintains a principled distance from all religions.
  4. Citizens are forced to follow a specific religion.

Answer: The state maintains a principled distance from all religions.

Explanation: In India, secularism means that the state does not officially promote any one religion as the state religion. Instead, it maintains a principled distance from all religions, treating all religions equally and allowing individuals the freedom to practice their own faith without state interference.

3. Who plays a crucial role in making laws in India by debating and passing bills?

  1. The President
  2. The Judiciary
  3. The Parliament
  4. The Chief Minister

Answer: The Parliament

Explanation: The Parliament, consisting of the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, is the supreme legislative body in India. Its primary function is to debate, discuss, and pass bills, which then become laws after receiving the President's assent.

4. What is the main function of the Judiciary in India?

  1. To make laws
  2. To implement laws
  3. To interpret laws and administer justice
  4. To represent India internationally

Answer: To interpret laws and administer justice

Explanation: The Judiciary's main function is to interpret the laws made by the legislature and implemented by the executive. It ensures that laws are applied fairly, resolves disputes, and administers justice according to the Constitution.

5. Which of the following groups is often considered 'marginalised' in India due to historical and social reasons?

  1. Upper-caste wealthy individuals
  2. People living in urban areas
  3. Adivasis (Indigenous people)
  4. Government employees

Answer: Adivasis (Indigenous people)

Explanation: Adivasis, or indigenous people, have historically faced marginalisation in India due to factors like displacement from their lands, loss of traditional livelihoods, and discrimination, leading to their social and economic exclusion.

6. Which of the following is a measure taken by the government to confront marginalisation?

  1. Promoting discrimination against certain groups
  2. Ignoring the needs of minority communities
  3. Implementing reservation policies for Scheduled Castes and Tribes
  4. Restricting access to education for certain sections

Answer: Implementing reservation policies for Scheduled Castes and Tribes

Explanation: Reservation policies for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) are a key measure taken by the Indian government to confront historical marginalisation. These policies aim to provide opportunities in education and employment to these communities, helping them overcome disadvantages and achieve equality.

7. Which of the following is an example of a 'public facility'?

  1. A private car
  2. A luxury resort
  3. A public bus service
  4. A personal computer

Answer: A public bus service

Explanation: Public facilities are essential services provided by the government for the benefit of all citizens. A public bus service is a classic example, as it is accessible to everyone and aims to serve the community's transportation needs, unlike private goods or services.

8. What is the primary goal of 'Law and Social Justice' in India?

  1. To maintain the status quo and existing inequalities
  2. To ensure that laws only benefit the wealthy
  3. To protect the weak from the strong and ensure fair treatment for all
  4. To allow individuals to break laws without consequences

Answer: To protect the weak from the strong and ensure fair treatment for all

Explanation: The primary goal of 'Law and Social Justice' in India is to use legal frameworks to protect the vulnerable and ensure fair treatment for all sections of society. It aims to address inequalities, prevent exploitation, and promote a just and equitable society where everyone has equal rights and opportunities.

9. What does the term 'secularism' primarily mean in the Indian context?

  1. Promotion of one religion by the state
  2. Complete separation of religion from the state
  3. State neutrality towards all religions and non-interference in religious practices
  4. Suppression of all religious activities

Answer: State neutrality towards all religions and non-interference in religious practices

Explanation: In the Indian context, secularism means that the state does not officially promote any one religion as the state religion. Instead, it maintains a principled distance from all religions, treating all equally and not interfering in religious practices unless they violate fundamental rights or public order. It's not about complete separation, but about neutrality and equal respect.

10. Who plays a crucial role in passing a new law in India, after it has been introduced in Parliament?

  1. The President
  2. The Chief Justice of India
  3. The Prime Minister
  4. The Governor of a state

Answer: The President

Explanation: For a bill to become a law in India, it must be passed by both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) and then receive the assent of the President. The President's assent is the final step that makes a bill into an Act of Parliament, i.e., a law.

11. What is the primary role of the Judiciary in India?

  1. To make new laws
  2. To implement government policies
  3. To interpret laws and administer justice
  4. To represent India in international forums

Answer: To interpret laws and administer justice

Explanation: The primary role of the Judiciary in India is to interpret the laws made by the legislature, administer justice by resolving disputes, and uphold the Constitution. It ensures that laws are applied fairly and that citizens' rights are protected.

12. Which of the following is a constitutional provision aimed at confronting marginalisation?

  1. Imposing a ban on all religious festivals
  2. Providing reservations in education and employment for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes
  3. Encouraging private schools to admit only wealthy students
  4. Removing all government subsidies for essential goods

Answer: Providing reservations in education and employment for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes

Explanation: The Indian Constitution includes provisions like reservations in education and employment for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs). These affirmative action policies are designed to address historical injustices and ensure that marginalised communities have equal opportunities and representation.

13. What is the main purpose of laws related to 'minimum wages'?

  1. To ensure that employers pay workers less than a certain amount
  2. To guarantee a basic standard of living for workers by setting a floor for wages
  3. To allow employers to exploit workers without legal consequences
  4. To encourage child labour in industries

Answer: To guarantee a basic standard of living for workers by setting a floor for wages

Explanation: Minimum wage laws are enacted to ensure that workers receive a fair and basic income that allows them to meet their essential needs and maintain a decent standard of living. They prevent employers from paying extremely low wages and exploiting their workforce.

14. What is the primary role of the Lok Sabha in the Indian Parliament?

  1. To represent the states
  2. To elect the President of India
  3. To make laws and control the executive
  4. To interpret the Constitution

Answer: To make laws and control the executive

Explanation: The Lok Sabha, also known as the House of the People, is the lower house of the Indian Parliament. Its primary role is to make laws, discuss national issues, and hold the executive (government) accountable through various mechanisms like questions and debates.

15. Which of the following is the highest court in the Indian judicial system?

  1. High Court
  2. District Court
  3. Supreme Court
  4. Subordinate Court

Answer: Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court of India is the highest judicial body in the country. It is the final court of appeal and its decisions are binding on all other courts in India.

16. What is the purpose of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989?

  1. To promote inter-caste marriages
  2. To provide reservations in government jobs
  3. To protect SCs and STs from discrimination and violence
  4. To establish separate schools for SCs and STs

Answer: To protect SCs and STs from discrimination and violence

Explanation: The Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, was enacted to protect members of the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes from various forms of discrimination, humiliation, and violence, and to provide for special courts for the trial of such offences.

17. Which of the following is an example of a 'public facility' provided by the government?

  1. Private car manufacturing
  2. Luxury hotels
  3. Public healthcare services
  4. Designer clothing stores

Answer: Public healthcare services

Explanation: Public facilities are essential services provided by the government for the benefit of all citizens. Public healthcare services, like government hospitals and clinics, are a prime example, ensuring access to medical care for everyone, regardless of their ability to pay.

18. What is the main objective of laws related to 'minimum wages'?

  1. To ensure workers get maximum possible wages
  2. To prevent exploitation of workers by ensuring a basic living wage
  3. To encourage child labour
  4. To reduce the number of available jobs

Answer: To prevent exploitation of workers by ensuring a basic living wage

Explanation: Minimum wage laws are enacted to prevent the exploitation of workers by employers. They ensure that workers receive a basic minimum amount of remuneration for their labour, which is considered necessary for a decent standard of living, thereby promoting social justice.

19. What is one of the key ways the government tries to confront marginalisation?

  1. Ignoring the problems of marginalised groups.
  2. Implementing reservation policies for education and jobs.
  3. Forcing marginalised groups to change their culture.
  4. Preventing marginalised groups from participating in politics.

Answer: Implementing reservation policies for education and jobs.

Explanation: One of the key ways the Indian government tries to confront marginalisation is by implementing reservation policies. These policies aim to provide opportunities in education and government jobs to historically disadvantaged groups like Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes, to help them overcome historical inequalities and achieve social justice.

20. What is the main purpose of laws like the Minimum Wages Act?

  1. To allow employers to pay any wage they deem fit
  2. To ensure workers are paid a fair and decent wage
  3. To encourage child labour
  4. To reduce the number of available jobs

Answer: To ensure workers are paid a fair and decent wage

Explanation: The Minimum Wages Act is a crucial law related to social justice. Its main purpose is to ensure that workers are paid a fair and decent wage, preventing exploitation by employers and guaranteeing a basic standard of living. This promotes economic justice and protects the rights of labourers.

21. Who plays a crucial role in the 'making of laws' in India by introducing bills and debating them?

  1. The President of India
  2. The Prime Minister
  3. Members of Parliament (MPs)
  4. The Chief Justice of India

Answer: Members of Parliament (MPs)

Explanation: Members of Parliament (MPs) are elected representatives who introduce bills, participate in debates, and vote on proposed laws in both houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha). The President gives assent to bills after they are passed by both houses, making them laws.

22. What is the primary function of the Judiciary in India?

  1. To make laws for the country.
  2. To implement laws made by the Parliament.
  3. To interpret laws and administer justice.
  4. To represent India in international forums.

Answer: To interpret laws and administer justice.

Explanation: The primary function of the Judiciary is to interpret the laws made by the legislature, ensure their proper application, and administer justice by resolving disputes and upholding the rule of law. The legislature makes laws, and the executive implements them.

23. Which of the following groups is often subjected to 'marginalisation' in India?

  1. Wealthy industrialists
  2. Upper-caste individuals
  3. Adivasis (Indigenous people)
  4. Government officials

Answer: Adivasis (Indigenous people)

Explanation: Marginalisation refers to the process by which certain groups are pushed to the fringes of society, often facing discrimination, lack of opportunities, and social exclusion. Adivasis, Dalits, and other minority groups have historically faced and continue to face marginalisation in India.

24. What is one of the key ways the Indian Constitution attempts to 'confront marginalisation'?

  1. By promoting a single dominant culture.
  2. By establishing a system of reservations for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  3. By restricting freedom of speech for minority groups.
  4. By encouraging private schools to exclude certain communities.

Answer: By establishing a system of reservations for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

Explanation: The Indian Constitution includes provisions like reservations for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) in education and government jobs. This affirmative action aims to address historical injustices and ensure their equitable representation and participation in society, thereby confronting marginalisation.

25. What is the main purpose of 'Law and Social Justice' in India?

  1. To ensure that all laws benefit only the rich.
  2. To create laws that promote inequality in society.
  3. To protect the vulnerable and ensure fair treatment for all sections of society.
  4. To allow individuals to break laws without consequences.

Answer: To protect the vulnerable and ensure fair treatment for all sections of society.

Explanation: The concept of 'Law and Social Justice' in India aims to use legal frameworks to address social inequalities, protect the rights of the vulnerable, and ensure that all individuals, regardless of their background, receive fair treatment and have access to justice. It seeks to create a more equitable society.
