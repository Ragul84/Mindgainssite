26. In the story 'A Shirt in the Market', who is the last person to benefit the most from the sale of the shirt to the consumer?

  1. The cotton farmer
  2. The textile mill owner
  3. The garment exporter
  4. The large showroom owner

Answer: The large showroom owner

Explanation: The story 'A Shirt in the Market' illustrates the chain of production and trade, highlighting how profits are distributed. While all parties in the chain earn something, the large showroom owner, who sells the finished shirt to the final consumer, typically adds a significant markup and benefits the most from the final sale price, especially when compared to the meager earnings of the cotton farmer or the workers in the textile mill.

27. Which of the following is an example of a public healthcare facility in India?

  1. Private clinic run by a single doctor
  2. A large hospital owned by a business corporation
  3. Community Health Centre (CHC)
  4. A pharmacy selling medicines

Answer: Community Health Centre (CHC)

Explanation: Community Health Centres (CHCs) are part of the public healthcare system in India, established by the government to provide healthcare services to people, especially in rural areas, at affordable or no cost. Private clinics and corporate hospitals are part of the private healthcare system, while a pharmacy sells medicines but doesn't provide comprehensive healthcare services.

28. In a democratic government, who is responsible for making laws for the entire state?

  1. The Chief Minister alone
  2. The Governor
  3. The Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs)
  4. The District Collector

Answer: The Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs)

Explanation: In a state government, the Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs) are elected representatives who sit in the Legislative Assembly. Their primary role is to discuss issues, debate policies, and make laws for the state. The Chief Minister leads the government, the Governor is the ceremonial head, and the District Collector is an administrative officer.

29. What does the term 'media' primarily refer to?

  1. Only television news channels
  2. All forms of communication that reach a large audience
  3. Newspapers and magazines only
  4. Personal conversations between two people

Answer: All forms of communication that reach a large audience

Explanation: Media is a broad term that encompasses all forms of communication that are used to reach a large number of people, or a mass audience. This includes television, radio, newspapers, magazines, the internet, and social media. Personal conversations are not considered mass media.

30. Which of the following is an example of a weekly market?

  1. A large shopping mall open every day
  2. A small shop selling groceries in a neighbourhood
  3. A market that sets up on a specific day of the week in different localities
  4. An online store selling clothes

Answer: A market that sets up on a specific day of the week in different localities

Explanation: A weekly market is a temporary market that is set up on a particular day of the week in different places. Vendors bring their goods for sale for that one day and then move to a different location on another day. Shopping malls, small shops, and online stores are not examples of weekly markets.

31. What is the main objective of the 'Struggles for Equality'?

  1. To gain more wealth for a specific group
  2. To establish dominance of one community over another
  3. To ensure that all people are treated with dignity and have equal rights
  4. To promote a single religion in the country

Answer: To ensure that all people are treated with dignity and have equal rights

Explanation: The 'Struggles for Equality' chapter highlights various movements and efforts by different groups of people to achieve equal treatment, dignity, and rights in society, regardless of their caste, religion, gender, or economic status. It's about challenging discrimination and injustice.

32. In the context of 'On Equality', what does 'dignity' refer to?

  1. Having a lot of money
  2. Being respected and valued as a human being
  3. Holding a high position in society
  4. Wearing expensive clothes

Answer: Being respected and valued as a human being

Explanation: Dignity, in the context of equality, means that every individual, regardless of their background, status, or circumstances, deserves to be treated with respect and to feel valued as a human being. It's about inherent worth, not material possessions or social standing.

33. What is a common challenge faced by small farmers in the 'A Shirt in the Market' story?

  1. They have direct access to international buyers.
  2. They often have to borrow money at high interest rates from traders.
  3. They can easily set their own prices for their produce.
  4. They own large tracts of land and modern machinery.

Answer: They often have to borrow money at high interest rates from traders.

Explanation: The story 'A Shirt in the Market' illustrates how small farmers often lack capital and are forced to borrow money from traders or moneylenders, often at high interest rates. This puts them in a vulnerable position and reduces their bargaining power, as they are then obligated to sell their produce to these lenders at low prices.

34. Which of the following is a key feature of a democratic government, 'On Equality'?

  1. All citizens are considered equal before the law.
  2. Only a select few individuals have the right to vote.
  3. The government makes decisions without public consultation.
  4. People are not allowed to express their opinions freely.

Answer: All citizens are considered equal before the law.

Explanation: The chapter 'On Equality' emphasizes that in a democratic government, a fundamental principle is that all citizens, regardless of their background, caste, religion, or gender, are considered equal before the law. This ensures fairness and justice for everyone. Options B, C, and D describe characteristics that are contrary to the principles of a democratic and egalitarian society.
