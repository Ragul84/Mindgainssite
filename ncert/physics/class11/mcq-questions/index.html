26. What is the fundamental principle of the law of conservation of momentum?

  1. The total momentum of a system always increases over time.
  2. The total momentum of an isolated system of interacting particles remains constant.
  3. Momentum is conserved only if external forces are acting on the system.
  4. The momentum of individual particles within a system is always conserved.

Answer: The total momentum of an isolated system of interacting particles remains constant.

Explanation: The law of conservation of momentum states that for an isolated system (one where no external forces act), the total momentum of the system remains constant over time, regardless of interactions between particles within the system.

27. A body of mass 'm' is at rest on a horizontal table. If a small horizontal force 'F' is applied, and the body remains at rest, which of the following best explains this observation?

  1. Newton's second law (F=ma) is contradicted in this scenario.
  2. The applied force 'F' is not strong enough to overcome the body's inertia.
  3. There is an opposing horizontal force, such as static friction, that balances the applied force 'F', resulting in zero net force.
  4. The mass 'm' of the body is too large for the applied force 'F' to cause any acceleration.

Answer: There is an opposing horizontal force, such as static friction, that balances the applied force 'F', resulting in zero net force.

Explanation: If a body remains at rest despite an applied force, it means the net force acting on it is zero. This is consistent with Newton's second law (F_net = ma, where a=0). The opposing force is typically static friction, which adjusts its magnitude to match the applied force up to a certain limit, thus keeping the body at rest.

28. Which of the following is the primary reason for the occurrence of rolling friction?

  1. The continuous sliding of one surface over another.
  2. The strong adhesive forces between the rolling body and the surface.
  3. The momentary deformation of the surfaces in contact, leading to energy dissipation.
  4. The air resistance acting on the rolling body.

Answer: The momentary deformation of the surfaces in contact, leading to energy dissipation.

Explanation: Rolling friction arises from the complex process of continuous deformation and recovery of the surfaces in contact. As the rolling body moves, the surfaces deform slightly, creating a finite contact area. This deformation and subsequent recovery dissipate energy, which is manifested as rolling friction.

29. What is the significance of the 'optimum speed' (v_o = (R g tan θ)^(1/2)) for a vehicle on a banked road?

  1. At this speed, the vehicle experiences the maximum possible frictional force.
  2. It is the speed at which the vehicle is most likely to skid off the road.
  3. At this speed, the horizontal component of the normal reaction provides the entire centripetal force, eliminating the need for friction.
  4. It is the minimum speed required to safely navigate the banked curve.

Answer: At this speed, the horizontal component of the normal reaction provides the entire centripetal force, eliminating the need for friction.

Explanation: The optimum speed on a banked road is the specific speed at which the horizontal component of the normal reaction force is exactly sufficient to provide the necessary centripetal force. This means that no frictional force is required to keep the vehicle on the curve, leading to minimal wear and tear on the tyres and a safer ride.

30. Why are free-body diagrams considered essential tools in solving mechanics problems?

  1. They help in calculating the internal forces within a system.
  2. They simplify the problem by ignoring all forces acting on the system.
  3. They clearly define the system and identify all external forces acting on it.
  4. They are primarily used for visualizing the motion of the system, not for force analysis.

Answer: They clearly define the system and identify all external forces acting on it.

Explanation: Free-body diagrams are crucial because they isolate the system of interest and graphically represent all external forces acting *on* that system. This clear visualization helps in applying Newton's laws correctly by ensuring all relevant forces are accounted for and internal forces are excluded from the analysis of the system's motion.

31. In mechanics, terms like 'friction', 'normal reaction', 'tension', and 'weight' fundamentally represent what, and how should they be generally expressed for clarity?

  1. They represent different types of energy, and should be expressed as 'energy of A by B'.
  2. They are all types of forces, and should be reduced to the phrase 'force on A by B'.
  3. They represent different forms of momentum, and should be expressed as 'momentum of A by B'.
  4. They are all scalar quantities, and their direction is not important.

Answer: They are all types of forces, and should be reduced to the phrase 'force on A by B'.

Explanation: All these terms ('friction', 'normal reaction', 'tension', 'weight', 'centripetal force') are specific manifestations or contexts of the fundamental concept of 'force'. For clarity and consistency in mechanics, any force should be precisely described using the format 'force on A by B', indicating the object experiencing the force (A) and the object exerting the force (B).

32. A batsman deflects a ball of mass 0.15 kg by an angle of 45° without changing its initial speed of 54 km/h. What is the magnitude of the impulse imparted to the ball? (Given: √2 ≈ 1.414)

  1. 0.76 Ns
  2. 1.15 Ns
  3. 1.72 Ns
  4. 2.25 Ns

Answer: 1.72 Ns

Explanation: Mass of the ball, m = 0.15 kg.
Initial speed, u = 54 km/h = 54 * (1000/3600) m/s = 15 m/s.
Final speed, v = 15 m/s (speed remains unchanged).
Angle of deflection, θ = 45°.
Impulse imparted, J = Change in momentum, Δp = m(v⃗ - u⃗).
The magnitude of the change in velocity, |Δv⃗| = |v⃗ - u⃗|, can be found using the law of cosines:
|Δv⃗|² = |v⃗|² + |u⃗|² - 2|v⃗||u⃗|cos(θ)
|Δv⃗|² = 15² + 15² - 2(15)(15)cos(45°)
|Δv⃗|² = 2 * 15² - 2 * 15² * (1/√2)
|Δv⃗|² = 2 * 15² * (1 - 1/√2)
|Δv⃗| = 15 * √(2 * (1 - 1/√2)) = 15 * √(2 - √2)
|Δv⃗| = 15 * √(2 - 1.414) = 15 * √(0.586) ≈ 15 * 0.7655 ≈ 11.4825 m/s.
Magnitude of impulse, |J| = m * |Δv⃗| = 0.15 kg * 11.4825 m/s ≈ 1.722 Ns.

33. When two vectors are subjected to a scalar product (dot product) operation, the resulting quantity is:

  1. Always a vector quantity
  2. Always a scalar quantity
  3. Sometimes a scalar, sometimes a vector, depending on the vectors
  4. A dimensionless quantity

Answer: Always a scalar quantity

Explanation: The scalar product, also known as the dot product, of two vectors yields a scalar quantity. This means the result has only magnitude and no direction. For example, work done (W = F ⋅ d) is a scalar quantity.

34. In the context of work done by a variable force F(x) over a small displacement Δx, how is the work done (ΔW) best approximated?

  1. ΔW = F(x) / Δx
  2. ΔW = F(x) + Δx
  3. ΔW = F(x) ⋅ Δx
  4. ΔW = F(x) - Δx

Answer: ΔW = F(x) ⋅ Δx

Explanation: When the displacement Δx is very small, the variable force F(x) can be considered approximately constant over that interval. Therefore, the work done (ΔW) is approximated as the product of the force and the displacement, i.e., ΔW = F(x) ⋅ Δx. This forms the basis for calculating work done by integration for larger displacements.

35. In the relation F = -dV(h)/dh for gravitational force, what does the negative sign primarily signify?

  1. The force is always attractive.
  2. The force is directed upwards, opposite to the potential energy gradient.
  3. The force is directed downwards, opposite to the conventional positive direction for height 'h'.
  4. The potential energy is decreasing with increasing height.

Answer: The force is directed downwards, opposite to the conventional positive direction for height 'h'.

Explanation: The negative sign in F = -dV(h)/dh indicates that the gravitational force acts in a direction opposite to the increase in potential energy. If 'h' is taken as positive upwards, then an increase in 'h' leads to an increase in potential energy, but the gravitational force acts downwards. Hence, the negative sign signifies that the gravitational force is directed downwards, opposite to the upward positive direction for height 'h'.

36. A bob undergoing vertical circular motion has its string slacken at the highest point C. If the string is cut at this exact instant, what will be the subsequent motion of the bob?

  1. It will fall straight down vertically.
  2. It will continue in a circular path due to inertia.
  3. It will execute projectile motion with a horizontal projection.
  4. It will move upwards for some time before falling down.

Answer: It will execute projectile motion with a horizontal projection.

Explanation: When the string slackens at the highest point C, the tension (T_C) becomes zero. At this moment, the gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force (mg = mv_C²/L). If the string is cut, the bob will have a horizontal velocity (v_C) and will be under the influence of gravity. This scenario is identical to an object being kicked horizontally from a cliff, resulting in projectile motion with a horizontal projection.

37. Which of the following pairs correctly differentiates between a conservative and a non-conservative force, along with an appropriate example for each?

  1. Conservative: Friction (conserves mechanical energy); Non-conservative: Spring force (does not conserve mechanical energy)
  2. Conservative: Gravitational force (conserves mechanical energy); Non-conservative: Air resistance (does not conserve mechanical energy)
  3. Conservative: Applied force (conserves mechanical energy); Non-conservative: Magnetic force (does not conserve mechanical energy)
  4. Conservative: Normal force (conserves mechanical energy); Non-conservative: Elastic force (does not conserve mechanical energy)

Answer: Conservative: Gravitational force (conserves mechanical energy); Non-conservative: Air resistance (does not conserve mechanical energy)

Explanation: Conservative forces, such as gravitational force and spring force, allow for the conservation of mechanical energy (potential + kinetic). The work done by a conservative force depends only on the initial and final positions, not on the path taken. Non-conservative forces, such as friction and air resistance, dissipate mechanical energy, converting it into other forms like heat. The work done by a non-conservative force depends on the path taken.

38. Which of the following statements accurately defines an elastic collision based on energy conservation?

  1. An elastic collision is one where only the total linear momentum is conserved.
  2. An elastic collision is one where the total kinetic energy is conserved, but not necessarily the total linear momentum.
  3. An elastic collision is one where both the total linear momentum and the total kinetic energy of the system are conserved.
  4. An elastic collision is one where some kinetic energy is lost due to deformation, but momentum is conserved.

Answer: An elastic collision is one where both the total linear momentum and the total kinetic energy of the system are conserved.

Explanation: An elastic collision is characterized by the conservation of both total linear momentum and total kinetic energy of the system. This implies that there is no net loss of kinetic energy during the collision, meaning any deformation that occurs is fully recovered.

39. How does a 'scattering' event in physics differ from an everyday 'collision'?

  1. A 'collision' involves forces acting at a distance, while 'scattering' involves physical contact.
  2. A 'scattering' event always results in the objects sticking together, unlike a 'collision'.
  3. A 'collision' typically involves physical contact between bodies, whereas 'scattering' involves forces acting at a distance without necessarily touching.
  4. Both 'collision' and 'scattering' are identical terms used interchangeably in physics.

Answer: A 'collision' typically involves physical contact between bodies, whereas 'scattering' involves forces acting at a distance without necessarily touching.

Explanation: An everyday 'collision' typically implies physical contact between two bodies, like in billiards or carrom. In contrast, a 'scattering' event in physics involves interactions where forces act at a distance, and the bodies do not necessarily touch. Examples include a comet interacting with the Sun's gravity or an alpha particle being deflected by a nucleus.

40. For a particle moving under a potential energy function V(x) with total energy E, why can the particle not be found in regions where E < V(x)?

  1. Because the potential energy would be negative, which is impossible.
  2. Because the total energy would be negative, which is impossible.
  3. Because the kinetic energy (K = E - V(x)) would be negative, which is physically impossible.
  4. Because the force acting on the particle would be zero in such regions.

Answer: Because the kinetic energy (K = E - V(x)) would be negative, which is physically impossible.

Explanation: The total mechanical energy (E) of a particle is the sum of its kinetic energy (K) and potential energy (V), so E = K + V. Therefore, K = E - V. If E < V(x), then E - V(x) would be a negative value. Since kinetic energy (K = 1/2 mv²) must always be non-negative (as mass 'm' and velocity squared 'v²' are always positive or zero), a negative kinetic energy is physically impossible. Hence, the particle cannot exist in such regions.

41. The mass of air (m) passing through a circular area A in time t, when the wind flows at a velocity v perpendicular to the circle, is given by the formula:

  1. m = ρ × (A × v)
  2. m = ρ × (A × t)
  3. m = ρ × (A × v × t)
  4. m = ρ × (v × t)

Answer: m = ρ × (A × v × t)

Explanation: The mass of air (m) is the product of its density (ρ) and the volume of air passing through. The volume of air passing through the area A in time t is given by the product of the area and the distance covered by the wind, which is v × t. Therefore, the volume is A × v × t. So, m = ρ × (A × v × t).

42. To constrain a rigid body to exhibit only rotational motion, one must:

  1. Apply a force at its center of mass.
  2. Fix it along a straight line, which acts as its axis of rotation.
  3. Ensure all external forces are balanced.
  4. Provide it with a constant linear velocity.

Answer: Fix it along a straight line, which acts as its axis of rotation.

Explanation: A rigid body can be constrained to have only rotational motion by fixing it along a straight line. This fixed line then acts as its axis of rotation, preventing any translational movement of the body as a whole.

43. Which of the following is an example of rotational motion occurring about an axis that is NOT fixed?

  1. A spinning top on a table.
  2. A car wheel rotating while the car moves.
  3. An oscillating table fan.
  4. A merry-go-round in motion.

Answer: An oscillating table fan.

Explanation: An oscillating table fan is an example where rotational motion occurs about an axis that is not fixed. The fan blades rotate, but the entire fan assembly also oscillates, meaning its axis of rotation changes direction.

44. In Example 6.1, if three particles of unequal masses (100g, 150g, and 200g) are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle, why does the center of mass not coincide with the geometric center of the triangle?

  1. The triangle is equilateral.
  2. The particles are not point masses.
  3. The masses of the three particles are unequal.
  4. The triangle is not oriented symmetrically.

Answer: The masses of the three particles are unequal.

Explanation: The center of mass of a system of particles depends on both the positions and the masses of the particles. For the center of mass to coincide with the geometric center of a symmetrical object like an equilateral triangle, the masses distributed at its vertices must be identical. Since the masses (100g, 150g, and 200g) are unequal, the center of mass will be shifted towards the heavier masses, thus not coinciding with the geometric center.

45. Why do the internal forces generated during an explosion of a projectile not alter the trajectory of its center of mass?

  1. Internal forces are always balanced.
  2. The explosion occurs instantaneously.
  3. Only external forces can change the motion of the center of mass.
  4. The projectile's mass changes during the explosion.

Answer: Only external forces can change the motion of the center of mass.

Explanation: According to Newton's second law for a system of particles, the acceleration of the center of mass is determined solely by the net external force acting on the system. Internal forces, by definition, act between particles within the system and cancel out in pairs (Newton's third law), thus having no net effect on the motion of the center of mass. Therefore, the trajectory of the center of mass remains unchanged by internal forces like those from an explosion.

46. The magnitude of the vector product of two vectors 'a' and 'b', denoted as c = a × b, is given by:

  1. c = a + b
  2. c = ab cosθ
  3. c = ab sinθ
  4. c = a / b

Answer: c = ab sinθ

Explanation: The magnitude of the vector product (or cross product) of two vectors 'a' and 'b' is defined as c = ab sinθ, where 'a' and 'b' are the magnitudes of the respective vectors, and θ is the smaller angle between them. The '×' symbol signifies the cross product.

47. How is pure rotation of a rigid body characterized?

  1. All parts of the body have the same linear velocity.
  2. All parts of the body have the same angular acceleration.
  3. All parts of the body have the same angular velocity.
  4. The center of mass remains stationary.

Answer: All parts of the body have the same angular velocity.

Explanation: Pure rotation of a rigid body is characterized by all its constituent particles or parts having the same angular velocity at any given instant of time. In contrast, pure translation is characterized by all parts having the same linear velocity.

48. What are the dimensions of the moment of force (torque)?

  1. M L T⁻¹
  2. M L² T⁻²
  3. M L T⁻²
  4. M L⁻¹ T⁻²

Answer: M L² T⁻²

Explanation: Moment of force (torque, τ) is defined as the product of force (F) and the perpendicular distance (r) from the axis of rotation to the line of action of the force (τ = r × F). The dimensions of force are [M L T⁻²] and the dimensions of distance are [L]. Therefore, the dimensions of torque are [L] × [M L T⁻²] = [M L² T⁻²].

49. Under which conditions does the contribution of internal forces to the total torque on a system of particles become zero?

  1. Only if Newton's third law of motion holds.
  2. Only if internal forces are directed along the line joining the particles.
  3. If Newton's third law of motion holds AND internal forces are directed along the line joining the particles.
  4. If the system is in translational equilibrium.

Answer: If Newton's third law of motion holds AND internal forces are directed along the line joining the particles.

Explanation: The contribution of internal forces to the total torque on a system of particles becomes zero when two conditions are met: 1) Newton's third law of motion applies, ensuring that forces between any two particles are equal and opposite, and 2) these internal forces are directed along the line connecting the two particles. This alignment causes the torque generated by each action-reaction pair of internal forces to cancel out.

50. For a rigid body under the action of coplanar forces, which set of conditions ensures mechanical equilibrium?

  1. The sum of forces along one axis is zero, and the sum of torques about one point is zero.
  2. The sum of forces along any two perpendicular axes is zero, and the sum of torques about any axis perpendicular to the plane is zero.
  3. The sum of forces along any two perpendicular axes is zero, and the sum of torques about any point in the plane is zero.
  4. The sum of forces along one axis is zero, and the sum of torques about any axis perpendicular to the plane is zero.

Answer: The sum of forces along any two perpendicular axes is zero, and the sum of torques about any axis perpendicular to the plane is zero.

Explanation: For coplanar forces, mechanical equilibrium requires two conditions for translational equilibrium (sum of force components along any two perpendicular axes in the plane must be zero) and one condition for rotational equilibrium (sum of torque components along any axis perpendicular to the plane of forces must be zero).
