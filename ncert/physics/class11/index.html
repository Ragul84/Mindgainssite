1. Which of the following best describes the fundamental process involved in measuring any physical quantity?

  1. Comparing it with a certain basic, arbitrarily chosen, internationally accepted reference standard called a unit.
  2. Estimating its value based on prior knowledge and experience.
  3. Using a sophisticated instrument to determine its exact magnitude.
  4. Counting the number of times a standard object fits into the quantity.

Answer: Comparing it with a certain basic, arbitrarily chosen, internationally accepted reference standard called a unit.

Explanation: The measurement of any physical quantity fundamentally involves comparing it to a predefined, universally accepted reference standard, which is known as a unit. This comparison allows for consistent and reproducible measurements across different contexts.

2. If the period of oscillation of a simple pendulum is reported as 1.62 s, which digit is considered 'uncertain' and how does it affect the significant figures?

  1. The digit '1' is uncertain, resulting in one significant figure.
  2. The digit '6' is uncertain, resulting in two significant figures.
  3. The digit '2' is uncertain, resulting in three significant figures.
  4. All digits are certain, resulting in three significant figures.

Answer: The digit '2' is uncertain, resulting in three significant figures.

Explanation: In a measurement like 1.62 s, the digits '1' and '6' are considered reliable or certain. The last digit, '2', is the first uncertain digit. Its inclusion indicates the precision of the measurement, and thus, the measurement has three significant figures (1, 6, and 2).

3. What is the primary rule for maintaining accuracy when performing arithmetic operations with measured values having a limited number of significant figures?

  1. The result should always have more significant figures than any of the original measured values.
  2. The result should be rounded to the nearest whole number.
  3. The result must reflect the uncertainties in the original measured values and cannot be more accurate than the least accurate original measurement.
  4. The result should have the same number of significant figures as the most accurate original measurement.

Answer: The result must reflect the uncertainties in the original measured values and cannot be more accurate than the least accurate original measurement.

Explanation: When performing calculations with measured values, the accuracy of the result is limited by the least precise measurement. Therefore, the final result should not have more significant figures than the original data with the least number of significant figures, ensuring it reflects the inherent uncertainties.

4. Why is it recommended to retain one more significant figure in intermediate results of a multi-step computation?

  1. To make the calculations simpler and faster.
  2. To ensure the final answer is always an integer.
  3. To avoid the accumulation of rounding errors and prevent significant deviation from the accurate final result.
  4. To match the number of significant figures of the most precise measurement in the calculation.

Answer: To avoid the accumulation of rounding errors and prevent significant deviation from the accurate final result.

Explanation: Retaining one extra significant figure in intermediate steps (often called 'guard digit') helps to minimize the impact of rounding errors. Premature rounding at each step can compound these errors, leading to a final result that is less accurate than it should be.

5. What dimensional characteristic must the arguments of special functions like trigonometric, logarithmic, and exponential functions always possess?

  1. They must be expressed in SI units.
  2. They must be dimensionless.
  3. They must have the dimension of length.
  4. They must have the dimension of time.

Answer: They must be dimensionless.

Explanation: For mathematical consistency and physical validity, the arguments of special functions such as sin(θ), log(x), or e^y must always be dimensionless. This ensures that the function's output is also dimensionless or has a consistent dimension, regardless of the units used for the input quantities.

6. What is a 'system of units' defined as?

  1. A collection of only fundamental (base) units.
  2. A complete set of units, encompassing both fundamental (base) units and derived units.
  3. A set of units used exclusively for measuring length, mass, and time.
  4. Any arbitrary collection of units used in scientific measurements.

Answer: A complete set of units, encompassing both fundamental (base) units and derived units.

Explanation: A 'system of units' refers to a comprehensive and coherent collection that includes both the fundamental (or base) units (like meter, kilogram, second) and all the derived units (like Newton, Joule, Watt) that are formed from these base units. The SI system is a prime example.

7. One mole of an ideal gas at STP occupies 22.4 L. If the atomic volume of a mole of hydrogen is approximately 3.156 × 10⁻⁷ m³, what is the approximate ratio of molar volume to atomic volume, and why is it so large?

  1. Approximately 7.1 × 10², because gas molecules are very small.
  2. Approximately 7.1 × 10⁴, because gas molecules are widely separated with much empty space.
  3. Approximately 7.1 × 10⁻², because gas molecules are tightly packed.
  4. Approximately 7.1 × 10⁻⁴, because the atomic volume is much larger than molar volume.

Answer: Approximately 7.1 × 10⁴, because gas molecules are widely separated with much empty space.

Explanation: Molar volume = 22.4 L = 22.4 × 10⁻³ m³. Atomic volume ≈ 3.156 × 10⁻⁷ m³. Ratio = (22.4 × 10⁻³ m³) / (3.156 × 10⁻⁷ m³) ≈ 7.098 × 10⁴. This large ratio indicates that in a gas, molecules are widely separated, and the vast majority of the volume is empty space between them, unlike in liquids or solids.

8. How does differential calculus help determine instantaneous velocity when the position of an object is known as a function of time?

  1. By calculating the average velocity over a very small time interval.
  2. By finding the integral of the position function with respect to time.
  3. By taking the derivative of the position function with respect to time.
  4. By simply dividing the total displacement by the total time taken.

Answer: By taking the derivative of the position function with respect to time.

Explanation: In kinematics, if the position (x) of an object is given as a function of time (t), the instantaneous velocity (v) at any given moment can be precisely determined by taking the first derivative of the position function with respect to time, i.e., v = dx/dt.

9. In real-world physical situations, why are graphs of position, velocity, and acceleration typically smooth without sharp kinks?

  1. Physical quantities are always discrete, leading to smooth transitions.
  2. Changes in physical quantities are always continuous, making the functions differentiable at all points.
  3. Idealized models always assume smooth graphs for simplicity.
  4. Measurement errors always smooth out any sharp changes in real data.

Answer: Changes in physical quantities are always continuous, making the functions differentiable at all points.

Explanation: In physics, real-world changes in physical quantities like position, velocity, and acceleration are continuous. This continuity implies that the functions representing these quantities are differentiable at all points, which graphically translates to smooth curves without abrupt changes or sharp kinks. Abrupt changes would imply infinite acceleration or velocity, which is physically impossible.

10. Galileo's law of odd numbers states that for a body falling from rest, the distances traversed in successive equal time intervals are in the ratio 1:3:5:.... Which of the following equations is fundamental to deriving this law?

  1. v = u + at
  2. v² = u² + 2as
  3. s = ut + ½at²
  4. s = (u+v)/2 * t

Answer: s = ut + ½at²

Explanation: Galileo's law of odd numbers for a body falling from rest (initial velocity u=0) is derived using the equation of motion s = ut + ½at². Since u=0 and a=g (acceleration due to gravity), the distance fallen is y = ½gt². By calculating the distance fallen at times τ, 2τ, 3τ, etc., and then finding the difference in distances for successive equal time intervals, the 1:3:5 ratio emerges.

11. Under what condition can negative acceleration lead to an increase in an object's speed?

  1. When the object is moving in the positive direction and acceleration is negative.
  2. When the object is moving in the negative direction and acceleration is positive.
  3. When both the object's velocity and acceleration are in the negative direction.
  4. Negative acceleration always decreases speed, never increases it.

Answer: When both the object's velocity and acceleration are in the negative direction.

Explanation: Speed is the magnitude of velocity. Negative acceleration means the acceleration vector points in the negative direction. If an object is already moving in the negative direction (negative velocity), and the acceleration is also in the negative direction, then the acceleration is acting in the same direction as the velocity. This causes the magnitude of the velocity (speed) to increase. For example, a car moving left (negative velocity) and braking hard (negative acceleration, assuming positive is right) will slow down. But if it's moving left and the engine is pushing it harder left (negative acceleration), its speed will increase.

12. A police van moving at 30 km/h fires a bullet with a muzzle speed of 150 m/s at a thief's car speeding away at 192 km/h in the same direction. What is the speed with which the bullet hits the thief's car?

  1. 105 m/s
  2. 150 m/s
  3. 158.33 m/s
  4. 53.33 m/s

Answer: 105 m/s

Explanation: First, convert all speeds to m/s: Police van speed = 30 km/h = 30 * (5/18) m/s ≈ 8.33 m/s. Thief's car speed = 192 km/h = 192 * (5/18) m/s ≈ 53.33 m/s. Muzzle speed of bullet = 150 m/s. The speed of the bullet with respect to the ground is the muzzle speed plus the van's speed (since it's fired in the same direction): 150 m/s + 8.33 m/s = 158.33 m/s. The speed with which the bullet hits the thief's car is the relative speed between the bullet and the car: |158.33 m/s - 53.33 m/s| = 105 m/s.

13. What are the two essential conditions for two vectors, A and B, to be considered equal?

  1. They must have the same magnitude and be parallel.
  2. They must have the same direction and be collinear.
  3. They must have the same magnitude and the same direction.
  4. They must originate from the same point and have the same magnitude.

Answer: They must have the same magnitude and the same direction.

Explanation: For two vectors to be considered equal, they must satisfy two fundamental conditions: they must have precisely the same magnitude (length) and they must point in exactly the same direction. If either of these conditions is not met, the vectors are not equal.

14. If rain is falling vertically with velocity 'v_r' and wind is blowing horizontally with velocity 'v_w', what is the magnitude of the resultant velocity of the rain as observed by someone on the ground?

  1. v_r + v_w
  2. |v_r - v_w|
  3. √(v_r² + v_w²)
  4. √(v_r² - v_w²)

Answer: √(v_r² + v_w²)

Explanation: Since the vertical velocity of rain and the horizontal velocity of wind are perpendicular to each other, their resultant velocity can be found using the Pythagorean theorem. The magnitude of the resultant velocity (R) is given by R = √(v_r² + v_w²).

15. The statement 'each component of the resultant vector R is the sum of the corresponding components of A and B' is significant because it implies:

  1. Vector addition is always commutative.
  2. Vector addition can be simplified into scalar additions along perpendicular axes.
  3. The magnitude of the resultant vector is always the sum of the magnitudes of individual vectors.
  4. Vectors can only be added if they are collinear.

Answer: Vector addition can be simplified into scalar additions along perpendicular axes.

Explanation: This statement highlights the power of vector resolution. By breaking down vectors into their components along mutually perpendicular axes (like x, y, and z), the complex process of vector addition is reduced to simple algebraic (scalar) addition of corresponding components. This simplifies calculations significantly, as the effect of the resultant vector along any axis is simply the sum of the individual vector's effects along that same axis.

16. How is instantaneous acceleration determined graphically from an object's motion?

  1. By calculating the slope of the position-time graph at a specific point.
  2. By finding the area under the velocity-time graph.
  3. By considering the average acceleration over increasingly smaller time intervals (Δt → 0).
  4. By measuring the tangent to the acceleration-time graph.

Answer: By considering the average acceleration over increasingly smaller time intervals (Δt → 0).

Explanation: Instantaneous acceleration is defined as the limit of the average acceleration as the time interval (Δt) approaches zero. Graphically, this means we look at the average acceleration over progressively smaller time intervals. As Δt approaches zero, the average acceleration converges to the instantaneous acceleration, and its direction is the direction of the change in velocity (Δv) in that limit. This is equivalent to finding the slope of the tangent to the velocity-time graph at that instant.

17. For a projectile launched with initial velocity v₀ at an angle θ₀ with the horizontal, what is the formula for the time taken to reach its maximum height (tₘ)?

  1. tₘ = (v₀ cos θ₀) / g
  2. tₘ = (v₀ sin θ₀) / g
  3. tₘ = (2v₀ sin θ₀) / g
  4. tₘ = (v₀² sin² θ₀) / (2g)

Answer: tₘ = (v₀ sin θ₀) / g

Explanation: At the maximum height of a projectile's trajectory, the vertical component of its velocity (vₚ) becomes zero. Using the first equation of motion for vertical motion, vₚ = uₚ + at, where uₚ = v₀ sin θ₀ and a = -g. So, 0 = v₀ sin θ₀ - gtₘ. Rearranging this gives tₘ = (v₀ sin θ₀) / g.

18. In the derivation of centripetal acceleration for uniform circular motion, which geometric property is crucial for relating the change in position and velocity vectors?

  1. The Pythagorean theorem applied to the position vectors.
  2. The similarity between the triangle formed by position vectors and the triangle formed by velocity vectors.
  3. The law of cosines applied to the velocity vectors.
  4. The fact that the magnitude of velocity remains constant.

Answer: The similarity between the triangle formed by position vectors and the triangle formed by velocity vectors.

Explanation: The key insight is that the triangle formed by the position vectors (r, r', Δr) and the triangle formed by the velocity vectors (v, v', Δv) are similar. This similarity arises because the velocity vector is always perpendicular to the position vector, meaning the angle between r and r' is equal to the angle between v and v'. This allows us to set up a ratio of corresponding sides, which is fundamental in deriving the magnitude of centripetal acceleration.

19. Which of the following expressions correctly defines the instantaneous velocity (v) of an object at time t?

  1. v = Δr / Δt
  2. v = lim (Ιt→∞) (Δr / Δt)
  3. v = lim (Δt→0) (Δr / Δt)
  4. v = dr / dt, where dr is the total displacement.

Answer: v = lim (Δt→0) (Δr / Δt)

Explanation: Instantaneous velocity is defined as the limiting value of the average velocity as the time interval Δt approaches zero. This is mathematically represented as the derivative of the position vector (r) with respect to time (t), i.e., v = dr/dt. Option 2 is incorrect because the limit should be as Δt approaches zero, not infinity. Option 0 represents average velocity, not instantaneous velocity. Option 3 is partially correct but the limit definition is more precise.

20. Consider three vectors A, B, and C that do not lie in the same plane. Can these three vectors add up to give a null vector (zero resultant)?

  1. Yes, if their magnitudes are appropriately chosen.
  2. Yes, if they form a closed triangle in 3D space.
  3. No, because their resultant (A+B) would not be coplanar with C.
  4. No, unless one of the vectors is a null vector itself.

Answer: No, because their resultant (A+B) would not be coplanar with C.

Explanation: For three vectors A, B, and C to sum to a null vector (A + B + C = 0), it implies that A + B = -C. The vector (A + B) is the resultant of A and B, and it must lie in the plane formed by A and B. For A + B to be equal to -C, vector C must also lie in the same plane as A and B. Therefore, if the three vectors are not coplanar, their sum cannot be a null vector. This statement is true.

21. According to the NCERT Class 11 Physics text, what was the primary focus of the chapter preceding the discussion on laws of motion?

  1. Understanding the forces causing motion.
  2. Describing the motion of a particle in space quantitatively.
  3. Analyzing energy transformations in physical systems.
  4. Studying the properties of matter at rest.

Answer: Describing the motion of a particle in space quantitatively.

Explanation: The chapter 'Motion in a Plane' (or 'Kinematics') typically precedes 'Laws of Motion' in the NCERT Class 11 Physics syllabus. The primary concern of kinematics is to describe motion (position, velocity, acceleration) without considering the forces causing it. Therefore, describing the motion of a particle in space quantitatively is the correct answer.

22. What was Galileo's significant conclusion regarding the states of rest and uniform linear motion?

  1. Rest is a natural state, while uniform linear motion requires a continuous force.
  2. Both states are equivalent and imply no net force acting on the body.
  3. Uniform linear motion is a state of constant acceleration.
  4. Rest is a state of zero velocity, while uniform linear motion is a state of zero acceleration.

Answer: Both states are equivalent and imply no net force acting on the body.

Explanation: Galileo's experiments and reasoning led to the profound insight that the state of rest and the state of uniform linear motion (constant velocity) are fundamentally equivalent. In both cases, there is no net external force acting on the body. This concept laid the groundwork for Newton's First Law of Motion (Law of Inertia).

23. When a bus starts moving moderately, why is a passenger able to accelerate along with it without slipping?

  1. Due to the passenger's inertia, which keeps them moving forward.
  2. The normal force from the floor pushes the passenger forward.
  3. The static friction between the passenger's feet and the bus floor provides the necessary force.
  4. Air resistance pushes the passenger along with the bus.

Answer: The static friction between the passenger's feet and the bus floor provides the necessary force.

Explanation: For the passenger to accelerate with the bus, a force must act on them in the direction of the bus's acceleration. This force is provided by the static friction between the passenger's feet and the bus floor. If the acceleration is moderate, this static friction is sufficient to prevent slipping. Without friction, the passenger's feet would remain in their initial position relative to the ground, and the bus floor would slide forward beneath them.

24. Which of the following statements accurately represents Newton's Second Law of Motion?

  1. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  2. An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction unless acted upon by an unbalanced force.
  3. The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.
  4. Force is equal to mass times acceleration (F = ma).

Answer: The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.

Explanation: Newton's Second Law of Motion, in its most fundamental form, states that the net external force acting on an object is directly proportional to the rate of change of its momentum, and this change in momentum occurs in the direction of the applied force. While F=ma is a common derived form for constant mass, the definition involving momentum is more general. Option 0 is Newton's Third Law, and Option 1 is Newton's First Law.

25. Which of the following statements accurately describes forces according to Newton's Third Law?

  1. Forces always occur singly in nature.
  2. Mutual forces between two interacting bodies are always equal in magnitude and in the same direction.
  3. Forces always occur in pairs, and the mutual forces between two interacting bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  4. Forces can occur singly or in pairs, depending on the situation.

Answer: Forces always occur in pairs, and the mutual forces between two interacting bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

Explanation: Newton's Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This means forces never occur singly; they always come in pairs. These mutual forces are always equal in magnitude but act in opposite directions on the two interacting bodies.
