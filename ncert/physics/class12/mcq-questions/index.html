26. A proton and an alpha particle are accelerated through the same potential difference. The ratio of their de Broglie wavelengths (λ_proton : λ_alpha) is:

  1. 1:2
  2. 2√2:1
  3. √2:1
  4. 1:√2

Answer: 2√2:1

Explanation: The de Broglie wavelength is given by λ = h/p, where p is momentum. For a charged particle accelerated through a potential difference V, kinetic energy K = qV. Also, K = p²/2m, so p = √(2mK) = √(2mqV). Thus, λ = h/√(2mqV). For a proton, q_p = e, m_p = m. For an alpha particle, q_α = 2e, m_α = 4m. So, λ_p / λ_α = [h/√(2m_p q_p V)] / [h/√(2m_α q_α V)] = √(m_α q_α / m_p q_p) = √((4m)(2e) / (m)(e)) = √(8) = 2√2. Therefore, λ_proton : λ_alpha = 2√2 : 1.

27. In a Young's double-slit experiment, the intensity of light at a point on the screen where the path difference is λ/6 (λ is the wavelength of light) is I. If I₀ is the maximum intensity, then I is equal to:

  1. I₀/2
  2. 3I₀/4
  3. I₀
  4. I₀/4

Answer: 3I₀/4

Explanation: The intensity at a point in Young's double-slit experiment is given by I = I₀ cos²(φ/2), where φ is the phase difference. The phase difference is related to the path difference (Δx) by φ = (2π/λ)Δx. Given Δx = λ/6, φ = (2π/λ)(λ/6) = π/3. So, I = I₀ cos²( (π/3)/2 ) = I₀ cos²(π/6). Since cos(π/6) = √3/2, I = I₀ (√3/2)² = I₀ (3/4) = 3I₀/4.

28. A long straight wire carries a current I. A rectangular loop of wire with sides a and b is placed with its longer side parallel to the straight wire at a distance r from it. The mutual inductance between the straight wire and the loop is proportional to:

  1. ln(1 + b/r)
  2. ln(1 + a/r)
  3. ln(1 + a/(r+b))
  4. ln(1 + b/(r+a))

Answer: ln(1 + a/r)

Explanation: The mutual inductance M between a long straight wire and a rectangular loop is given by M = (μ₀a/2π) ln((r+b)/r) = (μ₀a/2π) ln(1 + b/r), where 'a' is the side of the loop parallel to the wire, 'b' is the side perpendicular to the wire, and 'r' is the distance of the closer side from the wire. In this question, the longer side 'b' is parallel to the straight wire, and the shorter side 'a' is perpendicular. So, the formula becomes M = (μ₀b/2π) ln((r+a)/r) = (μ₀b/2π) ln(1 + a/r). Therefore, the mutual inductance is proportional to ln(1 + a/r).

29. Which of the following phenomena cannot be explained by the wave nature of light?

  1. Diffraction
  2. Interference
  3. Photoelectric effect
  4. Polarization

Answer: Photoelectric effect

Explanation: Diffraction, interference, and polarization are all phenomena that can be successfully explained by the wave nature of light. However, the photoelectric effect, which involves the emission of electrons from a metal surface when light shines on it, cannot be explained by the wave theory. It requires the particle (photon) nature of light, where light energy is absorbed in discrete packets (quanta).

30. A series LCR circuit is connected to an AC source. At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is:

  1. R
  2. √(R² + (XL - XC)²)
  3. XL - XC
  4. R + XL + XC

Answer: R

Explanation: The impedance of a series LCR circuit is given by Z = √(R² + (XL - XC)²), where R is resistance, XL is inductive reactance, and XC is capacitive reactance. At resonance, the inductive reactance (XL) becomes equal to the capacitive reactance (XC), i.e., XL = XC. Therefore, (XL - XC) = 0, and the impedance at resonance simplifies to Z = √(R² + 0²) = R.

31. The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by E_n = -13.6/n² eV. The energy required to excite an electron from the ground state to the first excited state is:

  1. 13.6 eV
  2. 3.4 eV
  3. 10.2 eV
  4. 1.51 eV

Answer: 10.2 eV

Explanation: The ground state corresponds to n=1, so E₁ = -13.6/1² = -13.6 eV. The first excited state corresponds to n=2, so E₂ = -13.6/2² = -13.6/4 = -3.4 eV. The energy required to excite the electron from the ground state to the first excited state is the difference between these two energy levels: ΔE = E₂ - E₁ = (-3.4 eV) - (-13.6 eV) = 10.2 eV.

32. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a p-n junction diode in forward bias?

  1. The width of the depletion region decreases.
  2. The potential barrier decreases.
  3. The majority charge carriers move across the junction.
  4. The resistance of the junction increases significantly.

Answer: The resistance of the junction increases significantly.

Explanation: In forward bias, the external voltage opposes the potential barrier, causing it to decrease. This reduction in potential barrier allows majority charge carriers to easily cross the junction, leading to a significant current flow. Consequently, the width of the depletion region decreases, and the resistance of the junction *decreases* significantly, not increases. Therefore, the statement that the resistance increases significantly is incorrect.

33. A convex lens of focal length f is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length f'. The power of the combination is zero. Which of the following is true?

  1. f = f'
  2. f = -f'
  3. f = 2f'
  4. f' = 2f

Answer: f = f'

Explanation: The power of a lens is given by P = 1/f. For a combination of two lenses in contact, the total power P_total = P₁ + P₂. Here, P₁ = 1/f (for convex lens) and P₂ = 1/f' (for concave lens, where f' is typically given as a negative value for a concave lens, but if f' represents the magnitude of the focal length, then P₂ = -1/f'). If the power of the combination is zero, then P₁ + P₂ = 0. So, 1/f + 1/f' = 0. This implies 1/f = -1/f', or f = -f'. However, if f' is already taken as the focal length of the concave lens (which is negative), then 1/f + 1/f' = 0 means 1/f = -1/f'. If we consider the magnitude of focal lengths, then for the power to be zero, the powers must cancel out. A convex lens has positive power (1/f) and a concave lens has negative power (-1/|f'|). So, 1/f - 1/|f'| = 0, which means 1/f = 1/|f'|, or f = |f'|. Therefore, if f' refers to the magnitude of the focal length of the concave lens, then f = f'.

34. An electron is accelerated through a potential difference V. Its de Broglie wavelength is proportional to:

  1. V
  2. 1/V
  3. 1/√V
  4. √V

Answer: 1/√V

Explanation: The de Broglie wavelength is given by λ = h/p, where p is the momentum. For an electron accelerated through a potential difference V, its kinetic energy is eV. So, 1/2 mv² = eV, which means p = mv = √(2meV). Substituting this into the de Broglie wavelength formula, λ = h/√(2meV). Therefore, λ is proportional to 1/√V.

35. In a Young's double-slit experiment, if the separation between the slits is doubled, the fringe width will:

  1. Be halved
  2. Be doubled
  3. Remain unchanged
  4. Become four times

Answer: Be halved

Explanation: The fringe width (β) in Young's double-slit experiment is given by the formula β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, D is the distance of the screen from the slits, and d is the separation between the slits. If 'd' is doubled, then β' = λD/(2d) = β/2. Thus, the fringe width will be halved.

36. The energy of a photon of wavelength λ is given by:

  1. hc/λ
  2. hλ/c
  3. h/cλ
  4. cλ/h

Answer: hc/λ

Explanation: According to Planck's quantum theory, the energy of a photon (E) is directly proportional to its frequency (ν). So, E = hν, where h is Planck's constant. We also know that the speed of light (c) is related to frequency and wavelength by c = νλ, which means ν = c/λ. Substituting this into the energy equation, we get E = hc/λ.

37. Which of the following gates is known as a universal gate?

  1. OR gate
  2. AND gate
  3. NOT gate
  4. NAND gate

Answer: NAND gate

Explanation: NAND and NOR gates are known as universal gates because any other logic gate (AND, OR, NOT, XOR, XNOR) can be constructed using only NAND gates or only NOR gates. This makes them highly versatile in digital circuit design.

38. The phenomenon of electromagnetic induction is the production of:

  1. Electric current due to changing magnetic field.
  2. Magnetic field due to changing electric current.
  3. Electric field due to changing magnetic field.
  4. Magnetic field due to changing electric field.

Answer: Electric current due to changing magnetic field.

Explanation: Electromagnetic induction, as described by Faraday's laws, states that a changing magnetic flux through a coil induces an electromotive force (EMF), which in turn drives an electric current if the circuit is closed. Therefore, it is the production of electric current due to a changing magnetic field.

39. A proton and an alpha particle are accelerated through the same potential difference. The ratio of their de Broglie wavelengths (λ_p : λ_α) is:

  1. 1:1
  2. √2:1
  3. 2√2:1
  4. 1:2√2

Answer: 2√2:1

Explanation: The de Broglie wavelength is λ = h/√(2m qV). For a proton, mass m_p = m, charge q_p = e. For an alpha particle, mass m_α = 4m, charge q_α = 2e. Since V is the same for both: λ_p / λ_α = [h/√(2m_p q_p V)] / [h/√(2m_α q_α V)] = √(m_α q_α / m_p q_p) = √((4m)(2e) / (m)(e)) = √(8) = 2√2. So, λ_p : λ_α = 2√2 : 1.

40. The instantaneous current in an AC circuit is given by I = 2 sin(314t + π/6) A. The RMS value of the current is:

  1. 2 A
  2. √2 A
  3. 1 A
  4. 2/√2 A

Answer: √2 A

Explanation: The instantaneous current in an AC circuit is given by I = I₀ sin(ωt + φ), where I₀ is the peak current. In the given equation, I₀ = 2 A. The RMS (Root Mean Square) value of the current is given by I_rms = I₀/√2. Therefore, I_rms = 2/√2 = √2 A.

41. An electron is accelerated through a potential difference V. Its de Broglie wavelength is λ. If the accelerating potential is increased to 4V, the new de Broglie wavelength will be:

  1. λ/2
  2. 2λ
  3. λ/4
  4. 4λ

Answer: λ/2

Explanation: The de Broglie wavelength is given by λ = h/p, where p is the momentum. For an electron accelerated through a potential V, its kinetic energy is eV, so p = √(2meV). Therefore, λ = h/√(2meV). If the potential is increased to 4V, the new wavelength λ' = h/√(2me(4V)) = h/(2√(2meV)) = λ/2.

42. Which of the following phenomena is NOT explained by the wave nature of light?

  1. Interference
  2. Diffraction
  3. Polarization
  4. Photoelectric effect

Answer: Photoelectric effect

Explanation: Interference, diffraction, and polarization are all phenomena that can be explained by the wave nature of light. The photoelectric effect, however, demonstrates the particle nature of light (photons) and cannot be explained by classical wave theory.

43. A long straight wire carries a current I. A rectangular loop is placed near the wire as shown. If the current in the wire is increasing, the direction of the induced current in the loop will be:

  1. Clockwise
  2. Anticlockwise
  3. No induced current
  4. Depends on the dimensions of the loop

Answer: Anticlockwise

Explanation: According to Ampere's law, a current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field. For a straight wire, the magnetic field lines are concentric circles around the wire. Using the right-hand thumb rule, if the current is upwards, the magnetic field lines inside the loop (closer to the wire) will be directed into the page. As the current I increases, the magnetic flux into the page through the loop increases. By Lenz's law, the induced current will oppose this change. To oppose an increasing magnetic flux into the page, the induced current must create a magnetic field out of the page. Using the right-hand thumb rule again, an anticlockwise current in the loop will produce a magnetic field out of the page. Therefore, the induced current will be anticlockwise.

44. The energy of a photon of wavelength λ is E. What is the energy of a photon of wavelength λ/2?

  1. E/2
  2. 2E
  3. E
  4. 4E

Answer: 2E

Explanation: The energy of a photon is given by E = hc/λ, where h is Planck's constant and c is the speed of light. If the wavelength is λ/2, the new energy E' = hc/(λ/2) = 2hc/λ. Since E = hc/λ, then E' = 2E.

45. A convex lens of focal length f is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length f. The equivalent focal length of the combination is:

  1. f
  2. 2f
  3. 0
  4. Infinity

Answer: Infinity

Explanation: The formula for the equivalent focal length (F) of two thin lenses in contact is 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂. For a convex lens, the focal length is positive (+f). For a concave lens, the focal length is negative (-f). So, 1/F = 1/(+f) + 1/(-f) = 1/f - 1/f = 0. Therefore, 1/F = 0, which implies F = ∞ (infinity). This combination acts like a plane glass plate.

46. In a Young's double-slit experiment, if the separation between the slits is doubled and the distance between the slits and the screen is also doubled, the fringe width will:

  1. Remain unchanged
  2. Be halved
  3. Be doubled
  4. Be quadrupled

Answer: Remain unchanged

Explanation: The fringe width (β) in Young's double-slit experiment is given by the formula β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, D is the distance between the slits and the screen, and d is the separation between the slits. If d is doubled (2d) and D is doubled (2D), the new fringe width β' = λ(2D)/(2d) = λD/d. Thus, the fringe width remains unchanged.

47. The energy of a photon of light with wavelength λ is given by:

  1. hc/λ
  2. hλ/c
  3. h/cλ
  4. cλ/h

Answer: hc/λ

Explanation: The energy (E) of a photon is given by E = hν, where h is Planck's constant and ν is the frequency of light. The speed of light (c) is related to frequency and wavelength (λ) by c = νλ, so ν = c/λ. Substituting this into the energy equation, we get E = h(c/λ) = hc/λ.

48. Which of the following is a characteristic of a p-n junction diode in reverse bias?

  1. Large current flow
  2. Narrow depletion region
  3. High resistance
  4. Low potential barrier

Answer: High resistance

Explanation: In reverse bias, the positive terminal of the battery is connected to the n-side and the negative terminal to the p-side. This increases the width of the depletion region and the potential barrier, effectively opposing the flow of current. Consequently, the diode offers very high resistance to current flow, and only a very small leakage current flows.

49. The SI unit of magnetic flux is:

  1. Tesla
  2. Weber
  3. Henry
  4. Ampere-meter

Answer: Weber

Explanation: Magnetic flux (Φ) is defined as the product of the magnetic field strength (B) and the area (A) perpendicular to the field, i.e., Φ = B⋅A. The SI unit of magnetic field strength (B) is Tesla (T), and the SI unit of area (A) is square meter (m²). Therefore, the SI unit of magnetic flux is Tesla-meter² (T⋅m²), which is also known as Weber (Wb).

50. A current-carrying circular loop is placed in a uniform magnetic field. The net magnetic force on the loop is:

  1. Always zero
  2. Always non-zero
  3. Depends on the orientation of the loop
  4. Depends on the magnitude of the current

Answer: Always zero

Explanation: For a closed current-carrying loop placed in a uniform magnetic field, the net magnetic force acting on the loop is always zero. This is because the force on each segment of the loop is given by dF = I(dl x B), and when integrated over the entire closed loop, the vector sum of these forces cancels out, resulting in a net force of zero. However, a torque may act on the loop, causing it to rotate.
