1. A parallel plate capacitor has capacitance C. If the distance between the plates is halved and a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced between the plates, then the new capacitance becomes:

  1. 2KC
  2. KC/2
  3. C/(2K)
  4. C/(K)

Answer: 2KC

Explanation: The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by C = ε₀A/d, where A is the area of the plates and d is the distance between them. When the distance is halved, the new capacitance C' = ε₀A/(d/2) = 2(ε₀A/d) = 2C. When a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced, the capacitance becomes C'' = KC'. Therefore, the new capacitance is C'' = K(2C) = 2KC.

2. Two point charges +q and -q are placed at a distance 'r' from each other. The electric potential at the midpoint of the line joining the two charges is:

  1. 0
  2. kq/r
  3. 2kq/r
  4. -kq/r

Answer: 0

Explanation: The electric potential due to a point charge Q at a distance r is V = kQ/r. At the midpoint, the distance from each charge is r/2. The potential due to +q is V₁ = k(+q)/(r/2) = 2kq/r. The potential due to -q is V₂ = k(-q)/(r/2) = -2kq/r. The total potential at the midpoint is the algebraic sum of the individual potentials: V_total = V₁ + V₂ = (2kq/r) + (-2kq/r) = 0.

3. A current of 10 A flows through a conductor for 2 minutes. How much charge passes through any cross-section of the conductor during this time?

  1. 1200 C
  2. 20 C
  3. 120 C
  4. 200 C

Answer: 1200 C

Explanation: The relationship between current (I), charge (Q), and time (t) is given by I = Q/t. Therefore, Q = I × t. Given current I = 10 A and time t = 2 minutes. First, convert time to seconds: t = 2 × 60 = 120 seconds. Now, calculate the charge: Q = 10 A × 120 s = 1200 C.

4. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding magnetic field lines?

  1. Magnetic field lines form closed loops.
  2. Magnetic field lines do not intersect each other.
  3. The tangent to a magnetic field line at any point gives the direction of the magnetic field at that point.
  4. Magnetic field lines originate from the south pole and terminate at the north pole.

Answer: Magnetic field lines originate from the south pole and terminate at the north pole.

Explanation: Magnetic field lines originate from the North pole and terminate at the South pole outside the magnet, and inside the magnet, they go from South to North, thus forming continuous closed loops. Options A, B, and C are correct properties of magnetic field lines.

5. A metallic rod of length 'L' is rotated with an angular frequency 'ω' with one end hinged at the centre and the other end at the circumference of a circular metallic ring of radius 'R', about an axis passing through the centre and perpendicular to the plane of the ring. A uniform magnetic field 'B' parallel to the axis is present everywhere. The emf induced between the centre and the metallic ring is:

  1. BωR²/2
  2. BωR
  3. BωR²/4
  4. BωL²/2

Answer: BωR²/2

Explanation: The induced emf in a rotating rod in a uniform magnetic field is given by ε = (1/2)BωR², where R is the length of the rod. In this case, the length of the rod is R (radius of the ring). So, the induced emf is ε = (1/2)BωR².

6. The phase difference between the alternating current and voltage in a series LCR circuit at resonance is:

  1. 0 degrees
  2. 45 degrees
  3. 90 degrees
  4. 180 degrees

Answer: 0 degrees

Explanation: At resonance in a series LCR circuit, the inductive reactance (X_L) becomes equal to the capacitive reactance (X_C). As a result, the impedance of the circuit becomes purely resistive (Z = R). In a purely resistive circuit, the current and voltage are in phase, meaning the phase difference between them is 0 degrees.

7. Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the shortest wavelength?

  1. Radio waves
  2. X-rays
  3. Visible light
  4. Microwaves

Answer: X-rays

Explanation: The electromagnetic spectrum arranges waves by wavelength and frequency. In order of decreasing wavelength (and increasing frequency), the spectrum is: Radio waves, Microwaves, Infrared, Visible light, Ultraviolet, X-rays, Gamma rays. Therefore, X-rays have a shorter wavelength than radio waves, visible light, and microwaves.

8. According to Huygens' principle, every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets which spread out in all directions with the speed of light. The envelope of these secondary wavelets at any instant gives the new position of the wavefront. This principle explains:

  1. Reflection and refraction of light
  2. Diffraction of light
  3. Interference of light
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Huygens' principle is a fundamental concept in wave optics. It successfully explains the phenomena of reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference of light. It provides a geometrical construction for finding the new position of a wavefront at a later time.

9. The work function of a metal is 3.3 eV. Its threshold frequency will be approximately (Given h = 6.6 × 10⁻³⁴ J s):

  1. 8 × 10¹⁴ Hz
  2. 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz
  3. 3 × 10¹⁴ Hz
  4. 1 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Answer: 8 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Explanation: The work function (Φ) is related to the threshold frequency (ν₀) by the equation Φ = hν₀, where h is Planck's constant. First, convert the work function from eV to Joules: Φ = 3.3 eV × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/eV = 5.28 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Now, calculate the threshold frequency: ν₀ = Φ/h = (5.28 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) / (6.6 × 10⁻³⁴ J s) = 0.8 × 10¹⁵ Hz = 8 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

10. In a p-n junction diode, the depletion region is formed due to:

  1. Drifting of charge carriers
  2. Diffusion of charge carriers
  3. Recombination of electrons and holes
  4. Both diffusion and recombination of charge carriers

Answer: Both diffusion and recombination of charge carriers

Explanation: The depletion region in a p-n junction diode is formed primarily due to the diffusion of majority charge carriers (holes from p-side to n-side and electrons from n-side to p-side) across the junction. As these carriers diffuse, they leave behind immobile charged ions, creating an electric field. This diffusion leads to the recombination of electrons and holes near the junction, further contributing to the formation and width of the depletion region. So, both diffusion and recombination are key processes.

11. A parallel plate capacitor with air between the plates has a capacitance C. If a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced completely filling the space between the plates, the capacitance becomes:

  1. KC
  2. C/K
  3. C
  4. K^2C

Answer: KC

Explanation: The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by C = ε₀A/d, where A is the area of the plates, d is the distance between them, and ε₀ is the permittivity of free space. When a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced, the permittivity of the medium becomes ε = Kε₀. Therefore, the new capacitance C' = εA/d = (Kε₀)A/d = K(ε₀A/d) = KC. So, the capacitance increases by a factor of K.

12. An electron is accelerated through a potential difference V. Its de Broglie wavelength is λ. If the potential difference is increased to 4V, the new de Broglie wavelength will be:

  1. λ/2
  2. 2λ
  3. λ/4
  4. 4λ

Answer: λ/2

Explanation: The de Broglie wavelength is given by λ = h/p, where h is Planck's constant and p is the momentum. For an electron accelerated through a potential difference V, its kinetic energy is eV. So, 1/2 mv² = eV, which means p = √(2meV). Therefore, λ = h/√(2meV). If the potential difference is increased to 4V, the new wavelength λ' = h/√(2me(4V)) = h/(2√(2meV)) = λ/2. The de Broglie wavelength becomes half.

13. A convex lens of focal length f is immersed in water. How does its focal length change?

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases
  3. Remains the same
  4. Becomes zero

Answer: Increases

Explanation: The focal length of a lens is given by the lens maker's formula: 1/f = (n_lens/n_medium - 1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂). When the lens is in air, n_medium = 1. When immersed in water, n_medium = 1.33 (approximately). Since n_water > n_air, the term (n_lens/n_medium - 1) will decrease (as n_lens is typically around 1.5 for glass). A smaller value of this term implies a larger focal length. Thus, the focal length of the convex lens increases when immersed in water.

14. Which of the following phenomena confirms the transverse nature of light waves?

  1. Interference
  2. Diffraction
  3. Polarization
  4. Reflection

Answer: Polarization

Explanation: Polarization is the phenomenon that confirms the transverse nature of light waves. Only transverse waves can be polarized, meaning their oscillations can be restricted to a single plane. Longitudinal waves, like sound waves, cannot be polarized because their oscillations are already along the direction of wave propagation. Interference, diffraction, and reflection can occur for both transverse and longitudinal waves.

15. The energy of a photon of light with wavelength 500 nm is approximately:

  1. 2.48 eV
  2. 1.24 eV
  3. 4.96 eV
  4. 0.62 eV

Answer: 2.48 eV

Explanation: The energy of a photon is given by E = hc/λ, where h is Planck's constant (6.626 x 10⁻³⁴ J·s), c is the speed of light (3 x 10⁸ m/s), and λ is the wavelength. It's often convenient to use the approximation E (in eV) = 1240 / λ (in nm). So, E = 1240 / 500 = 2.48 eV.

16. A current of 1 A flows through a wire of length 1 m and resistance 1 Ω. The potential difference across the wire is:

  1. 1 V
  2. 0.5 V
  3. 2 V
  4. 10 V

Answer: 1 V

Explanation: According to Ohm's Law, V = IR, where V is the potential difference, I is the current, and R is the resistance. Given I = 1 A and R = 1 Ω, the potential difference V = (1 A) * (1 Ω) = 1 V. The length of the wire is extra information not needed for this calculation.

17. The phenomenon of electromagnetic induction is described by:

  1. Ampere's Law
  2. Gauss's Law for electricity
  3. Faraday's Law of electromagnetic induction
  4. Biot-Savart Law

Answer: Faraday's Law of electromagnetic induction

Explanation: Faraday's Law of electromagnetic induction states that a changing magnetic flux through a coil induces an electromotive force (EMF) in the coil. This is the fundamental principle behind electromagnetic induction. Ampere's Law relates magnetic fields to electric currents, Gauss's Law for electricity relates electric flux to enclosed charge, and Biot-Savart Law calculates the magnetic field produced by a current element.

18. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a nuclear force?

  1. It is charge-dependent.
  2. It is the strongest fundamental force in nature.
  3. It is short-ranged.
  4. It is spin-dependent.

Answer: It is charge-dependent.

Explanation: Nuclear forces (strong nuclear force) are primarily charge-independent. They act equally between proton-proton, neutron-neutron, and proton-neutron pairs, apart from the electrostatic repulsion between protons. They are indeed the strongest fundamental force, short-ranged (acting over distances of about 10⁻¹⁵ m), and spin-dependent.

19. A parallel plate capacitor with air between the plates has a capacitance C. If the distance between the plates is doubled and a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced between the plates, the new capacitance becomes:

  1. KC/2
  2. 2KC
  3. C/(2K)
  4. C/(K)

Answer: KC/2

Explanation: The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by C = ε₀A/d, where A is the area of the plates and d is the distance between them. When the distance is doubled, the capacitance becomes C' = ε₀A/(2d) = C/2. When a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced, the capacitance becomes C'' = K * C' = K * (C/2) = KC/2.

20. A straight conductor of length 'L' carrying a current 'I' is placed in a uniform magnetic field 'B' such that the conductor makes an angle 'θ' with the direction of the magnetic field. The magnitude of the magnetic force experienced by the conductor is:

  1. BILsinθ
  2. BILcosθ
  3. BIL
  4. B²IL

Answer: BILsinθ

Explanation: The magnetic force (F) experienced by a current-carrying conductor of length L, carrying current I, placed in a uniform magnetic field B, making an angle θ with the field, is given by the formula F = BILsinθ. This is a fundamental formula in magnetism.

21. Lenz's Law is a consequence of the law of conservation of:

  1. Charge
  2. Momentum
  3. Energy
  4. Mass

Answer: Energy

Explanation: Lenz's Law states that the direction of the induced current is such that it opposes the cause that produces it. If it did not oppose the cause, then the induced current would reinforce the change, leading to an ever-increasing current and energy, which would violate the law of conservation of energy. Therefore, Lenz's Law is a direct consequence of the law of conservation of energy.

22. In an LCR series circuit, at resonance, the impedance of the circuit is equal to:

  1. R
  2. √(R² + (XL - XC)²)
  3. XL - XC
  4. √(R² + (XL + XC)²)

Answer: R

Explanation: The impedance (Z) of an LCR series circuit is given by Z = √(R² + (XL - XC)²), where R is resistance, XL is inductive reactance, and XC is capacitive reactance. At resonance, XL = XC. Therefore, (XL - XC) = 0, and the impedance becomes Z = √(R² + 0²) = √R² = R. So, at resonance, the impedance is purely resistive.

23. A convex lens of focal length 'f' is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length 'f'. The focal length of the combination is:

  1. 0
  2. f
  3. 2f
  4. infinity

Answer: infinity

Explanation: For a combination of two thin lenses in contact, the equivalent focal length (F) is given by 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂. Here, f₁ = +f (convex lens) and f₂ = -f (concave lens). So, 1/F = 1/f + 1/(-f) = 1/f - 1/f = 0. This implies F = 1/0, which is infinity. Such a combination acts as a plane glass plate.

24. According to Bohr's model, the angular momentum of an electron in a stationary orbit is an integral multiple of:

  1. h/2π
  2. h
  3. 2πh
  4. h/π

Answer: h/2π

Explanation: One of the postulates of Bohr's atomic model states that electrons can revolve only in certain discrete, non-radiating orbits called stationary orbits. In these orbits, the angular momentum of the electron is quantized and is an integral multiple of h/2π, where 'h' is Planck's constant. So, L = n(h/2π), where n = 1, 2, 3, ...

25. A parallel plate capacitor with air between the plates has a capacitance C. If the distance between the plates is doubled and a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced filling the space between the plates, the new capacitance becomes:

  1. KC/2
  2. 2KC
  3. C/2K
  4. C/K

Answer: KC/2

Explanation: The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by C = ε₀A/d, where A is the area of the plates and d is the distance between them. When the distance is doubled, the new capacitance C' = ε₀A/(2d) = C/2. When a dielectric medium of dielectric constant K is introduced, the capacitance becomes C'' = K * C' = K * (C/2) = KC/2.
