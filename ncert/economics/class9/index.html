Class9 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class9 questions to revise the NCERT Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is NOT a fixed capital in the context of farming in Palampur?

  1. Tools and machines
  2. Buildings
  3. Raw materials
  4. Generators

Answer: Raw materials

Explanation: Fixed capital refers to assets that can be used in production over many years, such as tools, machines, and buildings. Raw materials, on the other hand, are working capital, as they are used up in the production process.

2. What is the main objective of production activities in Palampur?

  1. To earn maximum profit for landowners
  2. To provide goods and services that people want
  3. To create employment opportunities for landless labourers
  4. To promote sustainable farming practices

Answer: To provide goods and services that people want

Explanation: The primary aim of any production activity, including those in Palampur, is to produce goods and services that people desire and need. While other options might be secondary outcomes or considerations, the fundamental objective is to satisfy wants.

3. Which of the following activities is considered a non-farm activity in Palampur?

  1. Cultivation of wheat
  2. Dairy farming
  3. Sugarcane harvesting
  4. Potato farming

Answer: Dairy farming

Explanation: Non-farm activities are those that do not involve direct cultivation of land. Dairy farming, which involves rearing animals for milk, is a prominent non-farm activity in Palampur, unlike the other options which are directly related to crop cultivation.

4. What does 'human capital' primarily refer to?

  1. The physical assets owned by individuals
  2. The stock of skills and productive knowledge embodied in people
  3. The financial investments made by individuals
  4. The number of people living in a country

Answer: The stock of skills and productive knowledge embodied in people

Explanation: Human capital refers to the skills, education, and health of a population that contribute to its productive capacity. It's about the quality of the workforce, not just the quantity or physical assets.

5. Which of the following is a major cause of poverty in India?

  1. High economic growth rate
  2. Low population growth rate
  3. Unequal distribution of land and other resources
  4. High levels of literacy

Answer: Unequal distribution of land and other resources

Explanation: Unequal distribution of land and other productive assets is a significant structural cause of poverty in India, leading to a lack of opportunities and resources for a large section of the population. High economic growth and literacy generally help reduce poverty, while low population growth is not a direct cause of poverty.

6. The concept of 'poverty line' in India is based on:

  1. The number of unemployed people
  2. The level of education attained by individuals
  3. Minimum calorie requirement and monetary value of basic necessities
  4. The total wealth accumulated by a household

Answer: Minimum calorie requirement and monetary value of basic necessities

Explanation: In India, the poverty line is determined based on a minimum calorie requirement (for food) and the monetary expenditure needed to meet basic necessities like clothing, footwear, fuel, light, education, and medical requirements. It's a measure of minimum subsistence.

7. Which of the following is a component of food security?

  1. Availability of food
  2. Accessibility of food
  3. Affordability of food
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Food security exists when all people, at all times, have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. This definition encompasses availability, accessibility, and affordability.

8. What is the primary role of the Public Distribution System (PDS) in India?

  1. To provide free food to all citizens
  2. To regulate the prices of luxury goods
  3. To distribute essential food grains and other commodities at subsidized prices
  4. To promote organic farming practices

Answer: To distribute essential food grains and other commodities at subsidized prices

Explanation: The Public Distribution System (PDS) is a crucial government-sponsored chain of shops that distributes essential food grains (like wheat, rice) and other commodities (like sugar, kerosene) at highly subsidized prices to the poor and vulnerable sections of society, ensuring food security.

9. Which of the following is a significant indicator of human capital formation?

  1. Increase in population density
  2. Improvement in health and education levels
  3. Growth in agricultural output
  4. Expansion of land area under cultivation

Answer: Improvement in health and education levels

Explanation: Human capital formation refers to the process of increasing the knowledge, skills, and abilities of people. Investments in education and health are crucial for this, as they enhance productivity and contribute to economic growth.

10. What is the term used when the existing skills and knowledge of a country's population are considered as an asset?

  1. Physical capital
  2. Working capital
  3. Human capital
  4. Financial capital

Answer: Human capital

Explanation: Human capital is the stock of skill and productive knowledge embodied in human beings. It refers to the assets of a nation in terms of its existing productive skills and abilities of its people.

11. Which of the following is a common method used to estimate poverty in India?

  1. Measuring the number of luxury cars owned
  2. Calculating the per capita income of the richest 10%
  3. Using a poverty line based on minimum calorie requirement and monetary value
  4. Counting the number of people living in urban areas

Answer: Using a poverty line based on minimum calorie requirement and monetary value

Explanation: In India, the poverty line is estimated based on the minimum calorie requirement and the monetary expenditure needed to fulfill it. This helps in identifying individuals and families who cannot afford basic necessities.

12. What does 'vulnerability to poverty' refer to?

  1. The ability of a person to escape poverty easily
  2. The probability of certain communities or individuals becoming or remaining poor in the coming years
  3. The total number of poor people in a country
  4. The government's efforts to reduce poverty

Answer: The probability of certain communities or individuals becoming or remaining poor in the coming years

Explanation: Vulnerability to poverty describes the greater probability of certain communities (like backward castes, widows, disabled persons) or individuals of becoming or remaining poor in the future due to various risks and lack of opportunities.

13. Which sector is primarily responsible for transforming human beings into human capital?

  1. Agriculture
  2. Industry
  3. Education and Health
  4. Trade

Answer: Education and Health

Explanation: Investment in education and health transforms human beings into human capital. Education enhances skills and knowledge, while good health ensures productivity and reduces absenteeism, both crucial for economic contribution.

14. What does 'disguised unemployment' mean?

  1. People who are unemployed but actively looking for work
  2. People who are employed but not paid for their work
  3. People who appear to be employed but their contribution to production is zero
  4. People who are employed in illegal activities

Answer: People who appear to be employed but their contribution to production is zero

Explanation: Disguised unemployment occurs when more people are employed in a job than are actually needed. Even if some are removed, the total output does not decrease, indicating their marginal productivity is zero.

15. What is the 'poverty line' primarily used for?

  1. To identify the richest people in a country
  2. To measure the number of people living below a minimum subsistence level
  3. To determine the average income of a country
  4. To calculate the national debt

Answer: To measure the number of people living below a minimum subsistence level

Explanation: The poverty line is a threshold income or consumption level used to identify and measure the proportion of the population living in poverty, i.e., those who cannot afford a minimum standard of living.

16. What is the role of the Public Distribution System (PDS) in India?

  1. To provide free food to all citizens
  2. To distribute essential food grains at subsidised prices to the poor
  3. To export surplus food grains to other countries
  4. To encourage private sector participation in food distribution

Answer: To distribute essential food grains at subsidised prices to the poor

Explanation: The Public Distribution System (PDS) is a government-sponsored program in India that aims to ensure food security by distributing essential food grains (like wheat, rice, sugar, and kerosene) at highly subsidised prices to eligible households, especially those below the poverty line.

17. Which sector is generally associated with the problem of disguised unemployment?

  1. Industrial sector
  2. Service sector
  3. Agricultural sector
  4. Information Technology sector

Answer: Agricultural sector

Explanation: Disguised unemployment is most common in the agricultural sector, especially in developing countries. It occurs when more people are employed than are actually needed, and even if some are removed, production does not fall. This is often seen in family farming where all members work on a small plot of land.

18. Investment in human capital can be made through which of the following?

  1. Building new factories
  2. Improving education and health facilities
  3. Purchasing more land
  4. Developing new technologies

Answer: Improving education and health facilities

Explanation: Human capital refers to the skills, knowledge, and health of a population. Investing in education and health facilities directly enhances these attributes, making people more productive and valuable assets to the economy. While new technologies can be a result of human capital, the investment itself is in the people.

19. The concept of 'poverty line' in India is primarily based on:

  1. Income or consumption levels
  2. Access to education
  3. Availability of healthcare
  4. Ownership of assets

Answer: Income or consumption levels

Explanation: In India, the poverty line is estimated based on the minimum per capita consumption expenditure (or income) required to satisfy basic needs, including food, clothing, footwear, fuel, light, education, and medical requirements. It's a monetary threshold.

20. What is the primary objective of the Public Distribution System (PDS) in India?

  1. To promote private sector involvement in food grain trade
  2. To ensure food security by providing food grains at subsidized prices
  3. To encourage export of surplus food grains
  4. To increase the income of farmers

Answer: To ensure food security by providing food grains at subsidized prices

Explanation: The Public Distribution System (PDS) is a government-sponsored chain of shops that distribute essential commodities like food grains (wheat, rice), sugar, and kerosene at subsidized prices to the poor and vulnerable sections of society, thereby ensuring food security.

21. Which of the following is considered an investment in human capital?

  1. Buying new machinery for a factory
  2. Constructing a new road
  3. Providing education and training to people
  4. Purchasing raw materials for production

Answer: Providing education and training to people

Explanation: Investment in human capital involves expenditures on education, training, and health, which enhance the productive capacity and skills of the population, making them an asset for the economy.

22. What is the term used when the existing skills and knowledge of a country's population are enhanced through education and health?

  1. Physical capital formation
  2. Human capital formation
  3. Natural resource development
  4. Financial capital accumulation

Answer: Human capital formation

Explanation: Human capital formation refers to the process of acquiring and increasing the number of persons with skills, education, and experience which are critical for the economic and social development of the country. It's about improving the quality of the 'people as resource'.

23. Which of the following is a common indicator used to measure poverty in India?

  1. Literacy rate
  2. Per capita income
  3. Life expectancy at birth
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Poverty is a multi-dimensional concept. While per capita income is a direct economic indicator, social indicators like literacy rate and health indicators like life expectancy at birth are also crucial in understanding and measuring the extent of poverty, as they reflect deprivation in various aspects of life.

24. What is the main reason for the vulnerability of certain social groups to poverty?

  1. Lack of interest in work
  2. Limited access to resources and opportunities
  3. High population growth rates
  4. Geographical isolation

Answer: Limited access to resources and opportunities

Explanation: Vulnerability to poverty is primarily due to limited access to education, healthcare, land, and employment opportunities, which prevents certain social groups (like scheduled castes, scheduled tribes, and rural agricultural labourers) from improving their economic status.

25. What is the main reason for the high unemployment rate among educated youth in India?

  1. Lack of basic literacy skills
  2. Mismatch between education and job requirements
  3. Insufficient government job quotas
  4. Overpopulation in rural areas

Answer: Mismatch between education and job requirements

Explanation: A significant reason for unemployment among educated youth is the mismatch between the skills and knowledge acquired through education and the actual requirements of available jobs in the market. This is often referred to as 'educated unemployment'.
