26. What does the 'poverty line' primarily aim to define?

  1. The minimum income required to afford luxury goods
  2. The minimum nutritional requirement for survival
  3. The average income of a country's population
  4. The maximum income allowed for government benefits

Answer: The minimum nutritional requirement for survival

Explanation: The poverty line is a threshold that defines the minimum level of income or consumption expenditure needed to satisfy basic necessities, primarily food (nutritional requirements), clothing, shelter, education, and medical care. It's not about luxury or average income.

27. Which of the following is a component of the Public Distribution System (PDS) in India?

  1. Mid-day Meal Scheme
  2. Fair Price Shops
  3. Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)
  4. Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA)

Answer: Fair Price Shops

Explanation: Fair Price Shops (FPS) are an integral part of the Public Distribution System (PDS). They are government-controlled shops that distribute essential commodities like food grains, sugar, and kerosene at subsidized prices to eligible households. The other options are separate government schemes.

28. What is the primary objective of buffer stock in the context of food security?

  1. To export surplus food grains to other countries
  2. To store food grains for future emergencies or shortages
  3. To provide food grains to rich farmers at subsidized rates
  4. To encourage private traders to hoard food grains

Answer: To store food grains for future emergencies or shortages

Explanation: Buffer stock refers to the stock of food grains (wheat and rice) procured by the government through the Food Corporation of India (FCI). Its primary objective is to distribute these grains in deficit areas and among the poorer strata of society at a price lower than the market price, and to resolve the problem of food shortage during adverse weather conditions or calamities.

29. Which of the following is NOT a non-farm activity in the village of Palampur?

  1. Dairy farming
  2. Small-scale manufacturing
  3. Shopkeeping
  4. Farming

Answer: Farming

Explanation: Farming is the primary economic activity in Palampur and is considered a farm activity. Dairy farming, small-scale manufacturing, and shopkeeping are examples of non-farm activities that provide alternative sources of income for the villagers.

30. What is the main reason for the limited expansion of cultivated land in Palampur?

  1. Lack of irrigation facilities
  2. Government restrictions on land use
  3. Land is fixed and already under cultivation
  4. Lack of demand for agricultural products

Answer: Land is fixed and already under cultivation

Explanation: The chapter 'The Story of Village Palampur' highlights that land is a fixed factor of production. By the mid-1960s, the area under cultivation in India, including Palampur, had reached its maximum limit, meaning there was no scope to bring new land under cultivation.

31. When human capital is invested in education and health, it can yield a high return in the future. This statement best describes which concept?

  1. Physical capital
  2. Fixed capital
  3. Working capital
  4. Human capital formation

Answer: Human capital formation

Explanation: Human capital formation refers to the process of acquiring and increasing the number of persons with skills, education, and experience which are critical for the economic and political development of a country. Investing in education and health improves the productivity and earning capacity of people, leading to a higher return in the future.

32. Which of the following groups is most vulnerable to food insecurity in India?

  1. Landowning farmers
  2. Urban professionals
  3. Landless labourers and traditional artisans
  4. Government employees

Answer: Landless labourers and traditional artisans

Explanation: Landless labourers, traditional artisans, and people in urban slums are among the most vulnerable groups to food insecurity. They often have irregular incomes, limited access to resources, and are highly susceptible to economic shocks, making it difficult for them to afford sufficient food.

33. Seasonal hunger is a characteristic feature in which of the following areas?

  1. Urban areas with stable employment
  2. Rural areas during specific agricultural seasons
  3. Industrial zones with high wages
  4. Regions with year-round crop cultivation

Answer: Rural areas during specific agricultural seasons

Explanation: Seasonal hunger is prevalent in rural areas due to the seasonal nature of agricultural activities. During certain seasons, when there is less work (e.g., before harvesting or during lean periods), people, especially agricultural labourers, might face a lack of employment and thus, food. This is a key concept discussed in 'Food Security in India'.

34. What is the primary objective of the National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013?

  1. To provide free education to all children below 14 years of age.
  2. To ensure food and nutritional security by providing subsidized food grains to eligible households.
  3. To promote agricultural exports and increase farmers' income.
  4. To establish a network of cold storage facilities across the country.

Answer: To ensure food and nutritional security by providing subsidized food grains to eligible households.

Explanation: The National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013, aims to provide food and nutritional security in human life cycle approach, by ensuring access to adequate quantity of quality food at affordable prices to people to live a life with dignity. It legally entitles up to 75% of the rural population and 50% of the urban population to receive subsidized food grains under the Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS).
