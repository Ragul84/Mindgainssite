Class12 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class12 questions to revise the NCERT Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 330 questions.

1. What is the primary function of money that facilitates transactions by acting as an intermediary?

  1. Medium of Exchange
  2. Store of Value
  3. Unit of Account
  4. Standard of Deferred Payment

Answer: Medium of Exchange

Explanation: Money as a medium of exchange overcomes the double coincidence of wants inherent in the barter system, making trade easier.

2. The barter system is characterized by:

  1. Direct exchange of goods for goods
  2. Use of a common medium of exchange
  3. Absence of a common unit of account
  4. Both A and C

Answer: Both A and C

Explanation: Barter involves the direct exchange of goods and services without the use of money, and it lacks a standardized unit for measuring value.

3. Which of the following is a major drawback of the barter system?

  1. Lack of double coincidence of wants
  2. Excessive liquidity
  3. High inflation rates
  4. Efficient storage of wealth

Answer: Lack of double coincidence of wants

Explanation: The barter system requires that each party in a transaction has something the other desires, which is often difficult to find.

4. When you use money to buy groceries, money is primarily functioning as a:

  1. Medium of Exchange
  2. Measure of Value
  3. Standard of Deferred Payment
  4. Store of Value

Answer: Medium of Exchange

Explanation: Buying groceries involves using money to facilitate the transaction, which is its role as a medium of exchange.

5. The absence of a common medium of exchange in a barter economy leads to:

  1. Inefficiency in trade
  2. Rapid economic growth
  3. Abundant supply of all goods
  4. Reduced need for specialization

Answer: Inefficiency in trade

Explanation: Without a common medium of exchange, trading becomes cumbersome and limits the scope and efficiency of economic activities.

6. Money helps overcome the problem of the 'double coincidence of wants' by acting as a:

  1. Universal medium of exchange
  2. Unit of account
  3. Store of value
  4. Standard of deferred payment

Answer: Universal medium of exchange

Explanation: Money is universally accepted, meaning it can be exchanged for any good or service, thus eliminating the need for a direct swap of desired items.

7. The introduction of money significantly improved the efficiency of the economy by:

  1. Simplifying transactions
  2. Increasing unemployment
  3. Reducing the need for production
  4. Causing widespread debt

Answer: Simplifying transactions

Explanation: Money makes it easier to buy and sell, leading to more efficient allocation of resources and increased economic activity.

8. If a farmer wants to trade his wheat for vegetables, but the vegetable seller does not need wheat, this is an example of the problem of:

  1. Lack of double coincidence of wants
  2. Lack of a store of value
  3. Lack of a unit of account
  4. Lack of a standard of deferred payment

Answer: Lack of double coincidence of wants

Explanation: This scenario directly illustrates the 'double coincidence of wants' problem in a barter system.

9. Money's role as a 'unit of account' means it is used to:

  1. Measure the value of goods and services
  2. Facilitate immediate transactions
  3. Store purchasing power over time
  4. Enable future payments

Answer: Measure the value of goods and services

Explanation: Money provides a common yardstick for measuring and comparing the values of different goods and services.

10. True or False: In a barter economy, it is easy to save for future consumption.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: Saving in a barter system can be difficult as goods may perish or become obsolete, and finding someone to store them for you is challenging.

11. When money is used to settle debts that are due in the future, it is acting as a:

  1. Standard of Deferred Payment
  2. Medium of Exchange
  3. Store of Value
  4. Unit of Account

Answer: Standard of Deferred Payment

Explanation: This function of money allows for credit transactions and future obligations to be expressed and settled in monetary terms.

12. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?

  1. Facilitating the discovery of new species
  2. Acting as a medium of exchange
  3. Serving as a unit of account
  4. Being a store of value

Answer: Facilitating the discovery of new species

Explanation: Discovering new species is a scientific endeavor and not a function performed by money.

13. The difficulty in dividing certain goods for exchange in a barter system is a problem related to:

  1. Divisibility
  2. Portability
  3. Durability
  4. Acceptability

Answer: Divisibility

Explanation: Some goods, like a live animal, are difficult to divide into smaller units for exchange, posing a challenge in barter.

14. True or False: Money's role as a store of value allows individuals to save their purchasing power for future use.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: Money can be held and used later, preserving its value over time, although inflation can erode this value.

15. In a barter economy, the value of a good is determined by its ability to be exchanged for other goods, which is a form of:

  1. Relative valuation
  2. Absolute valuation
  3. Monetary valuation
  4. Fixed valuation

Answer: Relative valuation

Explanation: The value of a good in barter is its worth in terms of other specific goods, not a universal monetary price.

16. The barter system is inefficient because it requires a high degree of:

  1. Cooperation between traders
  2. Government intervention
  3. Technological advancement
  4. Financial regulation

Answer: Cooperation between traders

Explanation: The success of barter relies heavily on finding individuals who have what you want and want what you have, demanding significant coordination.

17. What is the primary limitation of the barter system that money solves?

  1. Lack of goods
  2. Double coincidence of wants
  3. Lack of production
  4. High cost of transport

Answer: Double coincidence of wants

Explanation: Barter requires both parties to simultaneously possess what the other wants, which is a significant inefficiency.

18. Which function of money allows it to be used to express the value of different goods in a common unit?

  1. Medium of exchange
  2. Store of value
  3. Unit of account
  4. Standard of deferred payments

Answer: Unit of account

Explanation: As a unit of account, money provides a common measure for pricing goods and services.

19. Money acts as a medium of exchange because it is:

  1. Widely accepted
  2. Made of gold
  3. Issued by the government
  4. Limited in supply

Answer: Widely accepted

Explanation: General acceptability is the fundamental characteristic that allows money to facilitate trades.

20. Which of the following is an example of 'Store of Value' function of money?

  1. Buying a book with cash
  2. Saving money in a bank account
  3. Paying a loan installment
  4. Comparing prices of two cars

Answer: Saving money in a bank account

Explanation: Saving money for future use demonstrates money's ability to transfer purchasing power from the present to the future.

21. The 'Double Coincidence of Wants' is a necessary condition for which of the following?

  1. Monetary economy
  2. Credit transactions
  3. Barter system
  4. Digital payments

Answer: Barter system

Explanation: In a barter system, trade can only occur if both parties want exactly what the other is offering.

22. Which function of money facilitates the borrowing and lending of money for future repayment?

  1. Medium of exchange
  2. Standard of deferred payments
  3. Store of value
  4. Unit of account

Answer: Standard of deferred payments

Explanation: Standard of deferred payments allows contracts to be made for payments to be made at a future date.

23. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of an ideal medium of exchange?

  1. Portability
  2. Divisibility
  3. Perishability
  4. Cognizability

Answer: Perishability

Explanation: Money should be durable; if it perishes quickly, it cannot store value or be exchanged easily.

24. In a barter economy, the lack of a common unit of value leads to:

  1. Easier trade
  2. Difficulty in calculating relative prices
  3. Increased production
  4. Elimination of trade

Answer: Difficulty in calculating relative prices

Explanation: Without a common unit, every good must be priced in terms of every other good, creating complex exchange ratios.

25. Which of these is considered 'Fiat Money'?

  1. Gold coins
  2. Silver bars
  3. Currency notes issued by the Central Bank
  4. Commodity money

Answer: Currency notes issued by the Central Bank

Explanation: Fiat money has no intrinsic value but is used as money because of government decree.
