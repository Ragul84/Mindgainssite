NCERT Economy practice

Class11 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class11 questions to revise the NCERT Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 337 questions.

1. The Indian economy from 1950 to 1990 was primarily characterized by:

  1. A completely free market economy
  2. A series of five-year plans with significant government intervention
  3. A focus solely on agricultural development
  4. Minimal government involvement in economic policies

Answer: A series of five-year plans with significant government intervention

Explanation: The period was marked by planned economic development through five-year plans, with the government playing a central role.

2. Which of the following was a key goal of the Indian economic plans between 1950 and 1990?

  1. Maximizing foreign investment
  2. Achieving self-sufficiency and reducing poverty
  3. Prioritizing consumer goods production
  4. Encouraging rapid privatization

Answer: Achieving self-sufficiency and reducing poverty

Explanation: The plans aimed at achieving self-sufficiency, reducing poverty, and promoting industrial growth.

3. A major merit of the regulated economy in India from 1950-1990 was:

  1. High market responsiveness
  2. Efficient resource allocation and structured development
  3. Minimal bureaucratic control
  4. Rapid innovation driven by competition

Answer: Efficient resource allocation and structured development

Explanation: The structured approach allowed for planned resource allocation and development.

4. One of the limitations of the Indian economic system between 1950 and 1990 was:

  1. Excessive market responsiveness
  2. Lack of structured development
  3. Inefficiencies and lack of market responsiveness
  4. Over-reliance on private sector initiatives

Answer: Inefficiencies and lack of market responsiveness

Explanation: The regulated nature led to inefficiencies and a reduced ability to respond to market demands.

5. Economic systems are defined as the ways a society organizes its:

  1. Political ideologies
  2. Cultural traditions
  3. Production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services
  4. International trade policies

Answer: Production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services

Explanation: This definition covers the core functions of any economic system.

6. The choice of an economic system can significantly impact a nation's:

  1. Climate and geography
  2. Welfare and development
  3. Artistic expressions
  4. Religious practices

Answer: Welfare and development

Explanation: The way an economy is structured directly affects the well-being and progress of its people.

7. Between 1950 and 1990, the Indian government played a significant role in:

  1. Reducing its economic influence
  2. Planning and implementing economic policies
  3. Promoting unregulated markets
  4. Divesting from key industries

Answer: Planning and implementing economic policies

Explanation: The government was actively involved in shaping the economy through planned policies.

8. The period 1950-1990 in India was characterized by a regulated economy, which implies:

  1. Decentralized decision-making
  2. Extensive government control and planning
  3. Dominance of private enterprise
  4. Free market competition

Answer: Extensive government control and planning

Explanation: A regulated economy signifies a high degree of government oversight and planning.

9. Economic systems vary in their levels of government control, private ownership, and:

  1. Social equality
  2. Market regulation
  3. Technological advancement
  4. Cultural diversity

Answer: Market regulation

Explanation: These are the key differentiating factors between various economic systems.

10. The Indian economy from 1950 to 1990 was characterized by a series of five-year plans aimed at developing various sectors such as agriculture and industry. These plans were part of a regulated economy.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: The provided text explicitly states that the plans were part of a regulated economy.

11. The goals of the Indian economic plans from 1950-1990 included achieving self-sufficiency, reducing poverty, and promoting industrial growth.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: The source material clearly lists these as the objectives of the economic plans during that period.

12. The limitations of the Indian economic approach from 1950-1990 included inefficiencies and a high degree of market responsiveness.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: The text states that a limitation was a *lack* of market responsiveness, not a high degree.

13. Economic systems are the ways in which a society organizes its production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: This is the fundamental definition of an economic system as provided in the text.

14. The choice of an economic system has no significant impact on the welfare and development of a nation.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: The source material explicitly states that the choice of an economic system *can significantly impact* welfare and development.

15. The Indian economy from 1950 to 1990 was characterized by a series of five-year plans aimed at developing various sectors such as agriculture and industry. These plans were part of a regulated economy, where the government played a significant role in planning and implementing economic policies. The goals included achieving self-sufficiency, reducing poverty, and promoting industrial growth. The merits of this approach included structured development and resource allocation, while limitations included inefficiencies and lack of market responsiveness.

  1. The Indian economy during 1950-1990 was a free market system with minimal government intervention.
  2. The primary goal was to increase foreign debt.
  3. The system aimed for self-sufficiency and had structured development but suffered from inefficiencies.
  4. The economy was characterized by high market responsiveness and rapid innovation.

Answer: The system aimed for self-sufficiency and had structured development but suffered from inefficiencies.

Explanation: This option accurately summarizes the key aspects mentioned in the provided text about the Indian economy from 1950-1990.

16. Which of the following is a primary characteristic of a Socialist Economy?

  1. Private ownership of means of production
  2. Collective or state ownership of means of production
  3. Price determination by market forces
  4. Profit maximization as the primary motive

Answer: Collective or state ownership of means of production

Explanation: In a socialist economy, the state or collective owns the means of production to ensure social welfare.

17. The 'Green Revolution' in India was primarily aimed at increasing the production of which crops?

  1. Pulses and Oilseeds
  2. Cotton and Jute
  3. Wheat and Rice
  4. Tea and Coffee

Answer: Wheat and Rice

Explanation: The Green Revolution focused on High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds for wheat and rice to achieve food self-sufficiency.

18. In a Mixed Economy, the coexistence of which two sectors is observed?

  1. Agriculture and Industry
  2. Public and Private sectors
  3. Domestic and Foreign sectors
  4. Rural and Urban sectors

Answer: Public and Private sectors

Explanation: A mixed economy combines elements of both capitalism (private) and socialism (public).

19. What was the primary objective of the Industrial Policy Resolution (IPR) of 1956?

  1. Complete privatization of industries
  2. Reducing the role of the public sector
  3. Establishing a dominant role for the public sector
  4. Eliminating all trade barriers

Answer: Establishing a dominant role for the public sector

Explanation: The IPR 1956 emphasized the leadership of the public sector in industrial development.

20. Which of the following is a feature of a Market Economy?

  1. Central planning authority
  2. Price mechanism
  3. State-determined production quotas
  4. Absence of competition

Answer: Price mechanism

Explanation: In a market economy, prices are determined by the interaction of demand and supply.

21. The 'Land Reforms' implemented after independence primarily focused on:

  1. Increasing land prices
  2. Abolition of intermediaries like Zamindars
  3. Promoting corporate farming
  4. Reducing the area under cultivation

Answer: Abolition of intermediaries like Zamindars

Explanation: Land reforms aimed to remove intermediaries to provide security of tenure to actual tillers.

22. Which of these is a central problem of any economic system?

  1. What to produce
  2. How to produce
  3. For whom to produce
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Every economy must decide what to produce, how to produce it, and who gets the output.

23. The 'Inward Looking Trade Strategy' is also known as:

  1. Export Promotion
  2. Import Substitution
  3. Free Trade
  4. Global Integration

Answer: Import Substitution

Explanation: Import substitution aims to replace imports with domestic production to protect local industries.

24. Which of the following is a characteristic of a Centrally Planned Economy?

  1. Consumer sovereignty
  2. Profit motive
  3. Central Planning Authority
  4. Laissez-faire approach

Answer: Central Planning Authority

Explanation: A centrally planned economy relies on a central authority to make all major economic decisions.

25. The primary goal of the Five Year Plans in India during 1950-1990 was:

  1. Rapid privatization
  2. Growth, Modernization, Self-reliance, and Equity
  3. Complete dependence on foreign aid
  4. Focusing only on the service sector

Answer: Growth, Modernization, Self-reliance, and Equity

Explanation: The plans aimed for overall economic growth, modernization of technology, self-reliance, and social equity.
