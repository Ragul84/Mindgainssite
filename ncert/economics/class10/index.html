Class10 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class10 questions to revise the NCERT Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is NOT a common development goal for people?

  1. More income
  2. Equal treatment
  3. Security
  4. Polluted environment

Answer: Polluted environment

Explanation: Development goals are aspirations that people have for a better life. More income, equal treatment, and security are all common aspirations. A polluted environment is detrimental to well-being and is therefore not a development goal; rather, a clean environment is a development goal.

2. The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of a country is the sum of the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year. Which sector contributes the most to India's GDP in recent years?

  1. Primary sector
  2. Secondary sector
  3. Tertiary sector
  4. Quaternary sector

Answer: Tertiary sector

Explanation: In India, like many developed economies, the tertiary (service) sector has become the largest contributing sector to the GDP. This includes services like banking, education, healthcare, IT, and transport. While the primary and secondary sectors are crucial, the service sector's share has grown significantly.

3. Which of the following is a major disadvantage of the barter system?

  1. Requires a double coincidence of wants
  2. Facilitates easy exchange of goods
  3. Promotes specialization
  4. Allows for deferred payments

Answer: Requires a double coincidence of wants

Explanation: The barter system involves direct exchange of goods without the use of money. Its major disadvantage is the 'double coincidence of wants,' meaning both parties must simultaneously desire what the other has to offer. This makes transactions difficult and inefficient, unlike money which acts as a medium of exchange.

4. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?

  1. Medium of exchange
  2. Store of value
  3. Measure of value
  4. Source of employment

Answer: Source of employment

Explanation: Money serves as a medium of exchange (facilitating transactions), a store of value (allowing wealth to be saved), and a measure of value (providing a common unit for pricing goods and services). While economic activity involving money can create employment, money itself is not a direct source of employment; it's a tool that facilitates economic activity.

5. Which of the following factors has NOT been a major driver of globalisation?

  1. Rapid improvement in technology
  2. Liberalisation of trade and investment policies
  3. Development of international organizations
  4. Increased government control over borders

Answer: Increased government control over borders

Explanation: Globalisation is the increasing interconnectedness of the world. Rapid technological advancements (especially in communication and transport), liberalisation of trade and investment policies, and the role of international organizations (like WTO) have all significantly driven globalisation. Increased government control over borders would generally hinder, rather than drive, globalisation.

6. What is the primary role of the World Trade Organization (WTO)?

  1. To provide loans to developing countries
  2. To promote international trade by reducing barriers
  3. To regulate global financial markets
  4. To resolve political disputes between nations

Answer: To promote international trade by reducing barriers

Explanation: The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an international organization that deals with the rules of trade between nations. Its main goal is to help trading countries conduct business as smoothly, predictably, and freely as possible by reducing trade barriers and establishing a framework for trade policies.

7. Which of the following is a fundamental right of consumers according to the Consumer Protection Act (COPRA)?

  1. Right to earn profit
  2. Right to be informed
  3. Right to refuse service
  4. Right to set prices

Answer: Right to be informed

Explanation: The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) grants several rights to consumers. The 'Right to be informed' is a fundamental right, ensuring consumers have access to complete and accurate information about the goods and services they purchase, including quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price.

8. If a consumer is not satisfied with the decision of a District Consumer Court, where can they appeal next?

  1. Supreme Court
  2. High Court
  3. State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
  4. International Court of Justice

Answer: State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission

Explanation: Under the Consumer Protection Act, there is a three-tier quasi-judicial machinery for redressal. If a consumer is not satisfied with the decision of the District Forum, they can appeal to the State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (State Commission). Appeals from the State Commission go to the National Commission, and then finally to the Supreme Court.

9. The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of a country is the sum of the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year. Which sector contributes the most to India's GDP in recent times?

  1. Primary sector
  2. Secondary sector
  3. Tertiary sector
  4. Informal sector

Answer: Tertiary sector

Explanation: In recent times, the tertiary sector (also known as the service sector) has emerged as the largest contributing sector to India's GDP. This includes services like banking, education, healthcare, IT, and transport. While the primary and secondary sectors are important, the service sector's growth has been significant.

10. Which of the following factors has NOT facilitated globalisation?

  1. Rapid improvement in technology
  2. Liberalisation of trade and investment policies
  3. Development of multinational corporations (MNCs)
  4. Increased trade barriers and protectionism

Answer: Increased trade barriers and protectionism

Explanation: Globalisation is the process of rapid integration or interconnection between countries. Factors like rapid technological improvements (especially in transport and communication), liberalisation of trade and investment policies, and the rise of MNCs have all significantly facilitated globalisation. Increased trade barriers and protectionism, on the other hand, hinder globalisation by restricting the free flow of goods, services, and capital.

11. Which of the following is a consumer right according to the Consumer Protection Act (COPRA)?

  1. Right to earn profit
  2. Right to be informed
  3. Right to refuse service
  4. Right to set prices

Answer: Right to be informed

Explanation: The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) grants several rights to consumers to protect their interests. The 'Right to be informed' means consumers have the right to be informed about the quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price of goods or services. The other options are not consumer rights; they relate to business operations or individual choices outside the scope of consumer protection.

12. If a consumer is dissatisfied with a product or service, where can they file a complaint under the Consumer Protection Act?

  1. Police station
  2. District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
  3. Local municipal office
  4. Income Tax Department

Answer: District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission

Explanation: Under the Consumer Protection Act (COPRA), a three-tier quasi-judicial machinery has been set up for redressal of consumer disputes. The District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (often called District Forum) is the first level where consumers can file complaints for goods or services valued up to a certain limit. Higher commissions exist at the state and national levels for larger claims or appeals.

13. Which of the following is NOT a common developmental goal for people?

  1. More income
  2. Equal treatment
  3. Security
  4. Polluted environment

Answer: Polluted environment

Explanation: Developmental goals vary from person to person, but generally include things like more income, equal treatment, freedom, security, and respect. A polluted environment is detrimental to well-being and is therefore not a developmental goal for anyone.

14. The primary sector is also known as the:

  1. Industrial sector
  2. Service sector
  3. Agricultural and related sector
  4. Manufacturing sector

Answer: Agricultural and related sector

Explanation: The primary sector involves activities that directly use natural resources, such as agriculture, forestry, fishing, and mining. Hence, it is often referred to as the agricultural and related sector.

15. Which of the following is a key characteristic of money?

  1. It is always made of precious metals.
  2. It is a medium of exchange.
  3. It has intrinsic value only.
  4. It is issued by private banks only.

Answer: It is a medium of exchange.

Explanation: The most fundamental characteristic of money is its function as a medium of exchange. It facilitates transactions by eliminating the need for a double coincidence of wants, which is a problem in a barter system.

16. Which of the following is an example of a formal source of credit in India?

  1. Moneylender
  2. Trader
  3. Cooperative society
  4. Employer

Answer: Cooperative society

Explanation: Formal sources of credit are supervised by the Reserve Bank of India and include banks and cooperative societies. Moneylenders, traders, and employers typically represent informal sources of credit.

17. What is the main objective of the World Trade Organization (WTO)?

  1. To provide loans to developing countries
  2. To promote free trade among countries
  3. To regulate global financial markets
  4. To protect domestic industries from foreign competition

Answer: To promote free trade among countries

Explanation: The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an international organization that aims to supervise and liberalize international trade. Its main objective is to promote free and fair trade among member countries by reducing trade barriers.

18. Which of the following is a right of consumers according to the Consumer Protection Act?

  1. Right to earn profit
  2. Right to be heard
  3. Right to pollute
  4. Right to exploit workers

Answer: Right to be heard

Explanation: The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) grants several rights to consumers, including the Right to be Heard, which means consumers have the right to present their grievances and be assured that their interests will receive due consideration at appropriate forums.

19. Which symbol ensures the quality of agricultural products in India?

  1. ISI mark
  2. AGMARK
  3. BIS mark
  4. FPO mark

Answer: AGMARK

Explanation: AGMARK is a certification mark employed on agricultural products in India, assuring their quality and purity. ISI mark is for industrial products, BIS mark is for various products (including some industrial), and FPO mark is for processed fruit products.

20. Which of the following is a formal source of credit in India?

  1. Moneylenders
  2. Traders
  3. Cooperative societies
  4. Employers

Answer: Cooperative societies

Explanation: Formal sources of credit are supervised by the Reserve Bank of India and follow specific rules and regulations. Cooperative societies, along with banks, are examples of formal sources of credit. Moneylenders, traders, and employers typically fall under informal sources, which often charge higher interest rates and lack regulatory oversight.

21. Which of the following is a consumer right according to the Consumer Protection Act?

  1. Right to earn profit
  2. Right to choose
  3. Right to refuse payment
  4. Right to sell goods

Answer: Right to choose

Explanation: The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) grants several rights to consumers. The 'Right to Choose' ensures that consumers have access to a variety of goods and services at competitive prices and are free to choose from them. The other options are not recognized consumer rights.

22. The sum of production in all three sectors (primary, secondary, and tertiary) during a particular year provides the country's:

  1. Net National Product
  2. Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
  3. Per Capita Income
  4. Human Development Index (HDI)

Answer: Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

Explanation: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period, usually a year. It is a key indicator of the economic health of a nation and is calculated by summing up the output of the primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors.

23. The process of rapid integration or interconnection between countries is known as:

  1. Privatization
  2. Liberalization
  3. Globalization
  4. Nationalization

Answer: Globalization

Explanation: Globalization refers to the increasing interconnectedness and interdependence of countries through the flow of goods, services, capital, technology, and people across borders. It involves the integration of economies, cultures, and societies worldwide. Privatization, liberalization, and nationalization are related economic policies but do not define the overarching process of global integration.

24. Which of the following factors has NOT facilitated globalization?

  1. Rapid improvement in technology
  2. Liberalization of trade and investment policies
  3. Development of multinational corporations (MNCs)
  4. Increased trade barriers and protectionism

Answer: Increased trade barriers and protectionism

Explanation: Globalization has been driven by several factors, including advancements in technology (especially in communication and transport), the removal of trade and investment barriers (liberalization), and the rise of multinational corporations. Increased trade barriers and protectionism, on the other hand, hinder globalization by restricting the free flow of goods and services between countries.

25. Which of the following is NOT a right of a consumer according to the Consumer Protection Act?

  1. Right to safety
  2. Right to be informed
  3. Right to choose
  4. Right to unlimited credit

Answer: Right to unlimited credit

Explanation: The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) grants several rights to consumers to protect their interests. These include the right to safety, the right to be informed, the right to choose, the right to seek redressal, the right to be heard, and the right to consumer education. The 'right to unlimited credit' is not a recognized consumer right under COPRA.
