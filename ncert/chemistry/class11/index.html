Class11 MCQs with answers

1. According to the provided text, what is the general definition of science?

  1. A collection of facts about the universe.
  2. A continuing human effort to systematise knowledge for describing and understanding nature.
  3. The study of living organisms and their interactions.
  4. The application of technology to solve real-world problems.

Answer: A continuing human effort to systematise knowledge for describing and understanding nature.

Explanation: The text explicitly states that 'Science can be viewed as a continuing human effort to systematise knowledge for describing and understanding nature.' This highlights the systematic and ongoing nature of scientific inquiry.

2. Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a natural dye stuff in ancient Indian texts like the Atharvaveda?

  1. Turmeric
  2. Madder
  3. Indigo
  4. Lac

Answer: Indigo

Explanation: The text mentions turmeric, madder, sunflower, orpiment, cochineal, and lac as dye stuffs. Indigo is a well-known natural dye but is not listed in the provided examples from the Atharvaveda.

3. Which statement accurately describes the arrangement and movement of constituent particles in gases?

  1. Particles are held very close to each other in an orderly fashion with not much freedom of movement.
  2. Particles are close to each other but can move around.
  3. Particles are far apart and their movement is easy and fast.
  4. Particles are randomly arranged but vibrate in fixed positions.

Answer: Particles are far apart and their movement is easy and fast.

Explanation: The text states that 'In gases, particles are far apart and their movement is easy and fast.' This contrasts with solids (orderly, little movement) and liquids (close, can move around).

4. Before the widespread adoption of the SI system, which two measurement systems were commonly used in different parts of the world?

  1. The Imperial System and the Metric System
  2. The English System and the Metric System
  3. The American System and the European System
  4. The SI System and the CGS System

Answer: The English System and the Metric System

Explanation: The text clearly states, 'Earlier, two different systems of measurement were being used in different parts of the world: the English System and the Metric System.'

5. Which of the following laboratory devices is NOT primarily used to measure the volume of liquids or prepare solutions of known volume?

  1. Graduated cylinder
  2. Burette
  3. Beaker
  4. Volumetric flask

Answer: Beaker

Explanation: The text lists graduated cylinders, burettes, pipettes, and volumetric flasks as devices for measuring liquid volume or preparing solutions. Beakers are generally used for holding, mixing, and heating liquids, but not for precise volume measurements.

6. If a measurement is recorded as 11.2 mL and no explicit uncertainty is stated, what is the understood uncertainty in this measurement?

  1. ±0.1 mL
  2. ±0.01 mL
  3. ±1 mL
  4. ±0.2 mL

Answer: ±0.1 mL

Explanation: The text explains that 'Unless otherwise stated, an uncertainty of ±1 in the last digit is always understood.' For 11.2 mL, the last digit is in the tenths place, so the uncertainty is ±0.1 mL.

7. What is the primary purpose of the Laws of Chemical Combinations?

  1. To describe the physical properties of elements.
  2. To explain how elements combine to form compounds.
  3. To determine the rate of chemical reactions.
  4. To classify different types of chemical bonds.

Answer: To explain how elements combine to form compounds.

Explanation: The text states, 'The primary purpose of the Laws of Chemical Combinations is to govern how elements combine to form compounds, outlining the fundamental principles that chemical reactions follow.'

8. On what standard is the present system of atomic masses based, and when was this standard agreed upon?

  1. Hydrogen-1 (¹H) in 1950
  2. Oxygen-16 (¹⁶O) in 1960
  3. Carbon-12 (¹²C) in 1961
  4. Nitrogen-14 (¹⁴N) in 1970

Answer: Carbon-12 (¹²C) in 1961

Explanation: The text clearly states, 'The present system of atomic masses is based on carbon-12 (¹²C) as the standard, and it was agreed upon in 1961.'

9. What is the approximate mass percentage of hydrogen and oxygen in water (H₂O)? (Given: Molar mass of H₂O = 18.02 g/mol)

  1. Hydrogen ≈ 88.79%, Oxygen ≈ 11.18%
  2. Hydrogen ≈ 11.18%, Oxygen ≈ 88.79%
  3. Hydrogen ≈ 50%, Oxygen ≈ 50%
  4. Hydrogen ≈ 25%, Oxygen ≈ 75%

Answer: Hydrogen ≈ 11.18%, Oxygen ≈ 88.79%

Explanation: To calculate the mass percentage:
Mass % of Hydrogen = (Mass of H in H₂O / Molar mass of H₂O) × 100 = (2 × 1.008 g/mol / 18.02 g/mol) × 100 ≈ 11.18%
Mass % of Oxygen = (Mass of O in H₂O / Molar mass of H₂O) × 100 = (16.00 g/mol / 18.02 g/mol) × 100 ≈ 88.79%

10. Which fundamental law forms the basis for balancing chemical equations?

  1. Law of Multiple Proportions
  2. Law of Definite Proportions
  3. Law of Conservation of Mass
  4. Avogadro's Law

Answer: Law of Conservation of Mass

Explanation: The Law of Conservation of Mass states that matter cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. Therefore, the total mass of the reactants must equal the total mass of the products, which implies that the number of atoms of each element must be equal on both sides of a balanced chemical equation.

11. Which statement correctly differentiates between elements and compounds, and lists the three common states of matter?

  1. Matter exists as solid, liquid, and gas. Elements are formed from two or more atoms in a fixed ratio, while compounds contain only one type of particle.
  2. Matter exists as solid, liquid, and gas. Elements contain particles of only one type, while compounds are formed when atoms of two or more elements combine in a fixed ratio.
  3. Matter exists as plasma, solid, and liquid. Elements are pure substances, and compounds are mixtures.
  4. Matter exists as solid, liquid, and gas. Elements are always monatomic, while compounds are always polyatomic.

Answer: Matter exists as solid, liquid, and gas. Elements contain particles of only one type, while compounds are formed when atoms of two or more elements combine in a fixed ratio.

Explanation: Matter commonly exists in three states: solid, liquid, and gas. An element is a pure substance consisting only of atoms that all have the same numbers of protons (i.e., the same atomic number). A compound is a substance formed when two or more chemical elements are chemically bonded together in a fixed ratio.

12. What is the concentration of sugar (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁) in mol L⁻¹ if 20 g of it are dissolved in enough water to make a final volume of 2 L? (Molar mass of C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ ≈ 342.3 g/mol)

  1. 0.0584 mol L⁻¹
  2. 0.0292 mol L⁻¹
  3. 0.1168 mol L⁻¹
  4. 0.0146 mol L⁻¹

Answer: 0.0292 mol L⁻¹

Explanation: First, calculate the number of moles of sugar:
Number of moles = Mass / Molar mass = 20 g / 342.3 g/mol ≈ 0.05843 mol
Then, calculate the concentration (Molarity):
Molarity = Moles / Volume = 0.05843 mol / 2 L ≈ 0.0292 mol L⁻¹

13. The word 'atom' is derived from a Greek word meaning:

  1. 'Smallest particle'
  2. 'Building block'
  3. 'Uncut-able' or 'non-divisible'
  4. 'Fundamental unit'

Answer: 'Uncut-able' or 'non-divisible'

Explanation: The word 'atom' originates from the Greek word 'a-tomio', which translates to 'uncut-able' or 'non-divisible', reflecting the ancient philosophical idea that atoms were the smallest, indivisible particles of matter.

14. What is the accepted value for the mass of an electron (mₑ)?

  1. 1.672 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
  2. 9.1094 × 10⁻³¹ kg
  3. 1.008 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
  4. 6.022 × 10²³ kg

Answer: 9.1094 × 10⁻³¹ kg

Explanation: The accepted value for the mass of an electron (mₑ) is approximately 9.1094 × 10⁻³¹ kg. The other options represent the mass of a proton, neutron, or Avogadro's number.

15. Which observation from Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering experiment led him to conclude that most of the space within an atom is empty?

  1. A small fraction of alpha-particles were deflected at large angles.
  2. Some alpha-particles bounced back completely.
  3. Most of the alpha-particles passed through the gold foil undeflected.
  4. Alpha-particles were absorbed by the gold foil.

Answer: Most of the alpha-particles passed through the gold foil undeflected.

Explanation: Rutherford observed that the vast majority of alpha-particles passed straight through the gold foil without any deflection. This indicated that there was very little resistance to their passage, implying that most of the atom's volume is empty space, with the positive charge and mass concentrated in a tiny nucleus.

16. For a neutral atom represented by the notation A_Z X, which of the following relationships is correct?

  1. Number of protons = Number of electrons = Mass number (A)
  2. Number of protons = Number of electrons = Atomic number (Z)
  3. Number of protons = Atomic number (Z), Number of electrons = Mass number (A)
  4. Number of electrons = Atomic number (Z), Number of protons = Mass number (A)

Answer: Number of protons = Number of electrons = Atomic number (Z)

Explanation: In a neutral atom, the number of positively charged protons must be equal to the number of negatively charged electrons to maintain electrical neutrality. The atomic number (Z) is defined as the number of protons in the nucleus. Therefore, for a neutral atom, the number of protons, the number of electrons, and the atomic number (Z) are all equal.

17. Which of the following correctly states the SI units for frequency and wavelength, and defines frequency?

  1. Frequency: hertz (Hz, s⁻¹), number of waves passing a point in one second; Wavelength: meter (m)
  2. Frequency: joule (J), energy per second; Wavelength: nanometer (nm)
  3. Frequency: second (s), time for one wave; Wavelength: angstrom (Å)
  4. Frequency: meter (m), distance per second; Wavelength: hertz (Hz)

Answer: Frequency: hertz (Hz, s⁻¹), number of waves passing a point in one second; Wavelength: meter (m)

Explanation: The SI unit for frequency (ν) is hertz (Hz), which is defined as the number of waves that pass a given point in one second (s⁻¹). The SI unit for wavelength (λ) is the meter (m).

18. Which of the following best approximates a black body in a physical setup?

  1. A highly polished silver surface
  2. A cavity with a tiny hole and no other openings
  3. A perfectly transparent glass prism
  4. A white, diffuse reflecting surface

Answer: A cavity with a tiny hole and no other openings

Explanation: A good physical approximation to a black body is a cavity with a tiny hole, which has no other opening. This setup allows for almost all incident radiation to be absorbed, mimicking the ideal black body behavior.

19. In the context of the photoelectric effect, how is a beam of light conceptually viewed, and what are its constituent particles called?

  1. As a continuous wave, with constituent particles called electrons
  2. As shooting a beam of particles, with constituent particles called photons
  3. As an electromagnetic field, with constituent particles called protons
  4. As a stream of energy, with constituent particles called neutrons

Answer: As shooting a beam of particles, with constituent particles called photons

Explanation: According to the photoelectric effect, a beam of light shining onto a metal surface is conceptually viewed as shooting a beam of particles. These constituent particles are called photons.

20. Balmer's formula for the visible lines of the hydrogen spectrum in terms of wavenumber (ν) is given by:

  1. ν = R_H (1/1² - 1/n²) cm⁻¹, where n = 2, 3, 4,...
  2. ν = 109,677 (1/2² - 1/n²) cm⁻¹, where n = 3, 4, 5,...
  3. ν = 109,677 (1/n² - 1/2²) cm⁻¹, where n = 1, 2, 3,...
  4. ν = R_H (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²) cm⁻¹, where n₁ < n₂

Answer: ν = 109,677 (1/2² - 1/n²) cm⁻¹, where n = 3, 4, 5,...

Explanation: Balmer showed that the visible lines of the hydrogen spectrum obey the formula: ν = 109,677 (1/2² - 1/n²) cm⁻¹, where n is an integer equal to or greater than 3 (i.e., n = 3, 4, 5,...). This formula specifically applies to the visible region of the hydrogen spectrum.

21. What does the negative sign for the electronic energy (E_n) in a hydrogen atom signify?

  1. The electron is gaining energy from the nucleus.
  2. The electron is repelled by the nucleus.
  3. The electron is bound to the nucleus and its energy is lower than that of a free electron at rest.
  4. The electron is in an excited state.

Answer: The electron is bound to the nucleus and its energy is lower than that of a free electron at rest.

Explanation: The negative sign for the electronic energy (E_n) in a hydrogen atom indicates that the energy of the electron in the atom is lower than the energy of a free electron at rest. It implies that the electron is bound to the nucleus and its energy is less than zero, which is the energy assigned to a free electron infinitely far from the nucleus.

22. De Broglie suggested the dual behaviour of electrons by drawing an analogy with which of the following?

  1. Sound waves, which exhibit both reflection and refraction.
  2. Water waves, which show interference and diffraction.
  3. Photons, which possess both momentum and wavelength.
  4. Alpha particles, which have both mass and charge.

Answer: Photons, which possess both momentum and wavelength.

Explanation: De Broglie drew an analogy with photons, stating that just as a photon has both momentum and wavelength, electrons should also have momentum as well as wavelength, thereby exhibiting dual particle and wave nature.

23. According to the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle, why can't the classical picture of electrons moving in fixed Bohr's orbits hold true?

  1. Electrons are too small to be observed directly.
  2. The uncertainty in an electron's velocity becomes too large if its position is precisely known, contradicting fixed orbits.
  3. Electrons continuously emit radiation, causing them to spiral into the nucleus.
  4. The gravitational force on electrons is negligible, preventing stable orbits.

Answer: The uncertainty in an electron's velocity becomes too large if its position is precisely known, contradicting fixed orbits.

Explanation: If one tries to find the exact location of an electron with high precision, the uncertainty in its velocity (Δv) would be so large that it contradicts the idea of electrons moving in fixed, well-defined orbits. This means precise statements of position and momentum must be replaced by probability statements, making Bohr's fixed orbits untenable.

24. Why is solving the Schrödinger equation for multi-electron atoms more challenging than for the hydrogen atom, and how is this difficulty typically addressed?

  1. Multi-electron atoms have more protons, making the equation more complex; it's addressed by ignoring electron-electron repulsion.
  2. The Schrödinger equation cannot be solved exactly for multi-electron atoms due to electron-electron repulsion; it's addressed using approximate methods, often with computers.
  3. Multi-electron atoms have varying isotopes, which complicates the equation; it's addressed by averaging atomic masses.
  4. The Schrödinger equation only applies to single-electron systems; it's addressed by using classical mechanics for multi-electron atoms.

Answer: The Schrödinger equation cannot be solved exactly for multi-electron atoms due to electron-electron repulsion; it's addressed using approximate methods, often with computers.

Explanation: The Schrödinger equation cannot be solved exactly for a multi-electron atom primarily due to the complex interactions (repulsions) between multiple electrons. This difficulty is overcome by using approximate methods, often aided by modern computers, to find solutions.

25. Which of the following correctly represents the relationship between the azimuthal quantum number (l) and the number of orbitals in a sub-shell?

  1. l=0 (s sub-shell) has 3 orbitals
  2. l=1 (p sub-shell) has 1 orbital
  3. l=2 (d sub-shell) has 5 orbitals
  4. l=3 (f sub-shell) has 9 orbitals

Answer: l=2 (d sub-shell) has 5 orbitals

Explanation: The number of orbitals in a sub-shell is given by the formula (2l+1). For l=0 (s sub-shell), number of orbitals = 2(0)+1 = 1. For l=1 (p sub-shell), number of orbitals = 2(1)+1 = 3. For l=2 (d sub-shell), number of orbitals = 2(2)+1 = 5. For l=3 (f sub-shell), number of orbitals = 2(3)+1 = 7.
