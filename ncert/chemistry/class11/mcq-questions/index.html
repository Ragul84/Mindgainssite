26. What is the primary purpose of a boundary surface diagram for an atomic orbital?

  1. To show the exact path of an electron around the nucleus.
  2. To represent the energy levels of electrons in an atom.
  3. To depict the region where the probability of finding an electron is very high (e.g., 90%).
  4. To illustrate the number of electrons present in an orbital.

Answer: To depict the region where the probability of finding an electron is very high (e.g., 90%).

Explanation: A boundary surface diagram is used to represent the shape of an orbital. It encloses a region in space where there is a high probability (typically around 90%) of finding the electron, as the exact position of an electron cannot be determined due to the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle.

27. How do the electrical interactions in a multi-electron atom differ from those in a hydrogen atom?

  1. In multi-electron atoms, only attraction between electrons and the nucleus exists, similar to hydrogen.
  2. Multi-electron atoms have only electron-electron repulsion, unlike hydrogen.
  3. In multi-electron atoms, there are additional electron-electron repulsion terms along with electron-nucleus attraction.
  4. Hydrogen atoms have stronger electron-nucleus attraction than multi-electron atoms.

Answer: In multi-electron atoms, there are additional electron-electron repulsion terms along with electron-nucleus attraction.

Explanation: In a hydrogen atom, the only electrical interaction is the attraction between the single electron and the nucleus. In multi-electron atoms, besides this electron-nucleus attraction, there are significant repulsion terms between every electron and all other electrons present in the atom, making the system more complex.

28. According to Pauli's exclusion principle, if two electrons occupy the same orbital, which of the following statements about their quantum numbers is correct?

  1. They must have different principal quantum numbers (n).
  2. They must have different azimuthal quantum numbers (l).
  3. They must have opposite spin quantum numbers (ms).
  4. They must have different magnetic quantum numbers (ml).

Answer: They must have opposite spin quantum numbers (ms).

Explanation: Pauli's exclusion principle states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers. If two electrons are in the same orbital, they will have the same values for n, l, and ml. Therefore, to satisfy the principle, they must have opposite spin quantum numbers (ms), typically +1/2 and -1/2.

29. After the 6s orbital is filled in Caesium (Cs) and Barium (Ba), in which orbitals do electrons primarily fill up from Lanthanum (La) to Mercury (Hg)?

  1. 5p and 6d orbitals
  2. 4f and 5d orbitals
  3. 6s and 6p orbitals
  4. 5f and 6d orbitals

Answer: 4f and 5d orbitals

Explanation: According to the Aufbau principle and the order of filling orbitals, after the 6s orbital is filled, the electrons start filling the 4f orbitals (for the lanthanides) and then the 5d orbitals (for the transition metals) in the period from Lanthanum (La) to Mercury (Hg).

30. How does the energy of orbitals within a given shell differ between a hydrogen atom and a multi-electron atom?

  1. In both, all orbitals within a shell have the same energy.
  2. In hydrogen, orbital energy depends on n and l; in multi-electron atoms, it depends only on n.
  3. In hydrogen, all orbitals within a shell have the same energy; in multi-electron atoms, orbital energy depends on both n and l.
  4. In multi-electron atoms, all orbitals within a shell have the same energy; in hydrogen, orbital energy depends on both n and l.

Answer: In hydrogen, all orbitals within a shell have the same energy; in multi-electron atoms, orbital energy depends on both n and l.

Explanation: For hydrogen and hydrogen-like species (e.g., He+, Li2+), the energy of an orbital depends only on the principal quantum number (n), meaning all orbitals within the same shell (e.g., 2s, 2p) have the same energy. However, in multi-electron atoms, due to electron-electron repulsions and shielding effects, the energy of an orbital depends on both the principal quantum number (n) and the azimuthal quantum number (l).

31. 2 × 10⁸ atoms of carbon are arranged side by side, forming a total length of 2.4 cm. What is the radius of a single carbon atom in picometers (pm)?

  1. 120 pm
  2. 60 pm
  3. 12 pm
  4. 6 pm

Answer: 60 pm

Explanation: Given: Total length = 2.4 cm, Number of atoms = 2 × 10⁸.
Total length = Number of atoms × Diameter of one atom.
Diameter = Total length / Number of atoms = 2.4 cm / (2 × 10⁸) = 1.2 × 10⁻⁸ cm.
Radius = Diameter / 2 = (1.2 × 10⁻⁸ cm) / 2 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁹ cm.
To convert to picometers: 1 cm = 10¹⁰ pm.
Radius = 6.0 × 10⁻⁹ cm × (10¹⁰ pm / 1 cm) = 60 pm.

32. Both Aluminum (Al) and Silicon (Si) have their unpaired electrons in the 3p orbital. Which element's unpaired electron will experience a greater effective nuclear charge, and why?

  1. Aluminum (Al), because it has fewer electrons, leading to less shielding.
  2. Silicon (Si), because it has a higher atomic number (Z) and thus more protons.
  3. Aluminum (Al), because its 3p orbital is closer to the nucleus.
  4. Silicon (Si), because it has more electron-electron repulsion, which increases effective nuclear charge.

Answer: Silicon (Si), because it has a higher atomic number (Z) and thus more protons.

Explanation: Silicon (Si) has an atomic number (Z) of 14, while Aluminum (Al) has Z=13. A higher atomic number means more protons in the nucleus. Even though both have their unpaired electrons in the 3p orbital and experience similar shielding from inner electrons, the increased positive charge in the nucleus of Silicon leads to a stronger attraction for its valence electrons, resulting in a greater effective nuclear charge (Zeff) compared to Aluminum.

33. According to Mendeleev's Periodic Law, the properties of elements are a periodic function of their:

  1. Atomic number
  2. Atomic mass
  3. Atomic weight
  4. Electronic configuration

Answer: Atomic weight

Explanation: Mendeleev's Periodic Law, formulated before the discovery of atomic number, stated that the properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic weights. This was the basis for his original periodic table.

34. Which scientist is credited with the reconfiguration of the periodic table by placing the actinoids below the lanthanoids, following his discovery of transuranium elements?

  1. Dmitri Mendeleev
  2. Henry Moseley
  3. Glenn T. Seaborg
  4. Ernest Rutherford

Answer: Glenn T. Seaborg

Explanation: Glenn T. Seaborg was instrumental in the discovery of several transuranium elements, starting with plutonium. His work led to the understanding that the actinoids should be placed as a separate series below the lanthanoids, reconfiguring the periodic table to its modern form. Element 106, Seaborgium (Sg), is named in his honour.

35. Why does the 5th period of the Periodic Table contain 18 elements?

  1. It fills the 5s and 5p orbitals only.
  2. It fills the 5s, 4d, and 5p orbitals.
  3. It fills the 5s, 5p, and 5d orbitals.
  4. It fills the 4s, 4p, and 4d orbitals.

Answer: It fills the 5s, 4d, and 5p orbitals.

Explanation: For the 5th period (n=5), the orbitals filled according to the Aufbau principle are 5s, 4d, and 5p. There is one 5s orbital, five 4d orbitals, and three 5p orbitals, totaling 1 + 5 + 3 = 9 orbitals. Since each orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons, a total of 9 * 2 = 18 electrons can be accommodated, hence 18 elements.

36. What percentage of known elements are classified as metals, and where are they typically found in the Periodic Table?

  1. Less than 50%, right side
  2. More than 78%, left side
  3. Approximately 60%, middle section
  4. Less than 20%, top right corner

Answer: More than 78%, left side

Explanation: Metals constitute a significant majority of the known elements, comprising more than 78%. They are generally located on the left side and in the center of the Periodic Table, separated from non-metals by a diagonal line of metalloids.

37. Given the isoelectronic species O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, and Mg²⁺, how would their ionic radii compare?

  1. They would all have the same radii because they are isoelectronic.
  2. Their radii would be different due to varying nuclear charges.
  3. O²⁻ would have the smallest radius, and Mg²⁺ would have the largest.
  4. Na⁺ would have the largest radius, and F⁻ would have the smallest.

Answer: Their radii would be different due to varying nuclear charges.

Explanation: While isoelectronic species have the same number of electrons, their nuclear charges (atomic numbers) are different. A higher nuclear charge leads to a stronger attraction for the electron cloud, pulling the electrons closer to the nucleus and resulting in a smaller ionic radius. Therefore, their radii would be different, with O²⁻ having the largest radius (lowest nuclear charge) and Mg²⁺ having the smallest radius (highest nuclear charge).

38. The first ionization enthalpy (ΔiH) values for Na, Mg, and Si are 496, 737, and 786 kJ mol⁻¹, respectively. Which value is most likely for the first ΔiH of Aluminium (Al)?

  1. 760 kJ mol⁻¹
  2. 575 kJ mol⁻¹
  3. 800 kJ mol⁻¹
  4. 450 kJ mol⁻¹

Answer: 575 kJ mol⁻¹

Explanation: The first ionization enthalpy for Al is expected to be lower than that of Mg (737 kJ mol⁻¹) despite Al having a higher nuclear charge. This is because Al has a 3p¹ electron, which is effectively shielded by the inner 3s² electrons. This shielding effect makes it easier to remove the 3p electron from Al compared to removing a 3s electron from Mg, leading to a lower ionization enthalpy for Al. Therefore, 575 kJ mol⁻¹ is the more appropriate value.

39. How is electronegativity related to the metallic and non-metallic properties of elements?

  1. Directly related to metallic properties, inversely related to non-metallic properties.
  2. Inversely related to metallic properties, directly related to non-metallic properties.
  3. Directly related to both metallic and non-metallic properties.
  4. Inversely related to both metallic and non-metallic properties.

Answer: Inversely related to metallic properties, directly related to non-metallic properties.

Explanation: Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract a shared pair of electrons. Non-metallic elements have a strong tendency to gain electrons, hence they are highly electronegative. Conversely, metallic elements tend to lose electrons and thus have low electronegativity. Therefore, electronegativity is directly related to non-metallic properties and inversely related to metallic properties.

40. What is the fundamental reason for the observed periodicity in the chemical and physical properties of elements?

  1. Their atomic masses.
  2. Their nuclear stability.
  3. Their electronic configuration.
  4. Their isotopic abundance.

Answer: Their electronic configuration.

Explanation: The periodicity in the properties of elements is fundamentally due to the recurrence of similar outer electronic configurations at regular intervals. It is the arrangement of electrons in the outermost shell that primarily dictates an element's chemical behavior and many physical properties.

41. Elements belonging to the same group in the periodic table exhibit similar physical and chemical properties primarily due to which of the following reasons?

  1. They have the same atomic mass.
  2. They possess the same number of valence electrons and similar outermost electronic configurations.
  3. They have the same number of core electrons.
  4. They are all found in the same physical state at room temperature.

Answer: They possess the same number of valence electrons and similar outermost electronic configurations.

Explanation: Elements in the same group have similar physical and chemical properties because their outermost electron shells contain the same number of valence electrons. This identical valence electron configuration dictates their bonding behavior and chemical reactivity.

42. Which of the following sets correctly lists three factors that significantly influence the chemistry of an element by affecting its valence shell electrons?

  1. Atomic number, isotopic mass, and density.
  2. Valence principal quantum number (n), nuclear charge (Z), and number of core electrons.
  3. Melting point, boiling point, and electronegativity.
  4. Crystal structure, magnetic properties, and electrical conductivity.

Answer: Valence principal quantum number (n), nuclear charge (Z), and number of core electrons.

Explanation: The chemistry of an element is primarily determined by its valence electrons. The valence principal quantum number (n) affects the energy level and distance of valence electrons, nuclear charge (Z) influences the attraction, and the number of core electrons provides shielding, all of which collectively impact the behavior of valence electrons.

43. An electrovalent bond is best defined as:

  1. A bond formed by the sharing of electrons between two atoms.
  2. A bond formed as a result of the electrostatic attraction between positive and negative ions.
  3. A bond formed when electrons are delocalized over multiple atoms.
  4. A bond formed between two non-metal atoms with similar electronegativity.

Answer: A bond formed as a result of the electrostatic attraction between positive and negative ions.

Explanation: An electrovalent bond, also known as an ionic bond, is formed due to the complete transfer of electrons from one atom to another, leading to the formation of oppositely charged ions. These ions are then held together by strong electrostatic forces of attraction.

44. The formal charge of an atom in a polyatomic molecule or ion is defined as the difference between:

  1. The number of protons and electrons in the atom.
  2. The number of bonding electrons and non-bonding electrons.
  3. The number of valence electrons of that atom in an isolated state and the number of electrons assigned to that atom in the Lewis structure.
  4. The electronegativity of the atom and the average electronegativity of the molecule.

Answer: The number of valence electrons of that atom in an isolated state and the number of electrons assigned to that atom in the Lewis structure.

Explanation: The formal charge of an atom in a polyatomic molecule or ion is calculated as: (Number of valence electrons in free atom) - (Number of non-bonding electrons) - (1/2 * Number of bonding electrons). This essentially represents the difference between the valence electrons of the isolated atom and the electrons it 'owns' in the Lewis structure.

45. What is the Lattice Enthalpy of an ionic solid?

  1. The energy released when one mole of an ionic compound is dissolved in water.
  2. The energy required to completely separate one mole of a solid ionic compound into its gaseous constituent ions.
  3. The energy required to melt one mole of an ionic solid.
  4. The energy released during the formation of one mole of an ionic solid from its gaseous ions.

Answer: The energy required to completely separate one mole of a solid ionic compound into its gaseous constituent ions.

Explanation: Lattice Enthalpy is a measure of the strength of the forces holding ions together in a solid ionic lattice. It is specifically defined as the energy needed to break apart one mole of a solid ionic compound into its individual gaseous ions.

46. Why cannot a single Lewis structure accurately represent the bonding in the ozone (O3) molecule?

  1. Ozone is a paramagnetic molecule.
  2. A single Lewis structure would show unequal bond lengths, while experimental data indicates identical bond lengths.
  3. Ozone has an odd number of valence electrons.
  4. The central oxygen atom in ozone does not obey the octet rule.

Answer: A single Lewis structure would show unequal bond lengths, while experimental data indicates identical bond lengths.

Explanation: A single Lewis structure for O3 would typically show one O=O double bond and one O-O single bond, implying different bond lengths. However, experimental evidence shows both O-O bonds are identical and intermediate in length, which is explained by resonance structures, not a single Lewis structure.

47. According to Fajan's rules, which combination of cation and anion sizes would lead to the greatest covalent character in an ionic bond?

  1. Large cation and large anion.
  2. Small cation and small anion.
  3. Large cation and small anion.
  4. Small cation and large anion.

Answer: Small cation and large anion.

Explanation: Fajan's rules state that covalent character in an ionic bond increases with a smaller cation (higher polarizing power) and a larger anion (more polarizable). A smaller cation can more effectively distort the electron cloud of a larger, more diffuse anion, leading to greater electron sharing and thus more covalent character.

48. The bent or angular shape of a water molecule (H2O) with an approximate bond angle of 104.5° is primarily due to:

  1. The presence of strong hydrogen bonding between water molecules.
  2. The repulsion between the two lone pairs of electrons on the central oxygen atom and the bond pairs.
  3. The small size of hydrogen atoms.
  4. The high electronegativity of oxygen.

Answer: The repulsion between the two lone pairs of electrons on the central oxygen atom and the bond pairs.

Explanation: According to VSEPR theory, the central oxygen atom in water has two bond pairs and two lone pairs. The lone pair-lone pair repulsion is stronger than lone pair-bond pair repulsion, which in turn is stronger than bond pair-bond pair repulsion. These strong repulsions from the lone pairs compress the H-O-H bond angle from the ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°) to approximately 104.5°, resulting in a bent shape.

49. Which of the following represents the electronic configuration of carbon in its excited state when forming the methane (CH4) molecule?

  1. [He] 2s1 2px1 2py1 2pz1
  2. [He] 2s2 2px1 2py1
  3. [He] 2s1 2px2 2py1
  4. [He] 2s2 2px2

Answer: [He] 2s1 2px1 2py1 2pz1

Explanation: In its ground state, carbon has the electronic configuration [He] 2s2 2px1 2py1. To form four bonds in methane, one electron from the 2s orbital is promoted to the vacant 2pz orbital, resulting in the excited state configuration [He] 2s1 2px1 2py1 2pz1. This allows for sp3 hybridization and the formation of four equivalent C-H bonds.

50. What is the hybridization of boron in BCl3, and what are its resulting molecular geometry and bond angle?

  1. sp2, trigonal planar, 120°
  2. sp3, tetrahedral, 109.5°
  3. sp, linear, 180°
  4. sp3d, trigonal bipyramidal, 90° and 120°

Answer: sp2, trigonal planar, 120°

Explanation: In BCl3, the central boron atom has three bond pairs and no lone pairs. To accommodate these three bond pairs, boron undergoes sp2 hybridization. This leads to a trigonal planar geometry with Cl-B-Cl bond angles of 120°.
