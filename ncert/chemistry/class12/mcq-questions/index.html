26. Identify the major product formed when propene reacts with HBr in the presence of a peroxide.

  1. 1-bromopropane
  2. 2-bromopropane
  3. 1,2-dibromopropane
  4. Propane

Answer: 1-bromopropane

Explanation: When propene reacts with HBr in the presence of a peroxide, the reaction follows an anti-Markovnikov's rule mechanism, which is a free radical addition. In this mechanism, the bromine atom adds to the less substituted carbon atom, and the hydrogen atom adds to the more substituted carbon atom, leading to the formation of 1-bromopropane.

27. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of an ideal solution?

  1. ΔH_mix > 0
  2. ΔV_mix < 0
  3. Obeys Raoult's Law over the entire range of concentration.
  4. Intermolecular forces between A-B are stronger than A-A and B-B.

Answer: Obeys Raoult's Law over the entire range of concentration.

Explanation: An ideal solution is characterized by obeying Raoult's Law over the entire range of concentration. For an ideal solution, ΔH_mix = 0 and ΔV_mix = 0, meaning there is no heat change or volume change upon mixing. Also, the intermolecular forces between A-B components are similar to those between A-A and B-B components.

28. What is the coordination number of the central metal ion in [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl?

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 8

Answer: 6

Explanation: In the complex [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl, 'en' (ethylenediamine) is a bidentate ligand, meaning each 'en' molecule forms two coordinate bonds with the central metal ion. There are two 'en' ligands, so they contribute 2 x 2 = 4 coordinate bonds. The 'Cl' (chloride) ligands are monodentate, and there are two of them, contributing 2 x 1 = 2 coordinate bonds. Therefore, the total coordination number of Co is 4 + 2 = 6.

29. Which of the following reagents can be used to distinguish between propanal and propanone?

  1. Tollens' reagent
  2. Fehling's solution
  3. Iodoform test
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Propanal is an aldehyde, and propanone is a ketone. Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution are used to distinguish aldehydes from ketones, as aldehydes give a positive test (silver mirror/red precipitate) while ketones do not. The iodoform test can also distinguish them because propanone (a methyl ketone) will give a positive iodoform test (yellow precipitate of CHI₃), while propanal will not.

30. For a first-order reaction, the time required for 99.9% completion is how many times the half-life of the reaction?

  1. 2
  2. 5
  3. 10
  4. 7

Answer: 10

Explanation: For a first-order reaction, the time for 99.9% completion (t_99.9%) can be calculated using the formula: t = (2.303/k) log(A₀/A). If 99.9% is completed, then A = 0.1% of A₀, so A₀/A = 1000. Thus, t_99.9% = (2.303/k) log(1000) = (2.303/k) * 3. The half-life (t_1/2) for a first-order reaction is (0.693/k). Since 2.303 * 3 ≈ 6.909, and 0.693 * 10 = 6.93, we can approximate t_99.9% ≈ 10 * t_1/2. More precisely, t_99.9% = (3 / 0.301) * t_1/2 ≈ 9.96 * t_1/2, which is approximately 10 times the half-life.

31. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic property of colloids?

  1. Tyndall effect
  2. Brownian movement
  3. Homogeneous nature
  4. Electrophoresis

Answer: Homogeneous nature

Explanation: Colloids are heterogeneous mixtures, not homogeneous. They consist of a dispersed phase and a dispersion medium. The other options – Tyndall effect (scattering of light), Brownian movement (random motion of colloidal particles), and electrophoresis (movement of charged colloidal particles under an electric field) – are all characteristic properties of colloids.

32. An aqueous solution of a substance gives a white precipitate with AgNO₃ solution. The precipitate is soluble in dilute ammonia solution. The substance could be:

  1. AgCl
  2. AgBr
  3. AgI
  4. Ag₂S

Answer: AgCl

Explanation: This question tests the characteristic reactions of silver halides. Silver chloride (AgCl) forms a white precipitate with AgNO₃ and is soluble in dilute ammonia solution due to the formation of the diamminesilver(I) complex, [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. Silver bromide (AgBr) forms a pale yellow precipitate and is sparingly soluble in dilute ammonia. Silver iodide (AgI) forms a yellow precipitate and is insoluble in dilute ammonia. Silver sulfide (Ag₂S) is a black precipitate and is not relevant here.

33. Which of the following polymers is a thermoplastic?

  1. Bakelite
  2. Melamine-formaldehyde resin
  3. Polyethylene
  4. Vulcanized rubber

Answer: Polyethylene

Explanation: Thermoplastics are polymers that can be softened on heating and hardened on cooling, and this process can be repeated. They have linear or slightly branched structures. Polyethylene is a thermoplastic. Bakelite and melamine-formaldehyde resin are thermosetting polymers, which undergo irreversible chemical changes on heating and cannot be reshaped. Vulcanized rubber is an elastomer, which has cross-links but is not a thermoplastic.

34. The number of atoms per unit cell in a body-centered cubic (BCC) lattice is:

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 4
  4. 6

Answer: 2

Explanation: In a body-centered cubic (BCC) lattice, there are 8 atoms at the corners and 1 atom at the body center. Each corner atom contributes 1/8 to the unit cell, so 8 × (1/8) = 1 atom from the corners. The atom at the body center contributes 1 whole atom. Therefore, the total number of atoms per unit cell in a BCC lattice is 1 + 1 = 2.

35. Which of the following is an example of a bidentate ligand?

  1. NH₃
  2. Cl⁻
  3. H₂O
  4. Ethylenediamine (en)

Answer: Ethylenediamine (en)

Explanation: A bidentate ligand is a ligand that can form two coordinate bonds with a central metal atom or ion. Ethylenediamine (en), with the formula H₂N-CH₂-CH₂-NH₂, has two nitrogen atoms, each capable of donating a lone pair of electrons to the metal, thus acting as a bidentate ligand. NH₃, Cl⁻, and H₂O are monodentate ligands, meaning they can form only one coordinate bond.

36. The IUPAC name of the compound (CH₃)₂CH-CH₂-CHO is:

  1. 2-methylbutanal
  2. 3-methylbutanal
  3. isopentanal
  4. 2,2-dimethylpropanal

Answer: 3-methylbutanal

Explanation: To name the compound (CH₃)₂CH-CH₂-CHO, we first identify the longest carbon chain containing the aldehyde functional group (-CHO). This chain has 4 carbons. The aldehyde carbon is always numbered as 1. So, the chain is -CH₂-CHO. The methyl group is attached to the third carbon from the aldehyde end. Therefore, the IUPAC name is 3-methylbutanal.

37. Which of the following compounds will exhibit optical isomerism?

  1. CH3CH2CH2CH3
  2. CH3CH(OH)COOH
  3. CH2=CH-CH2-CH3
  4. CH3-CCl2-CH3

Answer: CH3CH(OH)COOH

Explanation: Optical isomerism (enantiomerism) arises in molecules that are chiral, meaning they are non-superimposable on their mirror images. A common condition for chirality is the presence of a chiral carbon atom, which is a carbon atom bonded to four different groups. In CH3CH(OH)COOH (lactic acid), the central carbon atom is bonded to -CH3, -H, -OH, and -COOH groups, making it a chiral center. The other options lack a chiral carbon atom.

38. An aqueous solution of a substance shows elevation in boiling point. Which of the following statements is TRUE about this solution?

  1. Its freezing point will be higher than that of pure solvent.
  2. Its vapor pressure will be higher than that of pure solvent.
  3. It will have a lower osmotic pressure than pure solvent.
  4. Its freezing point will be lower than that of pure solvent.

Answer: Its freezing point will be lower than that of pure solvent.

Explanation: Elevation in boiling point is a colligative property, meaning it depends on the number of solute particles, not their identity. When a non-volatile solute is added to a solvent, it leads to: 1. Elevation in boiling point (boiling point increases). 2. Depression in freezing point (freezing point decreases). 3. Lowering of vapor pressure (vapor pressure decreases). 4. Increase in osmotic pressure. Therefore, if the boiling point is elevated, the freezing point will be lower than that of the pure solvent.

39. The correct order of increasing basicity of the following amines in aqueous solution is:

  1. NH3 < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH < (CH3)3N
  2. (CH3)3N < NH3 < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH
  3. NH3 < (CH3)3N < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH
  4. (CH3)3N < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH < NH3

Answer: NH3 < (CH3)3N < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH

Explanation: The basicity of amines in aqueous solution is influenced by two main factors: the +I effect of alkyl groups (which increases electron density on nitrogen, making it more available for protonation) and steric hindrance (which hinders the approach of a proton and solvation of the conjugate acid). In aqueous solution, the order is generally: Secondary amine > Primary amine > Tertiary amine > Ammonia. For methyl amines, the order is (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3. So, the increasing order is NH3 < (CH3)3N < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH.

40. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of an ideal solution?

  1. ΔH_mix = 0
  2. ΔV_mix = 0
  3. Obeys Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration.
  4. Intermolecular forces between A-B are stronger than A-A and B-B.

Answer: Intermolecular forces between A-B are stronger than A-A and B-B.

Explanation: An ideal solution is characterized by: 1. ΔH_mix = 0 (no heat absorbed or released on mixing). 2. ΔV_mix = 0 (no change in volume on mixing). 3. It obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations and temperatures. 4. The intermolecular forces of attraction between the solute (A) and solvent (B) molecules are of the same magnitude as those between A-A and B-B molecules. If A-B forces are stronger than A-A and B-B, it would lead to a negative deviation from Raoult's law, characteristic of a non-ideal solution.

41. Which of the following reactions represents the Kolbe's electrolytic reaction?

  1. R-COOH + NaOH → R-COONa + H2O
  2. 2R-COONa + 2H2O (electrolysis) → R-R + 2CO2 + 2NaOH + H2
  3. R-X + 2Na + X-R → R-R + 2NaX
  4. R-COOH + LiAlH4 → R-CH2OH

Answer: 2R-COONa + 2H2O (electrolysis) → R-R + 2CO2 + 2NaOH + H2

Explanation: Kolbe's electrolytic reaction involves the electrolysis of an aqueous solution of a sodium or potassium salt of a carboxylic acid to produce an alkane with an even number of carbon atoms. The general reaction is 2R-COONa + 2H2O (electrolysis) → R-R + 2CO2 + 2NaOH + H2.

43. Which of the following is an example of a non-reducing sugar?

  1. Glucose
  2. Fructose
  3. Sucrose
  4. Maltose

Answer: Sucrose

Explanation: Non-reducing sugars do not have a free anomeric carbon (aldehyde or ketone group) available to reduce other compounds. Sucrose is a disaccharide formed by a glycosidic linkage between the anomeric carbon of glucose and the anomeric carbon of fructose, thus blocking both reducing ends. Glucose, fructose, and maltose all have free anomeric carbons and are therefore reducing sugars.

44. Which of the following compounds will give a positive iodoform test?

  1. Propan-1-ol
  2. Propan-2-ol
  3. Ethanol
  4. Both B and C

Answer: Both B and C

Explanation: The iodoform test is given by compounds containing a CH₃CO- group or a CH₃CH(OH)- group. Propan-2-ol (CH₃CH(OH)CH₃) has the CH₃CH(OH)- group. Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) also has the CH₃CH(OH)- group (as it can be oxidized to acetaldehyde, CH₃CHO, which has the CH₃CO- group). Propan-1-ol (CH₃CH₂CH₂OH) does not have either of these groups. Therefore, both propan-2-ol and ethanol will give a positive iodoform test.

45. The correct order of increasing basicity for the following amines is:

  1. CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH < (CH₃)₃N < NH₃
  2. NH₃ < (CH₃)₃N < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH
  3. (CH₃)₃N < NH₃ < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH
  4. NH₃ < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH < (CH₃)₃N

Answer: NH₃ < (CH₃)₃N < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH

Explanation: The basicity of amines in aqueous solution is influenced by inductive effect (+I effect of alkyl groups), solvation effect, and steric hindrance. For methylamines in aqueous solution, the order of basicity is generally: Secondary amine > Primary amine > Tertiary amine > Ammonia. This is because the +I effect of two methyl groups in (CH₃)₂NH stabilizes the conjugate acid more effectively than one methyl group in CH₃NH₂. However, in (CH₃)₃N, steric hindrance by three methyl groups hinders the solvation of the protonated amine, making it less stable than the protonated primary and secondary amines. Therefore, the order is NH₃ < (CH₃)₃N < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH.

46. Which of the following statements is true for a spontaneous reaction?

  1. ΔG > 0
  2. ΔG < 0
  3. ΔG = 0
  4. ΔH > 0

Answer: ΔG < 0

Explanation: For a reaction to be spontaneous under constant temperature and pressure, the change in Gibbs free energy (ΔG) must be negative (ΔG < 0). If ΔG > 0, the reaction is non-spontaneous. If ΔG = 0, the reaction is at equilibrium. ΔH (enthalpy change) being positive indicates an endothermic reaction, which can be spontaneous or non-spontaneous depending on the entropy change and temperature.

47. The coordination number and oxidation state of Cr in K₃[Cr(C₂O₄)₃] are, respectively:

  1. 6, +3
  2. 3, +3
  3. 6, +2
  4. 3, +2

Answer: 6, +3

Explanation: In K₃[Cr(C₂O₄)₃], C₂O₄²⁻ (oxalate) is a bidentate ligand. Each oxalate ligand forms two coordinate bonds with the central metal ion. Since there are three oxalate ligands, the coordination number of Cr is 3 × 2 = 6.
To find the oxidation state of Cr: Let the oxidation state of Cr be x. The charge on potassium (K) is +1, and there are three K atoms, so +3. The charge on oxalate (C₂O₄) is -2, and there are three oxalate ligands, so 3 × (-2) = -6. The overall complex is neutral.
So, +3 + x + (-6) = 0
x - 3 = 0
x = +3
Therefore, the coordination number is 6 and the oxidation state is +3.

48. Which of the following polymers is a polyamide?

  1. Teflon
  2. Nylon 6,6
  3. Polythene
  4. PVC

Answer: Nylon 6,6

Explanation: Polyamides are polymers containing amide linkages (-CO-NH-) in their backbone. Nylon 6,6 is a synthetic polyamide formed by the condensation polymerization of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid. Teflon is a poly(tetrafluoroethene), polythene is poly(ethene), and PVC is poly(vinyl chloride); none of these contain amide linkages.

49. An aqueous solution of a substance gives a white precipitate with AgNO₃ solution. The precipitate is soluble in excess NH₄OH. The substance is likely to be:

  1. NaCl
  2. NaBr
  3. NaI
  4. Na₂SO₄

Answer: NaCl

Explanation: This is a characteristic test for halide ions. When AgNO₃ is added to a solution containing halide ions, silver halides precipitate.
- AgCl is a white precipitate, soluble in excess NH₄OH (forming [Ag(NH₃)₂]Cl).
- AgBr is a pale yellow precipitate, sparingly soluble in excess NH₄OH.
- AgI is a yellow precipitate, insoluble in excess NH₄OH.
- Na₂SO₄ would give no precipitate with AgNO₃ (unless Ag₂SO₄ precipitates, which is sparingly soluble, but not a white precipitate soluble in NH₄OH in this context).
Therefore, the substance is likely NaCl, as it forms a white precipitate (AgCl) that is soluble in excess NH₄OH.

50. The rate constant of a first-order reaction is 2.303 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. How long will it take for the concentration of the reactant to reduce to 1/10th of its initial value?

  1. 100 s
  2. 1000 s
  3. 2303 s
  4. 230.3 s

Answer: 1000 s

Explanation: For a first-order reaction, the integrated rate law is t = (2.303/k) log([A]₀/[A]).
Given k = 2.303 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ and [A] = [A]₀/10.
So, [A]₀/[A] = 10.
t = (2.303 / (2.303 × 10⁻³)) log(10)
t = (1 / 10⁻³) × 1
t = 1000 s.
