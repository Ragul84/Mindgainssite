1. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding the properties of transition elements?

  1. They exhibit variable oxidation states.
  2. They form colored ions.
  3. They are generally good reducing agents.
  4. They have high melting and boiling points.

Answer: They are generally good reducing agents.

Explanation: Transition elements generally exhibit variable oxidation states due to the involvement of both (n-1)d and ns electrons. They form colored ions due to d-d transitions. They have high melting and boiling points due to strong metallic bonding. However, transition elements, especially in higher oxidation states, tend to act as oxidizing agents rather than reducing agents. For example, KMnO4 and K2Cr2O7 are strong oxidizing agents.

2. The rate constant of a first-order reaction is 2.303 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. What will be the time required for the concentration of the reactant to reduce to 1/10th of its initial value?

  1. 100 s
  2. 230.3 s
  3. 1000 s
  4. 2303 s

Answer: 1000 s

Explanation: For a first-order reaction, the integrated rate law is given by: t = (2.303 / k) * log([A]₀ / [A]).
Given k = 2.303 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ and [A] = [A]₀ / 10.
So, t = (2.303 / (2.303 × 10⁻³)) * log([A]₀ / ([A]₀ / 10))
t = (1 / 10⁻³) * log(10)
t = 1000 * 1
t = 1000 s.

3. Which of the following compounds will NOT give a positive iodoform test?

  1. Ethanol
  2. Propan-2-ol
  3. Propanal
  4. Acetophenone

Answer: Propanal

Explanation: The iodoform test is given by compounds containing a CH₃CO- group or a CH₃CH(OH)- group.
Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) has a CH₃CH(OH)- group (after oxidation to acetaldehyde).
Propan-2-ol (CH₃CH(OH)CH₃) has a CH₃CH(OH)- group.
Acetophenone (C₆H₅COCH₃) has a CH₃CO- group.
Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO) does not have either of these groups. It has a CH₃CH₂CO- group, which does not react to give iodoform.

4. The correct order of increasing acidic strength for the following compounds is:

  1. Phenol < p-Nitrophenol < p-Cresol
  2. p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol
  3. p-Nitrophenol < Phenol < p-Cresol
  4. Phenol < p-Cresol < p-Nitrophenol

Answer: p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol

Explanation: The acidic strength of phenols is influenced by substituents on the benzene ring. Electron-withdrawing groups (like -NO₂) increase acidity by stabilizing the phenoxide ion through resonance and inductive effects. Electron-donating groups (like -CH₃) decrease acidity by destabilizing the phenoxide ion.
p-Nitrophenol has an electron-withdrawing -NO₂ group, making it the most acidic.
p-Cresol has an electron-donating -CH₃ group, making it the least acidic.
Phenol is in between.
Therefore, the order of increasing acidic strength is p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol.

5. Which of the following is a characteristic property of lyophilic colloids?

  1. They are easily coagulated by adding small amounts of electrolytes.
  2. They are irreversible in nature.
  3. They are generally less stable than lyophobic colloids.
  4. They are self-stabilizing and do not require stabilizing agents.

Answer: They are self-stabilizing and do not require stabilizing agents.

Explanation: Lyophilic colloids are 'solvent-loving' and are stable due to strong attractive forces between the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium. This strong interaction makes them self-stabilizing and reversible (if the dispersion medium is removed, the dispersed phase can be redispersed). They are not easily coagulated by electrolytes and are generally more stable than lyophobic colloids.

6. An organic compound 'A' (C₃H₆O) gives a positive Tollen's test and on oxidation with K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ gives 'B' (C₃H₆O₂). Compound 'A' is:

  1. Propanone
  2. Propanal
  3. Propan-1-ol
  4. Propanoic acid

Answer: Propanal

Explanation: The molecular formula C₃H₆O suggests an aldehyde or a ketone.
A positive Tollen's test indicates the presence of an aldehyde group (-CHO). Ketones do not give a positive Tollen's test. So, 'A' is an aldehyde.
Among the options, Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO) is an aldehyde with the formula C₃H₆O.
On oxidation, aldehydes are converted to carboxylic acids. Propanal on oxidation with K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺ will give Propanoic acid (CH₃CH₂COOH), which has the formula C₃H₆O₂.
Therefore, compound 'A' is Propanal.

7. The number of unpaired electrons in the gaseous ion Mn³⁺ (atomic number of Mn = 25) is:

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 5

Answer: 4

Explanation: The electronic configuration of Mn (atomic number 25) is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s².
To form Mn³⁺, three electrons are removed. The electrons are first removed from the 4s orbital and then from the 3d orbital.
So, Mn³⁺ configuration will be [Ar] 3d⁴.
In the 3d⁴ configuration, according to Hund's rule, the four electrons will occupy separate d-orbitals with parallel spins, resulting in 4 unpaired electrons.

8. Which of the following is a primary amine?

  1. N,N-Dimethylaniline
  2. Diethylamine
  3. tert-Butylamine
  4. N-Methylaniline

Answer: tert-Butylamine

Explanation: A primary amine (1°) has one alkyl or aryl group attached to the nitrogen atom (-NH₂).
N,N-Dimethylaniline is a tertiary amine (C₆H₅N(CH₃)₂).
Diethylamine is a secondary amine ((C₂H₅)₂NH).
tert-Butylamine ((CH₃)₃C-NH₂) has only one alkyl group (tert-butyl) attached to the nitrogen atom, making it a primary amine.
N-Methylaniline is a secondary amine (C₆H₅NHCH₃).

9. The osmotic pressure of a solution can be increased by:

  1. Decreasing the temperature.
  2. Increasing the volume of the solution.
  3. Increasing the concentration of the solute.
  4. Adding a non-volatile solvent.

Answer: Increasing the concentration of the solute.

Explanation: The osmotic pressure (π) is given by the formula π = iCRT, where i is the van't Hoff factor, C is the molar concentration of the solute, R is the gas constant, and T is the absolute temperature.
From the formula, osmotic pressure is directly proportional to the concentration of the solute (C) and the absolute temperature (T).
Therefore, increasing the concentration of the solute will increase the osmotic pressure. Decreasing temperature will decrease it. Increasing the volume of the solution (for a fixed amount of solute) will decrease the concentration and thus decrease osmotic pressure. Adding a non-volatile solvent will decrease the concentration of the solute, thereby decreasing osmotic pressure.

10. Which of the following reagents can be used to distinguish between phenol and benzoic acid?

  1. Neutral FeCl₃ solution
  2. Tollen's reagent
  3. Sodium bicarbonate solution
  4. Bromine water

Answer: Sodium bicarbonate solution

Explanation: Phenol is a weak acid, while benzoic acid is a stronger acid.
Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) is a weak base. Benzoic acid, being a stronger acid than carbonic acid, reacts with sodium bicarbonate to produce CO₂ gas with effervescence. Phenol, being a weaker acid than carbonic acid, does not react with sodium bicarbonate.
Neutral FeCl₃ solution gives a characteristic violet coloration with phenol, but not with benzoic acid. However, this is a test for phenols, not a general distinction based on acidity.
Tollen's reagent is used to detect aldehydes. Neither phenol nor benzoic acid will give a positive Tollen's test.
Bromine water reacts with both phenol (decolorizes bromine water and forms a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol) and benzoic acid (under specific conditions, but not a clear distinction for acidity).
Therefore, sodium bicarbonate solution is the most appropriate reagent to distinguish between phenol and benzoic acid based on their acidic strengths.

11. Which of the following is the strongest acid?

  1. CH₃COOH
  2. ClCH₂COOH
  3. Cl₂CHCOOH
  4. Cl₃CCOOH

Answer: Cl₃CCOOH

Explanation: The acidity of carboxylic acids increases with the presence of electron-withdrawing groups (like -Cl) attached to the α-carbon. The more the number of electron-withdrawing groups, the stronger the acid, as they stabilize the carboxylate anion by dispersing the negative charge through inductive effect. Therefore, trichloroacetic acid (Cl₃CCOOH) is the strongest acid among the given options.

12. An example of a lyophilic colloid is:

  1. Gold sol
  2. Starch solution
  3. Ferric hydroxide sol
  4. Arsenic sulphide sol

Answer: Starch solution

Explanation: Lyophilic colloids are 'solvent-loving' colloids, meaning they have a strong affinity for the dispersion medium. Starch solution is a classic example of a lyophilic colloid because starch readily dissolves/disperses in water to form a stable colloidal solution. Gold sol, ferric hydroxide sol, and arsenic sulphide sol are examples of lyophobic colloids, which are 'solvent-hating' and require stabilizing agents to prevent coagulation.

13. The IUPAC name of the compound [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ is:

  1. Pentamminechlorocobalt(III) chloride
  2. Chloropentamminecobalt(III) chloride
  3. Pentamminechlorocobalt(II) chloride
  4. Chloropentamminecobalt(II) chloride

Answer: Pentamminechlorocobalt(III) chloride

Explanation: In the complex [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂, the ligands are five ammonia molecules (ammine) and one chloride ion (chloro). The ligands are named alphabetically, so ammine comes before chloro. The oxidation state of cobalt (Co) needs to be determined. Let the oxidation state of Co be x. NH₃ is neutral (0), Cl inside the coordination sphere is -1, and the two Cl outside are -1 each. So, x + 5(0) + (-1) + 2(-1) = 0, which gives x - 3 = 0, so x = +3. Thus, the IUPAC name is Pentamminechlorocobalt(III) chloride.

14. Which of the following reagents will distinguish between propanal and propanone?

  1. Fehling's solution
  2. Grignard reagent
  3. Tollens' reagent
  4. Both A and C

Answer: Both A and C

Explanation: Propanal is an aldehyde, while propanone is a ketone. Aldehydes can be easily oxidized, while ketones are resistant to mild oxidation. Both Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent are mild oxidizing agents that specifically react with aldehydes to give a positive test (red precipitate for Fehling's, silver mirror for Tollens'). Ketones do not react with these reagents. Therefore, both Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent can distinguish between propanal and propanone. Grignard reagent reacts with both aldehydes and ketones.

15. The molar conductivity of an electrolyte increases with dilution because:

  1. Number of ions per unit volume increases.
  2. Ionic mobility decreases.
  3. Degree of dissociation increases.
  4. Interionic forces of attraction increase.

Answer: Degree of dissociation increases.

Explanation: Molar conductivity (Λm) is defined as the conductivity of a solution containing one mole of electrolyte. For weak electrolytes, with dilution, the degree of dissociation (α) increases, leading to a greater number of ions in the solution. For strong electrolytes, while the number of ions remains constant, the interionic attraction decreases upon dilution, allowing ions to move more freely, thus increasing ionic mobility. However, the most significant factor contributing to the increase in molar conductivity with dilution, especially for weak electrolytes, is the increase in the degree of dissociation. The number of ions per unit volume actually decreases with dilution, and interionic forces decrease, not increase.

16. The reaction of an alkyl halide with sodium metal in dry ether is known as:

  1. Wurtz reaction
  2. Fittig reaction
  3. Wurtz-Fittig reaction
  4. Friedel-Crafts reaction

Answer: Wurtz reaction

Explanation: The Wurtz reaction involves the coupling of two alkyl halides (R-X) in the presence of sodium metal and dry ether to form a higher alkane (R-R). The Fittig reaction involves two aryl halides, and the Wurtz-Fittig reaction involves one alkyl halide and one aryl halide. Friedel-Crafts reaction is an electrophilic aromatic substitution reaction.

17. Which of the following is a non-reducing sugar?

  1. Glucose
  2. Fructose
  3. Maltose
  4. Sucrose

Answer: Sucrose

Explanation: Reducing sugars are carbohydrates that can act as reducing agents due to the presence of a free aldehyde or ketone group (or hemiacetal/hemiketal group that can open to form an aldehyde/ketone). Glucose, fructose, and maltose all have such groups and are thus reducing sugars. Sucrose, on the other hand, is a disaccharide formed by a glycosidic linkage between the C1 of α-glucose and the C2 of β-fructose. This linkage involves the anomeric carbons of both monosaccharides, meaning neither glucose nor fructose can open up to form a free aldehyde or ketone group. Therefore, sucrose is a non-reducing sugar.

18. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding the properties of d-block elements?

  1. They generally form coloured ions.
  2. They exhibit variable oxidation states.
  3. They are good reducing agents.
  4. They form interstitial compounds.

Answer: They are good reducing agents.

Explanation: d-block elements, due to their tendency to lose electrons and form positive ions, are generally good oxidizing agents, not reducing agents. They tend to get oxidized themselves, thereby reducing other species. The other options are characteristic properties of d-block elements.

19. An aqueous solution of a substance shows elevation in boiling point. Which of the following statements is true about the solution?

  1. It has a lower vapor pressure than pure solvent.
  2. It has a higher freezing point than pure solvent.
  3. It has a lower osmotic pressure than pure solvent.
  4. It is an ideal solution.

Answer: It has a lower vapor pressure than pure solvent.

Explanation: Elevation in boiling point is a colligative property that occurs when a non-volatile solute is added to a solvent. This addition leads to a decrease in the vapor pressure of the solution compared to the pure solvent. A lower vapor pressure means more energy is required to reach the boiling point, hence elevation in boiling point. The freezing point would be lowered, and osmotic pressure would be higher (for a given concentration) compared to pure solvent. It does not necessarily imply an ideal solution.

20. Which of the following reactions is an example of a Sandmeyer reaction?

  1. C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₂O → C₆H₅OH + N₂ + HCl
  2. C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + CuCl/HCl → C₆H₅Cl + N₂
  3. C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + KI → C₆H₅I + N₂ + KCl
  4. C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + HBF₄ → C₆H₅F + N₂ + BF₃

Answer: C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + CuCl/HCl → C₆H₅Cl + N₂

Explanation: The Sandmeyer reaction involves the replacement of the diazonium group (-N₂⁺Cl⁻) by a halogen (Cl, Br) or a cyanide group using copper(I) salts (CuCl/HCl, CuBr/HBr, or CuCN/KCN). Option (B) shows the replacement of the diazonium group by chlorine using CuCl/HCl, which is a classic Sandmeyer reaction. Option (A) is hydrolysis, (C) is a Gattermann reaction variation (without copper powder), and (D) is Balz-Schiemann reaction.

21. The primary structure of proteins refers to:

  1. The three-dimensional folding of the polypeptide chain.
  2. The sequence of amino acids in the polypeptide chain.
  3. The arrangement of two or more polypeptide chains.
  4. The formation of α-helix and β-pleated sheet structures.

Answer: The sequence of amino acids in the polypeptide chain.

Explanation: The primary structure of a protein is defined by the specific linear sequence of amino acids linked together by peptide bonds. This sequence is unique to each protein and determines its higher-order structures and biological function. Options (A), (C), and (D) describe tertiary, quaternary, and secondary structures, respectively.

22. Which of the following is a diamagnetic complex?

  1. [NiCl₄]²⁻
  2. [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻
  3. [CoF₆]³⁻
  4. [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺

Answer: [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻

Explanation: To determine diamagnetism, we need to check for the absence of unpaired electrons.
1. [NiCl₄]²⁻: Ni is in +2 oxidation state (d⁸). Cl⁻ is a weak field ligand. In tetrahedral geometry, it will have 2 unpaired electrons (paramagnetic).
2. [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻: Ni is in +2 oxidation state (d⁸). CN⁻ is a strong field ligand. In square planar geometry, all 8 d-electrons pair up, resulting in 0 unpaired electrons (diamagnetic).
3. [CoF₆]³⁻: Co is in +3 oxidation state (d⁶). F⁻ is a weak field ligand. It will have 4 unpaired electrons (paramagnetic).
4. [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺: Fe is in +2 oxidation state (d⁶). H₂O is a weak field ligand. It will have 4 unpaired electrons (paramagnetic).

23. What is the product formed when phenol reacts with chloroform in the presence of dilute NaOH, followed by hydrolysis?

  1. Benzaldehyde
  2. Salicylic acid
  3. Salicylaldehyde
  4. Benzoic acid

Answer: Salicylaldehyde

Explanation: This reaction is known as the Reimer-Tiemann reaction. In this reaction, phenol reacts with chloroform (CHCl₃) in the presence of an alkali (NaOH) to introduce an aldehyde group (-CHO) at the ortho position of the phenolic -OH group. The intermediate formed is then hydrolyzed to give salicylaldehyde (o-hydroxybenzaldehyde).

24. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of physisorption?

  1. It is highly specific.
  2. It is reversible.
  3. It involves weak van der Waals forces.
  4. It has a low enthalpy of adsorption.

Answer: It is highly specific.

Explanation: Physisorption (physical adsorption) is non-specific, meaning that a given adsorbent will adsorb almost any gas to some extent. It is reversible, involves weak van der Waals forces, and has a low enthalpy of adsorption (typically 20-40 kJ/mol). Chemisorption, on the other hand, is highly specific.

25. The IUPAC name of the compound CH₃-CH(CH₃)-CH₂-CHO is:

  1. 3-Methylbutanal
  2. 2-Methylbutanal
  3. 3-Methylbutyraldehyde
  4. Isovaleraldehyde

Answer: 3-Methylbutanal

Explanation: To name the compound, first identify the longest carbon chain containing the aldehyde group (-CHO). This chain has 4 carbons, making it a 'butanal'. The aldehyde carbon is always numbered as 1. So, the methyl group is at position 3. Therefore, the IUPAC name is 3-Methylbutanal.
