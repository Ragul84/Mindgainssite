1. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT regarding the properties of transition elements?

  1. They exhibit variable oxidation states.
  2. They form colored ions.
  3. They are generally good reducing agents.
  4. They have high melting and boiling points.

Answer: They are generally good reducing agents.

Explanation: Transition elements generally exhibit variable oxidation states due to the involvement of both (n-1)d and ns electrons. They form colored ions due to d-d transitions. They have high melting and boiling points due to strong metallic bonding. However, transition elements, especially in higher oxidation states, tend to act as oxidizing agents rather than reducing agents. For example, KMnO4 and K2Cr2O7 are strong oxidizing agents.

2. The rate constant of a first-order reaction is 2.303 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. What will be the time required for the concentration of the reactant to reduce to 1/10th of its initial value?

  1. 100 s
  2. 230.3 s
  3. 1000 s
  4. 2303 s

Answer: 1000 s

Explanation: For a first-order reaction, the integrated rate law is given by: t = (2.303 / k) * log([A]₀ / [A]). Given k = 2.303 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ and [A] = [A]₀ / 10. So, t = (2.303 / (2.303 × 10⁻³)) * log([A]₀ / ([A]₀ / 10)) t = (1 / 10⁻³) * log(10) t = 1000 * 1 t = 1000 s.

3. Which of the following compounds will NOT give a positive iodoform test?

  1. Ethanol
  2. Propan-2-ol
  3. Propanal
  4. Acetophenone

Answer: Propanal

Explanation: The iodoform test is given by compounds containing a CH₃CO- group or a CH₃CH(OH)- group. Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) has a CH₃CH(OH)- group (after oxidation to acetaldehyde). Propan-2-ol (CH₃CH(OH)CH₃) has a CH₃CH(OH)- group. Acetophenone (C₆H₅COCH₃) has a CH₃CO- group. Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO) does not have either of these groups. It has a CH₃CH₂CO- group, which does not react to give iodoform.

4. The correct order of increasing acidic strength for the following compounds is:

  1. Phenol < p-Nitrophenol < p-Cresol
  2. p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol
  3. p-Nitrophenol < Phenol < p-Cresol
  4. Phenol < p-Cresol < p-Nitrophenol

Answer: p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol

Explanation: The acidic strength of phenols is influenced by substituents on the benzene ring. Electron-withdrawing groups (like -NO₂) increase acidity by stabilizing the phenoxide ion through resonance and inductive effects. Electron-donating groups (like -CH₃) decrease acidity by destabilizing the phenoxide ion. p-Nitrophenol has an electron-withdrawing -NO₂ group, making it the most acidic. p-Cresol has an electron-donating -CH₃ group, making it the least acidic. Phenol is in between. Therefore, the order of increasing acidic strength is p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol.

5. Which of the following is a characteristic property of lyophilic colloids?

  1. They are easily coagulated by adding small amounts of electrolytes.
  2. They are irreversible in nature.
  3. They are generally less stable than lyophobic colloids.
  4. They are self-stabilizing and do not require stabilizing agents.

Answer: They are self-stabilizing and do not require stabilizing agents.

Explanation: Lyophilic colloids are 'solvent-loving' and are stable due to strong attractive forces between the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium. This strong interaction makes them self-stabilizing and reversible (if the dispersion medium is removed, the dispersed phase can be redispersed). They are not easily coagulated by electrolytes and are generally more stable than lyophobic colloids.
