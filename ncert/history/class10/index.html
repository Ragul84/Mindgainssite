Class10 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class10 questions to revise the NCERT History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following treaties recognised Greece as an independent nation?

  1. Treaty of Constantinople, 1832
  2. Treaty of Vienna, 1815
  3. Treaty of Versailles, 1919
  4. Treaty of Frankfurt, 1871

Answer: Treaty of Constantinople, 1832

Explanation: The Treaty of Constantinople, signed in 1832, officially recognised Greece as an independent nation, marking a significant victory for the Greek War of Independence against the Ottoman Empire. The other treaties listed are associated with different historical events and outcomes.

2. What was the main purpose of the Rowlatt Act, passed by the British in 1919?

  1. To promote Indian industries
  2. To allow detention of political prisoners without trial for two years
  3. To grant self-governance to Indians
  4. To introduce universal adult suffrage

Answer: To allow detention of political prisoners without trial for two years

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act, passed in 1919, was a repressive measure by the British government that gave them enormous powers to repress political activities and allowed for the detention of political prisoners without trial for up to two years. This act was widely opposed by Indians and led to widespread protests.

3. Which of the following was a major consequence of the Great Depression (1929-1939) on India?

  1. Increase in agricultural prices and exports
  2. Rise in industrial production and employment
  3. Sharp fall in agricultural prices and exports, severely impacting peasants
  4. Strengthening of the British colonial economy

Answer: Sharp fall in agricultural prices and exports, severely impacting peasants

Explanation: The Great Depression had a devastating impact on India, particularly on its agricultural sector. Agricultural prices plummeted, and exports declined sharply, leading to immense hardship for peasants and farmers who were already burdened by colonial taxes and debts. This economic crisis further fueled the nationalist movement.

4. Which of the following was a key feature of the 'proto-industrialisation' phase in Europe?

  1. Mass production in large factories using steam power
  2. Production carried out in rural households for international markets
  3. Exclusive reliance on handloom weaving for domestic consumption
  4. Complete absence of merchant involvement in production

Answer: Production carried out in rural households for international markets

Explanation: Proto-industrialisation refers to the phase of industrial development before the advent of factories. During this period, production was largely carried out in rural households, often by peasant families, for international markets. Merchants played a crucial role in supplying raw materials and collecting finished goods, thus integrating rural producers into a global commercial network.

5. Who among the following wrote 'Hind Swaraj'?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Mahatma Gandhi
  3. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  4. Subhas Chandra Bose

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: 'Hind Swaraj' (Indian Home Rule) was written by Mahatma Gandhi in 1909. In this book, Gandhi expressed his views on Swaraj, modern civilization, and the role of non-violence in India's struggle for independence. It remains a foundational text for understanding his philosophy.

6. Which of the following was a significant impact of the invention of the printing press by Gutenberg?

  1. It led to the decline of literacy rates across Europe.
  2. It made books more expensive and exclusive to the elite.
  3. It democratized access to knowledge and ideas, leading to wider public debate.
  4. It primarily benefited the Church by restricting the spread of dissenting ideas.

Answer: It democratized access to knowledge and ideas, leading to wider public debate.

Explanation: Gutenberg's invention of the printing press in the mid-15th century revolutionized communication. It significantly reduced the cost and time of producing books, making them more accessible to a wider audience. This led to a surge in literacy, the spread of new ideas, and facilitated public debate, challenging existing power structures and contributing to intellectual and religious reforms.

7. Which of the following best describes the 'Corn Laws' in Britain?

  1. Laws promoting the export of corn to other countries.
  2. Laws restricting the import of corn to protect domestic producers.
  3. Laws establishing a fixed price for corn for consumers.
  4. Laws encouraging the cultivation of corn in colonies.

Answer: Laws restricting the import of corn to protect domestic producers.

Explanation: The Corn Laws were protectionist tariffs and trade restrictions on imported food and grain (corn) enforced in Great Britain between 1815 and 1846. Their primary purpose was to keep grain prices high to benefit domestic landowners and farmers, but they led to high food prices for the working class and were eventually repealed.

8. The term 'nation-state' implies a state where:

  1. The majority of its citizens, and not merely its rulers, came to develop a sense of common identity and shared history.
  2. A single monarch rules over diverse ethnic groups without any common identity.
  3. The state is controlled by a foreign power.
  4. Economic prosperity is the sole basis of national unity.

Answer: The majority of its citizens, and not merely its rulers, came to develop a sense of common identity and shared history.

Explanation: A nation-state is a political entity where the majority of its citizens, and not just its rulers, share a common identity, culture, language, or history. This shared sense of belonging and collective identity is a defining characteristic, distinguishing it from multi-ethnic empires or states ruled by external powers.

9. Mahatma Gandhi decided to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 due to which of the following incidents?

  1. The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
  2. The Chauri Chaura incident
  3. The Rowlatt Act protests
  4. The Khilafat Movement's decline

Answer: The Chauri Chaura incident

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement after the violent incident at Chauri Chaura in February 1922. In this incident, a crowd of peasants set fire to a police station, killing several policemen. Gandhi believed that the movement was turning violent and that the satyagrahis needed to be properly trained before continuing the struggle.

10. Which of the following was a major impact of the Great Depression on India?

  1. Increase in industrial production
  2. Rise in agricultural prices
  3. Sharp decline in agricultural exports and prices
  4. Growth of urban employment

Answer: Sharp decline in agricultural exports and prices

Explanation: The Great Depression (1929-1939) severely impacted India, particularly its agricultural sector. Indian agricultural exports declined sharply, and prices of agricultural goods plummeted, causing immense hardship for farmers and peasants. This led to widespread rural indebtedness and poverty.

11. Which of the following inventions was crucial for the rapid expansion of cotton textile production in Britain during the 18th century?

  1. Steam engine
  2. Spinning Jenny
  3. Power loom
  4. Flying Shuttle

Answer: Spinning Jenny

Explanation: The Spinning Jenny, invented by James Hargreaves in 1764, significantly sped up the spinning process, allowing multiple spindles to be operated by a single worker. This invention dramatically increased the supply of yarn, which was a bottleneck in cotton textile production, thus paving the way for further mechanisation and expansion.

12. The term 'Gomasthas' in the context of the Indian textile industry during colonial rule referred to:

  1. Indian merchants who traded in textiles
  2. Skilled weavers who produced fine quality cloth
  3. Paid supervisors appointed by the East India Company to supervise weavers
  4. Local moneylenders who provided loans to weavers

Answer: Paid supervisors appointed by the East India Company to supervise weavers

Explanation: Gomasthas were paid supervisors appointed by the East India Company to supervise weavers, collect supplies, and examine the quality of cloth. Their introduction often led to conflicts with weavers, as they were outsiders with no social ties to the villages and often acted arrogantly, forcing weavers to sell their produce at low prices.

13. Which of the following was a significant consequence of the invention of the printing press in Europe?

  1. Decline in literacy rates
  2. Increase in the cost of books
  3. Widespread dissemination of ideas and knowledge
  4. Strengthening of oral traditions

Answer: Widespread dissemination of ideas and knowledge

Explanation: The invention of the printing press by Gutenberg in the mid-15th century revolutionised communication. It led to the mass production of books, making them more accessible and affordable. This, in turn, facilitated the widespread dissemination of ideas, knowledge, and information, contributing to increased literacy and the spread of new intellectual movements like the Reformation.

14. The 'Corn Laws' in Britain were primarily aimed at:

  1. Encouraging the export of corn
  2. Restricting the import of corn
  3. Promoting the cultivation of corn within Britain
  4. Subsidizing corn farmers

Answer: Restricting the import of corn

Explanation: The Corn Laws were protectionist tariffs and trade restrictions on imported food and grain (corn) enforced in Great Britain between 1815 and 1846. They were designed to keep grain prices high to benefit domestic landowners and farmers, but they led to high food prices for the working class and were eventually repealed.

15. Who founded the 'Depressed Classes Association' in 1930?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Jawaharlal Nehru
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: B.R. Ambedkar founded the Depressed Classes Association in 1930 to organise the Dalits and demand separate electorates for them. This was a crucial step in his efforts to uplift the marginalised communities in India.

16. Which of the following was a major impact of the Great Depression (1929-1939) on India?

  1. Increase in industrial production
  2. Rise in agricultural prices
  3. Sharp decline in agricultural exports and prices
  4. Growth of urban employment

Answer: Sharp decline in agricultural exports and prices

Explanation: The Great Depression severely impacted India, particularly its agricultural sector. Indian exports and imports nearly halved between 1928 and 1934, and agricultural prices plummeted, causing immense hardship for farmers and peasants.

17. What was the primary purpose of the 'Spinning Jenny' invented by James Hargreaves?

  1. To weave cloth more efficiently
  2. To spin multiple threads simultaneously
  3. To power steam engines
  4. To print patterns on fabric

Answer: To spin multiple threads simultaneously

Explanation: The Spinning Jenny, invented by James Hargreaves in 1764, was a multi-spindle spinning frame that allowed a worker to spin multiple spools of yarn simultaneously. This significantly increased the efficiency of yarn production, a key development in the early Industrial Revolution.

18. Which of the following best describes the 'Gutenberg Press'?

  1. A hand-operated printing press using wooden blocks
  2. A mechanical printing press with movable metal types
  3. A machine for binding books
  4. A device for making paper

Answer: A mechanical printing press with movable metal types

Explanation: Johannes Gutenberg developed the first mechanical printing press with movable metal type in the 1430s. This invention revolutionised book production, making books more accessible and affordable, and played a crucial role in the spread of knowledge.

19. Who was the author of the famous novel 'Anandmath', which included the hymn 'Vande Mataram'?

  1. Rabindranath Tagore
  2. Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay
  3. Swami Vivekananda
  4. Mahatma Gandhi

Answer: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay

Explanation: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay wrote the Bengali novel 'Anandmath' in 1882. The hymn 'Vande Mataram' from this novel became a powerful symbol of Indian nationalism and was widely sung during the freedom struggle.

20. The 'Silk Routes' are a good example of pre-modern trade and cultural links between which regions?

  1. Europe and Africa
  2. Asia and Europe
  3. North America and South America
  4. Australia and Asia

Answer: Asia and Europe

Explanation: The Silk Routes were ancient networks of trade routes that connected the East and West, primarily linking Asia (especially China and India) with Europe and parts of Africa. They facilitated the exchange of goods, ideas, religions, and technologies for centuries.

21. What was the main reason for the decline of the Indian textile industry in the 19th century under British rule?

  1. Lack of skilled labour
  2. Competition from machine-made goods from Britain
  3. High cost of raw materials
  4. Absence of demand for Indian textiles

Answer: Competition from machine-made goods from Britain

Explanation: The primary reason for the decline of the Indian textile industry was the fierce competition from machine-made textiles imported from Britain. British industrialisation led to cheaper, mass-produced goods that undercut the traditional Indian handloom industry, which also faced discriminatory tariffs and policies.

22. Who authored the book 'Hind Swaraj'?

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Mahatma Gandhi
  3. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  4. Subhas Chandra Bose

Answer: Mahatma Gandhi

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi authored 'Hind Swaraj' (Indian Home Rule) in 1909, expressing his views on Swaraj, modern civilization, and passive resistance. It is a foundational text for understanding his philosophy of non-violent resistance and self-rule.

23. Which of the following inventions was crucial for speeding up the spinning process in the 18th century?

  1. Steam Engine
  2. Powerloom
  3. Spinning Jenny
  4. Flying Shuttle

Answer: Spinning Jenny

Explanation: The Spinning Jenny, invented by James Hargreaves in 1764, significantly sped up the spinning process by allowing a single worker to operate multiple spindles simultaneously. While the Flying Shuttle (John Kay) improved weaving, and the Powerloom (Edmund Cartwright) and Steam Engine (James Watt) were later developments, the Spinning Jenny was key to the initial acceleration of spinning.

24. Who among the following was a prominent figure of the Romantic movement in Germany, known for collecting folk tales?

  1. Otto von Bismarck
  2. Giuseppe Mazzini
  3. Johann Gottfried Herder
  4. Napoleon Bonaparte

Answer: Johann Gottfried Herder

Explanation: Johann Gottfried Herder was a German philosopher and literary critic who championed the idea of a 'volksgeist' (common culture and destiny) and played a significant role in the Romantic movement, emphasizing the importance of folk culture, language, and traditions in nation-building. The Grimm Brothers, inspired by this movement, later collected folk tales.

25. The Rowlatt Act (1919) authorised the British government to:

  1. Impose a salt tax on Indians
  2. Arrest political prisoners without trial for two years
  3. Grant dominion status to India
  4. Allow Indians to join the Indian Civil Service

Answer: Arrest political prisoners without trial for two years

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act, passed in 1919, gave the British government enormous powers to repress political activities, allowing for the detention of political prisoners without trial for up to two years. This act was widely condemned by Indian leaders and led to widespread protests.
