26. What was the main purpose of the 'Corn Laws' in Britain?

  1. To encourage the import of corn
  2. To restrict the import of corn
  3. To promote the export of corn
  4. To regulate the price of corn in international markets

Answer: To restrict the import of corn

Explanation: The Corn Laws were a series of tariffs and trade restrictions on imported food and grain (corn) enforced in Great Britain between 1815 and 1846. Their main purpose was to protect British landowners and farmers from foreign competition by keeping grain prices high, but they led to high food prices and hardship for the working class.

27. Which of the following was the first book printed by Gutenberg?

  1. The Bible
  2. The Canterbury Tales
  3. The Decameron
  4. The Divine Comedy

Answer: The Bible

Explanation: Johannes Gutenberg is credited with inventing the movable type printing press around 1440. His most famous and arguably first major printed work was the Gutenberg Bible, also known as the 42-line Bible, completed around 1455. This marked a revolutionary moment in the history of print culture.

28. Who led the Peasant Movement in Awadh during the Non-Cooperation Movement?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Baba Ramchandra
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: Baba Ramchandra

Explanation: Baba Ramchandra was a prominent sanyasi who led the peasant movement in Awadh. He had previously worked as an indentured labourer in Fiji and returned to India to organise the peasants against the talukdars and landlords who demanded high rents and a variety of other cesses. He played a crucial role in mobilising the rural population during the Non-Cooperation Movement.

29. Which of the following inventions was crucial for the early industrialisation of Britain?

  1. The printing press
  2. The steam engine
  3. The automobile
  4. The telegraph

Answer: The steam engine

Explanation: The steam engine, perfected by James Watt in 1781, was a revolutionary invention that powered factories, locomotives, and ships. It provided a reliable and efficient source of power, enabling the mechanisation of production processes and driving the growth of various industries during the Age of Industrialisation.

30. Who wrote 'Gulamgiri' in 1873, criticising the caste system?

  1. Jyotiba Phule
  2. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Mahatma Gandhi
  4. Periyar E.V. Ramaswamy

Answer: Jyotiba Phule

Explanation: Jyotiba Phule, a prominent social reformer from Maharashtra, wrote 'Gulamgiri' (Slavery) in 1873. In this book, he fiercely criticised the caste system and the injustices faced by the lower castes, dedicating his work to the American movement to free slaves.

31. The term 'Zollverein' refers to a:

  1. Political union of German states
  2. Customs union formed in Prussia
  3. Military alliance against France
  4. Religious movement in Germany

Answer: Customs union formed in Prussia

Explanation: The Zollverein was a customs union formed in Prussia in 1834, which was joined by most of the German states. It abolished tariff barriers and reduced the number of currencies from over thirty to just two, facilitating economic integration and fostering a sense of national unity among the German states.

32. Which of the following was a key feature of the 'proto-industrialisation' period?

  1. Factory-based production
  2. Mass production of goods
  3. Decentralised production in rural households
  4. Use of advanced machinery

Answer: Decentralised production in rural households

Explanation: Proto-industrialisation refers to the period before the advent of factories, where large-scale industrial production for an international market took place in rural households. Merchants supplied raw materials to peasants and artisans, who then produced goods in their homes, often using simple tools. This system was decentralised and relied on family labour.

33. Which of the following events is considered a major turning point in the process of creating a nation-state in Europe, leading to the establishment of a unified German Empire?

  1. The Frankfurt Parliament of 1848
  2. The Congress of Vienna of 1815
  3. The Franco-Prussian War of 1870-1871
  4. The Greek War of Independence

Answer: The Franco-Prussian War of 1870-1871

Explanation: The Franco-Prussian War (1870-1871) was a decisive conflict that led to the unification of Germany under Prussian leadership. Otto von Bismarck, the Chief Minister of Prussia, orchestrated this war, which resulted in the defeat of France and the proclamation of the German Empire in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles in January 1871. This event marked the culmination of the German unification process. The Frankfurt Parliament failed to achieve unification, the Congress of Vienna aimed to restore monarchies, and the Greek War of Independence was about Greek liberation, not German unification.

34. During the Non-Cooperation Movement, which of the following groups was NOT explicitly called upon by Mahatma Gandhi to surrender their titles or boycott government institutions?

  1. Students
  2. Lawyers
  3. Industrialists
  4. Civil Servants

Answer: Industrialists

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922) called for a widespread boycott of British institutions and symbols of colonial rule. Students were asked to leave government-controlled schools and colleges, lawyers to boycott courts, and civil servants to resign from their posts. However, there was no explicit call for industrialists to surrender titles or boycott their businesses as part of the initial non-cooperation program. While some industrialists might have supported the movement, the direct boycott calls were aimed at government-affiliated roles and institutions.

35. The 'Great Depression' of the 1930s had a profound impact on the global economy. Which of the following was a primary cause of this economic downturn?

  1. The rise of protectionist trade policies in Europe
  2. The collapse of the US stock market in 1929
  3. The widespread adoption of the gold standard by all major economies
  4. The end of World War I and demobilization

Answer: The collapse of the US stock market in 1929

Explanation: The Great Depression was primarily triggered by the collapse of the US stock market in 1929, often referred to as 'Black Tuesday'. This event led to a widespread panic, a sharp decline in consumer confidence and spending, bank failures, and a contraction of credit. While protectionist policies and the gold standard played a role in exacerbating and prolonging the depression, the initial and most significant catalyst was the stock market crash. The end of World War I preceded the depression by over a decade and was not its direct cause.
