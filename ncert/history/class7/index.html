Class7 MCQs with answers

1. Which of the following terms was used in the medieval period to refer to a foreigner, someone who was not part of that society or culture?

  1. Hindustan
  2. Jati
  3. Pardesi
  4. Shreni

Answer: Pardesi

Explanation: In the medieval period, the term 'Pardesi' was used to describe a foreigner, someone who was not from the same village or community. The meaning of 'foreigner' has changed over time; in earlier times, it could even mean someone from a different village, not necessarily a different country. 'Hindustan' referred to a geographical region, 'Jati' to a sub-caste, and 'Shreni' to a guild of merchants or artisans.

2. Which South Indian kingdom was known for its powerful navy and for building magnificent temples like the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur?

  1. Pallavas
  2. Chalukyas
  3. Cholas
  4. Rashtrakutas

Answer: Cholas

Explanation: The Cholas, particularly under rulers like Rajaraja I and Rajendra I, were renowned for their strong navy which helped them expand their influence overseas. They were also great patrons of art and architecture, responsible for constructing grand temples such as the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur, which is a prime example of Dravidian architecture.

3. Who was the first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate?

  1. Raziyya Sultan
  2. Nur Jahan
  3. Jahanara Begum
  4. Mumtaz Mahal

Answer: Raziyya Sultan

Explanation: Raziyya Sultan (1236-1240) was the daughter of Sultan Iltutmish and the first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate. She faced significant challenges from the nobility due to her gender but proved to be a capable and intelligent ruler. Nur Jahan was a powerful Mughal empress, while Jahanara Begum and Mumtaz Mahal were prominent Mughal women but not rulers of the Sultanate.

4. Which Mughal emperor introduced the policy of 'Sulh-i kul' or 'universal peace'?

  1. Babur
  2. Humayun
  3. Akbar
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Emperor Akbar (1556-1605) is credited with introducing the policy of 'Sulh-i kul', which means 'universal peace' or 'absolute peace'. This policy emphasized tolerance and respect for all religions and social groups, aiming to create harmony within his diverse empire. He discussed religious matters with people of different faiths in the Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri.

5. Which of the following statements best describes the 'Gonds'?

  1. They were a powerful nomadic pastoralist tribe in the Himalayas.
  2. They were a tribal community in central India who practiced shifting cultivation.
  3. They were a group of artisans who migrated from Persia to India.
  4. They were a warrior clan who served in the Mughal army.

Answer: They were a tribal community in central India who practiced shifting cultivation.

Explanation: The Gonds were a large tribal community found in central India, particularly in regions like present-day Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Andhra Pradesh. They practiced shifting cultivation and lived in forested areas. Their kingdom, Garha Katanga, was a powerful state.

6. Who was a prominent Bhakti saint from Maharashtra who composed abhangas and emphasized devotion to Vitthala?

  1. Shankara
  2. Ramanuja
  3. Tukaram
  4. Basavanna

Answer: Tukaram

Explanation: Tukaram (17th century) was a famous Bhakti saint from Maharashtra, known for his devotional poetry called 'abhangas' dedicated to Vitthala (a form of Vishnu). He belonged to the Varkari tradition. Shankara and Ramanuja were influential philosophers, and Basavanna was a prominent figure in the Virashaiva movement in Karnataka.

7. Which language developed in the Awadh region and was used by many Bhakti saints to compose their devotional songs?

  1. Bengali
  2. Oriya
  3. Awadhi
  4. Malayalam

Answer: Awadhi

Explanation: Awadhi is a dialect of Hindi spoken primarily in the Awadh region of Uttar Pradesh. Many Bhakti saints, including Tulsidas (who wrote the Ramcharitmanas), composed their devotional works in Awadhi, making it an important language for regional cultural expression during that period.

8. Which of the following groups emerged as a powerful regional state in the 18th century, known for their military prowess and administrative system?

  1. Rajputs of Marwar
  2. Sikhs in Punjab
  3. Nawabs of Awadh
  4. Marathas in Deccan

Answer: Marathas in Deccan

Explanation: The Marathas, under leaders like Shivaji, emerged as a formidable power in the Deccan region during the 17th and 18th centuries. They established a strong military and administrative system, challenging Mughal authority and eventually expanding their influence across a large part of India, becoming a significant regional state in the 18th century.

9. The Chola kingdom was famous for its powerful navy and well-organized administration. Which of the following was a significant feature of Chola administration?

  1. Village assemblies (ur and sabha)
  2. Centralized imperial court
  3. Feudal lords controlling land
  4. Direct rule by the king over all villages

Answer: Village assemblies (ur and sabha)

Explanation: The Chola administration was known for its effective village assemblies, particularly the 'ur' (assembly of common villagers) and 'sabha' (assembly of brahmin landholders), which managed local affairs efficiently. This decentralized aspect of local self-governance was a hallmark of Chola rule.

10. Many tribal societies were divided into clans or 'khels'. Which of the following is an example of a tribal community that lived in the north-eastern part of the subcontinent?

  1. Gonds
  2. Ahoms
  3. Bhil
  4. Kolis

Answer: Ahoms

Explanation: The Ahoms were a prominent tribal community that established a powerful kingdom in the Brahmaputra valley in the north-eastern part of the subcontinent. Gonds were found in central India, Bhils in western and central India, and Kolis in Gujarat and Maharashtra.

11. Which Bhakti saint rejected all forms of ritualism, outward display of piety, and social differences, emphasizing devotion to a formless God?

  1. Shankara
  2. Ramanuja
  3. Kabir
  4. Chaitanyadeva

Answer: Kabir

Explanation: Kabir was a prominent Bhakti saint who strongly criticized all forms of external worship, caste distinctions, and religious rituals. He preached a universal path to God through devotion (bhakti) and emphasized the unity of all religions, often referring to God as formless and beyond attributes. Shankara and Ramanuja were philosophers who developed different schools of thought, and Chaitanyadeva emphasized devotion to Krishna.

12. The Kathak dance form originated in which region of India?

  1. Odisha
  2. Kerala
  3. North India
  4. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: North India

Explanation: Kathak is a major classical dance form of North India. Its name is derived from 'katha', meaning story, and 'kathakar', meaning storyteller. It evolved from the storytelling tradition in temples and later flourished in the courts of Mughal emperors and regional rulers.

13. Which of the following groups emerged as a powerful political force in the Deccan region during the 18th century?

  1. Jats
  2. Sikhs
  3. Marathas
  4. Rajputs

Answer: Marathas

Explanation: The Marathas emerged as a formidable power in the Deccan region during the 18th century, challenging Mughal authority and establishing their own independent kingdom under leaders like Shivaji. The Jats and Sikhs were powerful in other regions, and Rajputs had established their kingdoms earlier.

14. Which of the following rulers was the first to establish his capital at Delhi?

  1. Prithviraj Chauhan
  2. Ananga Pala Tomara
  3. Qutb al-Din Aibak
  4. Iltutmish

Answer: Ananga Pala Tomara

Explanation: Delhi first became the capital of a kingdom under the Tomara Rajputs. Ananga Pala Tomara is specifically mentioned as one of the early Tomara rulers who established their capital at Delhi in the 12th century. Prithviraj Chauhan ruled Delhi after the Tomaras, while Qutb al-Din Aibak and Iltutmish were early rulers of the Delhi Sultanate, who continued to use Delhi as their capital.

15. Akbar's policy of 'Sulh-i kul' (universal peace) aimed at:

  1. Promoting only Islam as the state religion
  2. Maintaining peace through military conquest
  3. Tolerance and harmony among different religious groups
  4. Establishing a uniform legal code for all citizens

Answer: Tolerance and harmony among different religious groups

Explanation: Akbar's 'Sulh-i kul' policy was a cornerstone of his administration, emphasizing tolerance and harmony among people of different religions and social backgrounds. It was based on the idea of justice, honesty, and peace, and was developed with the help of his advisors like Abul Fazl. It was a departure from religious exclusivity and aimed at creating a stable and inclusive empire, not through military conquest or promoting a single religion, but through mutual respect.

16. The Gonds, a prominent tribal community, practiced shifting cultivation. What is another name for shifting cultivation?

  1. Terrace farming
  2. Jhum cultivation
  3. Commercial farming
  4. Plantation agriculture

Answer: Jhum cultivation

Explanation: Shifting cultivation is also known as 'jhum cultivation' or 'slash-and-burn agriculture'. In this method, a patch of forest land is cleared by cutting trees and burning the vegetation. The ashes fertilize the soil, and crops are grown for a few years until the soil loses its fertility. Then, the cultivators move to a new patch of land. Terrace farming is practiced on hillsides, commercial farming is for profit, and plantation agriculture involves growing a single cash crop on a large estate.

17. Which of the following Bhakti saints rejected all forms of ritualism, outward display of piety, and social differences based on birth?

  1. Ramanuja
  2. Shankara
  3. Kabir
  4. Tulsidas

Answer: Kabir

Explanation: Kabir was a prominent Bhakti saint who strongly criticized all forms of external worship, ritualism, and the caste system. He emphasized the oneness of God, whether called Allah, Ram, or Hari, and preached a path of devotion that transcended religious boundaries and social distinctions. Ramanuja and Shankara were influential philosophers who developed different schools of thought within Hinduism, while Tulsidas was known for his devotion to Rama and his composition of the Ramcharitmanas, but Kabir's critique of ritualism and social hierarchy was particularly radical.

18. The miniature paintings of the Kangra school, which flourished in the mid-18th century, were inspired by:

  1. Mughal court art
  2. Buddhist monastic traditions
  3. Vaishnavite devotionalism
  4. Persian epic poetry

Answer: Vaishnavite devotionalism

Explanation: The Kangra school of miniature painting, which developed in the Himalayan foothills, was deeply inspired by Vaishnavite devotionalism, particularly the themes of Krishna and Radha. These paintings often depicted scenes from the Bhagavad Purana and Gita Govinda, characterized by delicate lines, vibrant colors, and a lyrical quality. While Mughal art influenced earlier Rajput painting, the Kangra school developed a distinct style rooted in Hindu devotional themes.

19. After the decline of the Mughal Empire in the 18th century, which of the following groups emerged as a powerful regional state in the Deccan?

  1. Rajputs
  2. Sikhs
  3. Marathas
  4. Jats

Answer: Marathas

Explanation: The Marathas, under leaders like Shivaji and later the Peshwas, emerged as a formidable power in the Deccan region during the decline of the Mughal Empire in the 18th century. They established a vast empire and challenged Mughal authority effectively. While Rajputs, Sikhs, and Jats also formed regional powers, the Marathas were particularly dominant in the Deccan and played a crucial role in shaping 18th-century Indian politics.

20. Who was the founder of the Chola dynasty?

  1. Rajaraja I
  2. Rajendra I
  3. Vijayalaya
  4. Parantaka I

Answer: Vijayalaya

Explanation: Vijayalaya was the founder of the Chola dynasty. He captured the Kaveri delta from the Muttaraiyar in the mid-ninth century and built the town of Thanjavur and a temple for goddess Nishumbhasudini there. Rajaraja I and Rajendra I were later powerful Chola rulers.

21. Which ruler shifted his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad in the 14th century?

  1. Alauddin Khalji
  2. Ghiyasuddin Tughluq
  3. Muhammad Tughluq
  4. Firoz Shah Tughluq

Answer: Muhammad Tughluq

Explanation: Muhammad Tughluq was the Sultan who shifted his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) in the Deccan during the 14th century. This decision, along with others, led to significant administrative challenges and public discontent.

22. Who was the Mughal emperor during the Battle of Panipat in 1526?

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Babur
  4. Shah Jahan

Answer: Babur

Explanation: The First Battle of Panipat in 1526 was fought between Babur and Ibrahim Lodi, marking the beginning of the Mughal Empire in India. Akbar, Humayun, and Shah Jahan were later Mughal emperors.

23. The Gonds lived in a vast forested region called:

  1. Malwa
  2. Gondwana
  3. Awadh
  4. Bengal

Answer: Gondwana

Explanation: The Gonds lived in a vast forested region called Gondwana, which literally means 'country inhabited by Gonds'. This region is primarily located in central India.

24. Who among the following was a prominent Sufi saint who taught that devotion to God and service to humanity were the most important aspects of religion?

  1. Shankara
  2. Ramanuja
  3. Mirabai
  4. Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti

Answer: Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti

Explanation: Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti was a prominent Sufi saint who settled in Ajmer and emphasized devotion to God and service to humanity. Shankara and Ramanuja were Bhakti saints, and Mirabai was a devotee of Krishna.

25. Which language developed in the region of Awadh and was used by many Bhakti saints?

  1. Bengali
  2. Oriya
  3. Awadhi
  4. Malayalam

Answer: Awadhi

Explanation: Awadhi is the language that developed in the region of Awadh. Many Bhakti saints, including Tulsidas, used Awadhi in their compositions, most notably in the Ramcharitmanas.
