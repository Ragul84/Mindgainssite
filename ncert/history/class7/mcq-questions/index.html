26. Which of the following was NOT a successor state that emerged in the 18th century?

  1. Awadh
  2. Bengal
  3. Hyderabad
  4. Mughal Empire

Answer: Mughal Empire

Explanation: Awadh, Bengal, and Hyderabad were all successor states that emerged from the decline of the Mughal Empire in the 18th century. The Mughal Empire itself was in decline and not a successor state.

27. Who was the founder of the Chola dynasty, who captured the Kaveri delta from the Muttaraiyar?

  1. Rajaraja I
  2. Rajendra I
  3. Vijayalaya
  4. Parantaka I

Answer: Vijayalaya

Explanation: Vijayalaya was the founder of the Chola dynasty. He belonged to a minor chiefly family of the Uraiyur Cholas and captured the Kaveri delta from the Muttaraiyar in the mid-ninth century. Rajaraja I and Rajendra I were later powerful Chola rulers who expanded the empire significantly.

28. Who was the Mughal emperor who introduced the policy of 'Sulh-i kul' or 'universal peace'?

  1. Babur
  2. Humayun
  3. Akbar
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar was the Mughal emperor who introduced the policy of 'Sulh-i kul' or 'universal peace'. This policy was based on the idea of tolerance and non-discrimination among people of different religions. It emphasized justice, peace, and harmony, and was a cornerstone of his administrative and religious policies. Babur founded the Mughal empire, Humayun was his son, and Aurangzeb was known for his more orthodox religious policies.

29. Which of the following tribal groups was very powerful in the north-western part of the subcontinent during the 13th and 14th centuries?

  1. Gonds
  2. Ahoms
  3. Khokhars
  4. Bhils

Answer: Khokhars

Explanation: The Khokhars were a very powerful tribal group in the Punjab region (north-western part of the subcontinent) during the 13th and 14th centuries. The Gonds were found in central India, Ahoms in the Brahmaputra valley, and Bhils in western and central India.

30. Who was the Bhakti saint from Maharashtra who composed devotional songs in Marathi and emphasized the equality of all people?

  1. Shankara
  2. Ramanuja
  3. Tukaram
  4. Basavanna

Answer: Tukaram

Explanation: Tukaram was a prominent Bhakti saint from Maharashtra who composed numerous abhangas (devotional songs) in Marathi. He emphasized devotion to Vitthala (a form of Vishnu) and preached the equality of all people, irrespective of their caste or social status. Shankara and Ramanuja were philosophers from South India, and Basavanna was associated with the Virashaiva movement in Karnataka.

31. Which regional culture developed around the Jagannatha cult in Puri, Odisha?

  1. Bengal
  2. Kerala
  3. Odisha
  4. Rajasthan

Answer: Odisha

Explanation: The regional culture of Odisha developed significantly around the Jagannatha cult in Puri. The temple of Jagannatha at Puri became a central pilgrimage site, and the worship of Jagannatha (a form of Vishnu) became a focal point for the identity of the region. Many rulers, including the Ganga dynasty, dedicated their kingdom to the deity.

32. Which of the following states emerged as a powerful independent kingdom in the 18th century, founded by Sa'adat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk?

  1. Hyderabad
  2. Awadh
  3. Bengal
  4. Mysore

Answer: Awadh

Explanation: Awadh emerged as a powerful independent kingdom in the 18th century, founded by Sa'adat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk. He was appointed subadar of Awadh in 1722 and quickly consolidated his power, reducing Mughal influence and establishing a virtually independent state. Hyderabad was founded by Asaf Jah, and Bengal by Murshid Quli Khan. Mysore was under Tipu Sultan and Haidar Ali.

33. Which of the following terms was used to refer to a 'foreigner' in the medieval period, meaning someone who was not part of that society or culture?

  1. Hindustani
  2. Mughal
  3. Pardesi
  4. Sultan

Answer: Pardesi

Explanation: In the medieval period, the term 'Pardesi' was used to describe a foreigner, someone who was not from the same village, society, or culture. This is different from the modern meaning of 'foreigner' which often refers to someone from another country. For example, a forest-dweller might have been a 'pardesi' for a city-dweller, even if they lived in the same region. 'Hindustani' referred to people of Hindustan, 'Mughal' was a dynasty, and 'Sultan' was a ruler.

34. Who was the founder of the Mughal Empire in India?

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Babur
  4. Shah Jahan

Answer: Babur

Explanation: Babur was the founder of the Mughal Empire in India. He defeated Ibrahim Lodi in the First Battle of Panipat in 1526, marking the beginning of Mughal rule. Akbar, Humayun, and Shah Jahan were later Mughal emperors who expanded and consolidated the empire.

35. Which of the following was a major characteristic of the Bhakti movement?

  1. Emphasis on elaborate rituals and sacrifices
  2. Belief in the importance of a personal deity and devotion
  3. Strict adherence to the caste system
  4. Promotion of Sanskrit as the only language for religious texts

Answer: Belief in the importance of a personal deity and devotion

Explanation: The Bhakti movement, which emerged in different parts of India from the 8th century onwards, emphasized intense devotion (bhakti) to a personal deity as the path to salvation. Bhakti saints often rejected elaborate rituals, the caste system, and the exclusive use of Sanskrit, instead promoting local languages and simple forms of worship accessible to all.
