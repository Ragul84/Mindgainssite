26. Who founded the 'Arya Samaj' to reform Hindu society and promote Vedic values?

  1. Raja Ram Mohan Roy
  2. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
  3. Swami Dayanand Saraswati
  4. Jyotirao Phule

Answer: Swami Dayanand Saraswati

Explanation: Swami Dayanand Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875. He advocated for a return to the Vedas, condemned idol worship, caste system, and child marriage, and promoted women's education and widow remarriage.

27. The 'Quit India Movement' was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in which year?

  1. 1920
  2. 1930
  3. 1942
  4. 1947

Answer: 1942

Explanation: The Quit India Movement was a major civil disobedience movement launched by Mahatma Gandhi in August 1942, demanding an end to British rule in India. It called for immediate independence and was a significant turning point in the struggle for freedom.

28. Who was the last powerful Mughal ruler before the British established their dominance in India?

  1. Aurangzeb
  2. Akbar
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Aurangzeb

Explanation: Aurangzeb was the last powerful Mughal ruler. After his death in 1707, many Mughal governors (subadars) and big zamindars began asserting their authority and establishing regional kingdoms, which weakened the central Mughal power and paved the way for the British to establish their dominance.

29. What was the main purpose of the Permanent Settlement introduced by the British in 1793?

  1. To encourage industrial growth in India
  2. To fix the revenue that zamindars had to pay to the Company
  3. To promote education among the Indian population
  4. To establish a uniform legal system across India

Answer: To fix the revenue that zamindars had to pay to the Company

Explanation: The Permanent Settlement, introduced by Lord Cornwallis in 1793, aimed to fix the revenue that zamindars had to pay to the Company. The zamindars were recognised as owners of the land and were expected to collect rent from the peasants and pay revenue to the Company. This system was intended to ensure a regular flow of revenue for the Company and encourage investment in agriculture.

30. Which tribal group from the Chota Nagpur region is known for its rebellion against the British and Dikus (outsiders) in the late 19th century?

  1. Gonds
  2. Santhals
  3. Mundas
  4. Bhils

Answer: Mundas

Explanation: The Mundas, a tribal group from the Chota Nagpur region, are well-known for their rebellion led by Birsa Munda against the British and Dikus (outsiders) in the late 19th century. They envisioned a 'Golden Age' where they would live freely without the interference of outsiders.

31. Which of the following was a major immediate cause of the Revolt of 1857?

  1. The Doctrine of Lapse
  2. The introduction of the Enfield rifles with greased cartridges
  3. High land revenue demands
  4. The abolition of Sati

Answer: The introduction of the Enfield rifles with greased cartridges

Explanation: While the Doctrine of Lapse and high land revenue demands were underlying causes of discontent, the immediate spark for the Revolt of 1857 was the introduction of the new Enfield rifles. The cartridges for these rifles were rumoured to be greased with the fat of cows and pigs, which was offensive to both Hindu and Muslim sepoys, leading to widespread outrage and mutiny.

32. Who among the following was a prominent social reformer who worked for the upliftment of women and against the caste system in 19th-century India?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Raja Ram Mohan Roy
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: Raja Ram Mohan Roy

Explanation: Raja Ram Mohan Roy was a prominent social reformer in 19th-century India. He founded the Brahmo Sabha (later Brahmo Samaj) and actively campaigned against social evils like Sati, child marriage, and the caste system, advocating for women's rights and education.

33. The Vernacular Press Act of 1878 was enacted by the British to:

  1. Promote Indian languages
  2. Control and censor Indian language newspapers
  3. Encourage scientific education
  4. Improve agricultural practices

Answer: Control and censor Indian language newspapers

Explanation: The Vernacular Press Act of 1878 was a highly controversial law enacted by the British government to control and censor Indian language newspapers. It allowed the government to confiscate the printing presses and other assets of newspapers that published 'seditious' material, effectively curbing freedom of expression.

34. When did India become independent from British rule?

  1. 15th August 1947
  2. 26th January 1950
  3. 2nd October 1869
  4. 1st January 1900

Answer: 15th August 1947

Explanation: India gained independence from British rule on 15th August 1947. This date marks the end of nearly two centuries of British colonial rule and the birth of India as an independent nation.

35. The term 'Dikus' used by tribal communities in India referred to:

  1. British officials
  2. Local landlords
  3. Outsiders like moneylenders, traders, and missionaries
  4. Fellow tribal members

Answer: Outsiders like moneylenders, traders, and missionaries

Explanation: The term 'Dikus' was used by tribal communities in India to refer to outsiders, including moneylenders, traders, missionaries, and Hindu landlords, who were seen as encroaching upon their land, resources, and way of life, and exploiting them.
