Class6 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class6 questions to revise the NCERT History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is NOT a source that historians use to study the past?

  1. Manuscripts
  2. Inscriptions
  3. Newspaper articles from today
  4. Archaeological remains

Answer: Newspaper articles from today

Explanation: Historians use manuscripts (handwritten records), inscriptions (writings on hard surfaces), and archaeological remains (objects from the past) to reconstruct history. Newspaper articles from today describe current events, not the distant past, and therefore are not sources for studying ancient history.

2. The earliest people who lived in the subcontinent were skilled gatherers. What did they primarily gather for food?

  1. Meat from domesticated animals
  2. Fruits, roots, nuts, seeds, and leaves
  3. Grains grown in fields
  4. Fish caught with nets

Answer: Fruits, roots, nuts, seeds, and leaves

Explanation: The earliest people were hunter-gatherers. As gatherers, they collected wild plant produce like fruits, roots, nuts, seeds, and leaves for their sustenance. Domestication of animals and cultivation of grains came much later.

3. Which of the following cities is NOT associated with the Indus Valley Civilisation?

  1. Harappa
  2. Mohenjodaro
  3. Lothal
  4. Pataliputra

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: Harappa, Mohenjodaro, and Lothal are all important sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation, known for their well-planned cities and unique artifacts. Pataliputra was the capital of the Mauryan Empire and other later empires, much after the decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation.

4. The Rigveda is a collection of:

  1. Rules for kings
  2. Hymns in praise of various gods and goddesses
  3. Stories about ancient battles
  4. Instructions for building houses

Answer: Hymns in praise of various gods and goddesses

Explanation: The Rigveda is the oldest Veda and consists of over a thousand hymns (suktas) dedicated to various gods and goddesses like Indra, Agni, and Soma. These hymns were recited and chanted during rituals.

5. What was the main purpose of performing sacrifices by the rajas in ancient times?

  1. To entertain the people
  2. To show their power and gain support from the people and other rajas
  3. To teach children about religion
  4. To celebrate harvest festivals

Answer: To show their power and gain support from the people and other rajas

Explanation: Rajas performed grand sacrifices like the Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice) to assert their authority, demonstrate their power, and gain recognition and support from other rajas and their subjects. These rituals were often elaborate and involved many priests.

6. Gautama Buddha taught that suffering and unhappiness are caused by:

  1. Lack of wealth
  2. Desires and cravings
  3. Hard work
  4. Living in cities

Answer: Desires and cravings

Explanation: Gautama Buddha's core teaching, known as the Four Noble Truths, states that suffering (dukkha) is inherent in life, and its cause is craving or desire (tanha). He taught that by overcoming these desires, one can achieve peace and enlightenment.

7. Who was the founder of the Mauryan Empire?

  1. Ashoka
  2. Bindusara
  3. Chandragupta Maurya
  4. Samudragupta

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire around 321 BCE, overthrowing the Nanda dynasty. Ashoka and Bindusara were his successors, and Samudragupta belonged to the later Gupta Empire.

8. What was the main purpose of the 'Sangam literature' in ancient South India?

  1. To record the history of the Mauryan Empire
  2. To describe the life and culture of people in Tamilakam
  3. To teach Sanskrit grammar
  4. To document trade routes to China

Answer: To describe the life and culture of people in Tamilakam

Explanation: Sangam literature is a collection of Tamil poems composed in assemblies (sangams) of poets. It provides valuable insights into the social, economic, political, and cultural life of the people of Tamilakam (ancient South India) during that period.

9. The earliest people who lived in the subcontinent were primarily:

  1. Farmers
  2. Hunters and gatherers
  3. Traders
  4. Kings

Answer: Hunters and gatherers

Explanation: The earliest people were hunters and gatherers. They hunted wild animals, fished, and gathered fruits, roots, nuts, leaves, and eggs for their food. Farming, trading, and kingships developed much later in history.

10. Which of these cities is NOT associated with the Indus Valley Civilisation?

  1. Harappa
  2. Mohenjodaro
  3. Lothal
  4. Pataliputra

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: Harappa, Mohenjodaro, and Lothal are all important sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation, known for their well-planned cities. Pataliputra was the capital of the Mauryan Empire and other later empires, much after the decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation.

11. What was the main purpose of building stupas?

  1. To serve as palaces for kings
  2. To store grains for the community
  3. To house relics of the Buddha or his followers
  4. To act as marketplaces for trade

Answer: To house relics of the Buddha or his followers

Explanation: Stupas were important Buddhist structures. They were mounds where the relics (bodily remains like teeth, bone, or ashes, or objects used by him) of the Buddha or his followers were placed. They were places of worship and pilgrimage, not palaces, granaries, or markets.

12. Siddhartha Gautama, the founder of Buddhism, attained enlightenment under a tree at:

  1. Sarnath
  2. Lumbini
  3. Bodh Gaya
  4. Kushinagar

Answer: Bodh Gaya

Explanation: Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under a peepal tree at Bodh Gaya in Bihar, after which he became known as the Buddha. Lumbini was his birthplace, Sarnath was where he gave his first sermon, and Kushinagar was where he passed away.

13. Ashoka was a famous ruler of which empire?

  1. Gupta Empire
  2. Mauryan Empire
  3. Chola Empire
  4. Kushan Empire

Answer: Mauryan Empire

Explanation: Ashoka was one of the most famous rulers of the Mauryan Empire. He was the first ruler who tried to take his message to the people through inscriptions, and he is known for his policy of Dhamma after the Kalinga War.

14. The famous iron pillar, known for its rust-resistant quality, is located in:

  1. Sanchi
  2. Ajanta
  3. Mehrauli (Delhi)
  4. Ellora

Answer: Mehrauli (Delhi)

Explanation: The famous iron pillar, known for its remarkable resistance to rust over centuries, is located at Mehrauli in Delhi. It is an excellent example of the skill of Indian craftspersons during the Gupta period. Sanchi, Ajanta, and Ellora are known for their stupas, caves, and temples, respectively.

15. Which of the following cities is NOT associated with the Harappan Civilisation?

  1. Mohenjodaro
  2. Harappa
  3. Lothal
  4. Pataliputra

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: Mohenjodaro, Harappa, and Lothal are all important sites of the Harappan (Indus Valley) Civilisation, known for their well-planned cities. Pataliputra was the capital of the Mauryan Empire, which emerged much later than the Harappan Civilisation.

16. What was the main reason for the rise of 'mahajanapadas' around 2500 years ago?

  1. The invention of the wheel
  2. The development of iron tools and agriculture
  3. The discovery of new trade routes
  4. The decline of religious practices

Answer: The development of iron tools and agriculture

Explanation: The development of iron tools led to better agricultural practices (e.g., clearing forests, deeper ploughing), which produced surplus food. This surplus supported larger populations and led to the growth of powerful states or 'mahajanapadas' with standing armies and fortified capitals.

17. Siddhartha Gautama, the founder of Buddhism, attained enlightenment under a Bodhi tree at which place?

  1. Sarnath
  2. Lumbini
  3. Bodh Gaya
  4. Kushinagar

Answer: Bodh Gaya

Explanation: Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, after which he became known as the Buddha. Lumbini is his birthplace, Sarnath is where he gave his first sermon, and Kushinagar is where he passed away.

18. Who was the most famous ruler of the Mauryan Empire, known for spreading the message of Dhamma?

  1. Chandragupta Maurya
  2. Bindusara
  3. Ashoka
  4. Samudragupta

Answer: Ashoka

Explanation: Ashoka was the most famous Mauryan ruler. After the Kalinga War, he adopted Buddhism and dedicated his life to spreading the message of Dhamma, which emphasized peace, non-violence, and respect for all beings.

19. The famous iron pillar, known for its rust-resistant quality, is located in which city?

  1. Harappa
  2. Pataliputra
  3. Delhi
  4. Sanchi

Answer: Delhi

Explanation: The famous Iron Pillar, known for its remarkable resistance to rust even after centuries, is located in the Qutub Minar complex in Mehrauli, Delhi. It is a testament to the advanced metallurgical skills of ancient India, likely from the Gupta period.

20. The earliest people who lived in the subcontinent were skilled gatherers. What did they primarily gather?

  1. Metals and minerals
  2. Roots, fruits, and other forest produce
  3. Animal skins for clothing
  4. Clay for pottery

Answer: Roots, fruits, and other forest produce

Explanation: The earliest people were hunter-gatherers. As gatherers, they collected roots, fruits, and other forest produce for their food. They did not primarily gather metals, animal skins (though they used them), or clay for pottery in the earliest stages.

21. Which of the following cities was NOT part of the Harappan Civilisation?

  1. Mohenjodaro
  2. Harappa
  3. Lothal
  4. Pataliputra

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: Mohenjodaro, Harappa, and Lothal were all important cities of the Harappan Civilisation, known for their well-planned structures and urban development. Pataliputra, on the other hand, was the capital of the Magadhan empire much later, during the time of the Mauryas and Guptas, and is not associated with the Harappan Civilisation.

22. Who was the founder of Jainism?

  1. Gautama Buddha
  2. Mahavira
  3. Ashoka
  4. Vardhamana

Answer: Mahavira

Explanation: While Vardhamana is the birth name of Mahavira, Mahavira is widely recognized as the 24th and last Tirthankara who systematized and propagated Jainism in its present form. Gautama Buddha founded Buddhism, and Ashoka was a Mauryan emperor.

23. Which of the following was the capital of the Mauryan Empire?

  1. Ujjain
  2. Taxila
  3. Pataliputra
  4. Sanchi

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: Pataliputra (modern-day Patna) was the magnificent capital city of the Mauryan Empire, from where emperors like Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka ruled their vast empire. Ujjain and Taxila were important provincial centers, and Sanchi is famous for its stupas.

24. What was the main occupation of people living in villages during ancient times?

  1. Crafts and trade
  2. Farming and animal rearing
  3. Administration and governance
  4. Mining and metallurgy

Answer: Farming and animal rearing

Explanation: In ancient villages, the primary occupations were farming (agriculture) and animal rearing (pastoralism). These activities provided food and other necessities for the community. Crafts, trade, administration, and mining were more prominent in towns, cities, or specialized regions.

25. What was the main reason for the rise of 'Janapadas' and 'Mahajanapadas' in ancient India?

  1. The invention of the wheel
  2. The development of agriculture and the use of iron tools
  3. The discovery of new trade routes
  4. The spread of Buddhism and Jainism

Answer: The development of agriculture and the use of iron tools

Explanation: The development of agriculture, especially with the use of iron tools for clearing forests and ploughing, led to increased food production. This surplus food supported larger populations and allowed for the emergence of settled communities, which eventually grew into Janapadas and then powerful Mahajanapadas.
