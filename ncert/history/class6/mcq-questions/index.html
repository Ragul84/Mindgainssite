26. Which Mauryan emperor is famous for giving up war after the Kalinga War and embracing Buddhism?

  1. Chandragupta Maurya
  2. Bindusara
  3. Ashoka
  4. Samudragupta

Answer: Ashoka

Explanation: Emperor Ashoka is renowned for his conversion to Buddhism after witnessing the immense violence and suffering caused by the Kalinga War. He then dedicated his life to spreading Dhamma (Dharma) through peaceful means. Chandragupta Maurya founded the empire, Bindusara was his son, and Samudragupta belonged to the later Gupta dynasty.

27. What was a 'gramabhojaka' in ancient Indian villages?

  1. A type of pottery
  2. A wealthy landowner who often acted as a village headman
  3. A soldier in the king's army
  4. A religious priest

Answer: A wealthy landowner who often acted as a village headman

Explanation: The 'gramabhojaka' was a powerful and influential figure in ancient villages, especially in the northern part of the subcontinent. He was often the largest landowner, had slaves and hired labourers, collected taxes for the king, and sometimes even acted as a judge or policeman.

28. Which of the following cities is NOT considered part of the Harappan Civilisation?

  1. Mohenjodaro
  2. Harappa
  3. Lothal
  4. Pataliputra

Answer: Pataliputra

Explanation: Mohenjodaro, Harappa, and Lothal are all important sites of the Harappan Civilisation (also known as the Indus Valley Civilisation). Pataliputra was the capital of the Mauryan Empire and other later kingdoms, much after the decline of the Harappan cities.

29. What was the main purpose of performing sacrifices (yajnas) by rajas in ancient times?

  1. To entertain the public
  2. To show their power and gain recognition as rulers
  3. To teach children about religion
  4. To celebrate good harvests

Answer: To show their power and gain recognition as rulers

Explanation: Performing grand sacrifices like the Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice) was a way for rajas to assert their authority, demonstrate their power, and gain recognition from other rajas and the people as powerful rulers of their janapadas.

30. Gautama Buddha taught that suffering is caused by:

  1. Lack of wealth
  2. Desire and craving
  3. Hard work
  4. Eating too much food

Answer: Desire and craving

Explanation: Gautama Buddha's core teaching, known as the Four Noble Truths, states that suffering (dukkha) is an inherent part of life, and its cause is desire, craving, and attachment (tanha). He taught that by overcoming these desires, one can achieve peace (Nirvana).

31. Who was the first emperor of the Mauryan Empire?

  1. Ashoka
  2. Bindusara
  3. Chandragupta Maurya
  4. Chanakya

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire around 321 BCE. Ashoka was his grandson, and Bindusara was his son. Chanakya (also known as Kautilya) was Chandragupta's advisor.

32. What was a 'Sangam' in ancient South India?

  1. A type of ancient coin
  2. A large public bath
  3. Assemblies of poets who produced literature
  4. A fortified city wall

Answer: Assemblies of poets who produced literature

Explanation: The 'Sangam' literature refers to a collection of Tamil literary works produced during assemblies (sangams) of poets held in the ancient Tamil country. These assemblies were patronized by chiefs and kings, and the literature provides valuable insights into the life and culture of that period.

33. Which of the following is NOT a primary source of information for studying the past?

  1. Newspaper articles written today about ancient events
  2. Ancient coins found during an excavation
  3. Inscriptions carved on rocks and pillars
  4. Remains of old buildings and pottery

Answer: Newspaper articles written today about ancient events

Explanation: Primary sources are direct evidence from the past, like ancient coins, inscriptions, and remains of buildings. Newspaper articles written today about ancient events are secondary sources because they are interpretations or analyses of primary sources, not direct evidence from the time period itself.

34. The site of Mehrgarh, located near the Bolan Pass, is significant because it provides evidence of:

  1. The earliest use of iron tools
  2. The beginning of agriculture and animal rearing
  3. The construction of the first large cities
  4. The invention of the wheel

Answer: The beginning of agriculture and animal rearing

Explanation: Mehrgarh is one of the earliest known sites where people began to learn farming and animal rearing. Archaeological findings at Mehrgarh include evidence of wheat and barley cultivation, and the domestication of sheep and goats, marking a crucial transition from hunting-gathering to a settled way of life.

35. The Great Bath, a large rectangular tank, was a prominent structure found in which Harappan city?

  1. Lothal
  2. Harappa
  3. Mohenjodaro
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Mohenjodaro

Explanation: The Great Bath is one of the most well-known and impressive structures discovered at the archaeological site of Mohenjodaro. It was likely used for special public rituals or ceremonies, indicating the advanced planning and engineering capabilities of the Harappan civilization.
