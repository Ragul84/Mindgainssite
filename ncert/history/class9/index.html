Class9 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class9 questions to revise the NCERT History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following events is considered the storming of the Bastille, marking the beginning of the French Revolution?

  1. July 14, 1789
  2. August 10, 1792
  3. September 21, 1792
  4. October 5, 1789

Answer: July 14, 1789

Explanation: The storming of the Bastille prison on July 14, 1789, was a pivotal moment that symbolized the beginning of the French Revolution. It represented the people's defiance against the monarchy and its oppressive rule.

2. Who was the leader of the Bolshevik Party during the Russian Revolution?

  1. Leon Trotsky
  2. Vladimir Lenin
  3. Joseph Stalin
  4. Alexander Kerensky

Answer: Vladimir Lenin

Explanation: Vladimir Lenin was the charismatic and influential leader of the Bolshevik Party, which played a crucial role in the October Revolution of 1917, leading to the establishment of the Soviet Union.

3. What was the name of Hitler's secret state police force, notorious for its brutality and suppression of dissent?

  1. SA (Sturmabteilung)
  2. SS (Schutzstaffel)
  3. Gestapo
  4. Wehrmacht

Answer: Gestapo

Explanation: The Gestapo was the official secret police of Nazi Germany and German-occupied Europe. It was instrumental in enforcing Nazi policies, suppressing opposition, and carrying out atrocities against perceived enemies of the state.

4. Which of the following was a primary reason for the British colonial government's interest in controlling forests in India?

  1. To promote biodiversity conservation
  2. To provide recreational areas for the public
  3. To meet the demand for timber for shipbuilding and railways
  4. To empower local forest-dwelling communities

Answer: To meet the demand for timber for shipbuilding and railways

Explanation: The British colonial government's primary interest in Indian forests was to secure a steady supply of timber, especially for shipbuilding for the Royal Navy and for railway sleepers, which were crucial for expanding their colonial infrastructure and trade.

5. Which of the following pastoral communities is primarily found in the mountains of Jammu and Kashmir?

  1. Gujjars and Bakarwals
  2. Gaddis
  3. Bhotiyas
  4. Raikas

Answer: Gujjars and Bakarwals

Explanation: The Gujjars and Bakarwals are prominent pastoral communities in Jammu and Kashmir. They are transhumant, moving with their herds of sheep and goats between summer pastures in the high mountains and winter pastures in the lower hills.

6. The 'Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen' was a significant document of the French Revolution. Which of the following was NOT one of its key principles?

  1. Liberty
  2. Equality
  3. Fraternity
  4. Divine Right of Kings

Answer: Divine Right of Kings

Explanation: The 'Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen' championed the principles of liberty, equality, and fraternity, emphasizing individual rights and popular sovereignty. The 'Divine Right of Kings' was a concept that the revolution actively sought to dismantle, as it justified absolute monarchical rule.

7. What was the main objective of Stalin's collectivisation program in the Soviet Union?

  1. To promote individual land ownership
  2. To increase agricultural production and eliminate private farms
  3. To encourage industrial growth in urban areas
  4. To decentralize economic planning

Answer: To increase agricultural production and eliminate private farms

Explanation: Stalin's collectivisation program aimed to consolidate individual landholdings into large, state-controlled collective farms (kolkhozes). The primary objective was to increase agricultural output to feed the growing industrial workforce and to eliminate the kulaks (wealthy peasants) as a class, who were seen as an obstacle to socialist transformation.

8. The 'Forest Act of 1865' in British India had a significant impact on forest-dwelling communities. What was its primary consequence?

  1. It granted local communities exclusive rights to forest resources.
  2. It allowed unrestricted access to all forest areas for everyone.
  3. It classified forests as state property and restricted local access and traditional practices.
  4. It established national parks for wildlife protection.

Answer: It classified forests as state property and restricted local access and traditional practices.

Explanation: The Forest Act of 1865 (and subsequent amendments) was crucial in establishing state control over forests. It declared forests as state property, restricting the traditional rights of local communities to collect forest produce, graze cattle, and practice shifting cultivation, thereby disrupting their livelihoods and way of life.

9. Which of the following events marked the beginning of the French Revolution?

  1. The storming of the Bastille
  2. The Tennis Court Oath
  3. The execution of Louis XVI
  4. The Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen

Answer: The storming of the Bastille

Explanation: The storming of the Bastille prison on July 14, 1789, is widely considered the symbolic start of the French Revolution. It represented a direct challenge to the King's authority and the oppressive old regime.

10. What was the main objective of Hitler's 'Lebensraum' policy?

  1. To establish a democratic government in Germany
  2. To expand German territory and provide living space for the Aryan race
  3. To promote international peace and cooperation
  4. To develop Germany's industrial capacity

Answer: To expand German territory and provide living space for the Aryan race

Explanation: Hitler's 'Lebensraum' (living space) policy aimed at acquiring vast territories in Eastern Europe, particularly from the Soviet Union, to resettle Germans and provide resources for the 'Aryan race', reflecting his expansionist and racist ideology.

11. Which of the following was a key reason for the decline of traditional pastoralist communities under colonial rule?

  1. Introduction of new agricultural technologies
  2. Establishment of new trade routes
  3. Restriction of movement and creation of forest reserves
  4. Increased demand for pastoral products

Answer: Restriction of movement and creation of forest reserves

Explanation: Colonial governments often restricted the movement of pastoralist communities, creating forest reserves and imposing taxes, which severely limited their access to grazing lands and disrupted their traditional way of life, leading to their decline.

12. The term 'Kulaks' in Russia referred to:

  1. Industrial workers
  2. Wealthy peasants
  3. Landless labourers
  4. Members of the nobility

Answer: Wealthy peasants

Explanation: In the context of the Russian Revolution and collectivization, 'Kulaks' referred to wealthy peasants who owned more land and livestock than others. They were often seen as enemies of the state by the Bolsheviks and were targeted during collectivization.

13. What was the 'Reichstag Fire Decree' issued by Hitler in 1933?

  1. A decree establishing the Nazi Party as the sole political party
  2. A decree suspending civil liberties and allowing for arbitrary arrests
  3. A decree announcing Germany's withdrawal from the League of Nations
  4. A decree initiating the persecution of Jews

Answer: A decree suspending civil liberties and allowing for arbitrary arrests

Explanation: The Reichstag Fire Decree, issued shortly after the Reichstag building was set on fire, suspended fundamental civil liberties such as freedom of speech, assembly, and press. It allowed for the arbitrary arrest and detention of political opponents, effectively dismantling democratic rights in Germany.

14. The 'Scientific Forestry' introduced by the British in India primarily aimed at:

  1. Conserving biodiversity
  2. Promoting sustainable forest management for local communities
  3. Maximizing timber production for colonial needs
  4. Encouraging traditional forest practices

Answer: Maximizing timber production for colonial needs

Explanation: Scientific Forestry, introduced by the British, focused on managing forests for the production of commercially valuable timber like teak and sal, primarily to meet the demands of the British Empire for shipbuilding and railways, often at the expense of local communities' traditional rights and forest diversity.

15. Which of the following was a significant impact of the Enclosure Movement on pastoralists in England?

  1. Increased access to common grazing lands
  2. Forced displacement and loss of traditional pastures
  3. Greater autonomy in managing their herds
  4. Improved economic conditions for all pastoralists

Answer: Forced displacement and loss of traditional pastures

Explanation: The Enclosure Movement in England involved fencing off common lands, which had historically been used by pastoralists for grazing. This led to the loss of their traditional pastures, forcing many to seek alternative livelihoods or migrate, significantly disrupting their way of life.

16. Which of the following events is considered the immediate cause for the storming of the Bastille during the French Revolution?

  1. The King's order to troops to move into Paris and the rising price of bread.
  2. The formation of the National Assembly by the Third Estate.
  3. The execution of Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette.
  4. The declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen.

Answer: The King's order to troops to move into Paris and the rising price of bread.

Explanation: The storming of the Bastille on July 14, 1789, was triggered by a combination of factors, primarily the King's order to troops to move into Paris, which created fear and suspicion among the citizens, and the severe food shortages leading to soaring bread prices, which fueled popular unrest and anger.

17. What was the name of the secret state police in Nazi Germany, notorious for its brutal methods?

  1. SA (Sturmabteilung)
  2. SS (Schutzstaffel)
  3. Gestapo
  4. Wehrmacht

Answer: Gestapo

Explanation: The Gestapo was the official secret police of Nazi Germany and German-occupied Europe. It was infamous for its ruthless tactics, including arbitrary arrests, torture, and executions, to suppress dissent and maintain Nazi control.

18. The British colonial government introduced the 'Brandis System' of forest management in India. What was its primary objective?

  1. To encourage local communities to manage forests sustainably.
  2. To promote shifting cultivation among tribal groups.
  3. To establish a scientific system for timber extraction and forest conservation.
  4. To convert all forest land into agricultural land.

Answer: To establish a scientific system for timber extraction and forest conservation.

Explanation: Dietrich Brandis, the first Inspector General of Forests in India, introduced a scientific system of forest management. Its primary objective was to regulate timber extraction, ensure a sustainable supply of wood for the British Empire, and conserve forests through systematic planning and control, often at the expense of local communities' traditional rights.

19. Which of the following was a key demand of the Third Estate in France before the revolution?

  1. Abolition of all taxes for the nobility.
  2. Voting by head in the Estates General.
  3. Restoration of absolute monarchy.
  4. Expansion of the First and Second Estates' privileges.

Answer: Voting by head in the Estates General.

Explanation: The Third Estate, representing the common people, demanded voting by head in the Estates General. This was crucial because under the old system, each estate had one vote, meaning the First and Second Estates (clergy and nobility) could always outvote the Third Estate, even though the Third Estate represented the vast majority of the population.

20. What was the main purpose of Stalin's collectivisation program in the Soviet Union?

  1. To promote individual land ownership among peasants.
  2. To increase industrial production by shifting labor from agriculture.
  3. To modernize agriculture and increase grain production for industrialization.
  4. To distribute land equally among all peasants.

Answer: To modernize agriculture and increase grain production for industrialization.

Explanation: Stalin's collectivisation program aimed to modernize agriculture and increase grain production to support rapid industrialization. It involved forcing peasants to surrender their individual landholdings and work on large, state-controlled collective farms (kolkhozes), often leading to widespread famine and resistance.

21. The 'Forest Acts' introduced by the British in India had a significant impact on pastoralist communities. What was one of the major consequences?

  1. They were granted exclusive rights to graze their animals in reserved forests.
  2. Their traditional grazing lands were restricted or closed off, leading to hardship.
  3. They were encouraged to adopt settled agriculture instead of pastoralism.
  4. The Acts provided financial incentives for pastoralists to expand their herds.

Answer: Their traditional grazing lands were restricted or closed off, leading to hardship.

Explanation: The Forest Acts classified forests into Reserved, Protected, and Village Forests. This severely restricted the access of pastoralist communities to their traditional grazing lands, forcing them to seek new pastures, pay high taxes, or face penalties, leading to significant hardship and disruption of their livelihoods.

22. Which of the following was a key feature of Hitler's ideology regarding race?

  1. Belief in the equality of all races.
  2. Promotion of a multi-ethnic German state.
  3. The idea of a 'pure' Aryan race as superior and the need to eliminate 'inferior' races.
  4. Emphasis on cultural diversity within Germany.

Answer: The idea of a 'pure' Aryan race as superior and the need to eliminate 'inferior' races.

Explanation: A central tenet of Hitler's Nazi ideology was the belief in racial purity and the superiority of the 'Aryan' race (which he associated with Nordic Germans). This ideology led to the persecution and systematic extermination of groups deemed 'inferior,' particularly Jews, Roma, and others.

23. Which treaty, signed after World War I, was seen as particularly harsh on Germany and contributed to the rise of Nazism?

  1. Treaty of Versailles
  2. Treaty of Paris
  3. Treaty of Trianon
  4. Treaty of Brest-Litovsk

Answer: Treaty of Versailles

Explanation: The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1919, imposed severe reparations, territorial losses, and military restrictions on Germany. Many Germans felt humiliated and resentful, creating fertile ground for extremist ideologies like Nazism to gain support. The other treaties were significant but not the primary one impacting Germany in this context.

24. What was the main objective of the Forest Acts introduced by the British colonial government in India?

  1. To promote sustainable forestry practices
  2. To protect indigenous forest communities
  3. To control and exploit forest resources for colonial interests
  4. To encourage afforestation and biodiversity

Answer: To control and exploit forest resources for colonial interests

Explanation: The British colonial government introduced Forest Acts primarily to gain control over valuable forest resources like timber, which were crucial for shipbuilding and railway expansion. These acts often restricted the traditional rights of forest-dwelling communities and prioritized colonial economic interests over ecological sustainability or local welfare.

25. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of pastoral nomadic communities?

  1. Settled agriculture and permanent dwellings
  2. Reliance on industrial production for livelihood
  3. Seasonal migration with their livestock in search of pastures
  4. Exclusive dependence on hunting and gathering

Answer: Seasonal migration with their livestock in search of pastures

Explanation: Pastoral nomadic communities are defined by their practice of moving seasonally with their herds of livestock (like sheep, goats, cattle, or camels) in search of fresh pastures and water. This migratory lifestyle is essential for their survival and livelihood, distinguishing them from settled agriculturalists or industrial workers.
