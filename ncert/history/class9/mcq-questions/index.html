26. What was the 'Reign of Terror' during the French Revolution primarily characterized by?

  1. Economic prosperity and social stability
  2. Mass executions of perceived enemies of the revolution
  3. The establishment of a constitutional monarchy
  4. Peaceful negotiations with European powers

Answer: Mass executions of perceived enemies of the revolution

Explanation: The 'Reign of Terror' (1793-1794) was a period of extreme violence and political repression during the French Revolution. It was characterized by the systematic execution of thousands of people, including nobles, clergy, and anyone suspected of being an enemy of the revolution, often by guillotine, under the leadership of Maximilien Robespierre.

27. The collectivisation program in the Soviet Union, introduced by Stalin, aimed to:

  1. Promote individual land ownership among peasants
  2. Increase agricultural production by consolidating small farms into large state-controlled farms
  3. Encourage free-market principles in agriculture
  4. Reduce government control over the agricultural sector

Answer: Increase agricultural production by consolidating small farms into large state-controlled farms

Explanation: Stalin's collectivisation program aimed to replace private farms with large, state-controlled collective farms (kolkhozes). The primary goal was to increase agricultural output to feed the industrial workforce and export grain, believing that larger farms would be more efficient and easier to manage, despite widespread resistance from peasants.

28. What was the 'Holocaust' during World War II?

  1. A series of Allied bombing campaigns against German cities
  2. The systematic genocide of approximately six million Jews by the Nazi regime
  3. The battle for control of Stalingrad between Germany and the Soviet Union
  4. The Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor

Answer: The systematic genocide of approximately six million Jews by the Nazi regime

Explanation: The Holocaust refers to the state-sponsored systematic persecution and annihilation of European Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators between 1933 and 1945. It was a genocide that resulted in the murder of approximately six million Jews, along with millions of others deemed 'undesirable' by the Nazi regime.

29. What was the name of Hitler's secret state police force?

  1. SA (Sturmabteilung)
  2. SS (Schutzstaffel)
  3. Gestapo
  4. Wehrmacht

Answer: Gestapo

Explanation: The Gestapo was the official secret police of Nazi Germany and German-occupied Europe. It was instrumental in suppressing opposition and enforcing Nazi ideology through terror and surveillance.

30. Which of the following was a primary reason for the British colonial government's interest in forests in India?

  1. To promote biodiversity conservation
  2. To provide land for tribal communities
  3. To meet the demand for timber for shipbuilding and railways
  4. To establish national parks for tourism

Answer: To meet the demand for timber for shipbuilding and railways

Explanation: The British colonial government had a significant demand for timber, especially for building ships for the Royal Navy and for constructing railway sleepers. This led to extensive deforestation and the implementation of forest laws to control timber resources.

32. Which of the following was NOT a cause of the French Revolution?

  1. Economic crisis and high food prices
  2. Absolute monarchy and social inequality
  3. The rise of industrial capitalism
  4. Influence of Enlightenment ideas

Answer: The rise of industrial capitalism

Explanation: While industrial capitalism would emerge later, it was not a direct cause of the French Revolution. The revolution was primarily fueled by economic hardship, the rigid social hierarchy of the Old Regime, the absolute power of the monarchy, and the spread of Enlightenment ideals of liberty and equality.

33. The 'Bloody Sunday' incident in Russia, which occurred in 1905, involved:

  1. The assassination of Tsar Nicholas II
  2. A peaceful procession of workers fired upon by the police
  3. A major peasant uprising against landlords
  4. The establishment of the Duma

Answer: A peaceful procession of workers fired upon by the police

Explanation: On January 22, 1905, a peaceful procession of workers led by Father Gapon marched to the Winter Palace to present a petition to the Tsar. They were brutally fired upon by the police and Cossacks, resulting in hundreds of deaths and injuries. This event, known as 'Bloody Sunday', sparked widespread unrest and was a significant precursor to the 1917 revolution.

34. Which of the following events is considered the immediate cause for the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789?

  1. The storming of the Bastille
  2. The Tennis Court Oath
  3. The calling of the Estates General
  4. The publication of 'The Social Contract' by Rousseau

Answer: The calling of the Estates General

Explanation: While the storming of the Bastille (Option A) is a symbolic start, and the Tennis Court Oath (Option B) was a pivotal moment, the immediate cause that brought representatives together and highlighted the deep-seated issues was the calling of the Estates General (Option C) by King Louis XVI to approve new taxes. This assembly had not met for 175 years and its convocation itself was a sign of the crisis, directly leading to the demands for reform and the subsequent revolutionary events. 'The Social Contract' (Option D) was an influential philosophical work, but not an immediate event triggering the revolution.

35. In the context of the Russian Revolution, what was the primary demand of the 'April Theses' put forward by V.I. Lenin in 1917?

  1. Immediate withdrawal from World War I, land to the peasants, and nationalization of banks.
  2. Establishment of a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy.
  3. Support for the Provisional Government and continuation of the war effort.
  4. Formation of a new international socialist organization.

Answer: Immediate withdrawal from World War I, land to the peasants, and nationalization of banks.

Explanation: Lenin's 'April Theses' were a crucial set of demands upon his return to Russia in April 1917. His three main demands were: (1) the war (World War I) be brought to an end, (2) land be transferred to the peasants, and (3) banks be nationalised. These demands directly challenged the policies of the Provisional Government and aimed to move the revolution towards a socialist phase. Options B and C represent the aims of other political factions or the existing government, while Option D was a broader long-term goal but not the primary immediate demand of the Theses.
