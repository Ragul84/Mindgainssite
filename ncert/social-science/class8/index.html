Class8 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class8 questions to revise the NCERT Social Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 291 questions.

1. In which year did the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II grant the Diwani of Bengal to the East India Company?

  1. 1757
  2. 1764
  3. 1765
  4. 1772

Answer: 1765

Explanation: The Mughal Emperor granted the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the Company in 1765.

2. What does the term 'Diwani' specifically refer to in the context of the Mughal administration?

  1. The right to maintain an army
  2. The right to collect land revenue
  3. The right to appoint governors
  4. The right to build forts

Answer: The right to collect land revenue

Explanation: Diwani was the right granted to the East India Company to collect revenues from the provinces of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.

3. Which battle paved the way for the East India Company to secure the Diwani rights from the Mughal Emperor?

  1. Battle of Plassey
  2. Battle of Buxar
  3. Third Battle of Panipat
  4. Battle of Wandiwash

Answer: Battle of Buxar

Explanation: The victory at the Battle of Buxar in 1764 forced the Mughal Emperor to grant the Diwani rights to the Company.

4. How did the grant of Diwani benefit the East India Company financially?

  1. It allowed them to stop trading in textiles
  2. It eliminated the need to import gold and silver from Britain
  3. It allowed them to pay taxes to the Mughal Emperor
  4. It gave them the right to mint their own currency

Answer: It eliminated the need to import gold and silver from Britain

Explanation: The Company could now use the revenue collected from Bengal to purchase Indian goods and maintain troops.

5. Who was the Mughal Emperor who granted the Diwani of Bengal to the East India Company?

  1. Aurangzeb
  2. Bahadur Shah Zafar
  3. Shah Alam II
  4. Farrukhsiyar

Answer: Shah Alam II

Explanation: Shah Alam II was the Mughal Emperor who issued the farman granting Diwani rights in 1765.

6. What was the primary objective of the East India Company after becoming the Diwan of Bengal?

  1. To establish a democratic government
  2. To maximize revenue collection
  3. To promote local Indian industries
  4. To return the land to the peasants

Answer: To maximize revenue collection

Explanation: The Company wanted to increase revenue to fund its trade and military expenses without importing bullion.

7. Which of the following was a consequence of the Company's revenue policies in Bengal?

  1. Increase in agricultural productivity
  2. Prosperity for the local artisans
  3. Economic crisis and famine in Bengal
  4. Reduction in the tax burden on peasants

Answer: Economic crisis and famine in Bengal

Explanation: The Company's insistence on high revenue led to an economic collapse and contributed to the Great Bengal Famine of 1770.

8. Before becoming the Diwan, how did the East India Company purchase Indian goods for export?

  1. By using local revenue
  2. By importing gold and silver from Britain
  3. By trading spices from Indonesia
  4. By borrowing from local bankers

Answer: By importing gold and silver from Britain

Explanation: Previously, the Company had to import gold and silver (bullion) from Britain to buy cotton and silk from India.

9. What happened to the artisans of Bengal after the Company took over the Diwani?

  1. They were given subsidies
  2. They were forced to sell goods at low prices
  3. They moved to Britain to work in factories
  4. They became partners with the Company

Answer: They were forced to sell goods at low prices

Explanation: The Company used its political power to force artisans to sell their goods at prices much lower than the market rate.

10. The Company's role changed from a trading company to a _________ after the grant of Diwani.

  1. Religious organization
  2. Territorial colonial power
  3. Diplomatic mission
  4. Charitable institution

Answer: Territorial colonial power

Explanation: The grant of Diwani transformed the Company from a mere merchant entity into a governing administrative power.

11. Which province, along with Bengal, was included in the Diwani grant?

  1. Punjab
  2. Bihar
  3. Awadh
  4. Madras

Answer: Bihar

Explanation: The Diwani grant covered the provinces of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.

12. What was the impact of the Company's revenue demands on the peasantry?

  1. Peasants became wealthier
  2. Peasants were encouraged to grow cash crops
  3. Peasants were pushed into deep debt and poverty
  4. Peasants were given ownership of the land

Answer: Peasants were pushed into deep debt and poverty

Explanation: The high revenue demands forced peasants to borrow from moneylenders, leading to widespread poverty.

13. The Company's desire to increase revenue led to the decline of which industry in Bengal?

  1. Iron smelting
  2. Textile weaving
  3. Ship building
  4. Salt mining

Answer: Textile weaving

Explanation: The textile industry declined as weavers were coerced into unfavorable contracts and low wages.

14. Which of these was a result of the Company's administrative changes in Bengal?

  1. Better welfare for the poor
  2. Establishment of a fair judicial system
  3. Neglect of traditional land management
  4. Increase in the number of local zamindars

Answer: Neglect of traditional land management

Explanation: The Company focused on revenue extraction and ignored the traditional responsibility of maintaining land and irrigation.

15. The Diwani right allowed the Company to act as the ________ of the province.

  1. Chief Justice
  2. Chief Revenue Collector
  3. Military Commander
  4. Religious Head

Answer: Chief Revenue Collector

Explanation: The Diwan is the chief financial administrator responsible for revenue collection.

16. The Company stopped importing gold and silver from Britain after becoming the Diwan.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: True; they used the local revenue to buy goods, eliminating the need for bullion imports.

17. The Mughal Emperor retained full control over the administration of Bengal after 1765.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: False; the Company became the de facto ruler, and the Emperor's power became nominal.

18. What did the 'Diwani' right allow the East India Company to do?

  1. Collect land revenue
  2. Administer criminal justice
  3. Command the Mughal army
  4. Build forts in South India

Answer: Collect land revenue

Explanation: The Diwani right gave the Company the authority to collect revenues from the provinces of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.

19. Which system of land revenue was introduced by Lord Cornwallis in 1793?

  1. Mahalwari System
  2. Permanent Settlement
  3. Ryotwari System
  4. Munro System

Answer: Permanent Settlement

Explanation: The Permanent Settlement fixed the land revenue permanently to ensure a regular flow of income for the Company.

20. Under the Permanent Settlement, who were recognized as the owners of the land?

  1. The Peasants
  2. The Ryots
  3. The Zamindars
  4. The Village Headmen

Answer: The Zamindars

Explanation: Zamindars were made legal owners of the land and were responsible for collecting revenue from peasants.

21. The Mahalwari system was primarily introduced in which region of India?

  1. Bengal
  2. Madras Presidency
  3. North Western Provinces of Bengal Presidency
  4. Bombay Presidency

Answer: North Western Provinces of Bengal Presidency

Explanation: The Mahalwari system was implemented in the North Western Provinces of the Bengal Presidency.

22. Who developed the Ryotwari system in South India?

  1. Lord Cornwallis
  2. Thomas Munro
  3. Warren Hastings
  4. Lord Dalhousie

Answer: Thomas Munro

Explanation: Thomas Munro developed the Ryotwari system where revenue was settled directly with the cultivators (ryots).

23. What was the primary reason for the Company's insistence on growing indigo in India?

  1. It was used for food
  2. High demand in the European textile industry
  3. It was easy to grow in deserts
  4. It was used for medicine

Answer: High demand in the European textile industry

Explanation: Indigo provided a rich blue color highly valued by textile manufacturers in Europe.

24. Which of the following was a method of indigo cultivation where peasants grew indigo on 25% of their land?

  1. Nij cultivation
  2. Ryoti cultivation
  3. Mahalwari cultivation
  4. Permanent cultivation

Answer: Ryoti cultivation

Explanation: In Ryoti cultivation, planters forced ryots to sign contracts to grow indigo on a portion of their land.

25. In 'Nij' cultivation, on whose land was indigo grown?

  1. The peasant's land
  2. The planter's own land
  3. Common village land
  4. Government land

Answer: The planter's own land

Explanation: Nij cultivation refers to the cultivation of indigo on the planter's own land or land leased from others.
