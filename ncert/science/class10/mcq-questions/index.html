26. Which of the following sets correctly lists three different forms of calcium carbonate?

  1. Gypsum, Plaster of Paris, Quicklime
  2. Limestone, Chalk, Marble
  3. Baking soda, Washing soda, Bleaching powder
  4. Quartz, Feldspar, Mica

Answer: Limestone, Chalk, Marble

Explanation: As per the textbook, limestone, chalk, and marble are all different naturally occurring forms of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃).

27. What are the common products formed when an acid reacts with a base, and when a metallic oxide reacts with an acid?

  1. Hydrogen gas and oxygen gas
  2. Salt and water
  3. Carbon dioxide and water
  4. Metal and hydrogen gas

Answer: Salt and water

Explanation: Both neutralization reactions (acid + base) and reactions between metallic oxides (which are basic in nature) and acids produce salt and water as the common products. For example, HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O and CuO + 2HCl → CuCl₂ + H₂O.

28. Why can't hydrogen ions (H+) exist alone in an aqueous solution, and how are they typically represented?

  1. They are highly unstable and decompose into hydrogen atoms; represented as H(aq).
  2. They readily combine with water molecules; represented as H₃O⁺ or H⁺(aq).
  3. They are too small to exist independently; represented as H₂⁺.
  4. They immediately react with dissolved oxygen; represented as HO⁺.

Answer: They readily combine with water molecules; represented as H₃O⁺ or H⁺(aq).

Explanation: Hydrogen ions (H+) are highly reactive and cannot exist independently in an aqueous solution. They readily combine with water molecules (H₂O) to form hydronium ions (H₃O⁺). Therefore, they are represented as H⁺(aq) or H₃O⁺ to reflect this association with water.

29. How does a universal indicator help in determining the strength of an acid or base?

  1. It changes colour only in strong acids and strong bases.
  2. It shows a single colour for all acidic solutions and another for all basic solutions.
  3. It shows different colours at different concentrations of hydrogen ions, indicating strength.
  4. It measures the exact pH value using a digital display.

Answer: It shows different colours at different concentrations of hydrogen ions, indicating strength.

Explanation: A universal indicator is a mixture of several indicators that exhibits a range of colour changes depending on the concentration of hydrogen ions (pH) in a solution. This allows for a more precise determination of the acidic or basic nature and strength of a solution compared to single indicators.

30. What is the optimal pH range for the proper functioning of the human body?

  1. 5.0 to 6.5
  2. 7.0 to 7.8
  3. 8.0 to 9.5
  4. 6.0 to 7.0

Answer: 7.0 to 7.8

Explanation: The human body maintains a very narrow pH range for its various physiological processes to function optimally. This range is typically between 7.0 and 7.8. Any significant deviation from this range can be detrimental to health.

31. Which acid causes the burning pain from stinging nettle leaves, and what is a traditional remedy for it?

  1. Citric acid; rubbing with lemon juice.
  2. Acetic acid; applying vinegar.
  3. Methanoic acid; rubbing with dock plant leaf.
  4. Lactic acid; washing with milk.

Answer: Methanoic acid; rubbing with dock plant leaf.

Explanation: The stinging hairs of nettle leaves inject methanoic acid (also known as formic acid), which causes the burning pain. A traditional and effective remedy is to rub the affected area with the leaf of a dock plant, which often grows near nettles and contains basic compounds that neutralize the acid.

32. What is 'water of crystallisation'?

  1. Water absorbed by a substance from the atmosphere.
  2. Water molecules trapped within the crystal lattice of a salt in a fixed number.
  3. Water formed during a chemical reaction.
  4. Water used to dissolve a salt.

Answer: Water molecules trapped within the crystal lattice of a salt in a fixed number.

Explanation: Water of crystallisation refers to the fixed number of water molecules that are chemically combined with one formula unit of a salt in its crystalline state. For example, in CuSO4.5H2O, there are five water molecules of crystallisation.

33. Which ions are primarily responsible for the acidic nature of a substance when dissolved in water?

  1. OH⁻(aq) ions
  2. Cl⁻(aq) ions
  3. H⁺(aq) ions
  4. Na⁺(aq) ions

Answer: H⁺(aq) ions

Explanation: The presence and formation of H⁺(aq) ions (or H₃O⁺ ions) in an aqueous solution are characteristic of acidic substances. These ions are responsible for the acidic properties like sour taste and reaction with bases.

34. Why do acids not exhibit acidic behaviour in the absence of water?

  1. Acids become basic in the absence of water.
  2. Water acts as a catalyst for acidic reactions.
  3. Acids require water to dissociate and produce hydrogen ions (H⁺).
  4. The absence of water makes acids less concentrated.

Answer: Acids require water to dissociate and produce hydrogen ions (H⁺).

Explanation: Acids show acidic behaviour because they dissociate in water to produce hydrogen ions (H⁺) or hydronium ions (H₃O⁺). In the absence of water, acids remain in their molecular form and cannot release these ions, hence they do not exhibit acidic properties.

35. How are elements generally categorized based on their properties?

  1. Solids, liquids, and gases
  2. Organic and inorganic
  3. Metals and non-metals
  4. Reactive and non-reactive

Answer: Metals and non-metals

Explanation: Elements are broadly classified into metals and non-metals based on their distinct physical and chemical properties, such as conductivity, malleability, ductility, and reactivity.

36. Before observing the appearance of metal samples, why is it recommended to clean their surfaces with sandpaper?

  1. To make them less reactive.
  2. To remove any dull layer like oxides or tarnishing.
  3. To increase their electrical conductivity.
  4. To make them easier to handle.

Answer: To remove any dull layer like oxides or tarnishing.

Explanation: Cleaning metal surfaces with sandpaper helps to remove any dull, unreactive layer (often an oxide layer) that forms on exposure to air. This reveals the true lustrous surface of the metal, which is a characteristic property.

37. Which two metals have exceptionally low melting points, allowing them to melt when held in the palm of your hand?

  1. Iron and Copper
  2. Sodium and Potassium
  3. Gallium and Caesium
  4. Lead and Mercury

Answer: Gallium and Caesium

Explanation: Gallium and Caesium are known for their very low melting points, which are close to room temperature. This unique property allows them to melt when held in the warmth of a human palm.

38. Which of the following metals is considered a poor conductor of heat?

  1. Aluminium
  2. Copper
  3. Lead
  4. Silver

Answer: Lead

Explanation: While most metals are good conductors of heat, lead is an exception and is known to be a relatively poor conductor of heat compared to other common metals like copper, aluminium, and silver.

39. What observation is made when an iron nail is placed in a copper sulphate solution?

  1. The solution turns colourless and the nail dissolves.
  2. The solution remains blue, and the nail becomes shiny.
  3. The blue colour of the solution fades, and a reddish-brown coating forms on the nail.
  4. The solution turns green, and the nail rusts quickly.

Answer: The blue colour of the solution fades, and a reddish-brown coating forms on the nail.

Explanation: Iron is more reactive than copper. When an iron nail is placed in copper sulphate solution, iron displaces copper from the solution. This results in the fading of the blue colour (due to the formation of iron sulphate) and the deposition of reddish-brown copper metal on the iron nail.

40. Which of the following best explains the chemical reactivity of elements?

  1. Their tendency to achieve a completely filled outermost shell.
  2. Their atomic mass and density.
  3. Their ability to form covalent bonds only.
  4. Their melting and boiling points.

Answer: Their tendency to achieve a completely filled outermost shell.

Explanation: The chemical reactivity of elements is primarily determined by their tendency to attain a stable electronic configuration, typically a completely filled valence shell (octet or duet), by gaining, losing, or sharing electrons.

41. In the ionic compound MgCl₂, identify the cation and anion.

  1. Cation: Chloride ion (Cl⁻), Anion: Magnesium ion (Mg²⁺)
  2. Cation: Magnesium ion (Mg²⁺), Anion: Chloride ion (Cl⁻)
  3. Cation: Magnesium (Mg), Anion: Chlorine (Cl)
  4. Cation: Magnesium ion (Mg⁺), Anion: Chloride ion (Cl²⁻)

Answer: Cation: Magnesium ion (Mg²⁺), Anion: Chloride ion (Cl⁻)

Explanation: In MgCl₂, Magnesium (Mg) loses two electrons to form a positively charged ion (cation), Mg²⁺. Chlorine (Cl) gains one electron to form a negatively charged ion (anion), Cl⁻. Since there are two chloride ions for every magnesium ion, the formula is MgCl₂.

42. Consider four metals A, B, C, and D. Metal B displaces Iron from Iron(II) sulphate solution, while metals A, C, and D do not. Which metal is the most reactive?

  1. Metal A
  2. Metal B
  3. Metal C
  4. Metal D

Answer: Metal B

Explanation: A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its salt solution. Since metal B displaces Iron from Iron(II) sulphate, it indicates that B is more reactive than Iron. As A, C, and D do not displace Iron, B is the most reactive among the given metals.

43. Why are highly reactive metals like aluminium used as reducing agents to extract metals of lower reactivity from their compounds?

  1. They have a lower melting point, making the process easier.
  2. They are readily available and inexpensive.
  3. They have a stronger affinity for oxygen compared to less reactive metals.
  4. They form stable alloys with the extracted metals.

Answer: They have a stronger affinity for oxygen compared to less reactive metals.

Explanation: Highly reactive metals such as aluminium are strong reducing agents because they have a greater tendency to lose electrons and a stronger affinity for oxygen. This allows them to displace less reactive metals from their oxides or other compounds by taking away the oxygen or other non-metal.

44. What is 'gangue' in the context of metallurgy?

  1. The pure metal extracted from an ore.
  2. The unwanted earthy or rocky impurities present in an ore.
  3. A chemical reagent used to purify metals.
  4. The molten form of a metal during extraction.

Answer: The unwanted earthy or rocky impurities present in an ore.

Explanation: Gangue refers to the undesirable impurities, such as sand, silicates, and other rocky materials, that are naturally associated with the ore from which a metal is to be extracted. These impurities must be removed before metal extraction.

45. The blackening of silver articles when exposed to air is due to the formation of which chemical substance?

  1. Silver oxide
  2. Silver chloride
  3. Silver sulphide
  4. Silver nitrate

Answer: Silver sulphide

Explanation: Silver articles react with sulfur compounds (like hydrogen sulphide) present in the air to form a layer of silver sulphide (Ag₂S), which is black in colour, leading to the tarnishing of silver.

46. What is an alloy?

  1. A compound formed by the chemical reaction of two metals.
  2. A heterogeneous mixture of a metal and a non-metal.
  3. A homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal.
  4. A pure form of a metal obtained after refining.

Answer: A homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal.

Explanation: An alloy is a homogeneous mixture formed by combining two or more elements, at least one of which is a metal. This can be a mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal, to achieve desired properties.

47. Which of the following methods can be used to distinguish between a metal and a non-metal using a hammer and an electrical circuit?

  1. Metals are brittle when hammered, and non-metals conduct electricity.
  2. Metals are malleable when hammered, and non-metals conduct electricity.
  3. Metals are malleable when hammered, and non-metals are poor conductors of electricity.
  4. Metals are brittle when hammered, and non-metals are poor conductors of electricity.

Answer: Metals are malleable when hammered, and non-metals are poor conductors of electricity.

Explanation: Metals are generally malleable (can be hammered into sheets) and good conductors of electricity. Non-metals are typically brittle (break into pieces when hammered) and are poor conductors of electricity (insulators).

48. Which of the following statements best describes allotropes of carbon?

  1. They are different forms of carbon with similar physical and chemical properties.
  2. They are different forms of carbon with varying physical properties but identical chemical properties.
  3. They are compounds of carbon with other elements, having different physical properties.
  4. They are isotopes of carbon, differing in their atomic mass but having similar properties.

Answer: They are different forms of carbon with varying physical properties but identical chemical properties.

Explanation: Allotropes are different structural forms of the same element. For carbon, allotropes like diamond and graphite have vastly different physical properties (e.g., hardness, conductivity) but exhibit the same chemical reactions because they are made of the same element, carbon.

49. How are saturated and unsaturated carbon compounds primarily differentiated based on their bonding?

  1. Saturated compounds have carbon-hydrogen bonds, while unsaturated compounds have carbon-oxygen bonds.
  2. Saturated compounds contain only single bonds between carbon atoms, while unsaturated compounds have double or triple bonds between carbon atoms.
  3. Saturated compounds are linear, while unsaturated compounds are branched.
  4. Saturated compounds are solids, while unsaturated compounds are liquids at room temperature.

Answer: Saturated compounds contain only single bonds between carbon atoms, while unsaturated compounds have double or triple bonds between carbon atoms.

Explanation: The key distinction lies in the type of carbon-carbon bonds. Saturated compounds have the maximum number of hydrogen atoms possible, meaning all carbon-carbon bonds are single. Unsaturated compounds have fewer hydrogen atoms due to the presence of at least one double or triple bond between carbon atoms.

50. Ethyne (C2H2) is a hydrocarbon. To satisfy the valency of each carbon atom in ethyne, what type of bond must exist between the two carbon atoms?

  1. A single bond
  2. A double bond
  3. A triple bond
  4. No bond, they are separate atoms

Answer: A triple bond

Explanation: Carbon has a valency of 4. In C2H2, each carbon atom is bonded to one hydrogen atom. To complete its valency of 4, each carbon atom needs 3 more bonds. These three bonds are formed with the other carbon atom, resulting in a triple bond between the two carbon atoms.
