Class10 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class10 questions to revise the NCERT Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following statements accurately describes the process of photosynthesis?

  1. It is an anabolic process where light energy is converted into chemical energy.
  2. It is a catabolic process where chemical energy is converted into light energy.
  3. It is an anabolic process where chemical energy is converted into mechanical energy.
  4. It is a catabolic process where mechanical energy is converted into chemical energy.

Answer: It is an anabolic process where light energy is converted into chemical energy.

Explanation: Photosynthesis is an anabolic process because it involves the synthesis of complex organic molecules (glucose) from simpler inorganic molecules (carbon dioxide and water). This process utilizes light energy, converting it into chemical energy stored in the bonds of glucose.

2. A student observes that a plant kept in a dark room for several days has yellowed leaves. Which of the following pigments is most likely deficient in this plant?

  1. Chlorophyll
  2. Carotenoids
  3. Anthocyanins
  4. Xanthophylls

Answer: Chlorophyll

Explanation: Chlorophyll is the primary green pigment responsible for absorbing light energy during photosynthesis. In the absence of light, plants cannot synthesize chlorophyll, leading to its degradation and the unmasking of other pigments like carotenoids, which give a yellowish appearance to the leaves. This phenomenon is known as etiolation.

3. Which of the following is the primary site for the absorption of water and minerals in most terrestrial plants?

  1. Root hairs
  2. Stomata
  3. Lenticels
  4. Guard cells

Answer: Root hairs

Explanation: Root hairs are microscopic extensions of epidermal cells of the root. They significantly increase the surface area of the root, allowing for efficient absorption of water and dissolved mineral salts from the soil. Stomata are involved in gas exchange, lenticels in gas exchange in woody stems, and guard cells regulate stomatal opening.

4. During respiration, glucose is broken down to release energy. Which of the following is the correct sequence of events in aerobic respiration?

  1. Glycolysis → Krebs cycle → Electron transport chain
  2. Krebs cycle → Glycolysis → Electron transport chain
  3. Electron transport chain → Glycolysis → Krebs cycle
  4. Glycolysis → Electron transport chain → Krebs cycle

Answer: Glycolysis → Krebs cycle → Electron transport chain

Explanation: Aerobic respiration begins with glycolysis, which occurs in the cytoplasm and breaks down glucose into pyruvate. Pyruvate then enters the mitochondria, where it is converted to acetyl-CoA, which enters the Krebs cycle. Finally, the electron transport chain, also in the mitochondria, produces the majority of ATP.

5. Which of the following statements best describes the function of xylem in plants?

  1. Transport of water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves.
  2. Transport of prepared food from leaves to other parts of the plant.
  3. Storage of food and water in the stem.
  4. Exchange of gases between the plant and the atmosphere.

Answer: Transport of water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves.

Explanation: Xylem is a complex tissue responsible for the unidirectional transport of water and dissolved mineral nutrients from the roots upwards to all aerial parts of the plant. Phloem, on the other hand, transports prepared food. Storage and gas exchange are functions of other tissues.

6. Which of the following statements accurately describes the function of the small intestine in the human digestive system?

  1. It is the primary site for the complete digestion and absorption of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.
  2. It is mainly involved in the absorption of water and some minerals.
  3. It secretes bile and stores it for fat emulsification.
  4. It initiates the digestion of proteins through the action of pepsin.

Answer: It is the primary site for the complete digestion and absorption of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.

Explanation: The small intestine is a crucial organ where the final stages of digestion occur, and almost all nutrient absorption takes place. Enzymes from the pancreas and intestinal wall, along with bile from the liver, work together to break down carbohydrates, proteins, and fats into their simplest forms, which are then absorbed into the bloodstream.

7. A student observes that when a small amount of dilute hydrochloric acid is added to a test tube containing zinc granules, a gas is produced. Which of the following tests would confirm the identity of this gas?

  1. Bringing a glowing splint near the mouth of the test tube, which then reignites.
  2. Passing the gas through limewater, which turns milky.
  3. Bringing a burning splint near the mouth of the test tube, which extinguishes with a 'pop' sound.
  4. Testing the gas with moist blue litmus paper, which turns red.

Answer: Bringing a burning splint near the mouth of the test tube, which extinguishes with a 'pop' sound.

Explanation: When dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with zinc granules, hydrogen gas is produced. Hydrogen gas is known to burn with a 'pop' sound when a burning splint is brought near it. This is a characteristic test for hydrogen gas. A glowing splint reigniting indicates oxygen, limewater turning milky indicates carbon dioxide, and moist blue litmus turning red indicates an acidic gas.

8. Which of the following is an example of a displacement reaction?

  1. 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)
  2. AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq)
  3. Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)
  4. CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq)

Answer: Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

Explanation: A displacement reaction is one where a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound. In the reaction Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s), iron (Fe) is more reactive than copper (Cu), so it displaces copper from copper sulphate solution. The other options represent decomposition, double displacement, and combination reactions, respectively.

9. Which of the following statements is true regarding the flow of energy in an ecosystem?

  1. Energy flows unidirectionally from producers to consumers and is recycled back to producers.
  2. Energy flows bidirectionally between producers and consumers, with some loss at each trophic level.
  3. Energy flows unidirectionally from producers to consumers, and a significant portion is lost as heat at each trophic level.
  4. Energy is created by producers and completely consumed by decomposers.

Answer: Energy flows unidirectionally from producers to consumers, and a significant portion is lost as heat at each trophic level.

Explanation: Energy flow in an ecosystem is unidirectional, meaning it moves from producers (autotrophs) to consumers (heterotrophs) and eventually to decomposers. At each transfer from one trophic level to the next, a significant amount of energy (typically around 90%) is lost as heat to the environment, following the second law of thermodynamics. Energy is not recycled back to producers in the same way matter is.

10. A student sets up an experiment to study the process of photosynthesis. Which of the following conditions is essential for the successful completion of this process?

  1. Absence of carbon dioxide and presence of light.
  2. Presence of oxygen and absence of light.
  3. Presence of carbon dioxide and presence of light.
  4. Absence of water and presence of light.

Answer: Presence of carbon dioxide and presence of light.

Explanation: Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms use sunlight to synthesize foods with the help of carbon dioxide and water. Therefore, both carbon dioxide and light are essential requirements for photosynthesis to occur. Oxygen is a product, not a reactant, and water is a crucial reactant.

11. Which of the following statements accurately describes the process of photosynthesis?

  1. It is an anabolic process where light energy is converted into chemical energy.
  2. It is a catabolic process where chemical energy is converted into light energy.
  3. It is an anabolic process where chemical energy is converted into mechanical energy.
  4. It is a catabolic process where mechanical energy is converted into chemical energy.

Answer: It is an anabolic process where light energy is converted into chemical energy.

Explanation: Photosynthesis is an anabolic process because it involves the synthesis of complex organic molecules (glucose) from simpler inorganic molecules (carbon dioxide and water). This synthesis requires an input of energy, which is provided by light, thus converting light energy into chemical energy stored in the bonds of glucose.

12. A student observes that a plant kept in a dark room for several days has pale yellow leaves. Which essential component for photosynthesis is most likely missing?

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Water
  3. Chlorophyll
  4. Sunlight

Answer: Sunlight

Explanation: Sunlight is crucial for photosynthesis as it provides the energy required to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. In the absence of sunlight, chlorophyll cannot perform its function, leading to the degradation of chlorophyll and the appearance of pale yellow leaves (chlorosis).

13. Which of the following is the primary site for the absorption of water and minerals in most terrestrial plants?

  1. Leaves
  2. Stems
  3. Roots
  4. Flowers

Answer: Roots

Explanation: Roots are specialized structures in terrestrial plants designed for anchoring the plant and, more importantly, for absorbing water and dissolved mineral nutrients from the soil. They possess root hairs that increase the surface area for efficient absorption.

14. During respiration, glucose is broken down to release energy. This process primarily occurs in which cellular organelle?

  1. Nucleus
  2. Chloroplast
  3. Mitochondria
  4. Ribosome

Answer: Mitochondria

Explanation: Mitochondria are often referred to as the 'powerhouses' of the cell because they are the primary sites for cellular respiration, where glucose is oxidized to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the main energy currency of the cell.

15. Which of the following statements correctly describes the function of xylem in plants?

  1. Transports food from leaves to other parts of the plant.
  2. Transports water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.
  3. Stores excess food in the form of starch.
  4. Helps in the exchange of gases during photosynthesis.

Answer: Transports water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.

Explanation: Xylem is a complex tissue in vascular plants responsible for the transport of water and dissolved mineral nutrients from the roots upwards to all other parts of the plant. This process is known as transpiration pull.

16. Which of the following statements best describes the fundamental change that occurs to a substance during a chemical reaction?

  1. Its physical state changes from solid to liquid or gas.
  2. Its mass increases or decreases significantly.
  3. Its nature and identity are transformed into new substances.
  4. Its temperature always rises due to energy release.

Answer: Its nature and identity are transformed into new substances.

Explanation: A chemical reaction involves the breaking and formation of chemical bonds, leading to the transformation of the initial substance(s) into new substances with different chemical properties and identities. Changes in physical state or temperature are often accompanying phenomena but not the fundamental change itself.

17. A chemical equation is considered balanced when:

  1. The number of molecules on the LHS equals the number of molecules on the RHS.
  2. The total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products.
  3. The number of atoms of each element is the same on both the LHS and RHS.
  4. The coefficients of the reactants and products are all whole numbers.

Answer: The number of atoms of each element is the same on both the LHS and RHS.

Explanation: Balancing a chemical equation ensures that the Law of Conservation of Mass is upheld. This law states that matter cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction, meaning the number of atoms of each element must remain constant on both sides of the equation.

18. The method of balancing chemical equations by making trials with the smallest whole number coefficients is known as the:

  1. Stoichiometric method
  2. Algebraic method
  3. Hit-and-trial method
  4. Conservation of mass method

Answer: Hit-and-trial method

Explanation: The 'hit-and-trial method' is a common approach for balancing chemical equations, especially for simpler reactions. It involves systematically adjusting the coefficients in front of the chemical formulas until the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides of the equation.

19. The formation of water from hydrogen and oxygen (2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)) is an example of a combination reaction because:

  1. It involves the release of heat.
  2. Two reactants combine to form a single product.
  3. Water is formed as a liquid.
  4. Hydrogen and oxygen are gases.

Answer: Two reactants combine to form a single product.

Explanation: A combination reaction is defined as a reaction in which two or more reactants combine to form a single product. In this case, hydrogen and oxygen combine to form only water.

20. When ferrous sulphate (FeSO₄) is heated, it undergoes a decomposition reaction. What are the solid and gaseous products formed?

  1. Solid: Iron oxide (FeO); Gaseous: Sulfur dioxide (SO₂)
  2. Solid: Ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃); Gaseous: Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and Sulfur trioxide (SO₃)
  3. Solid: Iron (Fe); Gaseous: Sulfur trioxide (SO₃)
  4. Solid: Ferrous sulfide (FeS); Gaseous: Oxygen (O₂)

Answer: Solid: Ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃); Gaseous: Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and Sulfur trioxide (SO₃)

Explanation: Upon heating, ferrous sulphate decomposes into ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃) as a solid residue, and two gaseous products, sulphur dioxide (SO₂) and sulphur trioxide (SO₃), are evolved. This is a classic example of thermal decomposition.

21. Why is it crucial to clean iron nails with sandpaper before immersing them in copper sulphate solution in Activity 1.9?

  1. To make the nails shiny and aesthetically pleasing.
  2. To increase the surface area of the nails for faster reaction.
  3. To remove any rust or impurities, ensuring direct contact for the reaction.
  4. To prevent the copper sulphate solution from evaporating quickly.

Answer: To remove any rust or impurities, ensuring direct contact for the reaction.

Explanation: Iron nails often have a layer of rust (iron oxides) or other impurities on their surface. Cleaning with sandpaper removes this layer, exposing a fresh, reactive iron surface. This ensures that the displacement reaction between iron and copper sulphate can proceed effectively and allows for accurate observation of the chemical changes.

22. What is a 'precipitate' and what defines a 'precipitation reaction'?

  1. A precipitate is a soluble substance formed, and a precipitation reaction produces a gas.
  2. A precipitate is an insoluble substance formed, and a precipitation reaction produces such an insoluble substance.
  3. A precipitate is a liquid product, and a precipitation reaction involves heat release.
  4. A precipitate is a solid reactant, and a precipitation reaction consumes a solid.

Answer: A precipitate is an insoluble substance formed, and a precipitation reaction produces such an insoluble substance.

Explanation: A precipitate is a solid that forms out of a liquid solution during a chemical reaction and is insoluble in that solution. A precipitation reaction is any chemical reaction that results in the formation of such an insoluble solid.

23. Which type of product is characteristic of a precipitation reaction?

  1. Gaseous products
  2. Soluble salts
  3. Insoluble salts
  4. Liquid products

Answer: Insoluble salts

Explanation: Precipitation reactions are defined by the formation of an insoluble solid, which is typically an insoluble salt. This insoluble salt separates from the solution as a precipitate.

24. Which of the following statements best describes a precipitation reaction?

  1. A reaction where two gases combine to form a liquid.
  2. A reaction where an insoluble solid is formed when two soluble solutions are mixed.
  3. A reaction where a solid dissolves completely in a liquid.
  4. A reaction where a liquid turns into a gas upon heating.

Answer: A reaction where an insoluble solid is formed when two soluble solutions are mixed.

Explanation: A precipitation reaction is characterized by the formation of an insoluble solid, called a precipitate, when two soluble ionic compounds in an aqueous solution react. For example, when barium chloride solution reacts with sodium sulphate solution, an insoluble white precipitate of barium sulphate is formed: BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq).

25. What colour changes are observed when methyl orange indicator is added to an acidic solution and a basic solution, respectively?

  1. Yellow in acidic, Red in basic
  2. Red in acidic, Yellow in basic
  3. Blue in acidic, Red in basic
  4. Colourless in acidic, Pink in basic

Answer: Red in acidic, Yellow in basic

Explanation: Methyl orange is an acid-base indicator. In an acidic solution, it turns red or orange-red. In contrast, when added to a basic solution, it turns yellow.
