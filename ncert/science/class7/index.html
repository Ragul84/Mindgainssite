Class7 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class7 questions to revise the NCERT Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 358 questions.

1. Which of the following is an example of a parasitic plant?

  1. Cuscuta (Amarbel)
  2. Mushroom
  3. Green Algae
  4. Venus Flytrap

Answer: Cuscuta (Amarbel)

Explanation: Cuscuta, also known as Amarbel, is a parasitic plant that lacks chlorophyll and obtains its nutrition by growing on other plants (host plants) and drawing nutrients from them. Mushrooms are saprophytes, green algae are autotrophs, and Venus Flytrap is an insectivorous plant.

2. The process of taking food into the body is called:

  1. Digestion
  2. Absorption
  3. Ingestion
  4. Egestion

Answer: Ingestion

Explanation: Ingestion is the process of taking food into the body. Digestion is the breakdown of complex food substances into simpler ones. Absorption is the process by which digested food passes into the blood vessels. Egestion is the removal of undigested food from the body.

3. Which of the following materials is a good conductor of heat?

  1. Wood
  2. Plastic
  3. Air
  4. Copper

Answer: Copper

Explanation: Copper is a metal and metals are generally good conductors of heat, meaning they allow heat to pass through them easily. Wood, plastic, and air are insulators, meaning they do not conduct heat well.

4. A substance that turns blue litmus red is generally a/an:

  1. Acid
  2. Base
  3. Salt
  4. Neutral substance

Answer: Acid

Explanation: Acids are substances that turn blue litmus paper red. Bases turn red litmus paper blue. Salts are formed from the reaction of an acid and a base and may be neutral, acidic, or basic depending on the strength of the acid and base. Neutral substances do not change the colour of litmus paper.

5. Which of the following is an example of a chemical change?

  1. Melting of ice
  2. Boiling of water
  3. Burning of wood
  4. Tearing of paper

Answer: Burning of wood

Explanation: Burning of wood is a chemical change because new substances (ash, smoke, carbon dioxide) are formed, and the change is irreversible. Melting of ice, boiling of water, and tearing of paper are physical changes as no new substances are formed, and the chemical composition remains the same.

6. The process of breakdown of food in the cell with the release of energy is called:

  1. Photosynthesis
  2. Respiration
  3. Digestion
  4. Transpiration

Answer: Respiration

Explanation: Respiration is the process by which organisms break down glucose (food) in their cells to release energy. Photosynthesis is the process by which plants make their own food. Digestion is the breakdown of food in the digestive system. Transpiration is the loss of water vapor from plants.

7. Which component of blood is responsible for clotting?

  1. Red blood cells
  2. White blood cells
  3. Plasma
  4. Platelets

Answer: Platelets

Explanation: Platelets are tiny blood cells that help your body form clots to stop bleeding. Red blood cells carry oxygen, white blood cells fight infection, and plasma is the liquid component of blood.

8. The transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower is called:

  1. Fertilization
  2. Germination
  3. Pollination
  4. Dispersal

Answer: Pollination

Explanation: Pollination is the process of transferring pollen grains from the anther (male part) to the stigma (female part) of a flower. Fertilization is the fusion of male and female gametes. Germination is the process by which a seed sprouts. Dispersal is the spreading of seeds or fruits away from the parent plant.

9. If a car travels a distance of 100 km in 2 hours, what is its average speed?

  1. 200 km/h
  2. 50 km/h
  3. 100 km/h
  4. 2 km/h

Answer: 50 km/h

Explanation: Speed is calculated by dividing the distance traveled by the time taken. So, Speed = Distance / Time = 100 km / 2 hours = 50 km/h.

10. Which of the following is a safety device used in electrical circuits?

  1. Switch
  2. Battery
  3. Fuse
  4. Bulb

Answer: Fuse

Explanation: A fuse is a safety device that protects electrical circuits from excessive current. If the current exceeds a safe limit, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit, preventing damage to appliances and potential hazards. A switch controls the flow of current, a battery provides power, and a bulb is a load.

11. When an acid reacts with a base, it forms:

  1. Only water
  2. Only salt
  3. Salt and water
  4. Acid and base

Answer: Salt and water

Explanation: The reaction between an acid and a base is called a neutralization reaction. In this reaction, an acid and a base react to form a salt and water. For example, hydrochloric acid (acid) reacts with sodium hydroxide (base) to form sodium chloride (salt) and water.

12. During exhalation, the diaphragm moves:

  1. Upwards
  2. Downwards
  3. Remains stationary
  4. Contracts

Answer: Upwards

Explanation: During exhalation, the diaphragm relaxes and moves upwards, pushing air out of the lungs. During inhalation, the diaphragm contracts and moves downwards, allowing air to enter the lungs.

13. The blood vessels that carry blood from the heart to all parts of the body are called:

  1. Veins
  2. Capillaries
  3. Arteries
  4. Lymph vessels

Answer: Arteries

Explanation: Arteries are blood vessels that carry oxygenated blood away from the heart to various parts of the body (except pulmonary artery). Veins carry deoxygenated blood back to the heart. Capillaries are tiny blood vessels that connect arteries and veins, facilitating the exchange of substances.

14. Which of the following is a safety device used in electrical circuits to prevent damage due to excessive current?

  1. Switch
  2. Bulb
  3. Fuse
  4. Battery

Answer: Fuse

Explanation: A fuse is a safety device that melts and breaks the circuit when the current flowing through it exceeds a safe limit, thus protecting electrical appliances and wiring from damage. A switch is used to turn a circuit on or off. A bulb produces light. A battery provides electrical energy.

15. A substance that turns blue litmus paper red is generally a/an:

  1. Acid
  2. Base
  3. Salt
  4. Neutral substance

Answer: Acid

Explanation: Acids are substances that turn blue litmus paper red. Bases turn red litmus paper blue. Salts and neutral substances generally do not change the color of litmus paper.

16. Which of the following is an example of a physical change?

  1. Burning of wood
  2. Rusting of iron
  3. Melting of ice
  4. Digestion of food

Answer: Melting of ice

Explanation: Melting of ice is a physical change because only the state of water changes from solid to liquid, but its chemical composition remains the same (H2O). Burning of wood, rusting of iron, and digestion of food are all chemical changes, as new substances are formed.

17. The tiny pores on the surface of leaves through which gaseous exchange takes place are called:

  1. Xylem
  2. Phloem
  3. Stomata
  4. Chloroplasts

Answer: Stomata

Explanation: Stomata are tiny pores present on the surface of leaves, primarily on the underside, through which plants exchange gases like carbon dioxide and oxygen with the atmosphere. Xylem and phloem are involved in transportation, and chloroplasts are sites of photosynthesis.

18. Which component of blood helps in clotting?

  1. Red blood cells
  2. White blood cells
  3. Plasma
  4. Platelets

Answer: Platelets

Explanation: Platelets are tiny cell fragments in the blood that play a crucial role in blood clotting. When a blood vessel is injured, platelets rush to the site and form a plug, helping to stop the bleeding. Red blood cells carry oxygen, white blood cells fight infection, and plasma is the liquid component of blood.

19. The transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma is called:

  1. Fertilization
  2. Germination
  3. Pollination
  4. Dispersal

Answer: Pollination

Explanation: Pollination is the process of transferring pollen grains from the anther (male reproductive part) to the stigma (female reproductive part) of a flower. Fertilization is the fusion of male and female gametes. Germination is the process by which a seed sprouts. Dispersal is the spreading of seeds or fruits.

20. Which blood component helps in the clotting of blood?

  1. Red blood cells
  2. White blood cells
  3. Platelets
  4. Plasma

Answer: Platelets

Explanation: Platelets are tiny cell fragments in the blood that play a crucial role in blood clotting. When a blood vessel is injured, platelets rush to the site and form a plug, initiating the clotting process to stop bleeding.

21. Which of the following is a safety device used in electric circuits?

  1. Switch
  2. Battery
  3. Fuse
  4. Wire

Answer: Fuse

Explanation: A fuse is a safety device that protects electrical circuits from excessive current. If the current exceeds a safe limit, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit, preventing damage to appliances and potential hazards. A switch controls the flow of current, a battery provides power, and a wire conducts electricity.

22. Which of these substances would turn blue litmus paper red?

  1. Baking soda solution
  2. Lemon juice
  3. Soap solution
  4. Distilled water

Answer: Lemon juice

Explanation: Acids turn blue litmus paper red. Lemon juice is acidic due to the presence of citric acid. Baking soda solution and soap solution are basic and would turn red litmus blue. Distilled water is neutral and would not change the colour of litmus paper.

23. Which of the following is a chemical change?

  1. Melting of ice
  2. Boiling of water
  3. Burning of wood
  4. Tearing of paper

Answer: Burning of wood

Explanation: Burning of wood is a chemical change because new substances (ash, smoke, carbon dioxide) are formed, and the change is generally irreversible. Melting of ice, boiling of water, and tearing of paper are physical changes as no new substances are formed, and the chemical composition remains the same.

24. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?

  1. Solar energy
  2. Wind energy
  3. Coal
  4. Hydropower

Answer: Coal

Explanation: Coal is a non-renewable source of energy because it takes millions of years to form and its reserves are finite. Solar energy, wind energy, and hydropower are renewable sources of energy as they can be replenished naturally over a relatively short period.

25. Which part of a flower develops into a fruit after fertilization?

  1. Stamen
  2. Petal
  3. Ovary
  4. Sepal

Answer: Ovary

Explanation: After fertilization, the ovary of a flower develops into the fruit, and the ovules inside the ovary develop into seeds. The stamen is the male reproductive part, petals attract pollinators, and sepals protect the bud.
