Class11 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class11 questions to revise the NCERT Biology syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 420 questions.

1. Nomenclature in biology is the process of standardising names for organisms. When is this process primarily possible?

  1. Only after an organism's evolutionary history is fully understood.
  2. When the organism is correctly described and its name is accurately attached to it (identification).
  3. After its genetic sequence has been completely mapped.
  4. Once its habitat and ecological role are thoroughly documented.

Answer: When the organism is correctly described and its name is accurately attached to it (identification).

Explanation: Nomenclature, the standardisation of naming, is only feasible when an organism has been accurately described and identified, ensuring that the assigned name correctly corresponds to that specific organism globally.

2. Which combination of information forms the essential basis for modern taxonomic studies?

  1. Only the external morphology and geographical distribution of organisms.
  2. The internal structure, cellular organisation, and reproductive patterns.
  3. External and internal structure, cell structure, developmental process, and ecological information.
  4. Genetic makeup and behavioral patterns exclusively.

Answer: External and internal structure, cell structure, developmental process, and ecological information.

Explanation: Modern taxonomic studies rely on a comprehensive understanding of organisms, including their external and internal structure, cellular organisation, developmental processes, and ecological information, to accurately classify them.

3. In the taxonomic hierarchy, how is the category 'Family' defined, and what features are used to characterise plant families?

  1. A group of related species with many similarities; only reproductive features are used for plants.
  2. A group of related genera with fewer similarities than genus/species; both vegetative and reproductive features are used for plants.
  3. A collection of unrelated genera; only vegetative features are used for plants.
  4. A single genus with diverse species; genetic features are primarily used for plants.

Answer: A group of related genera with fewer similarities than genus/species; both vegetative and reproductive features are used for plants.

Explanation: The taxonomic category 'Family' groups related genera, exhibiting fewer similarities compared to genus or species. For plants, families are characterised by considering both their vegetative (e.g., stem, leaves) and reproductive (e.g., flowers, fruits) features.

4. How are the fundamental principles of taxonomy, such as identification, naming, and classification, universally regulated to ensure consistency?

  1. Through national scientific committees that set guidelines for their respective countries.
  2. By individual scientists who propose new methods based on their research.
  3. Under international codes that ensure consistency and global acceptance.
  4. Through regional agreements that apply only to specific continents.

Answer: Under international codes that ensure consistency and global acceptance.

Explanation: To maintain consistency and achieve global acceptance, the basic principles of taxonomy, including identification, naming, and classification of organisms, are universally regulated by international codes (e.g., ICBN, ICZN).

5. What was the primary, non-scientific motivation behind early attempts to classify living organisms?

  1. To understand their evolutionary relationships.
  2. To use them for human purposes like food, shelter, and clothing.
  3. To document biodiversity for future generations.
  4. To develop a universal system for scientific communication.

Answer: To use them for human purposes like food, shelter, and clothing.

Explanation: Historically, early classifications of living organisms were driven by practical human needs, such as identifying sources for food, shelter, and clothing, rather than purely scientific inquiry.

6. According to Table 2.1 in NCERT, which of the following kingdoms lacks a nuclear membrane?

  1. Protista
  2. Fungi
  3. Monera
  4. Plantae

Answer: Monera

Explanation: As per Table 2.1, the Kingdom Monera (which includes bacteria and cyanobacteria) consists of prokaryotic organisms that lack a well-defined nuclear membrane, unlike Protista, Fungi, and Plantae which are eukaryotic.

7. What is the primary function of heterocysts in cyanobacteria like Nostoc and Anabaena?

  1. Photosynthesis
  2. Reproduction
  3. Fixing atmospheric nitrogen
  4. Movement

Answer: Fixing atmospheric nitrogen

Explanation: Heterocysts are specialized, thick-walled cells found in some filamentous cyanobacteria (e.g., Nostoc, Anabaena) that provide an anaerobic environment necessary for the enzyme nitrogenase to fix atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form for the organism.

8. Which of the following statements accurately describes the primary mode of nutrition for all protozoans and their evolutionary significance?

  1. Protozoans are autotrophs, synthesizing their own food, and are considered primitive relatives of plants.
  2. Protozoans are heterotrophs, living as predators or parasites, and are believed to be the primitive relatives of animals.
  3. Protozoans are saprophytes, feeding on dead organic matter, and are primitive relatives of fungi.
  4. Protozoans are chemosynthetic, obtaining energy from chemical reactions, and are primitive relatives of bacteria.

Answer: Protozoans are heterotrophs, living as predators or parasites, and are believed to be the primitive relatives of animals.

Explanation: The question asks about the primary mode of nutrition and evolutionary significance of protozoans. Protozoans are known to be heterotrophic, meaning they obtain nutrients by consuming other organisms or organic matter, often as predators or parasites. Their cellular organization and heterotrophic nature lead scientists to believe they are the primitive ancestors of animals. Options A, C, and D incorrectly describe their mode of nutrition or evolutionary lineage.

9. Which of the following sets correctly identifies three common examples of Phycomycetes and their typical habitats?

  1. Agaricus, Penicillium, Aspergillus; found in soil and decaying matter.
  2. Mucor, Rhizopus, Albugo; found in aquatic habitats, decaying wood in moist places, or as obligate plant parasites.
  3. Puccinia, Ustilago, Saccharomyces; found on living hosts or in sugary solutions.
  4. Neurospora, Morchella, Claviceps; found in diverse terrestrial environments.

Answer: Mucor, Rhizopus, Albugo; found in aquatic habitats, decaying wood in moist places, or as obligate plant parasites.

Explanation: The question asks for examples of Phycomycetes and their habitats. Mucor, Rhizopus (bread mould), and Albugo (parasitic on mustard) are classic examples of Phycomycetes. Their typical habitats include aquatic environments, decaying wood in damp conditions, and as obligate parasites on plants. The other options list fungi from different classes or incorrect habitats for Phycomycetes.

10. The phenomenon in plants where free-living or dependent life forms vary among different groups is known as:

  1. Metagenesis
  2. Alternation of generations
  3. Parthenogenesis
  4. Asexual reproduction

Answer: Alternation of generations

Explanation: The question describes the phenomenon of 'alternation of generations' in plants. This refers to the life cycle where there is a regular alternation between a multicellular haploid gametophyte and a multicellular diploid sporophyte. The degree of dependence or independence of these two phases varies across different plant groups. Metagenesis is seen in some animals, parthenogenesis is a form of asexual reproduction, and asexual reproduction is a broader term.

11. Which of the following criteria were primarily used by Whittaker for his elaborate five-kingdom classification?

  1. Gross superficial morphological characters, habit, and colour.
  2. Cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships.
  3. Presence or absence of a cell wall, locomotion, and habitat.
  4. Chromosome number, genetic makeup, and ecological niche.

Answer: Cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships.

Explanation: R.H. Whittaker's five-kingdom classification system (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) was based on several key criteria. These included cell structure (prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic), body organisation (unicellular vs. multicellular), mode of nutrition (autotrophic, heterotrophic, saprophytic), reproduction (sexual, asexual), and phylogenetic relationships (evolutionary history). Option A describes artificial systems, while C and D are incomplete or not the primary criteria for his system.

12. Early artificial systems of classification, such as Linnaeus's system, primarily used which of the following characteristics, and what was their major limitation?

  1. Phylogenetic relationships and evolutionary history; they failed to account for convergent evolution.
  2. Cell structure and body organisation; they could not differentiate between prokaryotes and eukaryotes.
  3. Gross superficial morphological characters like habit, colour, and androecium structure; they separated closely related species and gave equal weight to environmentally variable vegetative characters.
  4. Mode of nutrition and reproduction; they overlooked the importance of genetic diversity.

Answer: Gross superficial morphological characters like habit, colour, and androecium structure; they separated closely related species and gave equal weight to environmentally variable vegetative characters.

Explanation: Early artificial systems of classification, including Linnaeus's, relied on easily observable, superficial morphological characters such as habit, colour, number and shape of leaves, or the structure of the androecium. A major limitation of these systems was that they often separated closely related species based on a few arbitrary characteristics and gave equal importance to vegetative characters (which are often influenced by the environment) and sexual characteristics, leading to an unnatural grouping.

13. What are zoospores, and what is their primary role in algal reproduction?

  1. Non-motile, thick-walled spores that help algae survive adverse conditions.
  2. Flagellated (motile) asexual spores that germinate to form new algal plants.
  3. Sexual spores formed by the fusion of gametes, leading to genetic variation.
  4. Spores produced during fragmentation, primarily for vegetative propagation.

Answer: Flagellated (motile) asexual spores that germinate to form new algal plants.

Explanation: Zoospores are specifically defined as flagellated, motile asexual spores. They are a common means of asexual reproduction in many algae. Upon germination, these zoospores develop into new algal plants, effectively propagating the species without genetic recombination. Options A, C, and D describe other types of spores or reproductive methods, but not zoospores.

14. Which of the following pairs represents two common members of Rhodophyceae?

  1. Spirogyra and Volvox
  2. Fucus and Laminaria
  3. Polysiphonia and Porphyra
  4. Chlamydomonas and Ulothrix

Answer: Polysiphonia and Porphyra

Explanation: The question asks to identify two common members of Rhodophyceae, which are red algae. Polysiphonia and Porphyra are well-known examples of red algae. Spirogyra and Volvox are green algae (Chlorophyceae). Fucus and Laminaria are brown algae (Phaeophyceae). Chlamydomonas and Ulothrix are also green algae (Chlorophyceae).

15. Which of the following accurately describes the two distinct stages in the life cycle of mosses?

  1. A dominant sporophyte stage and a dependent gametophyte stage.
  2. A protonema stage (creeping, filamentous) developing from a spore, followed by a leafy stage (upright, with spirally arranged leaves) bearing sex organs.
  3. An independent thalloid gametophyte and a parasitic sporophyte.
  4. A rhizoid stage for anchorage and a sporangium stage for spore production.

Answer: A protonema stage (creeping, filamentous) developing from a spore, followed by a leafy stage (upright, with spirally arranged leaves) bearing sex organs.

Explanation: Mosses exhibit a distinct life cycle with two main stages. The first is the protonema stage, which is a creeping, green, branched, and filamentous structure that develops directly from a spore. The second is the leafy stage, which arises as a lateral bud from the secondary protonema. This leafy stage consists of upright, slender axes with spirally arranged leaves, anchored by multicellular rhizoids, and it is responsible for bearing the sex organs. Option A describes the life cycle of vascular plants, C describes liverworts, and D is an incomplete description.

16. Which of the following statements correctly describes specialized root structures and their associations in gymnosperms?

  1. All gymnosperms have adventitious roots with nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
  2. Pinus exhibits coralloid roots associated with N2-fixing cyanobacteria, while Cycas has mycorrhizal associations.
  3. Gymnosperms generally have tap roots; some, like Pinus, show mycorrhizal associations, and others, like Cycas, have coralloid roots with N2-fixing cyanobacteria.
  4. Only Cycas possesses tap roots, and all other gymnosperms have fibrous roots with fungal associations.

Answer: Gymnosperms generally have tap roots; some, like Pinus, show mycorrhizal associations, and others, like Cycas, have coralloid roots with N2-fixing cyanobacteria.

Explanation: The correct statement is that gymnosperms generally have tap roots. Pinus shows mycorrhizal associations (fungal association), and Cycas has specialized coralloid roots associated with N2-fixing cyanobacteria. Option A is incorrect as not all gymnosperms have adventitious roots, and the association mentioned is not universal. Option B incorrectly swaps the associations for Pinus and Cycas. Option D is incorrect as tap roots are common in gymnosperms, not just Cycas, and the generalization about fibrous roots is wrong.

17. Despite living in soil, bryophytes are dependent on water for sexual reproduction primarily because:

  1. Water is needed for the dispersal of spores.
  2. The antheridia and archegonia can only develop in aquatic environments.
  3. Water facilitates the transfer of male gametes to the female sex organs.
  4. Bryophytes absorb nutrients from water, which is essential for gamete formation.

Answer: Water facilitates the transfer of male gametes to the female sex organs.

Explanation: Bryophytes require water for sexual reproduction because the male gametes (sperms), produced in the antheridia, are motile and need a film of water to swim and reach the female sex organs (archegonia) for fertilization. Options A, B, and D are incorrect as spore dispersal is not directly related to sexual reproduction's water dependency, sex organs can develop on land, and nutrient absorption is a separate physiological process.

18. Identify the correct pairing of symmetry type with its definition and example.

  1. Asymmetry: Any plane passing through the center divides the organism into two identical halves (e.g., Coelenterates).
  2. Radial symmetry: Any plane passing through the central axis divides the organism into two identical halves (e.g., Sponges).
  3. Asymmetry: No plane passing through the center divides the organism into equal halves (e.g., most Sponges).
  4. Radial symmetry: Only one plane divides the organism into two identical halves (e.g., Echinoderms).

Answer: Asymmetry: No plane passing through the center divides the organism into equal halves (e.g., most Sponges).

Explanation: Asymmetry is characterized by the absence of any plane that can divide the organism into equal halves, as seen in most sponges. Radial symmetry means that any plane passing through the central axis divides the organism into two identical halves, examples include Coelenterates, Ctenophores, and Echinoderms. Option A incorrectly defines asymmetry and gives an incorrect example. Option B incorrectly defines radial symmetry and gives an incorrect example. Option D incorrectly describes radial symmetry, which allows multiple planes of division, not just one.

19. Which of the following statements accurately differentiates between the polyp and medusa forms of Cnidarians?

  1. Polyp is free-swimming and umbrella-shaped (e.g., Aurelia), while medusa is sessile and cylindrical (e.g., Hydra).
  2. Both polyp and medusa forms are sessile, but polyp has tentacles directed upwards and medusa downwards.
  3. Polyp is sessile, cylindrical, with mouth and tentacles upwards (e.g., Hydra), whereas medusa is free-swimming, umbrella-shaped, with mouth and tentacles downwards (e.g., Aurelia).
  4. Medusa is always colonial, while polyp is always solitary.

Answer: Polyp is sessile, cylindrical, with mouth and tentacles upwards (e.g., Hydra), whereas medusa is free-swimming, umbrella-shaped, with mouth and tentacles downwards (e.g., Aurelia).

Explanation: The polyp form is typically sessile, cylindrical, and has its mouth and tentacles directed upwards (e.g., Hydra, Adamsia). The medusa form is free-swimming, umbrella-shaped, and has its mouth and tentacles typically directed downwards (e.g., Aurelia). Option A incorrectly swaps the characteristics and examples. Option B incorrectly states both forms are sessile. Option D makes an incorrect generalization about colonial/solitary nature.

20. The phylum Annelida derives its name from the Latin word 'annulus'. What anatomical feature of these organisms reflects this naming convention?

  1. Their segmented body, which appears as a series of little rings.
  2. The presence of a ring-like nervous system.
  3. Their circular mouth opening.
  4. The ring of cilia around their larval stage.

Answer: Their segmented body, which appears as a series of little rings.

Explanation: The name Annelida comes from the Latin word 'annulus', meaning 'little ring'. This refers to the distinct segmentation of their body into metameres, which gives them a ring-like appearance. Options B, C, and D describe other features not directly related to the etymology of 'Annelida' or its primary defining characteristic.

21. The body of hemichordates is distinctly divided into which three regions?

  1. Head, thorax, and abdomen
  2. Proboscis, collar, and trunk
  3. Cephalothorax, abdomen, and tail
  4. Anterior, middle, and posterior segments

Answer: Proboscis, collar, and trunk

Explanation: Hemichordates have a cylindrical body that is divided into three distinct regions: an anterior proboscis, a collar, and a long trunk. Options A, C, and D represent body divisions found in other animal phyla (e.g., insects, crustaceans, general segmentation) but not specifically hemichordates.

22. Which of the following pairs correctly identifies specialized organs found in some Chondrichthyes and their respective examples?

  1. Electric organs (e.g., Trygon) and poison sting (e.g., Torpedo)
  2. Poison sting (e.g., Torpedo) and bioluminescent organs (e.g., Raja)
  3. Electric organs (e.g., Torpedo) and poison sting (e.g., Trygon)
  4. Lateral line system (e.g., Scoliodon) and swim bladder (e.g., Chimaera)

Answer: Electric organs (e.g., Torpedo) and poison sting (e.g., Trygon)

Explanation: Some members of Chondrichthyes possess specialized organs like electric organs, exemplified by Torpedo (electric ray), and a poison sting, exemplified by Trygon (stingray). Option A incorrectly swaps the examples. Option B incorrectly identifies bioluminescent organs as a specialized feature for Chondrichthyes and swaps the example for poison sting. Option D lists a general sensory system and a feature (swim bladder) typically absent in Chondrichthyes.

23. What is the most unique characteristic of mammals that distinguishes them from other animal groups, particularly concerning the nourishment of their offspring?

  1. Presence of hair on the body.
  2. Warm-blooded nature (homoiothermy).
  3. Presence of milk-producing glands (mammary glands) for nourishing young ones.
  4. Four-chambered heart.

Answer: Presence of milk-producing glands (mammary glands) for nourishing young ones.

Explanation: While mammals possess hair, are warm-blooded, and have a four-chambered heart, the most unique and defining characteristic related to the nourishment of their young ones is the presence of milk-producing mammary glands. This feature is exclusive to mammals and provides direct nourishment to their offspring. The other options are important mammalian characteristics but not as unique in the context of offspring nourishment.

24. Which of the following pairs correctly identifies a key defining characteristic for both Annelida and Arthropoda?

  1. Annelida: Jointed appendages; Arthropoda: Metameric segmentation
  2. Annelida: True coelom; Arthropoda: Most abundant group
  3. Annelida: Radial symmetry; Arthropoda: Open circulatory system
  4. Annelida: Exoskeleton; Arthropoda: Bilateral symmetry

Answer: Annelida: True coelom; Arthropoda: Most abundant group

Explanation: Annelids are characterized by metameric segmentation and the presence of a true coelom. Arthropods are the most abundant group of animals and are characterized by jointed appendages. Option B correctly identifies a key characteristic for each.

25. In early biological studies, how was the description of diverse life forms primarily achieved?

  1. Through genetic sequencing and molecular analysis.
  2. By observing gross structural features using naked eyes, magnifying lenses, and microscopes.
  3. Mainly by studying fossil records and evolutionary relationships.
  4. Through experimental manipulation of organisms in controlled environments.

Answer: By observing gross structural features using naked eyes, magnifying lenses, and microscopes.

Explanation: Initially, the description of diverse forms of life was made through observation, using naked eyes, magnifying lenses, and microscopes, primarily focusing on gross structural features, both external and internal.
