26. What is the primary function of the thimble-like structure covering the apex of the root?

  1. To absorb water and minerals from the soil.
  2. To facilitate gas exchange for respiration.
  3. To protect the tender root apex as it grows through the soil.
  4. To produce new root cells for growth.

Answer: To protect the tender root apex as it grows through the soil.

Explanation: The apex of the root is covered by a thimble-like structure called the root cap. Its primary function is to protect the tender apex of the root as it makes its way through the soil.

27. Which statement accurately differentiates between a simple and a compound leaf, considering the midrib and axillary bud?

  1. In a simple leaf, incisions reach the midrib, while in a compound leaf, they do not. An axillary bud is absent in compound leaves.
  2. A simple leaf has an entire lamina or incisions that don't touch the midrib. A compound leaf has incisions reaching the midrib, forming leaflets. An axillary bud is present in the petiole axil of both, but not in leaflet axils.
  3. Compound leaves always have a single midrib, whereas simple leaves have multiple. Axillary buds are only found in simple leaves.
  4. Simple leaves lack an axillary bud, while compound leaves always possess one at the base of each leaflet.

Answer: A simple leaf has an entire lamina or incisions that don't touch the midrib. A compound leaf has incisions reaching the midrib, forming leaflets. An axillary bud is present in the petiole axil of both, but not in leaflet axils.

Explanation: A leaf is simple when its lamina is entire or when incised, the incisions do not touch the midrib. A leaf is compound when the incisions of the lamina reach up to the midrib, breaking it into a number of leaflets. An axillary bud is present in the axil of the petiole in both simple and compound leaves, but not in the axil of the leaflets of a compound leaf.

28. In an epigynous flower, what is the nature of the ovary and where do the other floral parts arise?

  1. Superior ovary; floral parts arise below the ovary.
  2. Half-inferior ovary; floral parts arise at the same level as the ovary.
  3. Inferior ovary; floral parts arise above the ovary.
  4. Superior ovary; floral parts are fused with the ovary.

Answer: Inferior ovary; floral parts arise above the ovary.

Explanation: In epigynous flowers, the margin of the thalamus grows upward, completely enclosing the ovary and fusing with it. The other floral parts arise above the ovary. Consequently, the ovary in such flowers is considered inferior, e.g., guava.

29. Which of the following correctly defines placentation and provides an example of marginal placentation?

  1. Placentation is the arrangement of petals in a flower; marginal placentation is seen in sunflower.
  2. Placentation is the arrangement of ovules within the ovary; marginal placentation is characterized by ovules on a ridge along the ventral suture, as in pea.
  3. Placentation is the arrangement of sepals; marginal placentation involves ovules attached to the central axis, as in tomato.
  4. Placentation is the arrangement of stamens; marginal placentation is when ovules are scattered on the ovary wall, as in mustard.

Answer: Placentation is the arrangement of ovules within the ovary; marginal placentation is characterized by ovules on a ridge along the ventral suture, as in pea.

Explanation: Placentation refers to the arrangement of ovules within the ovary. In marginal placentation, the placenta forms a ridge along the ventral suture of the ovary, and the ovules are borne on this ridge, typically forming two rows. A classic example of marginal placentation is found in pea.

30. What are the protective sheaths that enclose the plumule and radicle, respectively?

  1. Pericarp and Testa
  2. Coleorhiza and Coleoptile
  3. Coleoptile and Coleorhiza
  4. Epicotyl and Hypocotyl

Answer: Coleoptile and Coleorhiza

Explanation: The sheath enclosing the plumule is called the coleoptile, and the sheath enclosing the radicle is called the coleorhiza.

31. Which statement accurately describes the key characteristics of a leaf?

  1. A leaf is an endogenous outgrowth of the stem, typically brown, primarily for support.
  2. A leaf is a lateral outgrowth of the stem developed exogenously at the node, usually green, and primarily performs photosynthesis.
  3. A leaf is a modified root structure, developed endogenously, primarily for water storage.
  4. A leaf is a reproductive organ, developed at the internode, primarily for seed dispersal.

Answer: A leaf is a lateral outgrowth of the stem developed exogenously at the node, usually green, and primarily performs photosynthesis.

Explanation: A leaf is a lateral outgrowth of the stem developed exogenously at the node. These are typically green in colour and primarily perform the function of photosynthesis.

32. Which of the following components are integral parts of the epidermal tissue system in plants?

  1. Epidermal cells, vascular bundles, and root hairs
  2. Stomata, trichomes, and parenchyma cells
  3. Epidermal cells, stomata, and epidermal appendages
  4. Guard cells, xylem, and phloem

Answer: Epidermal cells, stomata, and epidermal appendages

Explanation: The epidermal tissue system forms the outermost covering of the plant body and is composed of epidermal cells, stomata (pores for gas exchange), and epidermal appendages, which include structures like trichomes (hairs) and root hairs. Vascular bundles and parenchyma are part of other tissue systems.

33. The cortex of a dicotyledonous root is primarily characterized by:

  1. Thick-walled sclerenchyma cells with no intercellular spaces
  2. Several layers of thin-walled parenchyma cells with intercellular spaces
  3. Vascular bundles surrounded by a pericycle
  4. Compactly arranged collenchyma cells without intercellular spaces

Answer: Several layers of thin-walled parenchyma cells with intercellular spaces

Explanation: In a dicotyledonous root, the cortex is typically made up of multiple layers of thin-walled parenchyma cells. A key characteristic of these cells in the cortex is the presence of intercellular spaces, which facilitate gas exchange and storage.

34. In a dorsiventral leaf, how are stomata typically distributed on its surfaces?

  1. Equally distributed on both adaxial and abaxial surfaces
  2. More numerous on the adaxial (upper) epidermis than the abaxial (lower) epidermis
  3. Exclusively found on the adaxial (upper) epidermis
  4. More numerous on the abaxial (lower) epidermis, with the adaxial surface potentially lacking them

Answer: More numerous on the abaxial (lower) epidermis, with the adaxial surface potentially lacking them

Explanation: Dorsiventral leaves, common in dicots, show a clear differentiation between their upper (adaxial) and lower (abaxial) surfaces. Stomata are generally more abundant on the abaxial (lower) epidermis to minimize water loss due to direct sunlight, and the adaxial (upper) epidermis may even be devoid of stomata.

35. Which of the following statements accurately differentiates the performance of basic life functions in unicellular and complex multicellular organisms?

  1. In both, all functions are performed by specialized organs.
  2. Unicellular organisms perform all functions with a single cell, while multicellular organisms show division of labour among different cell groups.
  3. Multicellular organisms perform all functions with a single cell, while unicellular organisms show division of labour.
  4. Digestion, respiration, and reproduction are absent in unicellular organisms.

Answer: Unicellular organisms perform all functions with a single cell, while multicellular organisms show division of labour among different cell groups.

Explanation: Unicellular organisms, by definition, consist of a single cell that carries out all essential life processes like digestion, respiration, and reproduction. In contrast, complex multicellular organisms exhibit a high degree of organization, where different groups of cells, tissues, and organs are specialized to perform specific functions, demonstrating a clear division of labour.

36. What are the key structural and functional differences between the forelimbs and hind limbs of a frog?

  1. Forelimbs have five digits and are webbed for swimming, while hind limbs have four digits and are used for walking.
  2. Hind limbs have four digits and are smaller, while forelimbs have five digits and are larger for leaping.
  3. Forelimbs end in four digits, while hind limbs end in five digits, are larger, more muscular, and webbed, aiding in swimming, walking, and leaping.
  4. Both forelimbs and hind limbs have five digits and are equally muscular, primarily for burrowing.

Answer: Forelimbs end in four digits, while hind limbs end in five digits, are larger, more muscular, and webbed, aiding in swimming, walking, and leaping.

Explanation: A frog's forelimbs typically end in four digits, while its hind limbs are larger, more muscular, and end in five digits, which are often webbed. The webbed hind limbs are particularly adapted for swimming, but both sets of limbs contribute to various movements like walking, leaping, and burrowing.

37. How does lymph in a frog's circulatory system primarily differ from its blood?

  1. Lymph contains more proteins and RBCs than blood.
  2. Lymph lacks certain specific proteins and Red Blood Cells (RBCs) found in blood.
  3. Lymph has a higher concentration of oxygen compared to blood.
  4. Lymph contains platelets, which are absent in blood.

Answer: Lymph lacks certain specific proteins and Red Blood Cells (RBCs) found in blood.

Explanation: Lymph is a fluid that circulates in the lymphatic system. In frogs, similar to other vertebrates, lymph is formed from blood plasma that filters out of capillaries. It differs from blood mainly by lacking some specific proteins and, crucially, Red Blood Cells (RBCs), which are responsible for oxygen transport in blood.

38. Which of the following options correctly identifies two ways frogs are beneficial to mankind and the ecosystem?

  1. They are primary producers and decompose organic matter.
  2. They consume insects, protecting crops, and maintain ecological balance as a link in food chains.
  3. They produce oxygen through photosynthesis and are a source of timber.
  4. They are used for medicinal purposes and are a major source of honey.

Answer: They consume insects, protecting crops, and maintain ecological balance as a link in food chains.

Explanation: Frogs are highly beneficial. They act as natural pest controllers by consuming insects, thus protecting agricultural crops. Ecologically, they are vital components of food chains and food webs, serving as both predators and prey, which helps maintain the balance of the ecosystem. In some cultures, their muscular legs are also consumed as food.

39. Analyzing living tissues for elements and compounds within a reductionist approach helps answer questions about:

  1. The evolutionary history of organisms and their genetic mutations.
  2. The types of organic compounds present, their cellular functions, and how they contribute to gross physiological processes.
  3. The macroscopic anatomical structures and their comparative morphology.
  4. The behavioral patterns of organisms in their natural habitats.

Answer: The types of organic compounds present, their cellular functions, and how they contribute to gross physiological processes.

Explanation: The reductionist approach in biology involves breaking down complex systems into simpler components to understand their functions. Analyzing living tissues at the molecular level (elements and compounds) helps to identify the specific organic molecules present, understand their roles within cells, and ultimately explain how these molecular interactions contribute to larger physiological processes like digestion, excretion, memory, defense, and recognition.

40. Which of the following statements accurately describes the fundamental principle governing the origin of all cells?

  1. All cells spontaneously generate from non-living matter.
  2. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  3. Cells are formed by the fusion of various organic molecules.
  4. New cells are created through a process of cellular differentiation from a universal stem cell.

Answer: All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Explanation: The fundamental principle of cell theory, often attributed to Rudolf Virchow, states that 'Omnis cellula e cellula,' meaning all cells arise from pre-existing cells. This refutes the idea of spontaneous generation.

41. The classification of bacteria into Gram-positive and Gram-negative types is primarily based on:

  1. Their ability to cause disease.
  2. Their mode of nutrition.
  3. Differences in their cell envelopes and response to Gram staining.
  4. Their size and shape.

Answer: Differences in their cell envelopes and response to Gram staining.

Explanation: Gram staining is a differential staining technique that classifies bacteria based on the structural differences in their cell walls. Gram-positive bacteria have a thick peptidoglycan layer that retains the crystal violet stain, while Gram-negative bacteria have a thinner peptidoglycan layer and an outer membrane, preventing the retention of the stain.

42. Which technological advancement was crucial for the detailed study of the cell membrane's structure?

  1. The invention of the light microscope.
  2. The development of X-ray crystallography.
  3. The advent of the electron microscope.
  4. The discovery of DNA sequencing.

Answer: The advent of the electron microscope.

Explanation: The cell membrane is an extremely thin structure, beyond the resolution limits of a light microscope. The electron microscope, with its much higher resolving power, became available in the 1950s and allowed scientists to visualize the intricate details of the cell membrane's ultrastructure.

43. Which of the following sets of organelles are considered part of the endomembrane system due to their coordinated functions?

  1. Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes.
  2. Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi complex, lysosomes, and vacuoles.
  3. Ribosomes, nucleus, and cell wall.
  4. Centrioles, flagella, and cilia.

Answer: Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi complex, lysosomes, and vacuoles.

Explanation: The endomembrane system is a group of membranes and organelles in eukaryotic cells that work together to modify, package, and transport lipids and proteins. This system includes the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), Golgi apparatus (complex), lysosomes, and vacuoles. Although they have distinct structures and functions, their activities are interconnected and coordinated.

44. In Amoeba, the contractile vacuole plays a vital role in:

  1. Digestion of food particles.
  2. Movement and locomotion.
  3. Osmoregulation and excretion.
  4. Photosynthesis.

Answer: Osmoregulation and excretion.

Explanation: The contractile vacuole in Amoeba is a specialized organelle that actively pumps excess water out of the cell, preventing it from bursting due to osmosis. This process is crucial for osmoregulation (maintaining water balance) and also aids in the excretion of metabolic wastes.

45. The cytoskeleton, an elaborate network of filamentous proteinaceous structures in the cytoplasm, is primarily composed of:

  1. Cellulose, pectin, and lignin.
  2. DNA, RNA, and proteins.
  3. Microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments.
  4. Phospholipids, cholesterol, and carbohydrates.

Answer: Microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments.

Explanation: The cytoskeleton provides structural support, maintains cell shape, and is involved in cell movement and intracellular transport. It is a dynamic network made up of three main types of protein filaments: microtubules, microfilaments (actin filaments), and intermediate filaments.

46. Chromatin, the material that makes up chromosomes, is chemically composed of:

  1. Only DNA and RNA.
  2. DNA, histones, non-histone proteins, and RNA.
  3. Lipids, carbohydrates, and proteins.
  4. Only proteins and lipids.

Answer: DNA, histones, non-histone proteins, and RNA.

Explanation: Chromatin is a complex of DNA and proteins found inside the nucleus of eukaryotic cells. The DNA is tightly packaged around basic proteins called histones, forming nucleosomes. Additionally, non-histone proteins and RNA are also components of chromatin, playing roles in gene regulation and chromosome structure.

47. Which organelles are characterized as pigment-containing and are exclusively found in plant cells?

  1. Mitochondria.
  2. Ribosomes.
  3. Plastids.
  4. Lysosomes.

Answer: Plastids.

Explanation: Plastids are a diverse group of organelles found in plant cells and some eukaryotic algae. They are responsible for manufacturing and storing food, and often contain pigments. Chloroplasts (containing chlorophyll) are a type of plastid, as are chromoplasts (containing other pigments) and leucoplasts (for storage).

48. Living organisms share all elements with the earth's crust, but show a significantly higher relative abundance of carbon and hydrogen. What is the primary significance of this observation?

  1. It indicates that life forms are entirely distinct from the inorganic components of the earth.
  2. It highlights the unique organization and concentration of carbon and hydrogen to form complex organic molecules essential for life.
  3. It suggests that living organisms absorb carbon and hydrogen directly from the atmosphere.
  4. It implies that all elements found in the earth's crust are equally important for biological processes.

Answer: It highlights the unique organization and concentration of carbon and hydrogen to form complex organic molecules essential for life.

Explanation: The higher relative abundance of carbon and hydrogen in living organisms, despite sharing all elements with the earth's crust, signifies that life uniquely organizes and concentrates these elements to form complex organic molecules (biomolecules). This underscores the carbon-based nature of life and the fundamental role of these elements in building biological structures and functions.

49. What are the fundamental building blocks of nucleic acids (DNA and RNA), and how are these blocks structurally formed?

  1. Amino acids, formed by a central carbon atom bonded to an amino group, a carboxyl group, a hydrogen atom, and a variable side chain.
  2. Monosaccharides, which are simple sugars like glucose and fructose.
  3. Nucleotides, formed by a nitrogen base attached to a sugar, and a phosphate group esterified to that sugar.
  4. Fatty acids, which are long hydrocarbon chains with a carboxyl group at one end.

Answer: Nucleotides, formed by a nitrogen base attached to a sugar, and a phosphate group esterified to that sugar.

Explanation: The fundamental building blocks of nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) are nucleotides. Each nucleotide is structurally formed by a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group. The nitrogen base is attached to the sugar, and a phosphate group is esterified to the sugar.

50. Lipids are found in the acid-insoluble fraction during chemical analysis of tissues, yet they are not strictly considered macromolecules. What is the primary reason for this classification?

  1. Lipids are inorganic compounds, unlike true macromolecules.
  2. Their molecular weight typically does not exceed 800 Da, which is below the macromolecular range.
  3. They are easily soluble in water, preventing their classification as macromolecules.
  4. Lipids are only found in the cell membrane and not in the cytoplasm.

Answer: Their molecular weight typically does not exceed 800 Da, which is below the macromolecular range.

Explanation: Lipids are not strictly considered macromolecules because their molecular weight usually does not exceed 800 Da, which is typically below the macromolecular range (generally considered above 1000 Da). They appear in the acid-insoluble fraction because during tissue grinding, cell membranes break into vesicles that are not water-soluble and thus precipitate with the macromolecular fraction.
