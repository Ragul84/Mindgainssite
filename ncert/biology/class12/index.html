Class12 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class12 questions to revise the NCERT Biology syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 380 questions.

1. Which of the following best explains why reproduction is considered a vital process for the survival of species?

  1. It allows individual organisms to live longer.
  2. It ensures the continuation of life on Earth by replacing dying individuals.
  3. It helps in the evolution and adaptation of species.
  4. It is essential for the growth and development of organisms.

Answer: It ensures the continuation of life on Earth by replacing dying individuals.

Explanation: Reproduction is crucial because, without it, individual organisms die, and species cannot persist over time. It ensures the perpetuation of the species and the continuation of life.

2. In a flower, what does the androecium represent?

  1. The female reproductive organ, consisting of carpels.
  2. The sterile protective leaves, known as sepals.
  3. The male reproductive organ, consisting of a whorl of stamens.
  4. The attractive petals that aid in pollination.

Answer: The male reproductive organ, consisting of a whorl of stamens.

Explanation: The androecium is the collective term for the male reproductive parts of a flower, which are the stamens. Each stamen consists of a filament and an anther.

3. In the context of sexual reproduction in flowering plants, what do pollen grains represent?

  1. The female gametophytes.
  2. The male gametophytes.
  3. The diploid zygote.
  4. The sporophytic generation.

Answer: The male gametophytes.

Explanation: Pollen grains are the structures that contain the male gametes (sperm) and are therefore considered the male gametophytes in flowering plants.

4. Which of the following correctly describes the three main parts of a pistil and their functions?

  1. Stigma (pollen landing), Style (connects stigma to ovary), Ovary (contains ovules).
  2. Sepal (protection), Petal (attraction), Stamen (pollen production).
  3. Filament (supports anther), Anther (produces pollen), Pollen (contains male gametes).
  4. Ovule (contains egg cell), Egg cell (female gamete), Zygote (fertilized egg).

Answer: Stigma (pollen landing), Style (connects stigma to ovary), Ovary (contains ovules).

Explanation: The pistil, also known as the carpel, is the female reproductive unit of a flower. It consists of the stigma (receives pollen), the style (connects stigma to ovary), and the ovary (contains the ovules).

5. A mature angiosperm embryo sac is characterized by which of the following cellular organizations?

  1. 7-nucleate, 8-celled, with two synergids and one egg cell at the chalazal end.
  2. 8-nucleate, 7-celled, with three antipodal cells at the micropylar end.
  3. 8-nucleate, 7-celled, with an egg apparatus at the micropylar end and two polar nuclei in the central cell.
  4. 6-nucleate, 7-celled, with three antipodal cells and two synergids.

Answer: 8-nucleate, 7-celled, with an egg apparatus at the micropylar end and two polar nuclei in the central cell.

Explanation: A mature embryo sac is typically 8-nucleate and 7-celled. It has an egg apparatus (one egg cell and two synergids) at the micropylar end, three antipodal cells at the chalazal end, and a large central cell with two polar nuclei.

6. Which set of adaptations is characteristic of wind-pollinated flowers?

  1. Brightly colored petals, nectar guides, sticky pollen.
  2. Large, showy flowers, strong fragrance, small stigmas.
  3. Light and non-sticky pollen, well-exposed stamens, large feathery stigmas.
  4. Tubular flowers, hidden nectaries, pollen carried by insects.

Answer: Light and non-sticky pollen, well-exposed stamens, large feathery stigmas.

Explanation: Wind-pollinated flowers (anemophilous) have adaptations like light, non-sticky pollen for easy dispersal, well-exposed stamens to release pollen into the wind, and large, often feathery stigmas to efficiently capture airborne pollen.

7. Which of the following is an example of a floral reward that provides a safe place for insects to lay eggs?

  1. The nectar produced by orchids.
  2. The large, colorful petals of roses.
  3. The tallest flower of Amorphophallus.
  4. The sweet fragrance of jasmine.

Answer: The tallest flower of Amorphophallus.

Explanation: The Amorphophallus flower, known for its immense size, offers a unique floral reward by providing a safe breeding ground for insects, where they can lay their eggs.

8. How does the number of male gametes carried by the pollen tube differ in plants shedding pollen at a two-celled vs. a three-celled condition?

  1. In both cases, the pollen tube always carries only one male gamete.
  2. Pollen shed at two-celled condition carries two gametes immediately, while three-celled condition carries one.
  3. In two-celled condition, the generative cell divides to form two male gametes during pollen tube growth; in three-celled, two male gametes are already present.
  4. Pollen shed at three-celled condition never forms a pollen tube, while two-celled condition always forms two pollen tubes.

Answer: In two-celled condition, the generative cell divides to form two male gametes during pollen tube growth; in three-celled, two male gametes are already present.

Explanation: When pollen is shed at the two-celled stage (vegetative cell and generative cell), the generative cell divides into two male gametes during pollen tube growth. If pollen is shed at the three-celled stage (vegetative cell and two male gametes), the pollen tube already contains the two male gametes.

9. Which of the following statements accurately describes double fertilisation and its significance in flowering plants?

  1. It involves the fusion of two male gametes with two egg cells, leading to two zygotes.
  2. It is the simultaneous occurrence of syngamy and triple fusion, unique to angiosperms.
  3. It refers to the fusion of one male gamete with the egg cell and another with the antipodal cells.
  4. It is a process where two pollen grains fertilise a single ovule, forming a diploid embryo and a diploid endosperm.

Answer: It is the simultaneous occurrence of syngamy and triple fusion, unique to angiosperms.

Explanation: Double fertilisation is a characteristic feature of flowering plants (angiosperms). It involves two distinct fusion events: syngamy (fusion of one male gamete with the egg cell to form a diploid zygote) and triple fusion (fusion of the other male gamete with the two polar nuclei to form a triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus, PEN). This simultaneous occurrence makes it unique to this group.

10. Which of the following pairs correctly differentiates between non-albuminous and albuminous seeds?

  1. Non-albuminous seeds retain endosperm (e.g., wheat); Albuminous seeds consume endosperm (e.g., pea).
  2. Non-albuminous seeds have no residual endosperm (e.g., pea); Albuminous seeds retain endosperm (e.g., wheat).
  3. Non-albuminous seeds are monocotyledonous (e.g., maize); Albuminous seeds are dicotyledonous (e.g., groundnut).
  4. Non-albuminous seeds have a large embryo (e.g., castor); Albuminous seeds have a small embryo (e.g., barley).

Answer: Non-albuminous seeds have no residual endosperm (e.g., pea); Albuminous seeds retain endosperm (e.g., wheat).

Explanation: Non-albuminous (or ex-albuminous) seeds, such as pea and groundnut, have their endosperm completely consumed during embryo development, leaving no residual endosperm. Albuminous seeds, like wheat, maize, barley, and castor, retain a part of the endosperm as it is not fully utilized during embryo development.

11. What is the estimated record dormancy period for the oldest known viable seed, and to which plant species does it belong?

  1. 2,000 years, Phoenix dactylifera
  2. 5,000 years, Nelumbo nucifera
  3. 10,000 years, Lupinus arcticus
  4. 1,000 years, Papaver somniferum

Answer: 10,000 years, Lupinus arcticus

Explanation: The oldest known viable seed belongs to Lupinus arcticus (lupine). It was excavated from the Arctic Tundra and germinated and flowered after an estimated record of 10,000 years of dormancy.

12. A mature embryo sac is typically characterized by which of the following cellular and nuclear counts?

  1. 6-celled, 6-nucleate
  2. 7-celled, 8-nucleate
  3. 8-celled, 7-nucleate
  4. 7-celled, 7-nucleate

Answer: 7-celled, 8-nucleate

Explanation: A mature embryo sac, after meiosis and subsequent mitotic divisions, typically consists of 7 cells (one egg cell, two synergids, three antipodal cells, and one large central cell) and 8 nuclei (one in the egg cell, one in each synergid, one in each antipodal cell, and two polar nuclei in the central cell).

13. Why does self-pollination not result in seed formation in self-incompatible species?

  1. The pollen grains are genetically identical to the ovules, preventing fusion.
  2. A genetic mechanism inhibits pollen germination or pollen tube growth on the same plant's pistil.
  3. The stigma of self-incompatible species is physically unable to receive self-pollen.
  4. Self-pollination leads to the production of sterile seeds in these species.

Answer: A genetic mechanism inhibits pollen germination or pollen tube growth on the same plant's pistil.

Explanation: In self-incompatible species, a genetic mechanism prevents self-pollen from fertilizing the ovules of the same flower or other flowers on the same plant. This mechanism typically involves the inhibition of pollen germination on the stigma or the arrest of pollen tube growth within the pistil, thereby promoting cross-pollination and genetic diversity.

14. Which two types of cells line the inside of the seminiferous tubules, and what are their primary functions?

  1. Leydig cells (produce testosterone) and Sertoli cells (provide nutrition).
  2. Spermatogonia (undergo meiosis) and Interstitial cells (support sperm).
  3. Male germ cells (form sperm) and Sertoli cells (nourish germ cells).
  4. Epididymal cells (store sperm) and Spermatocytes (mature into sperm).

Answer: Male germ cells (form sperm) and Sertoli cells (nourish germ cells).

Explanation: The seminiferous tubules are lined by two main cell types: male germ cells (spermatogonia), which undergo meiotic divisions to form sperm, and Sertoli cells, which provide essential nutrition and support to the developing germ cells.

15. Which sequence correctly describes the parts of the oviduct from the ovary towards the uterus?

  1. Ampulla → Infundibulum → Isthmus
  2. Isthmus → Ampulla → Infundibulum
  3. Infundibulum → Ampulla → Isthmus
  4. Infundibulum → Isthmus → Ampulla

Answer: Infundibulum → Ampulla → Isthmus

Explanation: The oviduct (fallopian tube) consists of three parts. The infundibulum is the funnel-shaped part closest to the ovary. It opens into the wider ampulla. The last part, the isthmus, has a narrow lumen and directly joins the uterus.

16. What are the specific functions of Luteinising Hormone (LH) and Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) in male spermatogenesis?

  1. LH stimulates Sertoli cells; FSH stimulates Leydig cells.
  2. LH stimulates Leydig cells to secrete androgens; FSH stimulates Sertoli cells to secrete factors for spermiogenesis.
  3. LH directly stimulates sperm maturation; FSH promotes testosterone production.
  4. LH initiates meiosis in spermatogonia; FSH maintains the blood-testis barrier.

Answer: LH stimulates Leydig cells to secrete androgens; FSH stimulates Sertoli cells to secrete factors for spermiogenesis.

Explanation: In males, Luteinising Hormone (LH) acts on the Leydig cells, stimulating them to synthesize and secrete androgens, which are crucial for spermatogenesis. Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) acts on the Sertoli cells, stimulating the secretion of various factors that aid in the process of spermiogenesis (the maturation of spermatids into spermatozoa).

17. Which of the following statements accurately describes the menstrual cycle in human females?

  1. It is the reproductive cycle in all female mammals, beginning at birth and ceasing at menopause.
  2. It is the reproductive cycle in female primates, starting at puberty (menarche) and ending around 50 years of age (menopause).
  3. It is a continuous reproductive process in human females, regulated by male hormones.
  4. It is a cycle that begins at menarche and continues indefinitely throughout a female's life.

Answer: It is the reproductive cycle in female primates, starting at puberty (menarche) and ending around 50 years of age (menopause).

Explanation: The menstrual cycle is the reproductive cycle specific to female primates, including humans. It commences at puberty with the first menstruation, known as menarche, and typically concludes around the age of 50, an event termed menopause.

18. What event triggers the completion of the meiotic division of the secondary oocyte, and what are the resulting structures?

  1. The fusion of sperm and ovum nuclei, forming a zygote and a polar body.
  2. The entry of the sperm into the cytoplasm of the ovum, leading to a haploid ovum and a second polar body.
  3. The release of the secondary oocyte from the ovary, forming a first polar body and an ovum.
  4. The implantation of the embryo, resulting in an ootid and a polar body.

Answer: The entry of the sperm into the cytoplasm of the ovum, leading to a haploid ovum and a second polar body.

Explanation: The entry of the sperm into the cytoplasm of the ovum is the crucial event that induces the secondary oocyte to complete its meiotic division. This unequal division produces a haploid ovum (ootid) and a second polar body.

19. Immediately after implantation, how does the inner cell mass differentiate?

  1. It differentiates into trophoblast and inner cell mass.
  2. It forms an outer ectoderm and an inner endoderm, with mesoderm appearing later between them.
  3. It develops directly into the three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm simultaneously.
  4. It differentiates into the placenta and umbilical cord.

Answer: It forms an outer ectoderm and an inner endoderm, with mesoderm appearing later between them.

Explanation: Immediately following implantation, the inner cell mass (which forms the embryo) differentiates into two primary layers: an outer ectoderm and an inner endoderm. The third germ layer, the mesoderm, subsequently develops between these two layers.

20. When does the menstrual cycle typically begin in females, and what primarily induces the cyclical changes in the ovary and uterus?

  1. It begins at birth, and changes are induced by environmental factors.
  2. It starts after sexual maturation (puberty), and changes are induced by pituitary and ovarian hormones.
  3. It begins during childhood, and changes are induced by uterine contractions.
  4. It starts at menopause, and changes are induced by adrenal hormones.

Answer: It starts after sexual maturation (puberty), and changes are induced by pituitary and ovarian hormones.

Explanation: The menstrual cycle commences only after a female attains sexual maturation, a stage known as puberty. The cyclical changes observed in both the ovary and the uterus throughout this cycle are primarily regulated and induced by fluctuating levels of pituitary and ovarian hormones.

21. Which of the following hormones are primarily involved in inducing and completing parturition in humans?

  1. Progesterone and Estrogen
  2. Oxytocin, Estrogens, and Relaxin
  3. FSH and LH
  4. Prolactin and Growth Hormone

Answer: Oxytocin, Estrogens, and Relaxin

Explanation: Parturition is a complex process involving multiple hormones. Signals from the fully developed fetus and placenta initiate the 'foetal ejection reflex', triggering oxytocin release from the maternal pituitary, which causes strong uterine contractions. Estrogens from the placenta also increase uterine contractility, while relaxin helps in relaxing pelvic ligaments.

22. Which of the following groups are primarily responsible for disseminating information about reproduction-related aspects to create awareness among people?

  1. Only governmental agencies through print media.
  2. Parents, teachers, and friends, along with governmental and non-governmental agencies using various media.
  3. Only non-governmental organizations through audio-visual media.
  4. Medical professionals exclusively through clinical consultations.

Answer: Parents, teachers, and friends, along with governmental and non-governmental agencies using various media.

Explanation: Awareness about reproduction-related aspects is created through a multi-pronged approach. Governmental and non-governmental agencies utilize audio-visual and print media. Additionally, individuals such as parents, other close relatives, teachers, and friends play a significant role in disseminating this crucial information.

23. What was India's approximate population at the time of independence, and what was its approximate population in May 2011?

  1. 350 million at independence; 1 billion in May 2011.
  2. 350 million at independence; 1.2 billion in May 2011.
  3. 500 million at independence; 1.2 billion in May 2011.
  4. 1 billion at independence; 1.5 billion in May 2011.

Answer: 350 million at independence; 1.2 billion in May 2011.

Explanation: At the time of India's independence, its population was approximately 350 million. By May 2011, the population had significantly increased, crossing the 1.2 billion mark.

24. Which of the following correctly categorizes the three main types of Intra Uterine Devices (IUDs) with an appropriate example for each?

  1. Non-medicated (e.g., CuT), Copper-releasing (e.g., Lippes loop), Hormone-releasing (e.g., Progestasert).
  2. Non-medicated (e.g., Lippes loop), Copper-releasing (e.g., CuT), Hormone-releasing (e.g., Progestasert).
  3. Non-medicated (e.g., Progestasert), Copper-releasing (e.g., Lippes loop), Hormone-releasing (e.g., CuT).
  4. Non-medicated (e.g., LNG-20), Copper-releasing (e.g., Multiload 375), Hormone-releasing (e.g., Lippes loop).

Answer: Non-medicated (e.g., Lippes loop), Copper-releasing (e.g., CuT), Hormone-releasing (e.g., Progestasert).

Explanation: The three main categories of IUDs are: Non-medicated IUDs, exemplified by Lippes loop; Copper-releasing IUDs, such as CuT, Cu7, or Multiload 375; and Hormone-releasing IUDs, including Progestasert or LNG-20.

25. Which of the following is NOT explicitly mentioned as a potential ill-effect or side effect associated with the widespread use of contraceptive methods?

  1. Nausea
  2. Abdominal pain
  3. Infertility
  4. Irregular menstrual bleeding

Answer: Infertility

Explanation: The text mentions nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular menstrual bleeding, and even breast cancer as potential ill-effects. Infertility is not listed as a direct side effect of contraceptive methods in the provided text.
