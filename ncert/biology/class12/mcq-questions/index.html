26. Which of the following is NOT one of the three simple principles recommended to prevent Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)?

  1. Avoiding sex with unknown or multiple partners
  2. Always trying to use condoms during coitus
  3. Regular blood tests for early detection
  4. Consulting a qualified doctor for early detection and treatment if diagnosed

Answer: Regular blood tests for early detection

Explanation: The three principles mentioned are: (i) Avoid sex with unknown or multiple partners, (ii) Always try to use condoms during coitus, and (iii) In case of doubt, consult a qualified doctor for early detection and complete treatment. Regular blood tests are a good practice but not one of the three specific principles listed in the text.

27. Which of the following is a primary deterrent to the widespread adoption of advanced Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ARTs) in India,?

  1. Lack of public awareness
  2. High success rates
  3. Affordability only to a limited number of people
  4. Ethical concerns from the medical community

Answer: Affordability only to a limited number of people

Explanation: The text explicitly states that 'affordability only to a limited number of people' is a primary deterrent, along with expensive instrumentation, limited availability, and emotional, religious, and social factors.

28. A couple is generally considered infertile if they are unable to conceive or produce children after how many years of unprotected sexual cohabitation?

  1. 6 months
  2. 1 year
  3. 2 years
  4. 3 years

Answer: 2 years

Explanation: The text defines infertility as the inability to conceive or produce children even after 2 years of unprotected sexual cohabitation.

29. The branch of biology that scientifically deals with the inheritance and variation of characters from parents to offspring is known as:

  1. Ecology
  2. Genetics
  3. Evolution
  4. Physiology

Answer: Genetics

Explanation: The definition provided in the text directly corresponds to the field of Genetics.

30. What surprising observation did Mendel make in the F2 generation after self-pollinating the F1 tall pea plants?

  1. All F2 plants were tall.
  2. All F2 plants were dwarf.
  3. Some F2 plants were dwarf, reappearing after being absent in F1.
  4. The F2 generation showed a new intermediate height.

Answer: Some F2 plants were dwarf, reappearing after being absent in F1.

Explanation: Mendel was surprised to find that in the F2 generation, some of the offspring were 'dwarf', a character that was absent in the F1 generation, and reappeared. He observed 1/4th dwarf and 3/4th tall plants.

31. How are alleles distributed into gametes in a true-breeding dwarf (tt) pea plant?

  1. All gametes carry the allele 'T'.
  2. All gametes carry the allele 't'.
  3. Half the gametes carry 'T' and half carry 't'.
  4. No gametes carry any alleles.

Answer: All gametes carry the allele 't'.

Explanation: The text states that in true-breeding dwarf (tt) plants, all gametes carry the allele 't'. Similarly, in true-breeding tall (TT) plants, all gametes carry 'T'.

32. Based on his observations from monohybrid crosses, Mendel proposed two general rules, which are now known as the Principles or Laws of Inheritance. These are:

  1. Law of Independent Assortment and Law of Linkage
  2. Law of Dominance and Law of Segregation
  3. Law of Purity of Gametes and Law of Incomplete Dominance
  4. Law of Multiple Alleles and Law of Codominance

Answer: Law of Dominance and Law of Segregation

Explanation: The text explicitly states that Mendel proposed the First Law or Law of Dominance, and the Second Law or Law of Segregation based on his monohybrid cross observations.

33. Which of the following best explains how a modified allele can lead to a recessive phenotype?

  1. The modified allele produces a non-functional enzyme or no enzyme, making the original phenotype dependent on the unmodified (dominant) allele.
  2. The modified allele always produces a dominant trait, masking the effect of the unmodified allele.
  3. A modified allele causes the blending of traits, resulting in an intermediate phenotype.
  4. Recessive phenotypes only occur when both alleles are modified and non-functional.

Answer: The modified allele produces a non-functional enzyme or no enzyme, making the original phenotype dependent on the unmodified (dominant) allele.

Explanation: A recessive phenotype arises when a modified allele fails to produce a functional enzyme or produces no enzyme at all. In such cases, the expression of the original phenotype relies entirely on the presence and function of the unmodified, dominant allele. If the dominant allele is absent or non-functional, the recessive trait manifests.

34. In Mendel's dihybrid cross involving pea seed colour and shape, what were the characteristics of the F1 generation when yellow-round seeds were crossed with green-wrinkled seeds?

  1. All F1 plants produced green-wrinkled seeds.
  2. All F1 plants produced yellow-round seeds.
  3. F1 plants produced a mix of yellow-round and green-wrinkled seeds in a 1:1 ratio.
  4. F1 plants produced yellow-wrinkled and green-round seeds in equal proportions.

Answer: All F1 plants produced yellow-round seeds.

Explanation: Mendel crossed pea plants with dominant traits (yellow-coloured and round-shaped seeds) with plants having recessive traits (green-coloured and wrinkled-shaped seeds). The F1 generation uniformly displayed the dominant traits, meaning all seeds were yellow and round, demonstrating the dominance of these alleles.

35. Which of the following was NOT a reason for Mendel's work remaining unrecognised until 1900?

  1. Lack of easy communication to publicise his work widely.
  2. His concept of discrete, non-blending 'factors' contradicted the prevailing idea of continuous variation.
  3. His use of mathematical logic to explain biological phenomena was not accepted by many biologists.
  4. He failed to conduct experiments on a large enough scale to validate his findings.

Answer: He failed to conduct experiments on a large enough scale to validate his findings.

Explanation: Mendel's work was indeed extensive and well-documented. The primary reasons for its lack of recognition were poor communication, the revolutionary nature of his 'discrete unit' concept against the blending inheritance theory, the unconventional application of mathematics to biology, and the inability to provide physical evidence for his 'factors'.

36. In the context of Morgan's experiments, what does 'recombination' refer to?

  1. The fusion of two different gametes to form a zygote.
  2. The generation of non-parental gene combinations during inheritance.
  3. The process of DNA replication and repair.
  4. The segregation of alleles during gamete formation.

Answer: The generation of non-parental gene combinations during inheritance.

Explanation: Morgan's experiments on fruit flies led to the understanding of recombination, which is the process that results in offspring with combinations of traits different from those found in either parent. This occurs due to the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during meiosis, leading to new combinations of alleles on a chromosome.

37. According to the concept of polygenic inheritance, what primarily determines the darkness or lightness of skin colour in an individual?

  1. The presence of a single dominant gene for skin colour.
  2. Environmental factors like sun exposure.
  3. The number of each type of dominant and recessive alleles in the genotype.
  4. The sex of the individual.

Answer: The number of each type of dominant and recessive alleles in the genotype.

Explanation: Polygenic inheritance involves multiple genes contributing to a single trait. In the case of skin colour, the darkness or lightness is determined by the cumulative effect of several genes, specifically the proportion of dominant and recessive alleles present in an individual's genotype. More dominant alleles generally lead to darker skin, while more recessive alleles lead to lighter skin.

38. During spermatogenesis in human males, what types of gametes (sperm) are produced concerning sex chromosomes, and in what proportions?

  1. All sperm carry an X-chromosome.
  2. All sperm carry a Y-chromosome.
  3. 50% of sperm carry an X-chromosome and 50% carry a Y-chromosome.
  4. 75% of sperm carry an X-chromosome and 25% carry a Y-chromosome.

Answer: 50% of sperm carry an X-chromosome and 50% carry a Y-chromosome.

Explanation: Human males have XY sex chromosomes. During meiosis in spermatogenesis, the X and Y chromosomes segregate. Therefore, half of the sperm produced will receive an X-chromosome, and the other half will receive a Y-chromosome, resulting in a 50:50 proportion.

39. What are mutagens, and which of the following is an example of a mutagen?

  1. Substances that prevent mutations; DNA polymerase.
  2. Chemical and physical factors that induce mutations; UV radiation.
  3. Enzymes that repair DNA damage; Ligase.
  4. Proteins that regulate gene expression; Transcription factors.

Answer: Chemical and physical factors that induce mutations; UV radiation.

Explanation: Mutagens are agents that can cause a change in the genetic material (DNA) of an organism. UV radiation is a well-known physical mutagen that can cause DNA damage, leading to mutations.

40. Which genotype represents an individual who is a carrier for Sickle-cell Anaemia but does not show the diseased phenotype?

  1. HbAHbA
  2. HbAHbS
  3. HbSHbS
  4. HbA HbA HbS

Answer: HbAHbS

Explanation: Sickle-cell anaemia is an autosome-linked recessive trait. Individuals with the genotype HbAHbS are heterozygous; they carry one normal allele (HbA) and one sickle-cell allele (HbS). While they possess the sickle-cell trait, they are generally unaffected by the disease and are considered carriers.

41. Which of the following best distinguishes a chromosomal disorder from a gene disorder?

  1. Chromosomal disorders involve mutations in single genes, while gene disorders involve changes in chromosome number or structure.
  2. Chromosomal disorders are caused by the absence, excess, or abnormal arrangement of one or more chromosomes, whereas gene disorders result from mutations within single genes.
  3. Gene disorders are always inherited, while chromosomal disorders are always spontaneous.
  4. Chromosomal disorders affect only somatic cells, while gene disorders affect germ cells.

Answer: Chromosomal disorders are caused by the absence, excess, or abnormal arrangement of one or more chromosomes, whereas gene disorders result from mutations within single genes.

Explanation: Chromosomal disorders are characterized by alterations in the number or structure of chromosomes, such as trisomy or deletions. Gene disorders, on the other hand, are caused by specific mutations (changes in the DNA sequence) within a single gene. Option A incorrectly swaps the definitions. Options C and D are incorrect generalizations.

42. What is the primary function of a Punnett Square in genetics?

  1. To determine the exact genotype of an unknown individual.
  2. To calculate the mutation rate of a specific gene.
  3. To theoretically represent all possible combinations of gametes and their resulting offspring genotypes from a genetic cross.
  4. To visualize the physical location of genes on a chromosome.

Answer: To theoretically represent all possible combinations of gametes and their resulting offspring genotypes from a genetic cross.

Explanation: A Punnett Square is a diagram used to predict the outcome of a genetic cross. It systematically lists all possible gametes from each parent and combines them to show the potential genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring. It does not determine exact genotypes of unknown individuals (that's done by test crosses), calculate mutation rates, or visualize gene locations.

43. What is the genetic basis for Down's syndrome?

  1. Monosomy of chromosome 21
  2. Trisomy of chromosome 18
  3. Trisomy of chromosome 21
  4. Deletion on the short arm of chromosome 5

Answer: Trisomy of chromosome 21

Explanation: Down's syndrome, also known as Trisomy 21, is a chromosomal disorder caused by the presence of an extra copy of chromosome 21. This means individuals with Down's syndrome have three copies of chromosome 21 instead of the usual two. Monosomy of chromosome 21 is lethal, Trisomy 18 causes Edward's syndrome, and deletion on chromosome 5 causes Cri-du-chat syndrome.

44. Which major scientific realization regarding genetic material emerged over the hundred years following Mendel's work?

  1. Proteins are the primary genetic material.
  2. RNA is the sole genetic material in all organisms.
  3. DNA is the genetic material for the majority of organisms.
  4. Carbohydrates play a crucial role in genetic inheritance.

Answer: DNA is the genetic material for the majority of organisms.

Explanation: Following Mendel's groundbreaking work on inheritance, extensive research over the next century led to the definitive conclusion that DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) serves as the genetic material in most living organisms. While RNA is the genetic material in some viruses, and proteins are essential for gene expression, DNA holds the blueprint for life.

45. If one strand of a DNA molecule has the polarity 5'→3', what will be the polarity of its complementary strand?

  1. 5'→3'
  2. 3'→5'
  3. It can be either 5'→3' or 3'→5'
  4. The polarity is irrelevant for DNA function.

Answer: 3'→5'

Explanation: DNA strands are antiparallel. This means that if one strand runs in the 5' to 3' direction, its complementary strand must run in the opposite, or 3' to 5' direction. This antiparallel arrangement is crucial for DNA replication and transcription.

46. Given that the length of E. coli DNA is 1.36 mm and the average distance between two consecutive base pairs is 0.34 nm, calculate the approximate number of base pairs in E. coli.

  1. 4 x 10^5 base pairs
  2. 4 x 10^6 base pairs
  3. 4 x 10^7 base pairs
  4. 4 x 10^8 base pairs

Answer: 4 x 10^6 base pairs

Explanation: First, convert all units to meters: Length of E. coli DNA = 1.36 mm = 1.36 × 10^-3 meters. Distance between two consecutive base pairs = 0.34 nm = 0.34 × 10^-9 meters. Number of base pairs = (Total length of DNA) / (Distance between two consecutive base pairs) = (1.36 × 10^-3 m) / (0.34 × 10^-9 m) = 4 × 10^6 base pairs. Therefore, E. coli contains approximately 4 million base pairs.

47. Who conducted the landmark experiment in 1952 that unequivocally proved DNA to be the genetic material?

  1. Frederick Griffith
  2. Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, and Maclyn McCarty
  3. Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase
  4. James Watson and Francis Crick

Answer: Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase

Explanation: Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase, in their 1952 experiment using bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria), demonstrated that DNA, not protein, is the genetic material. They used radioactive isotopes to label DNA and protein separately and observed which molecule entered the bacterial cells to direct the synthesis of new viruses. Griffith's experiment showed transformation, Avery et al. identified the transforming principle as DNA, and Watson and Crick elucidated the structure of DNA.

48. How does RNA directly express characters, and what is DNA's dependence in this process?

  1. DNA directly codes for proteins, while RNA is a byproduct.
  2. RNA can directly code for protein synthesis, thereby expressing characters, whereas DNA relies on RNA for protein synthesis.
  3. Both DNA and RNA directly synthesize proteins independently.
  4. RNA only stores genetic information, and DNA is responsible for all protein synthesis.

Answer: RNA can directly code for protein synthesis, thereby expressing characters, whereas DNA relies on RNA for protein synthesis.

Explanation: RNA plays a direct role in gene expression by coding for the synthesis of proteins (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA are all involved in this process). These proteins then carry out various functions, leading to the expression of characters. DNA, while containing the genetic blueprint, is dependent on RNA (through transcription and translation) to synthesize proteins. The entire protein-synthesizing machinery is built around RNA molecules.

49. In the Meselson-Stahl experiment, E. coli was grown in a heavy nitrogen medium and then transferred to a light nitrogen medium. If the generation time for E. coli is 40 minutes, what would be the proportion of hybrid DNA and light DNA molecules after 80 minutes?

  1. 100% hybrid DNA
  2. 75% hybrid DNA, 25% light DNA
  3. 50% hybrid DNA, 50% light DNA
  4. 25% hybrid DNA, 75% light DNA

Answer: 50% hybrid DNA, 50% light DNA

Explanation: After 40 minutes (one generation) in the light medium, all DNA molecules would be hybrid (one heavy strand, one light strand). After another 40 minutes (total 80 minutes, or two generations), each hybrid molecule would replicate. This would result in two hybrid molecules and two light molecules (both strands light). Therefore, the proportion would be 50% hybrid DNA and 50% light DNA.

50. Which of the following is a consequence if both strands of DNA were simultaneously transcribed?

  1. Increased efficiency of protein synthesis
  2. Formation of double-stranded RNA, preventing translation
  3. Production of identical protein sequences from both strands
  4. Enhanced stability of mRNA molecules

Answer: Formation of double-stranded RNA, preventing translation

Explanation: If both DNA strands were transcribed simultaneously, the two resulting RNA molecules would be complementary to each other. These complementary RNA molecules would then bind to form a double-stranded RNA structure. This double-stranded RNA cannot be translated into protein, rendering the entire transcription process unproductive and futile.
