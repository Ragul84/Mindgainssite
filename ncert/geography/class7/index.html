Class7 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class7 questions to revise the NCERT Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which of the following is NOT a component of the natural environment?

  1. Land
  2. Air
  3. Buildings
  4. Water

Answer: Buildings

Explanation: The natural environment consists of all living and non-living things that occur naturally on Earth. Land, air, and water are all natural components. Buildings are man-made structures and thus part of the human-made environment, not the natural environment.

2. The innermost layer of the Earth is known as the:

  1. Crust
  2. Mantle
  3. Core
  4. Lithosphere

Answer: Core

Explanation: The Earth is made up of three main layers: the crust (outermost), the mantle (middle layer), and the core (innermost layer). The lithosphere includes the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle.

3. Which type of force is responsible for the formation of mountains and earthquakes?

  1. Exogenic forces
  2. Endogenic forces
  3. Gravitational forces
  4. Magnetic forces

Answer: Endogenic forces

Explanation: Endogenic forces are internal forces that originate inside the Earth and cause movements like earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and mountain building. Exogenic forces work on the surface of the Earth, like erosion and deposition.

4. Which gas is present in the atmosphere in the largest amount?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Nitrogen
  4. Argon

Answer: Nitrogen

Explanation: Nitrogen makes up approximately 78% of the Earth's atmosphere, making it the most abundant gas. Oxygen is about 21%, and other gases like carbon dioxide and argon are present in much smaller quantities.

5. The process by which water continuously changes its form and circulates between oceans, atmosphere, and land is called the:

  1. Carbon cycle
  2. Nitrogen cycle
  3. Water cycle
  4. Rock cycle

Answer: Water cycle

Explanation: The water cycle (also known as the hydrological cycle) describes the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It involves processes like evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff.

6. Slash and burn agriculture is also known as:

  1. Commercial farming
  2. Plantation agriculture
  3. Shifting cultivation
  4. Intensive subsistence farming

Answer: Shifting cultivation

Explanation: Slash and burn agriculture is a traditional farming method where forests are cut down and burned to clear land for cultivation. After a few years, the land loses its fertility, and farmers move to a new area. This practice is also known as shifting cultivation.

7. Which of the following is a cold desert?

  1. Sahara Desert
  2. Thar Desert
  3. Ladakh Desert
  4. Arabian Desert

Answer: Ladakh Desert

Explanation: The Ladakh Desert is a cold desert located in the Great Himalayas. The Sahara, Thar, and Arabian Deserts are all examples of hot deserts, characterized by high temperatures and very little rainfall.

8. The process by which water continuously changes its form and circulates between oceans, atmosphere, and land is known as the:

  1. Carbon Cycle
  2. Nitrogen Cycle
  3. Water Cycle
  4. Rock Cycle

Answer: Water Cycle

Explanation: The water cycle (also known as the hydrological cycle) describes the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It involves processes like evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff.

9. Shifting cultivation is also known as 'Jhum' cultivation in which part of India?

  1. Southern India
  2. Western India
  3. North-Eastern India
  4. Central India

Answer: North-Eastern India

Explanation: Shifting cultivation, where a patch of land is cleared, cultivated for a few years, and then abandoned for new land, is widely practiced in the North-Eastern states of India and is locally known as 'Jhum' cultivation.

10. What is the main reason for the sparse vegetation in deserts?

  1. High humidity
  2. Abundant rainfall
  3. Scarcity of water
  4. Fertile soil

Answer: Scarcity of water

Explanation: Deserts are defined by their extreme aridity and very low rainfall. The scarcity of water is the primary factor limiting plant growth and leading to sparse vegetation in desert regions.

11. The uppermost layer of the Earth's surface is called the:

  1. Core
  2. Mantle
  3. Crust
  4. Asthenosphere

Answer: Crust

Explanation: The Earth's interior is divided into three main layers: the crust, mantle, and core. The crust is the outermost solid layer, which is the thinnest of all layers.

12. Sudden vibrations of the Earth's crust are known as:

  1. Volcanoes
  2. Earthquakes
  3. Mountains
  4. Plateaus

Answer: Earthquakes

Explanation: Earthquakes are sudden shaking or trembling of the Earth's crust, caused by the movement of tectonic plates. Volcanoes are openings in the Earth's crust through which molten rock, ash, and gases erupt.

13. Which gas is most abundant in the atmosphere?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Carbon Dioxide
  3. Nitrogen
  4. Argon

Answer: Nitrogen

Explanation: Nitrogen makes up approximately 78% of the Earth's atmosphere, making it the most abundant gas. Oxygen is the second most abundant, at about 21%.

14. The process by which water continually changes its form and circulates between oceans, atmosphere, and land is called the:

  1. Carbon Cycle
  2. Nitrogen Cycle
  3. Water Cycle
  4. Rock Cycle

Answer: Water Cycle

Explanation: The water cycle, also known as the hydrological cycle, describes the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It involves processes like evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff.

15. Shifting cultivation is a practice commonly associated with which type of human environment interaction?

  1. Temperate grasslands
  2. Polar regions
  3. Tropical rainforests
  4. Mediterranean climate

Answer: Tropical rainforests

Explanation: Shifting cultivation, also known as 'slash and burn' agriculture, is a traditional farming method often practiced by indigenous communities in tropical rainforest regions. It involves clearing a patch of forest, cultivating it for a few years, and then moving to a new area once the soil fertility declines.

16. What is the primary reason for the sparse vegetation in desert regions?

  1. High humidity
  2. Abundant rainfall
  3. Scarcity of water
  4. Fertile soil

Answer: Scarcity of water

Explanation: Deserts are defined by their extreme aridity, meaning a severe scarcity of water. This lack of moisture makes it very difficult for most plants to grow, leading to sparse vegetation. High humidity and abundant rainfall are characteristic of non-desert regions, and desert soils are often not fertile due to lack of organic matter and water.

17. Which type of force causes earthquakes and volcanoes?

  1. Exogenic forces
  2. Endogenic forces
  3. Gravitational forces
  4. Magnetic forces

Answer: Endogenic forces

Explanation: Forces that act in the interior of the Earth are called endogenic forces. These forces can cause sudden movements like earthquakes and volcanoes, or slow movements that lead to mountain building. Exogenic forces, on the other hand, act on the surface of the Earth (e.g., erosion by wind, water).

18. Shifting cultivation is a practice commonly found in which type of human environment interaction?

  1. Pastoralism
  2. Commercial farming
  3. Subsistence farming
  4. Industrialization

Answer: Subsistence farming

Explanation: Shifting cultivation (or 'slash and burn') is a traditional agricultural practice where a patch of land is cleared, cultivated for a few years until the soil loses its fertility, and then abandoned for a new patch. This is a form of subsistence farming, where farmers grow food primarily for their own consumption rather than for sale.

19. Which type of force is responsible for sudden movements like earthquakes and volcanoes?

  1. Exogenic forces
  2. Endogenic forces
  3. Gravitational forces
  4. Erosional forces

Answer: Endogenic forces

Explanation: Endogenic forces are forces that originate in the interior of the Earth and cause sudden movements like earthquakes and volcanoes, as well as slow movements like mountain building. Exogenic forces work on the surface of the Earth, such as weathering and erosion.

20. What is the main occupation of people living in the hot deserts?

  1. Fishing
  2. Agriculture (intensive)
  3. Nomadic herding and some oasis farming
  4. Industrial manufacturing

Answer: Nomadic herding and some oasis farming

Explanation: In hot deserts, due to scarce water and harsh conditions, intensive agriculture is not possible. People often engage in nomadic herding, moving with their livestock in search of pasture and water. Farming is limited to oases where water is available.

21. Which layer of the Earth is the thinnest?

  1. Core
  2. Mantle
  3. Crust
  4. Outer Core

Answer: Crust

Explanation: The Earth's crust is the outermost and thinnest layer, ranging from about 5 km on the ocean floor to 35 km on the continental masses. The mantle and core are much thicker layers beneath the crust.

22. What is the sudden shaking of the Earth's crust called?

  1. Volcano
  2. Earthquake
  3. Tsunami
  4. Landslide

Answer: Earthquake

Explanation: An earthquake is a sudden shaking or trembling of the Earth's crust, caused by the movement of tectonic plates. A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust, a tsunami is a large ocean wave, and a landslide is the movement of rock, earth, or debris down a slope.

23. What is the process by which water continuously changes its form and circulates between oceans, atmosphere, and land?

  1. Evaporation
  2. Condensation
  3. Precipitation
  4. Water Cycle

Answer: Water Cycle

Explanation: The water cycle (or hydrological cycle) is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It involves processes like evaporation, condensation, and precipitation, but the overall continuous circulation is called the water cycle.

24. Which type of human settlement is typically found in rural areas where people are engaged in activities like agriculture?

  1. Urban settlement
  2. Permanent settlement
  3. Rural settlement
  4. Temporary settlement

Answer: Rural settlement

Explanation: Rural settlements are typically found in the countryside, characterized by a lower population density and people primarily engaged in primary activities like agriculture, fishing, forestry, and crafts. Urban settlements are cities and towns.

25. Shifting cultivation is a common practice in which type of human environment interaction region?

  1. Temperate grasslands
  2. Polar regions
  3. Tropical rainforests
  4. Mediterranean climate

Answer: Tropical rainforests

Explanation: Shifting cultivation (also known as 'slash and burn' agriculture) is a traditional farming method often practiced by tribal communities in tropical rainforest regions. The dense vegetation and fertile but quickly depleted soil make this method suitable for these areas, where land is cleared, cultivated for a few years, and then left to regenerate.
