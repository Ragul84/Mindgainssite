26. What is the primary reason for the scarcity of vegetation in deserts?

  1. Excessive rainfall
  2. High humidity
  3. Lack of water
  4. Fertile soil

Answer: Lack of water

Explanation: Deserts are defined by their extreme aridity, meaning they receive very little precipitation. This severe lack of water is the primary limiting factor for plant growth, leading to sparse and specialized vegetation adapted to dry conditions. Excessive rainfall, high humidity, and fertile soil would generally promote vegetation, not limit it.

27. Which of the following landforms is created by the erosional and depositional work of a river?

  1. Volcano
  2. Earthquake
  3. Meander
  4. Mountain

Answer: Meander

Explanation: Volcanoes and earthquakes are caused by internal forces of the Earth. Mountains are formed by tectonic plate movements. Meanders are large bends in a river's course, formed by the continuous erosion of the outer bank and deposition on the inner bank as the river flows across a plain.

28. Shifting cultivation is also known as 'Jhum' cultivation in which region of India?

  1. Deccan Plateau
  2. Thar Desert
  3. North-Eastern India
  4. Coastal Plains

Answer: North-Eastern India

Explanation: Shifting cultivation, where a patch of land is cleared, cultivated for a few years, and then abandoned for a new patch, is widely practiced in the North-Eastern states of India and is locally known as 'Jhum' cultivation.

29. What is the main occupation of people living in the Sahara Desert?

  1. Agriculture
  2. Fishing
  3. Mining
  4. Animal Rearing (Nomadic Herding)

Answer: Animal Rearing (Nomadic Herding)

Explanation: Due to the harsh climatic conditions and scarcity of water, extensive agriculture is not possible in the Sahara Desert. Fishing is not relevant in a desert. While some mining occurs, the primary traditional occupation for many communities like the Bedouins and Tuaregs is nomadic herding, rearing animals like goats, sheep, camels, and horses.

30. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of life in the Sahara Desert?

  1. Dense evergreen forests
  2. Abundant rainfall throughout the year
  3. Scanty vegetation and extreme temperatures
  4. Large rivers supporting agriculture

Answer: Scanty vegetation and extreme temperatures

Explanation: Deserts are characterized by extremely low rainfall, very high or very low temperatures, and sparse vegetation. The Sahara Desert is a hot desert known for its vast sandy stretches, extreme heat during the day, and very little plant life adapted to arid conditions.

31. The outermost solid layer of the Earth is known as the:

  1. Core
  2. Mantle
  3. Crust
  4. Atmosphere

Answer: Crust

Explanation: The Earth is made up of three main layers: the crust, mantle, and core. The crust is the outermost solid layer, which is relatively thin compared to the other layers. The mantle is below the crust, and the core is the innermost layer. The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding the Earth, not a solid layer of the Earth itself.

32. Which of the following is responsible for the formation of a waterfall?

  1. Wind erosion
  2. River erosion
  3. Glacial deposition
  4. Volcanic eruption

Answer: River erosion

Explanation: Waterfalls are formed when a river flows over a hard rock layer and then plunges down a steep slope or cliff. This process is primarily due to the erosional action of the river, where the softer rock layers are eroded faster than the harder ones, creating a vertical drop.

33. Which gas is most abundant in the Earth's atmosphere?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Carbon Dioxide
  3. Nitrogen
  4. Argon

Answer: Nitrogen

Explanation: Nitrogen makes up approximately 78% of the Earth's atmosphere, making it the most abundant gas. Oxygen is the second most abundant, at about 21%, followed by argon and carbon dioxide in much smaller percentages.

34. Which type of desert is characterized by extremely cold temperatures and covered in ice and snow?

  1. Hot desert
  2. Cold desert
  3. Coastal desert
  4. Rain shadow desert

Answer: Cold desert

Explanation: Deserts are regions with very low precipitation. While many people associate deserts with heat, cold deserts are characterized by extremely low temperatures, often below freezing, and are covered in ice and snow, such as the polar regions (e.g., Antarctica and the Arctic).
