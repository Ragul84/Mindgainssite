26. Tsunamis and earthquakes can cause significant damage to human life and property.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: The text explicitly states they can cause significant damage to human life and property.

27. From nature's standpoint, changes are considered either good or bad.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: From nature's standpoint, changes are neutral, neither good nor bad.

28. Which of the following is an example of a local and brief environmental change?

  1. Global warming
  2. Ozone layer depletion
  3. Hailstorms
  4. Seasonal changes

Answer: Hailstorms

Explanation: Hailstorms happen quickly and affect a small area, making them local and brief.

29. Global warming and ozone layer depletion are categorized as what type of environmental changes?

  1. Local and brief
  2. Global and long-term
  3. Neutral and short-term
  4. Desirable and local

Answer: Global and long-term

Explanation: These changes impact the entire planet over extended periods of time.

30. From nature's standpoint, environmental changes are considered to be:

  1. Always harmful
  2. Always beneficial
  3. Neutral
  4. Unpredictable

Answer: Neutral

Explanation: From nature's standpoint, changes are neutral, neither good nor bad.

31. Which of the following is considered a 'desirable' change in the environment?

  1. Earthquakes
  2. Ripening of fruits
  3. Floods
  4. Wars

Answer: Ripening of fruits

Explanation: The ripening of fruits is listed as a beneficial or desirable change.

32. Which of these is an example of an undesirable change caused by humans?

  1. Tornadoes
  2. Dust storms
  3. Wars
  4. Change of seasons

Answer: Wars

Explanation: Wars are human-caused events that are harmful and thus undesirable.

33. Environmental changes can vary greatly in which two dimensions?

  1. Color and shape
  2. Scale and duration
  3. Temperature and pressure
  4. Speed and direction

Answer: Scale and duration

Explanation: The text states that environmental changes vary greatly in scale and duration.

34. How are undesirable changes categorized?

  1. As neutral events
  2. As beneficial events
  3. As harmful events
  4. As brief events

Answer: As harmful events

Explanation: Undesirable changes, such as natural disasters and wars, are described as harmful.

35. Which of the following is a long-term global change?

  1. Dust storms
  2. Tornadoes
  3. Depletion of the ozone layer
  4. Hailstorms

Answer: Depletion of the ozone layer

Explanation: Ozone layer depletion impacts the entire planet over extended periods.

36. The change of seasons is an example of which type of change?

  1. Undesirable
  2. Desirable
  3. Neutral
  4. Local and brief

Answer: Desirable

Explanation: The change of seasons is listed as a beneficial or desirable change.

37. What determines whether a change is considered 'good' or 'bad'?

  1. The speed of the change
  2. The scale of the change
  3. The effect on people and the environment
  4. The duration of the change

Answer: The effect on people and the environment

Explanation: Evaluating effects on people and the environment determines if a change is good or bad.

38. Tornadoes and dust storms are examples of changes that:

  1. Affect the entire planet
  2. Happen quickly and affect a small area
  3. Are always desirable
  4. Occur over long periods

Answer: Happen quickly and affect a small area

Explanation: Tornadoes and dust storms are local and brief changes.

39. Which of the following is a natural disaster listed as an undesirable change?

  1. Ripening of fruits
  2. Change of seasons
  3. Earthquakes
  4. Global warming

Answer: Earthquakes

Explanation: Earthquakes and floods are listed as undesirable natural disasters.

40. Undesirable changes can occur over:

  1. Only short periods
  2. Only long periods
  3. Short or long periods of time
  4. Neither short nor long periods

Answer: Short or long periods of time

Explanation: The text states these changes can occur over short or long periods of time.

41. What is the nature of environmental changes from nature's standpoint?

  1. Positive
  2. Negative
  3. Neutral
  4. Harmful

Answer: Neutral

Explanation: From nature's standpoint, changes are neutral, neither good nor bad.

42. From nature's standpoint, environmental changes are neither good nor bad.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: Nature views these changes as neutral.

43. The ripening of fruits is considered an undesirable change.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: The ripening of fruits is listed as a desirable and beneficial change.

44. Global warming is a local and brief change.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: Global warming is global and long-term.

45. What is the primary characteristic of a disaster?

  1. A predictable and manageable event
  2. An unexpected and undesirable event causing significant harm
  3. A planned event to test emergency services
  4. A minor disruption to daily activities

Answer: An unexpected and undesirable event causing significant harm

Explanation: The text defines a disaster as an unexpected and undesirable event that causes significant harm or damage.

46. Which of these is categorized as a man-made disaster?

  1. Hurricanes
  2. Nuclear accidents
  3. Floods
  4. Earthquakes

Answer: Nuclear accidents

Explanation: Chemical spills and nuclear accidents are specifically mentioned as man-made disasters.

47. Why are disasters generally feared?

  1. Because they are always predictable
  2. Because they lead to loss of life, property damage, and disruption of daily activities
  3. Because they only occur in urban areas
  4. Because they are entirely within human control

Answer: Because they lead to loss of life, property damage, and disruption of daily activities

Explanation: The text states disasters are feared due to loss of life, property damage, and disruption of daily activities.

48. What is the benefit of understanding disasters?

  1. It allows humans to stop all natural forces
  2. It helps in preparing for and mitigating their effects
  3. It ensures that disasters never happen
  4. It eliminates the need for emergency services

Answer: It helps in preparing for and mitigating their effects

Explanation: Understanding disasters helps in preparing for and mitigating their effects.

49. How were disasters traditionally viewed?

  1. As man-made events
  2. As purely natural events with humans as helpless victims
  3. As manageable events
  4. As events caused by societal structures

Answer: As purely natural events with humans as helpless victims

Explanation: Traditionally, disasters were seen as purely natural events, with humans viewed as helpless victims.

50. What modern understanding is mentioned regarding the severity of disasters?

  1. Only natural forces determine the severity
  2. Human activities and societal structures also contribute to the impact
  3. Societal structures have no role in disaster impact
  4. Disasters are now entirely controllable by humans

Answer: Human activities and societal structures also contribute to the impact

Explanation: It is now understood that human activities and societal structures contribute to the impact and severity of disasters.
