26. The Standard Meridian of India passes through which of the following cities?

  1. Mumbai
  2. Kolkata
  3. Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh)
  4. Chennai

Answer: Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh)

Explanation: The Standard Meridian of India (82°30'E) passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh. This longitude is chosen as the standard meridian to ensure a uniform time across the country, minimizing the time difference between its eastern and western extremities.

27. The sudden onset of the monsoon, characterized by a sudden increase in rainfall and its continuity for several days, is known as:

  1. Monsoon retreat
  2. Monsoon burst
  3. Monsoon trough
  4. Monsoon break

Answer: Monsoon burst

Explanation: The 'monsoon burst' refers to the sudden and vigorous onset of the monsoon rains, typically occurring in early June over the Indian subcontinent. This concept is crucial for understanding the 'Climate' of India.

28. Which of the following is a major cause of the rapid growth of the Indian population since 1921?

  1. Increase in birth rate
  2. Decrease in death rate
  3. Increase in immigration
  4. Decrease in emigration

Answer: Decrease in death rate

Explanation: The rapid growth of the Indian population, especially after 1921, is primarily attributed to a significant decrease in the death rate due to improvements in healthcare, sanitation, and food supply, while the birth rate remained high. This is a key demographic trend discussed in the 'Population' chapter.

29. The Western Coastal Plain is narrower in the north and broader in the south. The northern part is called the Konkan, and the southern part is referred to as the:

  1. Coromandel Coast
  2. Northern Circar
  3. Malabar Coast
  4. Utkal Plain

Explanation: The southern part of the Western Coastal Plain is known as the Malabar Coast. The Coromandel Coast and Northern Circar are parts of the Eastern Coastal Plain. This detail is covered in the 'Physical Features of India' chapter when discussing coastal plains.

30. The 'loo' is a striking feature of the Indian climate during which season?

  1. Winter
  2. Monsoon
  3. Autumn
  4. Summer

Answer: Summer

Explanation: The 'loo' refers to strong, gusty, hot, dry winds blowing during the day over the north and northwestern plains of India, particularly during the summer season. This is a characteristic feature of the Indian 'Climate'.

31. Which of the following rivers forms the largest delta in the world?

  1. Godavari
  2. Mahanadi
  3. Ganga-Brahmaputra
  4. Indus

Answer: Ganga-Brahmaputra

Explanation: The Ganga-Brahmaputra delta, also known as the Sunderbans Delta, is the largest delta in the world. It is formed by the confluence of the Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers before they drain into the Bay of Bengal. The other rivers mentioned also form deltas, but none are as extensive as the Ganga-Brahmaputra delta.

32. The sudden onset of the monsoon, characterized by a sudden increase in rainfall and its continuation for several days, is known as:

  1. Monsoon retreat
  2. Monsoon burst
  3. Monsoon trough
  4. Monsoon break

Answer: Monsoon burst

Explanation: The sudden onset of the monsoon, marked by a rapid increase in rainfall and its persistence for several days, is termed the 'Monsoon burst'. Monsoon retreat refers to the withdrawal of the monsoon, monsoon trough is a low-pressure area, and monsoon break refers to periods of dry weather during the monsoon season.

33. Which type of natural vegetation is typically found in areas with more than 200 cm of rainfall?

  1. Tropical Thorn Forests
  2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
  3. Montane Forests
  4. Tropical Evergreen Forests

Answer: Tropical Evergreen Forests

Explanation: Tropical Evergreen Forests are found in areas receiving more than 200 cm of rainfall with a short dry season. These forests are characterized by dense, multi-layered vegetation and trees that do not shed their leaves simultaneously. Tropical Thorn Forests are found in arid regions, Tropical Deciduous Forests in areas with moderate rainfall, and Montane Forests in mountainous regions.

34. Which of the following is the most significant factor influencing the climate of India, leading to the distinct seasonal reversal of wind systems?

  1. The Himalayas
  2. The Indian Ocean
  3. The Tropic of Cancer
  4. The Peninsular Plateau

Answer: The Himalayas

Explanation: The Himalayas play a crucial role in influencing India's climate. They act as a barrier, preventing cold winds from Central Asia from entering the subcontinent, thus keeping winters milder than they would otherwise be. More importantly, they are responsible for trapping the monsoon winds, forcing them to shed their moisture over the Indian subcontinent, leading to the distinct seasonal reversal of wind systems (monsoons) that characterize India's climate. While the Indian Ocean provides moisture, and the Tropic of Cancer defines the tropical zone, and the Peninsular Plateau influences local weather, the Himalayas are the primary factor for the overall monsoon type of climate.

35. The 'Doab' is a distinctive feature found in the northern plains of India. What does the term 'Doab' literally refer to?

  1. A region of fertile alluvial soil
  2. The land between two rivers
  3. A confluence of three rivers
  4. A deltaic region formed by rivers

Answer: The land between two rivers

Explanation: The term 'Doab' is derived from two Persian words: 'do' meaning two and 'ab' meaning water or river. Therefore, a Doab literally refers to the land lying between two confluent rivers. These regions are typically very fertile due to the deposition of alluvial soil by the rivers, making them highly productive agricultural areas in the northern plains of India.
