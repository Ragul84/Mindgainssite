Class9 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class9 questions to revise the NCERT Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 311 questions.

1. Which of the following longitudes is considered the Standard Meridian of India?

  1. 82°30' E
  2. 82°30' W
  3. 97°25' E
  4. 68°7' E

Answer: 82°30' E

Explanation: The Standard Meridian of India is 82°30' E. This longitude passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh and is chosen to ensure a uniform time across the country, minimizing the time difference between its easternmost and westernmost points.

2. The highest peak in the Eastern Ghats is:

  1. Anaimudi
  2. Doda Betta
  3. Mahendragiri
  4. Kanchenjunga

Answer: Mahendragiri

Explanation: Mahendragiri (1,501 meters) is the highest peak in the Eastern Ghats. Anaimudi and Doda Betta are the highest peaks in the Western Ghats, while Kanchenjunga is in the Himalayas.

3. Which of the following rivers is known as the 'Dakshin Ganga' (Ganga of the South)?

  1. Krishna
  2. Godavari
  3. Mahanadi
  4. Cauvery

Answer: Godavari

Explanation: The Godavari River is often referred to as the 'Dakshin Ganga' due to its length, large drainage basin, and cultural significance, similar to the Ganga in North India.

4. Which of the following factors is primarily responsible for the monsoon type of climate in India?

  1. Altitude
  2. Latitude
  3. Pressure and wind systems
  4. Distance from the sea

Answer: Pressure and wind systems

Explanation: The monsoon type of climate in India is primarily influenced by the differential heating and cooling of land and water, which creates seasonal reversals in pressure and wind systems, leading to the monsoon winds.

5. In which type of natural vegetation are trees like Sal, Teak, Peepal, and Neem commonly found?

  1. Tropical Evergreen Forests
  2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
  3. Thorn Forests and Scrubs
  4. Montane Forests

Answer: Tropical Deciduous Forests

Explanation: Sal, Teak, Peepal, and Neem are characteristic species of Tropical Deciduous Forests, also known as Monsoon Forests. These trees shed their leaves for about six to eight weeks in dry summer to conserve water.

6. According to the 2011 Census, which state in India has the highest population density?

  1. Uttar Pradesh
  2. Maharashtra
  3. Bihar
  4. West Bengal

Answer: Bihar

Explanation: As per the 2011 Census, Bihar had the highest population density among Indian states, with 1106 persons per square kilometer.

7. The Tropic of Cancer does NOT pass through which of the following Indian states?

  1. Gujarat
  2. Madhya Pradesh
  3. Odisha
  4. Chhattisgarh

Answer: Odisha

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer passes through eight Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram. It does not pass through Odisha.

8. The 'Doab' is a distinctive feature found between which two major Himalayan divisions?

  1. Himadri and Himachal
  2. Himachal and Shiwaliks
  3. Lesser Himalayas and Greater Himalayas
  4. Kashmir Himalayas and Punjab Himalayas

Answer: Himachal and Shiwaliks

Explanation: The 'Doab' refers to the fertile land between two converging rivers. In the context of the Himalayas, the longitudinal valleys lying between the Himachal (Lesser Himalayas) and the Shiwaliks are known as Duns (e.g., Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun), which are a type of Doab.

9. Which parallel of latitude divides India into almost two equal halves?

  1. Tropic of Cancer (23°30' N)
  2. Equator (0°)
  3. Tropic of Capricorn (23°30' S)
  4. Arctic Circle (66°30' N)

Answer: Tropic of Cancer (23°30' N)

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer (23°30' N latitude) passes almost through the middle of India, dividing the country into two climatic zones: tropical to the south and subtropical to the north. This is a fundamental concept from the 'India - Size and Location' chapter.

10. The highest peak in the Himalayas located entirely within India is:

  1. Mount Everest
  2. Kanchenjunga
  3. K2 (Godwin Austen)
  4. Nanga Parbat

Answer: Kanchenjunga

Explanation: Kanchenjunga (8,586 meters) is the highest peak in the Himalayas that lies entirely within the geographical boundaries of India. Mount Everest is in Nepal, K2 is in the Karakoram range (partially disputed territory), and Nanga Parbat is in Pakistan-administered Kashmir. This question tests knowledge from the 'Physical Features of India' chapter.

11. Which of the following rivers flows through a rift valley?

  1. Ganga
  2. Brahmaputra
  3. Narmada
  4. Godavari

Answer: Narmada

Explanation: The Narmada river flows westward through a rift valley formed due to faulting. This is a distinctive feature of peninsular rivers and is covered in the 'Drainage' chapter. The other rivers mentioned do not flow through rift valleys.

12. The sudden onset of the monsoon, characterized by a sudden increase in rainfall, is known as:

  1. Monsoon retreat
  2. Monsoon burst
  3. Monsoon trough
  4. Monsoon break

Answer: Monsoon burst

Explanation: The 'monsoon burst' refers to the sudden and vigorous onset of the monsoon rains, typically in early June, after a period of dry weather. This phenomenon is a key characteristic of the Indian monsoon system, as discussed in the 'Climate' chapter.

13. Which type of natural vegetation is characterized by trees that shed their leaves during the dry season?

  1. Tropical Evergreen Forests
  2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
  3. Montane Forests
  4. Mangrove Forests

Answer: Tropical Deciduous Forests

Explanation: Tropical Deciduous Forests, also known as Monsoon Forests, are the most widespread forests in India. Their trees shed their leaves for about six to eight weeks in the dry summer to conserve water. This is a core concept from the 'Natural Vegetation and Wild Life' chapter.

14. The most significant cause of rapid population growth in India since 1951 has been:

  1. Increase in birth rate
  2. Decrease in death rate
  3. Increase in immigration
  4. Decrease in emigration

Answer: Decrease in death rate

Explanation: While birth rates remained high for a long time, the most significant factor contributing to rapid population growth in India, especially after independence, has been the sharp decline in the death rate due to improved healthcare, sanitation, and control of diseases. This is a key demographic trend discussed in the 'Population' chapter.

15. Which of the following is the southernmost point of the Indian Union?

  1. Kanyakumari
  2. Indira Point
  3. Rameswaram
  4. Sri Vijaya Puram

Answer: Indira Point

Explanation: Indira Point, located in the Great Nicobar Island of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, is the southernmost point of the Indian Union. Kanyakumari is the southernmost point of mainland India. This detail is covered in the 'India - Size and Location' chapter.

16. The Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats meet at the:

  1. Cardamom Hills
  2. Anai Malai Hills
  3. Nilgiri Hills
  4. Palani Hills

Answer: Nilgiri Hills

Explanation: The Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats converge at the Nilgiri Hills, which form a significant mountain range in the southern part of India. This geographical feature is important for understanding the physical divisions of India, as discussed in the 'Physical Features of India' chapter.

17. The 'Doab' is a distinctive feature found between two rivers. Which of the following best describes a 'Doab'?

  1. A triangular landmass surrounded by water on three sides.
  2. A fertile plain situated between two converging rivers.
  3. A narrow strip of land connecting two larger landmasses.
  4. A plateau region with steep slopes and flat top.

Answer: A fertile plain situated between two converging rivers.

Explanation: A 'Doab' (from 'do' meaning two and 'ab' meaning water/river) refers to the fertile land or plain located between two confluent rivers. This term is particularly common in the Indo-Gangetic Plain, where such regions are highly productive agriculturally.

18. Which of the following rivers is known as the 'Dakshin Ganga' (Ganga of the South) due to its length and cultural significance?

  1. Mahanadi
  2. Godavari
  3. Krishna
  4. Cauvery

Answer: Godavari

Explanation: The Godavari River is often referred to as the 'Dakshin Ganga' because it is the longest peninsular river in India and holds immense religious and cultural importance, similar to the Ganga in North India. It originates in the Western Ghats and flows eastward into the Bay of Bengal.

19. Which type of natural vegetation is characterized by trees that shed their leaves for about six to eight weeks in dry summer?

  1. Tropical Evergreen Forests
  2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
  3. Thorn Forests and Scrubs
  4. Montane Forests

Answer: Tropical Deciduous Forests

Explanation: Tropical Deciduous Forests, also known as Monsoon Forests, are the most widespread forests in India. Trees in these forests shed their leaves during the dry summer season (typically six to eight weeks) to conserve water, adapting to the seasonal availability of moisture.

20. Which of the following is a significant cause of the rapid growth of population in India since the mid-20th century?

  1. Increase in birth rate
  2. Decrease in death rate
  3. Increase in migration from other countries
  4. Decrease in literacy rate

Answer: Decrease in death rate

Explanation: The rapid growth of population in India, particularly since the mid-20th century, has been primarily due to a significant decrease in the death rate. This decline is attributed to improvements in public health, sanitation, medical facilities, and control of epidemic diseases, while the birth rate remained relatively high for a longer period.

21. What is the primary reason for the high density of population in the Northern Plains of India?

  1. Presence of numerous mineral resources
  2. Availability of fertile land and adequate water for agriculture
  3. Proximity to international trade routes
  4. Presence of a large number of industrial cities

Answer: Availability of fertile land and adequate water for agriculture

Explanation: The Northern Plains are one of the most densely populated regions in India due to the availability of fertile alluvial soil deposited by rivers like the Ganga, Indus, and Brahmaputra, which supports extensive agriculture. Additionally, the flat terrain and abundant water resources make it highly suitable for human settlement and economic activities.

22. Which of the following is the highest peak in the Himalayas located in India?

  1. Mount Everest
  2. Kanchenjunga
  3. Nanga Parbat
  4. Godwin Austen (K2)

Answer: Kanchenjunga

Explanation: While Mount Everest is the world's highest peak and K2 (Godwin Austen) is the second highest, Kanchenjunga (8,586 meters) is the highest peak in the Himalayas that is entirely located within the territory of India. Mount Everest is in Nepal and K2 is in the Karakoram range, which is part of the disputed territory.

23. The sudden onset of the monsoon, marked by a violent thunder and lightning, is known as:

  1. Monsoon retreat
  2. Monsoon burst
  3. Monsoon trough
  4. Monsoon break

Answer: Monsoon burst

Explanation: The 'monsoon burst' refers to the sudden and vigorous onset of the monsoon rains, typically in early June, which marks the beginning of the rainy season in India. It is characterized by a significant increase in rainfall intensity and often accompanied by thunderstorms.

24. Which type of natural vegetation is characterized by trees that shed their leaves in the dry season?

  1. Tropical Evergreen Forests
  2. Tropical Deciduous Forests
  3. Thorn Forests and Scrubs
  4. Montane Forests

Answer: Tropical Deciduous Forests

Explanation: Tropical Deciduous Forests, also known as Monsoon Forests, are characterized by trees that shed their leaves for about six to eight weeks in the dry summer season to conserve water. This adaptation helps them survive the dry periods.

25. Which of the following is a major cause of the rapid growth of population in India?

  1. Low birth rate
  2. High death rate
  3. Improved medical facilities
  4. Increased emigration

Answer: Improved medical facilities

Explanation: Improved medical facilities, better sanitation, and advancements in healthcare have significantly reduced the death rate in India, especially infant mortality. While the birth rate has also declined, the gap between a still relatively high birth rate and a much lower death rate has led to a rapid increase in population.
