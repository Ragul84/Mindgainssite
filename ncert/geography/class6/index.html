Class6 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class6 questions to revise the NCERT Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 300 questions.

1. Which celestial body is at the centre of the Solar System?

  1. Earth
  2. Moon
  3. Sun
  4. Mars

Answer: Sun

Explanation: The Sun is at the centre of the Solar System. All planets, including Earth, revolve around the Sun. This is known as the heliocentric model.

2. What is the imaginary line that divides the Earth into two equal halves, the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere?

  1. Prime Meridian
  2. Tropic of Cancer
  3. Equator
  4. Arctic Circle

Answer: Equator

Explanation: The Equator is the most important parallel of latitude, located at 0 degrees. It divides the Earth into two equal halves: the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere.

3. What causes day and night on Earth?

  1. Revolution of the Earth around the Sun
  2. Rotation of the Earth on its axis
  3. Tilt of the Earth's axis
  4. Moon's orbit around the Earth

Answer: Rotation of the Earth on its axis

Explanation: Day and night are caused by the Earth's rotation on its axis. As the Earth spins, different parts of its surface face the Sun, experiencing day, while the other side experiences night.

4. Which type of map shows natural features of the Earth such as mountains, plateaus, plains, rivers, and oceans?

  1. Political map
  2. Thematic map
  3. Physical map
  4. Road map

Answer: Physical map

Explanation: Physical maps (also called relief maps) show natural features of the Earth. Political maps show cities, towns, and countries, while thematic maps focus on specific information like rainfall or population.

5. Which of the following is NOT a major domain of the Earth?

  1. Lithosphere
  2. Atmosphere
  3. Hydrosphere
  4. Stratosphere

Answer: Stratosphere

Explanation: The four major domains of the Earth are the Lithosphere (land), Hydrosphere (water), Atmosphere (air), and Biosphere (living organisms). The Stratosphere is a layer within the Atmosphere, not a separate major domain.

6. Which mountain range forms the northern boundary of India?

  1. Western Ghats
  2. Eastern Ghats
  3. Himalayas
  4. Aravalli Range

Answer: Himalayas

Explanation: The Himalayas are the highest and most rugged mountain ranges in the world, forming a natural barrier along the northern border of India.

7. What is the standard meridian of India?

  1. 82°30' E
  2. 75°30' E
  3. 90°00' E
  4. 0°00' E

Answer: 82°30' E

Explanation: The Standard Meridian of India is 82°30' E. This longitude passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh and is used to calculate the Indian Standard Time (IST) to avoid time differences across the country.

8. What is the name given to the vast stretches of flat land in India?

  1. Deccan Plateau
  2. Coastal Plains
  3. Great Indian Desert
  4. Northern Plains

Answer: Northern Plains

Explanation: The Northern Plains are vast, flat, and fertile lands formed by the deposition of sediments by the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra rivers and their tributaries. They are one of the most densely populated regions of India.

9. What is the movement of the Earth around its own axis called?

  1. Revolution
  2. Rotation
  3. Orbit
  4. Inclination

Answer: Rotation

Explanation: Rotation is the movement of the Earth on its axis. This movement causes day and night. Revolution is the movement of the Earth around the Sun.

10. India is located in which continent?

  1. Africa
  2. Europe
  3. Asia
  4. Australia

Answer: Asia

Explanation: India is a vast country located in the southern part of the continent of Asia. It is the seventh-largest country in the world by area.

11. The time difference between the Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) and Indian Standard Time (IST) is approximately:

  1. 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT
  2. 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT
  3. 5 hours 30 minutes behind GMT
  4. 8 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT

Answer: 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT

Explanation: The Indian Standard Time (IST) is calculated based on the 82°30' E longitude, which passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh. This longitude is 5 hours and 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT).

12. Which ocean lies to the south of India?

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Arctic Ocean
  4. Indian Ocean

Answer: Indian Ocean

Explanation: The Indian Ocean is the only ocean named after a country, India. It lies to the south of the Indian subcontinent, while the Arabian Sea is to the west and the Bay of Bengal is to the east.

13. What is the local time at the Prime Meridian called?

  1. Indian Standard Time
  2. Greenwich Mean Time
  3. International Date Line
  4. Standard Time

Answer: Greenwich Mean Time

Explanation: The local time at the Prime Meridian (0° longitude) is known as Greenwich Mean Time (GMT). It serves as the basis for calculating time zones around the world.

14. Which type of map shows natural features of the Earth like mountains, plateaus, plains, rivers, and oceans?

  1. Political Map
  2. Thematic Map
  3. Physical Map
  4. Road Map

Answer: Physical Map

Explanation: Physical maps, also known as relief maps, show the natural features of the Earth. Political maps show cities, countries, and states, while thematic maps focus on specific information like rainfall or population.

15. Which ocean is named after a country?

  1. Atlantic Ocean
  2. Pacific Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Indian Ocean

Explanation: The Indian Ocean is the only ocean in the world named after a country, India. It is located to the south of Asia.

16. The standard meridian of India passes through which city?

  1. Mumbai
  2. Delhi
  3. Allahabad (Prayagraj)
  4. Kolkata

Answer: Allahabad (Prayagraj)

Explanation: The Standard Meridian of India (82°30'E) passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh, near Allahabad (now Prayagraj). This meridian is used to calculate the Indian Standard Time (IST).

17. Which ocean is named after our country, India?

  1. Atlantic Ocean
  2. Pacific Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Indian Ocean

Explanation: The Indian Ocean is the only ocean in the world named after a country, India. It lies to the south of the Indian subcontinent.

18. Which celestial body is at the center of our Solar System?

  1. Earth
  2. Moon
  3. Sun
  4. Mars

Answer: Sun

Explanation: The Sun is a star and is at the center of our Solar System. All the planets, including Earth, revolve around the Sun. This is known as the heliocentric model of the Solar System.

19. What is the imaginary line that divides the Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere?

  1. Prime Meridian
  2. Tropic of Cancer
  3. Equator
  4. Arctic Circle

Answer: Equator

Explanation: The Equator is an imaginary line of latitude that runs around the middle of the Earth, equidistant from the North Pole and the South Pole. It divides the Earth into two equal halves: the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere.

20. What is the approximate North-South extent of India in kilometers?

  1. 2900 km
  2. 3200 km
  3. 3500 km
  4. 2500 km

Answer: 3200 km

Explanation: The North-South extent of India, from Kashmir in the north to Kanyakumari in the south, is approximately 3,200 km. This vast extent contributes to the diverse geographical features of the country.

21. Which imaginary line passes through the middle of India, dividing it into almost two equal halves?

  1. Equator
  2. Prime Meridian
  3. Tropic of Cancer
  4. Arctic Circle

Answer: Tropic of Cancer

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N latitude) passes almost through the middle of India, dividing the country into two climatic zones: the tropical zone to the south and the subtropical zone to the north.

22. What is the name given to the small celestial bodies that revolve around the planets?

  1. Stars
  2. Galaxies
  3. Satellites
  4. Asteroids

Answer: Satellites

Explanation: Satellites are celestial bodies that revolve around planets. The Moon is a natural satellite of the Earth. There are also many man-made satellites launched into space to orbit Earth for various purposes.

23. Which of the following is NOT considered a major domain of the Earth?

  1. Lithosphere
  2. Atmosphere
  3. Biosphere
  4. Stratosphere

Answer: Stratosphere

Explanation: The major domains of the Earth are the Lithosphere (land), Hydrosphere (water), Atmosphere (air), and Biosphere (life). The Stratosphere is a layer within the Atmosphere, not a separate major domain.

24. The imaginary line passing through the centre of the Earth, on which it rotates, is called its:

  1. Orbit
  2. Equator
  3. Axis
  4. Meridian

Answer: Axis

Explanation: The axis is an imaginary line about which a body rotates. In the case of Earth, it's the imaginary line passing through its centre, from the North Pole to the South Pole, around which it spins.

25. Which ocean borders India to the south?

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Indian Ocean

Explanation: The Indian Ocean is the third-largest ocean in the world and borders India to its south. It is named after India due to its significant geographical position relative to the country.
