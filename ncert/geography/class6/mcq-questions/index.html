26. Which of the following is the largest continent in the world?

  1. Africa
  2. Europe
  3. Asia
  4. North America

Answer: Asia

Explanation: Asia is the largest continent, covering about one-third of the total land area of the Earth. It is also the most populous continent.

27. Which ocean is 'S' shaped and is flanked by the North and South Americas on the west and Europe and Africa on the east?

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Indian Ocean
  3. Atlantic Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Atlantic Ocean

Explanation: The Atlantic Ocean is the second largest ocean and is 'S' shaped. It is bordered by North and South America to the west and Europe and Africa to the east.

28. What is the southernmost point of the Indian mainland?

  1. Indira Point
  2. Kanyakumari
  3. Sri Vijaya Puram
  4. Chennai

Answer: Kanyakumari

Explanation: Kanyakumari is the southernmost tip of the Indian mainland. Indira Point is the southernmost point of the Indian Union, located in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, but it is not on the mainland.

29. Which mountain range lies to the north of India and is home to the world's highest peaks?

  1. Western Ghats
  2. Eastern Ghats
  3. Himalayas
  4. Aravalli Range

Answer: Himalayas

Explanation: The Himalayas are the highest and most rugged mountain ranges in the world, located to the north of India. They include some of the world's highest peaks like Mount Everest.

30. Which celestial body is known as the 'Morning Star' or 'Evening Star'?

  1. Mars
  2. Jupiter
  3. Venus
  4. Saturn

Answer: Venus

Explanation: Venus is often called the 'Morning Star' or 'Evening Star' because it is visible in the sky just before sunrise or just after sunset. It is the brightest object in the night sky after the Moon.

31. Which type of map shows details like mountains, plateaus, plains, rivers, and oceans?

  1. Political map
  2. Thematic map
  3. Physical map
  4. Road map

Answer: Physical map

Explanation: A physical map shows the natural features of the Earth, such as mountains, plateaus, plains, rivers, and oceans. These features are often depicted using different colours and shading to represent elevation and water bodies.

32. What causes the change of seasons on Earth?

  1. Rotation of the Earth on its axis
  2. Revolution of the Earth around the Sun on a tilted axis
  3. Movement of the Moon around the Earth
  4. Changes in the Sun's temperature

Answer: Revolution of the Earth around the Sun on a tilted axis

Explanation: The change of seasons is caused by the Earth's revolution around the Sun and the tilt of its axis. As the Earth orbits the Sun, different parts of the Earth receive more direct sunlight at different times of the year, leading to variations in temperature and daylight hours, which we experience as seasons.

33. Which of the following is NOT considered a planet in our Solar System?

  1. Mars
  2. Jupiter
  3. Pluto
  4. Earth

Answer: Pluto

Explanation: Pluto was once considered the ninth planet, but in 2006, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) reclassified it as a 'dwarf planet' because it has not cleared its orbit of other debris. Mars, Jupiter, and Earth are all recognized as planets in our Solar System.

34. The imaginary line that divides the Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere is called the:

  1. Tropic of Cancer
  2. Equator
  3. Prime Meridian
  4. Arctic Circle

Answer: Equator

Explanation: The Equator is the most important parallel of latitude. It is an imaginary line that runs exactly halfway between the North Pole and the South Pole, dividing the Earth into two equal halves: the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere. The Tropic of Cancer and Arctic Circle are other important parallels of latitude, while the Prime Meridian is a longitude.

35. When the Earth rotates on its axis, what phenomenon does it cause?

  1. Change of seasons
  2. Day and night
  3. Lunar eclipse
  4. Solar eclipse

Answer: Day and night

Explanation: The rotation of the Earth on its axis is the movement that causes day and night. As the Earth spins, different parts of its surface face the Sun, experiencing daylight, while the parts facing away from the Sun experience night. The change of seasons is caused by the Earth's revolution around the Sun and its tilted axis. Lunar and solar eclipses are astronomical events involving the alignment of the Sun, Earth, and Moon.
