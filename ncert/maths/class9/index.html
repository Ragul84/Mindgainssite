Class9 MCQs with answers

1. Which of the following is an irrational number?

  1. √4
  2. √9
  3. √7
  4. √16

Answer: √7

Explanation: An irrational number cannot be expressed as a simple fraction p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
√4 = 2 (rational)
√9 = 3 (rational)
√7 cannot be simplified to an integer or a simple fraction, so it is irrational.
√16 = 4 (rational)

2. If p(x) = x² - 2√2x + 1, then p(2√2) is:

  1. 1
  2. 0
  3. 2√2
  4. -1

Answer: 1

Explanation: To find p(2√2), substitute x = 2√2 into the polynomial:
p(2√2) = (2√2)² - 2√2(2√2) + 1
p(2√2) = (4 × 2) - (4 × 2) + 1
p(2√2) = 8 - 8 + 1
p(2√2) = 1

3. The perpendicular distance of the point P(3, 4) from the y-axis is:

  1. 3 units
  2. 4 units
  3. 5 units
  4. 7 units

Answer: 3 units

Explanation: The perpendicular distance of a point (x, y) from the y-axis is the absolute value of its x-coordinate. For the point P(3, 4), the x-coordinate is 3. Therefore, the perpendicular distance from the y-axis is 3 units.

4. The equation y = 3x + 5 has:

  1. A unique solution
  2. Exactly two solutions
  3. Infinitely many solutions
  4. No solution

Answer: Infinitely many solutions

Explanation: A linear equation in two variables (like y = 3x + 5) represents a straight line. Every point on this line is a solution to the equation. Since a line consists of infinitely many points, a linear equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions.

5. If two lines intersect, and the sum of two adjacent angles is 180°, then these angles are called:

  1. Complementary angles
  2. Supplementary angles
  3. Vertically opposite angles
  4. Alternate interior angles

Answer: Supplementary angles

Explanation: Two angles are called supplementary if their sum is 180°. When two lines intersect, adjacent angles form a linear pair, and the sum of angles in a linear pair is always 180°.

6. In ΔABC, if AB = AC and ∠B = 50°, then ∠A is:

  1. 50°
  2. 80°
  3. 100°
  4. 130°

Answer: 80°

Explanation: In ΔABC, since AB = AC, it is an isosceles triangle. The angles opposite to equal sides are equal. Therefore, ∠C = ∠B = 50°. The sum of angles in a triangle is 180°. So, ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°.
∠A + 50° + 50° = 180°
∠A + 100° = 180°
∠A = 180° - 100°
∠A = 80°

7. The diagonals of a parallelogram:

  1. Are always equal
  2. Bisect each other
  3. Are perpendicular to each other
  4. Bisect the angles

Answer: Bisect each other

Explanation: One of the fundamental properties of a parallelogram is that its diagonals bisect each other. This means they cut each other into two equal parts at their point of intersection. They are not always equal (only in rectangles), not always perpendicular (only in rhombuses), and do not always bisect the angles (only in rhombuses).

8. The area of an equilateral triangle with side 'a' is:

  1. (√3/4)a²
  2. (1/2)a²
  3. (√3/2)a²
  4. a²

Answer: (√3/4)a²

Explanation: The formula for the area of an equilateral triangle with side 'a' is (√3/4)a². This can be derived using Heron's formula or by finding the height of the triangle and using the formula (1/2) × base × height.

9. Which of the following is a solution to the equation 2x + 3y = 6?

  1. (0, 3)
  2. (3, 0)
  3. (1, 2)
  4. (2, 1)

Answer: (3, 0)

Explanation: To check which ordered pair is a solution, substitute the x and y values into the equation 2x + 3y = 6.
For (0, 3): 2(0) + 3(3) = 0 + 9 = 9 ≠ 6
For (3, 0): 2(3) + 3(0) = 6 + 0 = 6. This is a solution.
For (1, 2): 2(1) + 3(2) = 2 + 6 = 8 ≠ 6
For (2, 1): 2(2) + 3(1) = 4 + 3 = 7 ≠ 6
Thus, (3, 0) is the correct solution.

10. If two lines intersect, and the sum of two adjacent angles is 180°, then the angles are called:

  1. Complementary angles
  2. Vertically opposite angles
  3. Linear pair of angles
  4. Alternate interior angles

Answer: Linear pair of angles

Explanation: When two angles are adjacent and their sum is 180°, they form a linear pair. Complementary angles sum to 90°. Vertically opposite angles are equal. Alternate interior angles are formed when a transversal intersects two parallel lines.

11. The diagonals of a rhombus are 16 cm and 12 cm. The side of the rhombus is:

  1. 10 cm
  2. 12 cm
  3. 14 cm
  4. 20 cm

Answer: 10 cm

Explanation: In a rhombus, the diagonals bisect each other at right angles. Let the diagonals be d1 = 16 cm and d2 = 12 cm. When they bisect, they form four right-angled triangles. The half-diagonals are d1/2 = 16/2 = 8 cm and d2/2 = 12/2 = 6 cm. These half-diagonals are the legs of the right-angled triangles, and the side of the rhombus is the hypotenuse.
Using the Pythagorean theorem: side² = (d1/2)² + (d2/2)²
side² = 8² + 6²
side² = 64 + 36
side² = 100
side = √100
side = 10 cm

12. The equation 2x + 5y = 7 has:

  1. A unique solution
  2. Exactly two solutions
  3. Infinitely many solutions
  4. No solution

Answer: Infinitely many solutions

Explanation: A linear equation in two variables (like 2x + 5y = 7) represents a straight line in a Cartesian plane. Every point on this line is a solution to the equation. Since a line consists of infinitely many points, a linear equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions.

13. A quadrilateral has three acute angles. If each acute angle is 70°, then the fourth angle is:

  1. 150°
  2. 120°
  3. 100°
  4. 90°

Answer: 150°

Explanation: The sum of the angles in a quadrilateral is 360°.
Let the three acute angles be A, B, and C, and the fourth angle be D.
Given A = B = C = 70°.
So, A + B + C + D = 360°
70° + 70° + 70° + D = 360°
210° + D = 360°
D = 360° - 210°
D = 150°

14. The area of an equilateral triangle with side 'a' using Heron's formula is:

  1. [√3/4]a²
  2. [1/2]a²
  3. [√3/2]a²
  4. [1/4]a²

Answer: [√3/4]a²

Explanation: For an equilateral triangle with side 'a', all sides are equal: a, a, a.
First, calculate the semi-perimeter (s):
s = (a + a + a) / 2 = 3a / 2
Now, apply Heron's formula for the area (A):
A = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
A = √[(3a/2)(3a/2 - a)(3a/2 - a)(3a/2 - a)]
A = √[(3a/2)(a/2)(a/2)(a/2)]
A = √[(3a⁴)/16]
A = (√3 * √a⁴) / √16
A = (√3 * a²) / 4
A = [√3/4]a²

15. The equation 2x + 5y = 0 has:

  1. A unique solution
  2. Exactly two solutions
  3. Infinitely many solutions
  4. No solution

Answer: Infinitely many solutions

Explanation: A linear equation in two variables (like 2x + 5y = 0) represents a straight line in the Cartesian plane. Every point on this line is a solution to the equation. Since a line consists of infinitely many points, a linear equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions.

16. In ΔABC, if AB = AC and ∠B = 70°, then ∠A is:

  1. 70°
  2. 40°
  3. 110°
  4. 55°

Answer: 40°

Explanation: In ΔABC, if AB = AC, then the angles opposite to these equal sides are also equal. So, ∠C = ∠B = 70°. The sum of angles in a triangle is 180°. Therefore, ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°.
∠A + 70° + 70° = 180°
∠A + 140° = 180°
∠A = 180° - 140°
∠A = 40°

17. A quadrilateral has three acute angles. If each acute angle is 70°, then the measure of the fourth angle is:

  1. 150°
  2. 120°
  3. 100°
  4. 90°

Answer: 150°

Explanation: The sum of the angles in a quadrilateral is 360°. Let the three acute angles be A, B, and C, and the fourth angle be D.
A = 70°, B = 70°, C = 70°.
A + B + C + D = 360°
70° + 70° + 70° + D = 360°
210° + D = 360°
D = 360° - 210°
D = 150°

18. The diagonals of a rhombus are 16 cm and 12 cm. The perimeter of the rhombus is:

  1. 10 cm
  2. 20 cm
  3. 40 cm
  4. 80 cm

Answer: 40 cm

Explanation: In a rhombus, the diagonals bisect each other at right angles. Let the diagonals be d1 = 16 cm and d2 = 12 cm. When they bisect, they form four right-angled triangles. The half-diagonals will be d1/2 = 8 cm and d2/2 = 6 cm.
Let 's' be the side of the rhombus. Using the Pythagorean theorem in one of the right-angled triangles:
s² = (d1/2)² + (d2/2)²
s² = 8² + 6²
s² = 64 + 36
s² = 100
s = √100 = 10 cm
The perimeter of the rhombus = 4 × side = 4 × 10 cm = 40 cm.

19. If two lines intersect, then the vertically opposite angles are:

  1. Complementary
  2. Supplementary
  3. Equal
  4. Unequal

Answer: Equal

Explanation: When two lines intersect, they form two pairs of vertically opposite angles. A fundamental theorem in geometry states that vertically opposite angles are always equal.

20. A quadrilateral has angles 70°, 90°, 110°, and x. The value of x is:

  1. 90°
  2. 70°
  3. 110°
  4. 100°

Answer: 90°

Explanation: The sum of the interior angles of any quadrilateral is 360°.
So, 70° + 90° + 110° + x = 360°.
270° + x = 360°.
x = 360° - 270°.
x = 90°.

21. In the given figure, if AB || CD, ∠APQ = 50° and ∠PRD = 127°, then the value of x and y respectively are:

  1. x=50°, y=77°
  2. x=50°, y=127°
  3. x=77°, y=50°
  4. x=127°, y=50°

Answer: x=50°, y=77°

Explanation: Since AB || CD, and PQ is a transversal:
∠APQ = ∠PQR (Alternate interior angles)
So, x = 50°.
Also, ∠APQ + ∠PQC = 180° (Consecutive interior angles) - This is incorrect for finding y directly.
Alternatively, consider transversal PR:
∠APR = ∠PRD (Alternate interior angles)
∠APR = ∠APQ + ∠QPR
So, 50° + y = 127°
y = 127° - 50°
y = 77°
Therefore, x = 50° and y = 77°.

22. The class mark of the class interval 90-120 is:

  1. 90
  2. 105
  3. 120
  4. 210

Answer: 105

Explanation: The class mark of a class interval is the midpoint of the interval. It is calculated as (Lower Limit + Upper Limit) / 2.
For the class interval 90-120:
Class Mark = (90 + 120) / 2
Class Mark = 210 / 2
Class Mark = 105

  4. No solution

24. The area of a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm is:

  1. 6 cm²
  2. 7.5 cm²
  3. 10 cm²
  4. 12 cm²

Answer: 6 cm²

Explanation: First, check if the triangle is a right-angled triangle using the Pythagorean theorem: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, and 5² = 25. Since 3² + 4² = 5², it is a right-angled triangle. The area of a right-angled triangle is (1/2) × base × height. Here, base = 3 cm and height = 4 cm (or vice versa).
Area = (1/2) × 3 × 4 = (1/2) × 12 = 6 cm².
Alternatively, using Heron's formula:
Semi-perimeter (s) = (3 + 4 + 5) / 2 = 12 / 2 = 6 cm.
Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
Area = √[6(6-3)(6-4)(6-5)]
Area = √[6 × 3 × 2 × 1]
Area = √36
Area = 6 cm².

25. If two lines intersect, and the sum of two adjacent angles is 180°, then these angles form a:

  1. Complementary pair
  2. Supplementary pair
  3. Vertically opposite pair
  4. Linear pair

Answer: Linear pair

Explanation: When two lines intersect, and two adjacent angles sum up to 180°, they are said to form a linear pair. A linear pair of angles are always supplementary (sum to 180°) and share a common arm and vertex.
