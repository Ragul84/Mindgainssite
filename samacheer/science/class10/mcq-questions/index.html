Class10 MCQs with answers

26. In a parallel connection of resistors, the equivalent resistance is:

  1. Greater than the highest individual resistance
  2. Equal to the sum of individual resistances
  3. Less than the lowest individual resistance
  4. Equal to the highest individual resistance

Answer: Less than the lowest individual resistance

Explanation: The text explicitly states that the equivalent resistance in a parallel combination is less than the lowest of the individual resistances.

27. When 'n' resistors of equal resistance R are connected in series, the equivalent resistance is:

  1. R/n
  2. nR
  3. R
  4. 1/nR

Answer: nR

Explanation: The text states that when 'n' resistors of equal resistance R are connected in series, the equivalent resistance is 'nR'.

28. When 'n' resistors of equal resistance R are connected in parallel, the equivalent resistance is:

  1. nR
  2. R/n
  3. R
  4. n²R

Answer: R/n

Explanation: The text states that when 'n' resistors of equal resistances R are connected in parallel, the equivalent resistance is R/n.

29. Joule's law of heating states that the heat produced in a resistor is directly proportional to:

  1. The current passing through the resistor
  2. The resistance of the resistor only
  3. The square of the current passing through the resistor
  4. The potential difference across the resistor only

Answer: The square of the current passing through the resistor

Explanation: The text states that the heat produced in any resistor is directly proportional to the square of the current passing through the resistor, directly proportional to the resistance, and directly proportional to the time.

30. The mathematical expression of Joule's law of heating is:

  1. H = VIt
  2. H = I²Rt
  3. H = V²/R
  4. H = IRt

Answer: H = I²Rt

Explanation: The text gives the expression H = I²Rt (equation 4.20) as Joule's law of heating.

31. A fuse wire is connected in ____ in an electric circuit.

  1. parallel
  2. series
  3. both series and parallel
  4. none of the above

Answer: series

Explanation: The text states that the fuse wire is connected in series, in an electric circuit.

32. Nichrome is used as the heating element in electric appliances because:

  1. It has low resistivity
  2. It has high resistivity, high melting point, and is not easily oxidized
  3. It is a good conductor
  4. It has low melting point

Answer: It has high resistivity, high melting point, and is not easily oxidized

Explanation: The text states that Nichrome is used because: (i) it has high resistivity, (ii) it has a high melting point, (iii) it is not easily oxidized.

33. In a series circuit, if the circuit is interrupted at any point:

  1. Current can still pass through the other parts
  2. No current can pass through the circuit
  3. The current increases
  4. The voltage remains the same

Answer: No current can pass through the circuit

Explanation: The text states that if a series circuit is interrupted at any point in the loop, no current can pass through the circuit and hence no electric appliances connected in the circuit will work.

34. The wiring in a house consists of:

  1. Series circuits
  2. Parallel circuits
  3. Series-parallel circuits
  4. Only one loop circuits

Answer: Parallel circuits

Explanation: The text states that the wiring in a house consists of parallel circuits.

35. For a wire of length 10 m, resistance 2 Ω, and area of cross section 2 × 10⁻⁷ m², what is its resistivity?

  1. 2 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m
  2. 4 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m
  3. 6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m
  4. 8 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m

Answer: 4 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m

Explanation: According to Solved Problem-4, resistivity = RA/L = (2 × 2 × 10⁻⁷)/10 = 4 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m.

36. Plotting a graph for momentum on the Y-axis and time on the X-axis, the slope of the momentum-time graph gives

  1. Impulsive force
  2. Acceleration
  3. Force
  4. Rate of force

Answer: Force

Explanation: Slope = change in momentum / change in time = dp/dt = Force. Therefore, the slope of the momentum-time graph gives the force acting on the body.

37. The unit of 'g' is m s⁻². It can also be expressed as

  1. cm s⁻¹
  2. N kg⁻¹
  3. N m² kg⁻¹
  4. cm² s⁻²

Answer: N kg⁻¹

Explanation: Since g is acceleration, it can be expressed in any unit of acceleration. From F = ma, a = F/m, so the unit can also be written as N kg⁻¹.

38. One kilogram force equals to

  1. 9.8 dyne
  2. 9.8 × 10⁴ N
  3. 98 × 10⁴ dyne
  4. 980 dyne

Answer: 98 × 10⁴ dyne

Explanation: 1 kgf = 9.8 N. Converting to CGS units: 9.8 N × 10⁵ dyne/N = 9.8 × 10⁵ dyne = 98 × 10⁴ dyne.

39. The mass of a body is measured on planet Earth as M kg. When it is taken to a planet of radius half that of the Earth, then its value will be _____ kg

  1. 4M
  2. 2M
  3. M/4
  4. M

Answer: M

Explanation: Mass of a body is the amount of matter contained in it and is a constant quantity. It does not change with location, so the mass remains M kg.

40. If the Earth shrinks to 50% of its real radius, its mass remaining the same, the weight of a body on the Earth will

  1. decrease by 50%
  2. increase by 50%
  3. decrease by 25%
  4. increase by 300%

Answer: increase by 300%

Explanation: Weight is inversely proportional to the square of the radius (W ∝ 1/r²). If radius becomes half, weight becomes 4 times the original, which is an increase of 300%.

41. To project rockets, which of the following principle(s) is/are required?

  1. Newton's third law of motion
  2. Newton's law of gravitation
  3. law of conservation of linear momentum
  4. both a and c

Answer: both a and c

Explanation: Rockets work on Newton's third law (action and reaction — gases expelled downward push rocket upward) and the law of conservation of linear momentum. The total momentum of the system remains conserved.

42. Newton's third law is applicable

  1. for a body at rest
  2. for a body in motion
  3. both a & b
  4. only for bodies with equal masses

Answer: both a & b

Explanation: Newton's third law (for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction) is applicable to bodies whether they are at rest or in motion.

43. The unit of 'g' is m s^-2. It can also be expressed as

  1. cm s^-1
  2. N kg^-1
  3. N m^2 kg^-1
  4. cm^2 s^-2

Answer: N kg^-1

Explanation: Since F = ma, acceleration has units of N/kg, so g (acceleration due to gravity) can be expressed as N kg^-1.

44. The mass of a body is measured on planet Earth as M kg. When it is taken to a planet of radius half that of the Earth, its value will be ____ kg.

  1. 4 M
  2. 2 M
  3. M/4
  4. M

Answer: M

Explanation: Mass of a body is constant and does not change with location. Only weight changes due to variation in gravity.

45. In a common man's understanding of motion, a body needs a 'push' or 'pull' to move, bring to rest, or change its velocity. This 'push' or 'pull' is called as

  1. momentum
  2. impulse
  3. force
  4. inertia

Answer: force

Explanation: The text defines force as the 'push' or 'pull' that can move a body, bring it to rest, or change its velocity.

46. The amount of force required to produce an acceleration of 1 m s^-2 in a body of mass 1 kg is called

  1. dyne
  2. newton
  3. kilogram force
  4. gram force

Answer: newton

Explanation: The text defines unit force as the force required to produce an acceleration of 1 m s^-2 in a body of mass 1 kg, which is called a newton.

47. When a fielder pulls back his hands while catching a cricket ball, he experiences

  1. a larger force for a shorter interval of time
  2. a smaller force for a longer interval of time
  3. no change in force and time
  4. an impulsive force for a very short time

Answer: a smaller force for a longer interval of time

Explanation: The text explains that pulling back the hands increases the time of catch, so a smaller force acts for a longer time, reducing the impulse on hands.

48. The principle of conservation of linear momentum states that

  1. momentum of a single body is always conserved
  2. momentum changes when external force acts
  3. there is no change in the linear momentum of a system if no net external force acts
  4. momentum is conserved only for moving bodies

Answer: there is no change in the linear momentum of a system if no net external force acts

Explanation: The text states: 'There is no change in the linear momentum of a system of bodies as long as no net external force acts on them.'

49. Astronauts in an orbiting space shuttle around the Earth feel weightlessness because

  1. there is no gravity in space
  2. the shuttle is in free fall along with the astronauts
  3. they have no mass in space
  4. Earth's gravity does not act at that height

Answer: the shuttle is in free fall along with the astronauts

Explanation: The orbiting space station is in continuous free fall around the Earth along with the astronauts, so they experience weightlessness even though gravity acts on them.

50. According to Aristotle, what is the natural state of earthly bodies?

  1. Uniform motion
  2. Rest
  3. Circular motion
  4. Oscillatory motion

Answer: Rest

Explanation: According to Aristotle, the natural state of earthly bodies is 'rest'. He stated that a moving body naturally comes to rest without any external influence of force, and such motions are termed as 'natural motion' (force independent).
