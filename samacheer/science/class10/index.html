Class10 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class10 questions to revise the Samacheer Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 520 questions.

1. Inertia of a body depends on

  1. weight of the object
  2. acceleration due to gravity of the planet
  3. mass of the object
  4. Both a & b

Answer: mass of the object

Explanation: Inertia is a property of matter that depends only on the mass of the object; it does not depend on weight or gravity.

2. Impulse is equal to

  1. rate of change of momentum
  2. rate of force and time
  3. change of momentum
  4. rate of change of mass

Answer: change of momentum

Explanation: Impulse is defined as the change in momentum of a body and is equal to the product of force and time.

3. Newton's III law is applicable

  1. for a body at rest
  2. for a body in motion
  3. both a & b
  4. only for bodies with equal masses

Answer: both a & b

Explanation: Newton's third law of motion (action and reaction are equal and opposite) applies to bodies at rest as well as bodies in motion.

4. Plotting a graph for momentum on the Y-axis and time on X-axis, the slope of the momentum-time graph gives

  1. Impulsive force
  2. Acceleration
  3. Force
  4. Rate of force

Answer: Force

Explanation: Slope = change in momentum / change in time, which by Newton's second law equals the force acting on the body.

5. In which of the following sports is the turning effect of force used?

  1. swimming
  2. tennis
  3. cycling
  4. hockey

Answer: cycling

Explanation: In cycling, the turning effect of force (moment of force/torque) is used to steer the cycle by turning the handle.

6. Which of the following is correct?

  1. Rate of change of charge is electrical power.
  2. Rate of change of charge is current.
  3. Rate of change of energy is current.
  4. Rate of change of current is charge.

Answer: Rate of change of charge is current.

Explanation: Current is defined as the rate of change of charge with respect to time, I = dQ/dt. Hence, rate of change of charge is current.

7. SI unit of resistance is

  1. mho
  2. joule
  3. ohm
  4. ohm meter

Answer: ohm

Explanation: The SI unit of electrical resistance is the ohm (Ω), named after Georg Simon Ohm. Mho is the unit of conductance, joule is the unit of energy, and ohm-metre is the unit of resistivity.

8. In a simple circuit, why does the bulb glow when you close the switch?

  1. The switch produces electricity.
  2. Closing the switch completes the circuit.
  3. Closing the switch breaks the circuit.
  4. The bulb is getting charged.

Answer: Closing the switch completes the circuit.

Explanation: A switch works by completing or breaking the circuit. When the switch is closed, the circuit becomes complete, allowing current to flow through the bulb, which makes it glow.

9. Kilowatt hour is the unit of

  1. resistivity
  2. conductivity
  3. electrical energy
  4. electrical power

Answer: electrical energy

Explanation: Kilowatt hour (kWh) is a commercial unit of electrical energy. It represents the energy consumed by a device of 1 kilowatt power in 1 hour (1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J).

10. Who established the relation between potential difference and current, known as Ohm's Law?

  1. Georg Simon Ohm
  2. James Watt
  3. Michael Faraday
  4. Andre-Marie Ampere

Answer: Georg Simon Ohm

Explanation: According to the text, Georg Simon Ohm, a German physicist, established the relation between potential difference and current, which is known as Ohm's Law.

11. According to Ohm's Law, the steady current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to:

  1. The resistance of the conductor
  2. The potential difference between the two ends of the conductor
  3. The length of the conductor
  4. The area of cross-section of the conductor

Answer: The potential difference between the two ends of the conductor

Explanation: The text states that at a constant temperature, the steady current 'I' flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference 'V' between the two ends of the conductor.

12. The SI unit of resistance is:

  1. Volt
  2. Ampere
  3. Ohm
  4. Watt

Answer: Ohm

Explanation: The text explicitly states that the SI unit of resistance is ohm and it is represented by the symbol Ω.

13. Resistance of a conductor is defined as the ratio between:

  1. Current and potential difference
  2. Potential difference and current
  3. Length and area of cross-section
  4. Heat produced and time

Answer: Potential difference and current

Explanation: According to the text, the resistance of a conductor is defined as the ratio between the potential difference across the ends of the conductor and the current flowing through it.

14. The resistance of a conductor is said to be one ohm if a current of one ampere flows through it when a potential difference of ____ is maintained across its ends.

  1. ten volts
  2. one volt
  3. hundred volts
  4. half a volt

Answer: one volt

Explanation: The text states that resistance of a conductor is said to be one ohm if a current of one ampere flows through it when a potential difference of one volt is maintained across its ends.

15. Calculate the resistance of a conductor if a current of 2 A passes through it and the potential difference between its ends is 30 V.

  1. 10 Ω
  2. 20 Ω
  3. 15 Ω
  4. 60 Ω

Answer: 15 Ω

Explanation: Using Ohm's Law: R = V/I = 30/2 = 15 Ω, as shown in Solved Problem-3 in the text.

16. The resistance of any conductor is directly proportional to:

  1. The area of cross-section of the conductor
  2. The length of the conductor
  3. The current flowing through it
  4. The potential difference across it

Answer: The length of the conductor

Explanation: The text states that the resistance of any conductor 'R' is directly proportional to the length of the conductor 'L' and is inversely proportional to its area of cross section 'A'.

17. The resistance of a conductor is inversely proportional to:

  1. Its length
  2. Its area of cross-section
  3. The current through it
  4. The potential difference across it

Answer: Its area of cross-section

Explanation: According to the text, the resistance of a conductor is inversely proportional to its area of cross section 'A'.

18. The SI unit of electrical resistivity is:

  1. Ohm metre
  2. Ohm
  3. Ohm⁻¹ metre⁻¹
  4. Mho

Answer: Ohm metre

Explanation: The text states that the unit of electrical resistivity is ohm metre.

19. Electrical resistivity of a material is defined as the resistance of a conductor of:

  1. Unit length and unit area of cross section
  2. Unit length only
  3. Unit area of cross section only
  4. Any length and any area of cross section

Answer: Unit length and unit area of cross section

Explanation: The text defines electrical resistivity as the resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit area of cross section.

20. Which material has the highest resistivity equal to 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m and is used in making heating elements?

  1. Copper
  2. Aluminium
  3. Nichrome
  4. Chromium

Answer: Nichrome

Explanation: The text explicitly states that Nichrome is a conductor with the highest resistivity equal to 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m, and hence it is used in making heating elements.

21. The conductance of a material is mathematically defined as:

  1. Equal to its resistance
  2. The reciprocal of its resistance
  3. The product of its resistance and length
  4. The square of its resistance

Answer: The reciprocal of its resistance

Explanation: The text states that the conductance of a material is mathematically defined as the reciprocal of its resistance (R).

22. The unit of conductance is:

  1. Ohm
  2. Ohm⁻¹
  3. Volt
  4. Ampere

Answer: Ohm⁻¹

Explanation: According to the text, the unit of conductance is ohm⁻¹, which is also represented as 'mho'.

23. The reciprocal of electrical resistivity of a material is called:

  1. Resistance
  2. Conductance
  3. Electrical conductivity
  4. Current

Answer: Electrical conductivity

Explanation: The text states that the reciprocal of electrical resistivity of a material is called its electrical conductivity.

24. When three resistors of resistances 5 Ω, 3 Ω and 2 Ω are connected in series with a 10 V battery, what is the effective resistance?

  1. 8 Ω
  2. 9 Ω
  3. 10 Ω
  4. 30 Ω

Answer: 10 Ω

Explanation: According to Solved Problem-5, Rs = R1 + R2 + R3 = 5 + 3 + 2 = 10 Ω.

25. In a series connection of resistors, the equivalent resistance is:

  1. Less than the lowest individual resistance
  2. Equal to the lowest individual resistance
  3. Greater than the highest individual resistance
  4. Equal to the average of individual resistances

Answer: Greater than the highest individual resistance

Explanation: The text states that the equivalent resistance in a series combination is greater than the highest of the individual resistances.
