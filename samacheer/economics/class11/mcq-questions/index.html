26. Which of the following best describes the importance of the introduction in an Economics book?

  1. It provides detailed case studies
  2. It helps readers understand the subject's scope and significance
  3. It contains statistical data
  4. It lists all chapters

Answer: It helps readers understand the subject's scope and significance

Explanation: The introduction helps readers grasp the scope and significance.

27. Who stated that 'Consumption is the sole end and object of economic activity'?

  1. Adam Smith
  2. J. M. Keynes
  3. Alfred Marshall
  4. David Ricardo

Answer: J. M. Keynes

Explanation: The text explicitly attributes this quote to J. M. Keynes.

28. What is the definition of consumption?

  1. The production of goods for sale
  2. The exchange of goods for money
  3. The use of goods and services for satisfying wants
  4. The distribution of resources among people

Answer: The use of goods and services for satisfying wants

Explanation: Consumption is defined as the use of goods and services for satisfying wants.

29. At what levels is consumption studied in economics?

  1. Only at the micro level
  2. Only at the macro level
  3. Both at micro and macro levels
  4. Neither micro nor macro levels

Answer: Both at micro and macro levels

Explanation: The text states consumption is studied both at micro level and macro level.

30. Which of the following is considered the 'beginning of economic science'?

  1. Production
  2. Distribution
  3. Exchange
  4. Consumption

Answer: Consumption

Explanation: The text states that consumption is the beginning of economic science.

31. What determines the standard of living of the people?

  1. Only the quantity of consumption
  2. Only the quality of consumption
  3. Both quantity and quality of consumption
  4. The rate of production

Answer: Both quantity and quality of consumption

Explanation: The quantity and quality of consumption determine the standard of living.

32. What serves as the basis for human behaviour to buy and consume goods?

  1. Desires
  2. Wants
  3. Income
  4. Prices

Answer: Wants

Explanation: Wants are the basis for human behaviour to buy and consume goods.

33. Which of the following is an example of wants becoming habits?

  1. Buying a house
  2. Reading a newspaper in the morning
  3. Buying a car
  4. Traveling to a new city

Answer: Reading a newspaper in the morning

Explanation: The text gives reading a newspaper, drinking tea, or chewing pans as examples of habits.

34. Which characteristic of wants is illustrated by the choice between Idly, dosa, or chappathi?

  1. Wants are unlimited
  2. Wants are satiable
  3. Wants are alternative
  4. Wants are competitive

Answer: Wants are alternative

Explanation: Alternative ways to satisfy a particular want are called alternative wants.

35. What happens to human wants as civilization grows and development occurs?

  1. They decrease
  2. They remain constant
  3. They multiply
  4. They disappear

Answer: They multiply

Explanation: Human wants multiply with the growth of civilization and development.

36. Consider the following statements: I. Consumption is the end of production. II. In the absence of consumption, there can be no production. Which of the above is/are correct?

  1. Only I
  2. Only II
  3. Both I and II
  4. Neither I nor II

Answer: Both I and II

Explanation: The text states consumption is the end of production and that without it, no production, exchange, or distribution can exist.

37. Consider the following statements: I. In ordinary language, desire and want mean the same thing. II. In economics, desire and want have the same meaning. Which of the above is/are correct?

  1. Only I
  2. Only II
  3. Both I and II
  4. Neither I nor II

Explanation: In ordinary language they are the same, but in economics they have different meanings.

  4. A is false but R is true

Explanation: The continuous emergence of new wants is the reason why they are considered unlimited.
