Class11 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class11 questions to revise the Samacheer Chemistry syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 508 questions.

1. Which of the following is a career option in Chemistry?

  1. Lab Chemist
  2. Software Developer
  3. Graphic Designer
  4. Mechanical Engineer

Answer: Lab Chemist

Explanation: Lab Chemist is a career option in Chemistry, as mentioned in the textbook.

2. Which degree is NOT related to Chemistry?

  1. B.Sc. (Chemistry)
  2. B.Sc. (Polymer Chemistry)
  3. B.Sc. (Computer Science)
  4. B.Sc. (Industrial Chemistry)

Answer: B.Sc. (Computer Science)

Explanation: B.Sc. (Computer Science) is not related to Chemistry, while the other options are.

3. Which industry is NOT mentioned as a career avenue in Chemistry?

  1. Petrochemical Industry
  2. Textile Industry
  3. IT Industry
  4. Agrochemical Industry

Answer: IT Industry

Explanation: The IT Industry is not mentioned as a career avenue in Chemistry in the textbook.

4. Which industry is NOT directly related to Chemistry?

  1. Petrochemical Industry
  2. Textile Industry
  3. Information Technology
  4. Agrochemical Industry

Answer: Information Technology

Explanation: Information Technology is not directly related to Chemistry, unlike the other options.

5. Which degree is NOT mentioned as a Bachelor Degree in Chemistry?

  1. B.Sc. (Chemistry)
  2. B.Sc. (Industrial Chemistry)
  3. B.Sc. (Computer Science)
  4. B.Sc. (Polymer Chemistry)

Answer: B.Sc. (Computer Science)

Explanation: B.Sc. (Computer Science) is not mentioned as a Bachelor Degree in Chemistry in the textbook.

6. Which industry is NOT listed as a potential career field for Chemistry graduates?

  1. Petrochemical Industry
  2. Agrochemical Industry
  3. Textile Industry
  4. Information Technology Industry

Answer: Information Technology Industry

Explanation: The Information Technology Industry is not listed as a potential career field for Chemistry graduates in the textbook.

7. Which competitive exam is related to Chemistry careers?

  1. TNPSC
  2. UPSC
  3. CAT
  4. GATE

Answer: TNPSC

Explanation: TNPSC is mentioned as a competitive exam related to Chemistry careers in the textbook.

8. Which degree is NOT typically associated with Chemistry?

  1. B.Sc. (Chemistry)
  2. B.Sc. (Polymer Chemistry)
  3. B.Sc. (Computer Science)
  4. B.Sc. (Industrial Chemistry)

Answer: B.Sc. (Computer Science)

Explanation: B.Sc. (Computer Science) is unrelated to Chemistry, while the others are specialized Chemistry degrees.

9. Who was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1933 for his work related to atomic theory?

  1. Erwin Schrödinger
  2. J. J. Thomson
  3. Rutherford
  4. Bohr

Answer: Erwin Schrödinger

Explanation: Erwin Schrödinger received the Nobel Prize in 1933 for his contributions to atomic theory.

10. Which of the following statements about Schrödinger's interests is correct?

  1. He was only interested in physics
  2. He was interested in chemistry, physics, maths, and botany
  3. He focused solely on atomic spectra
  4. He was a mathematician only

Answer: He was interested in chemistry, physics, maths, and botany

Explanation: Schrödinger had wide interests including chemistry, physics, maths, and botany.

11. What was Schrödinger dissatisfied with in Bohr's orbit theory?

  1. The concept of electrons orbiting the nucleus
  2. The quantum condition in Bohr's orbit theory
  3. The idea of atomic spectra
  4. The wave nature of electrons

Answer: The quantum condition in Bohr's orbit theory

Explanation: Schrödinger was not satisfied with the quantum condition in Bohr's orbit theory.

12. Schrödinger proposed the wave equation which is now named after him. What does this equation describe?

  1. The energy levels of electrons
  2. The wave nature of particles
  3. The distribution of electrons in an atom
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Schrödinger's wave equation describes the wave nature of particles and electron distribution.

13. From which Greek word is the term 'atom' derived?

  1. A-tomos
  2. Atomos
  3. Atomios
  4. Atom

Answer: Atomos

Explanation: The word 'atom' is derived from the Greek 'atomos' meaning indivisible.

14. According to early atomic theory, atoms were considered to be:

  1. Divisible into smaller particles
  2. Indivisible and non-divisible
  3. Made of protons and neutrons only
  4. Composed of molecules

Answer: Indivisible and non-divisible

Explanation: Initially, atoms were considered indivisible until subatomic particles were discovered.

15. Which experiment revealed that atoms contain negatively charged particles called electrons?

  1. Rutherford's gold foil experiment
  2. J. J. Thomson's cathode ray experiment
  3. Millikan's oil drop experiment
  4. Davisson-Germer experiment

Answer: J. J. Thomson's cathode ray experiment

Explanation: J. J. Thomson's cathode ray experiment revealed electrons in atoms.

16. What model of atom did J. J. Thomson propose?

  1. Nuclear model
  2. Planetary model
  3. Plum pudding model
  4. Quantum mechanical model

Answer: Plum pudding model

Explanation: Thomson proposed the 'plum pudding' model with electrons embedded in a positive sphere.

17. In Thomson's model, the atom is described as a:

  1. Positively charged sphere with embedded electrons
  2. Nucleus surrounded by electrons
  3. Electrons orbiting the nucleus
  4. Indivisible particle

Answer: Positively charged sphere with embedded electrons

Explanation: Thomson's model describes the atom as a positively charged sphere with embedded electrons.

18. Match List I with List II: List I (Scientist) A. J.J. Thomson B. Ernest Rutherford C. Niels Bohr D. Erwin Schrödinger List II (Contribution) 1. Proposed nuclear model 2. Wave equation for electrons 3. Introduced quantum jumps 4. Discovered electrons

  1. A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
  2. A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2
  3. A-1, B-4, C-3, D-2
  4. A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4

Answer: A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2

Explanation: Thomson discovered the electron, Rutherford proposed the nuclear model, Bohr introduced quantum jumps between orbits and Schrödinger wrote the wave equation for electrons.

19. Which experiment led to the discovery of the nucleus?

  1. Rutherford's gold foil experiment
  2. Thomson's cathode ray experiment
  3. Millikan's oil drop experiment
  4. Davisson-Germer experiment

Answer: Rutherford's gold foil experiment

Explanation: Rutherford's gold foil experiment revealed the nucleus.

20. What was Rutherford's contribution to atomic models?

  1. Discovered electrons
  2. Proposed the planetary model
  3. Discovered the nucleus
  4. Developed quantum mechanics

Answer: Discovered the nucleus

Explanation: Rutherford discovered the nucleus and proposed the nuclear model.

21. Which of the following is NOT a subatomic particle?

  1. Electron
  2. Proton
  3. Neutron
  4. Atom

Answer: Atom

Explanation: Atom is not a subatomic particle; it is the basic unit of matter.

22. True or False: The atom was initially thought to be divisible into smaller particles.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: Initially, atoms were considered indivisible, but later subatomic particles were discovered.

23. Which scientist's model introduced the concept of electrons orbiting the nucleus in specific paths?

  1. Thomson
  2. Rutherford
  3. Bohr
  4. Schrödinger

Answer: Bohr

Explanation: Bohr's model introduced electrons orbiting the nucleus in specific paths.

24. Which principle states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of quantum numbers?

  1. Aufbau principle
  2. Hund's rule
  3. Pauli's exclusion principle
  4. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

Answer: Pauli's exclusion principle

Explanation: Pauli's exclusion principle states that no two electrons can have identical quantum numbers.

25. Assertion: Atomic spectra are determined by eigenvalue problems. Reason: Schrödinger proposed the wave equation.

  1. Both A and R true, R explains A
  2. Both true, R doesn't explain A
  3. A true, R false
  4. A false, R true

Answer: Both A and R true, R explains A

Explanation: Schrödinger's wave equation explains atomic spectra as eigenvalue problems.
