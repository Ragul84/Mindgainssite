Class12 MCQs with answers

1. Which institute is known for research in peptides and peptidomimetics?

  1. Indian Institute of Science (IISC)
  2. Indian Institute of Technology (IITM)
  3. National Chemical Laboratory (NCL)
  4. Indian Institute of Chemical Technology (IICT)

Answer: Indian Institute of Science (IISC)

Explanation: The Indian Institute of Science (IISC) in Bengaluru is known for its research in peptides and peptidomimetics.

2. What is the primary focus of the National Chemical Laboratory (NCL)?

  1. Catalysis and soft matter
  2. Energy and fuel research
  3. Molecular engineering of materials
  4. Chemical looping combustion

Answer: Catalysis and soft matter

Explanation: The National Chemical Laboratory (NCL) focuses on catalysis and soft matter, as highlighted in the textbook excerpts.

3. Which concept classifies acids and bases based on electron pair donation and acceptance?

  1. Arrhenius concept
  2. Lowry-Bronsted concept
  3. Lewis concept
  4. Ostwald’s dilution Law

Answer: Lewis concept

Explanation: The Lewis concept defines acids as electron pair acceptors and bases as electron pair donors, distinguishing it from other acid-base theories.

4. What does Ostwald’s dilution Law primarily describe?

  1. Ionisation of water
  2. Relationship between pH and pOH
  3. Dissociation constant of weak electrolytes
  4. Classification of acids and bases

Answer: Dissociation constant of weak electrolytes

Explanation: Ostwald’s dilution Law establishes the relationship between the dissociation constant (K) and the degree of dissociation (α) of a weak electrolyte.

5. Which of the following concepts is used to classify substances into acids and bases based on electron pair donation or acceptance?

  1. Arrhenius concept
  2. Lowry-Bronsted concept
  3. Lewis concept
  4. Ostwald’s dilution Law

Answer: Lewis concept

Explanation: The Lewis concept classifies acids as electron pair acceptors and bases as electron pair donors, distinguishing it from Arrhenius and Lowry-Bronsted concepts.

6. Which concept classifies substances into acids and bases based on proton donation and acceptance?

  1. Arrhenius concept
  2. Lowry-Bronsted concept
  3. Lewis concept
  4. Ostwald’s dilution Law

Answer: Lowry-Bronsted concept

Explanation: The Lowry-Bronsted concept defines acids as proton donors and bases as proton acceptors, distinguishing it from other acid-base theories.

7. Which of the following concepts classifies substances into acids and bases based on electron pair donation and acceptance?

  1. Arrhenius concept
  2. Lowry-Bronsted concept
  3. Lewis concept
  4. Ostwald’s dilution Law

Answer: Lewis concept

Explanation: The Lewis concept defines acids as electron pair acceptors and bases as electron pair donors, distinguishing it from other acid-base theories.

8. Which of the following research areas is NOT associated with the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru?

  1. Peptides and peptidomimetics
  2. Carbohydrate-protein interactions
  3. Chemical looping combustion (CLC)
  4. Functional soft and hybrid materials

Answer: Chemical looping combustion (CLC)

Explanation: Chemical looping combustion (CLC) is associated with the National Chemical Laboratory (NCL), Pune, not IISc, Bengaluru.

9. According to the learning objectives, which concept is used to classify substances into acids and bases?

  1. Arrhenius concept
  2. Lowry-Bronsted concept
  3. Lewis concept
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: Substances are classified into acids and bases based on Arrhenius, Lowry-Bronsted, and Lewis concepts.

10. வேகக் கணிதத்தில், 'first order reaction' என்றால் என்ன?

  1. வேகம் பொருளின் அளவுக்கு சார்ந்தது
  2. வேகம் நிலையானது
  3. வேகம் அதிகரிக்கிறது
  4. வேகம் குறைகிறது

Answer: வேகம் பொருளின் அளவுக்கு சார்ந்தது

Explanation: First order reaction இல் வேகம் பொருளின் அளவுக்கு சார்ந்தது.

11. வேகக் கணிதத்தில், 'second order reaction' என்றால் என்ன?

  1. வேகம் பொருளின் அளவுக்கு சார்ந்தது

12. What is metallurgy primarily concerned with?

  1. The study of metals
  2. The study of non-metals
  3. The study of organic compounds
  4. The study of gases

Answer: The study of metals

Explanation: Metallurgy is the science and technology of metals.

13. Which of the following metals is NOT typically found in its native state?

  1. Gold
  2. Silver
  3. Copper
  4. Iron

Answer: Iron

Explanation: Iron is usually found in a combined state, such as in ores.

14. What does the Ellingham diagram summarize?

  1. Thermodynamic stability of metals
  2. Electrochemical properties of metals
  3. Extraction methods of metals
  4. Concentration of ores

Answer: Thermodynamic stability of metals

Explanation: The Ellingham diagram summarizes the thermodynamic stability of various metal oxides.

15. Which principle is applied in the extraction of metals?

  1. Thermodynamic principles
  2. Biochemical principles
  3. Physical principles
  4. Optical principles

Answer: Thermodynamic principles

Explanation: Thermodynamic principles are crucial in metallurgical processes.

17. Which of the following is a method of concentrating ores?

  1. Flotation
  2. Distillation
  3. Filtration
  4. Evaporation

Answer: Flotation

Explanation: Flotation is a common method used to concentrate ores.

  3. A true R false
  4. A false R true

19. Which metal is extracted using the electrochemical principles?

  1. Iron
  2. Copper
  3. Gold
  4. Aluminum

Answer: Aluminum

Explanation: Aluminum is commonly extracted using electrochemical principles.

20. True or False: Only a few metals occur in their native state in nature.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: It is true that only a few metals occur in their native state.

21. Match List I with List II: List I (Metal) A. Gold B. Copper C. Zinc D. Silver E. Aluminium List II (Use) 1. Photography 2. Electrical wiring 3. Galvanization 4. Jewelry 5. Aircraft manufacturing

  1. A-4, B-2, C-3, D-1, E-5
  2. A-4, B-2, C-1, D-3, E-5
  3. A-4, B-2, C-3, D-5, E-1
  4. A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2, E-5

Answer: A-4, B-2, C-3, D-1, E-5
