Class12 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class12 questions to revise the Samacheer Physics syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 392 questions.

1. Who invented the first alternating current (AC) motor?

  1. Nikola Tesla
  2. Thomas Edison
  3. Michael Faraday
  4. James Clerk Maxwell

Answer: Nikola Tesla

Explanation: Nikola Tesla made breakthroughs in the production, transmission, and application of electric power, including inventing the first AC motor.

2. Which unit in the Physics Volume II covers 'Ray Optics'?

  1. Unit 6
  2. Unit 7
  3. Unit 8
  4. Unit 9

Answer: Unit 6

Explanation: Unit 6 in Physics Volume II is dedicated to 'Ray Optics', as per the textbook contents.

3. Which unit in the Class 12 Physics syllabus covers 'Ray Optics'?

  1. Unit 6
  2. Unit 7
  3. Unit 8
  4. Unit 9

Answer: Unit 6

Explanation: Unit 6 in the Class 12 Physics syllabus covers 'Ray Optics'.

4. Which unit in the Class 12 Physics syllabus covers 'Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter'?

  1. Unit 6
  2. Unit 7
  3. Unit 8
  4. Unit 9

Answer: Unit 8

Explanation: Unit 8 in the Class 12 Physics syllabus covers 'Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter'.

5. Who is credited with inventing the first alternating current (AC) motor?

  1. Nikola Tesla
  2. Thomas Edison
  3. Michael Faraday
  4. James Clerk Maxwell

Answer: Nikola Tesla

Explanation: Nikola Tesla invented the first alternating current (AC) motor and developed AC generation and transmission technology.

6. Which of the following phenomena is primarily responsible for the generation of alternating current (AC) in a power system?

  1. Electromagnetic Induction
  2. Electrostatic Induction
  3. Thermionic Emission
  4. Photoelectric Effect

Answer: Electromagnetic Induction

Explanation: Alternating current (AC) is generated primarily through electromagnetic induction, where a changing magnetic field induces an electric current in a conductor.

7. In the context of atomic and nuclear physics, which particle is responsible for mediating the strong nuclear force between nucleons?

  1. Gluon
  2. Photon
  3. W Boson
  4. Neutrino

Answer: Gluon

Explanation: The strong nuclear force, which holds protons and neutrons together in the nucleus, is mediated by particles called gluons.

8. In the context of atomic and nuclear physics, what is the primary reason for the stability of a nucleus?

  1. Strong nuclear force
  2. Electromagnetic force
  3. Gravitational force
  4. Weak nuclear force

Answer: Strong nuclear force

Explanation: The strong nuclear force is the fundamental force that binds protons and neutrons together in the nucleus, overcoming the repulsive electromagnetic force between protons.

9. Which of the following phenomena is primarily responsible for the generation of alternating current (AC) in electromagnetic induction?

  1. Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction
  2. Lenz's Law
  3. Ampere's Law
  4. Gauss's Law

Answer: Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

Explanation: Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction states that a change in magnetic flux through a coil induces an electromotive force (EMF), which is the fundamental principle behind the generation of alternating current (AC).

10. In the context of atomic and nuclear physics, which particle is emitted during beta decay?

  1. Electron
  2. Proton
  3. Neutron
  4. Alpha Particle

Answer: Electron

Explanation: During beta decay, a neutron in the nucleus is transformed into a proton, and an electron (beta particle) is emitted along with an antineutrino.

11. Which of the following phenomena is primarily responsible for the generation of alternating current in an AC generator?

  1. Electromagnetic Induction
  2. Thermoelectric Effect
  3. Photoelectric Effect
  4. Hall Effect

Answer: Electromagnetic Induction

Explanation: Alternating current in an AC generator is generated through electromagnetic induction, where a changing magnetic field induces an electric current in a conductor.

12. Which of the following is a key contribution of Nikola Tesla in the field of physics?

  1. Development of alternating current (AC) motor
  2. Discovery of electromagnetic waves
  3. Invention of the transistor
  4. Formulation of the theory of relativity

Answer: Development of alternating current (AC) motor

Explanation: Nikola Tesla is renowned for his development of the alternating current (AC) motor, which revolutionized the transmission and application of electric power.

13. In the context of atomic and nuclear physics, which phenomenon is primarily responsible for the emission of alpha particles?

  1. Nuclear fission
  2. Nuclear fusion
  3. Alpha decay
  4. Beta decay

Answer: Alpha decay

Explanation: Alpha decay is the process by which an unstable atomic nucleus emits an alpha particle, consisting of two protons and two neutrons, to become more stable.

14. Which of the following series of the hydrogen spectrum lies in the visible region?

  1. Lyman series
  2. Balmer series
  3. Paschen series
  4. Brackett series

Answer: Balmer series

Explanation: The Balmer series corresponds to transitions to n=2 and falls in the visible spectrum.

15. The radius of the nth orbit in Bohr's model is proportional to:

  1. n
  2. n^2
  3. 1/n
  4. 1/n^2

Answer: n^2

Explanation: According to Bohr's model, r = n^2 a0.

16. The energy of a photon is given by the formula:

  1. E = hf
  2. E = h/f
  3. E = mc^2
  4. E = hλ

Answer: E = hf

Explanation: Energy is the product of Planck's constant and frequency.

17. The de Broglie wavelength of an electron is inversely proportional to its:

  1. Mass
  2. Velocity
  3. Momentum
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: λ = h/mv; thus it is inversely proportional to mass, velocity, and momentum.

18. The binding energy per nucleon is maximum for which nucleus?

  1. Helium
  2. Iron
  3. Uranium
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Iron

Explanation: Iron (Fe-56) has the highest binding energy per nucleon, making it most stable.

19. Which particle is emitted during beta-minus decay?

  1. Proton
  2. Positron
  3. Electron
  4. Neutron

Answer: Electron

Explanation: Beta-minus decay involves the emission of an electron and an antineutrino.

20. The half-life of a radioactive element depends on:

  1. Temperature
  2. Pressure
  3. Nature of the element
  4. Amount of substance

Answer: Nature of the element

Explanation: Half-life is a characteristic property of the radioactive isotope.

21. The mass defect is converted into:

  1. Kinetic energy
  2. Binding energy
  3. Potential energy
  4. Thermal energy

Answer: Binding energy

Explanation: Mass defect is converted into binding energy via E=mc^2.

22. The ratio of the radius of the 2nd orbit to the 1st orbit in a hydrogen atom is:

  1. 2:1
  2. 4:1
  3. 1:2
  4. 1:4

Answer: 4:1

Explanation: r is proportional to n^2, so (2^2)/(1^2) = 4.

23. Which of the following is a characteristic of nuclear fusion?

  1. Occurs in heavy nuclei
  2. Requires low temperature
  3. Releases less energy than fission
  4. Occurs in stars

Answer: Occurs in stars

Explanation: Nuclear fusion powers stars like the Sun.

24. The work function of a metal depends on:

  1. Intensity of light
  2. Frequency of light
  3. Nature of the metal
  4. Surface area

Answer: Nature of the metal

Explanation: Work function is a material property specific to the metal.

25. The energy of an electron in the ground state of hydrogen is:

  1. -13.6 eV
  2. 13.6 eV
  3. -3.4 eV
  4. 0 eV

Answer: -13.6 eV

Explanation: The ground state energy of hydrogen is -13.6 eV.
