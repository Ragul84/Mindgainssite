Class10 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class10 questions to revise the Samacheer Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 204 questions.

1. Who is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. B.R. Ambedkar
  4. Subhas Chandra Bose

Answer: B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: B.R. Ambedkar is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution' for his pivotal role in drafting the Constitution.

2. Which article of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Equality?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32

Answer: Article 14

Explanation: Article 14 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Equality.

3. The Indian Parliament consists of how many houses?

  1. One
  2. Two
  3. Three
  4. Four

Answer: Two

Explanation: The Indian Parliament consists of two houses: the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha.

4. Which of the following is NOT a fundamental right in the Indian Constitution?

  1. Right to Freedom of Speech
  2. Right to Education
  3. Right to Property
  4. Right to Equality

Answer: Right to Property

Explanation: The Right to Property was removed from the list of fundamental rights and is now a legal right.

5. Which of the following is NOT a feature of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Federal System
  2. Parliamentary Democracy
  3. Unitary System
  4. Fundamental Rights

Answer: Unitary System

Explanation: The Indian Constitution is federal in nature, not unitary.

6. Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the Right to Equality?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32

Answer: Article 14

Explanation: Article 14 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Equality to all citizens.

7. The Directive Principles of State Policy are inspired by the Constitution of which country?

  1. USA
  2. UK
  3. Ireland
  4. Canada

Answer: Ireland

Explanation: The Directive Principles of State Policy in the Indian Constitution are inspired by the Constitution of Ireland.

8. Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the Right to Education?

  1. Article 21A
  2. Article 14
  3. Article 19
  4. Article 32

Answer: Article 21A

Explanation: Article 21A was inserted by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002, making the Right to Education a fundamental right for children aged 6 to 14.

9. Which part of the Indian Constitution deals with Citizenship?

  1. Part I
  2. Part II
  3. Part III
  4. Part IV

Answer: Part II

Explanation: Part II of the Indian Constitution (Articles 5 to 11) deals with citizenship.

10. Who has the power to regulate the right of citizenship by law in India?

  1. The President
  2. The Supreme Court
  3. The Parliament
  4. The Election Commission

Answer: The Parliament

Explanation: Article 11 empowers the Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law.

11. The Citizenship Act was passed by the Indian Parliament in which year?

  1. 1947
  2. 1950
  3. 1952
  4. 1955

Answer: 1955

Explanation: The Citizenship Act was passed in 1955.

12. Which of the following is NOT a way to acquire Indian citizenship under the Citizenship Act 1955?

  1. By Birth
  2. By Naturalisation
  3. By Inheritance
  4. By Registration

Answer: By Inheritance

Explanation: Inheritance is not a legal method; the methods are Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, and Incorporation of Territory.

13. What is the minimum residency requirement for citizenship by naturalisation for a foreign national?

  1. 5 years
  2. 7 years
  3. 10 years
  4. 12 years

Answer: 10 years

Explanation: A person must have resided in India for 12 months immediately preceding the application and 11 out of the previous 14 years.

14. Which of the following is a way to lose Indian citizenship?

  1. Renunciation
  2. Registration
  3. Naturalisation
  4. Incorporation

Answer: Renunciation

Explanation: Renunciation is the voluntary giving up of citizenship.

15. Consider the following statements: 1. India provides single citizenship to all its citizens. 2. A person can hold both Indian and American citizenship simultaneously. Which of the statements is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: India follows single citizenship; dual citizenship is not permitted.

16. Consider these statements regarding citizenship by descent: 1. A person born outside India can be a citizen if the father was a citizen at the time of birth. 2. This applies regardless of whether the birth was registered with the consulate. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Registration with the Indian consulate is mandatory for citizenship by descent.

17. Regarding the Citizenship Act 1955, consider: 1. Citizenship can be acquired by registration. 2. Registration is available to Persons of Indian Origin (PIO). Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Both statements are correct as per the 1955 Act.

18. Consider the following: 1. Termination occurs when a person voluntarily acquires citizenship of another country. 2. Deprivation is a compulsory termination by the government. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Termination is voluntary/automatic; Deprivation is a penalty imposed by the government.

19. Consider these statements about the OCI card: 1. OCI stands for Overseas Citizen of India. 2. OCI holders have the right to vote in Indian elections. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: OCI holders do not have voting rights or the right to hold public office.

20. Consider the following: 1. Citizenship by birth applies to those born in India on or after Jan 26, 1950. 2. Both parents must be citizens for the child to be a citizen by birth. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: The rules for birth citizenship changed over time; both parents being citizens is not always a requirement for birth in India.

21. Consider the following: 1. Incorporation of territory means adding a new land to India. 2. People normally resident in such territory become Indian citizens. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Both statements correctly describe citizenship by incorporation of territory.

22. Consider the following statements: 1. The Constitution provides permanent rules for citizenship. 2. The Constitution only provides citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: The Constitution only dealt with citizenship at the start; subsequent rules are governed by the 1955 Act.

23. Consider these statements: 1. A citizen can renounce citizenship by making a declaration. 2. Renunciation is an irrevocable act. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: Renunciation is the act of giving up citizenship, but it can be reclaimed through legal processes.

24. Consider the following: 1. Naturalisation requires the applicant to be of good character. 2. Knowledge of a language specified in the 8th Schedule is required. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: Both 1 and 2

Explanation: Both good character and language proficiency are requirements for naturalisation.

25. Consider these statements: 1. Single citizenship promotes national integration. 2. India's single citizenship is borrowed from the USA. Which is correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: 1 only

Explanation: India's single citizenship is borrowed from Britain, not the USA (which has dual citizenship).
