Class8 MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Class8 questions to revise the Samacheer Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 178 questions.

1. Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the Right to Equality?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32

Answer: Article 14

Explanation: Article 14 of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Equality to all citizens.

2. Who is known as the Father of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. B.R. Ambedkar
  4. Sardar Patel

Answer: B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is widely regarded as the Father of the Indian Constitution for his pivotal role in its drafting.

3. Which of the following is NOT a fundamental right under the Indian Constitution?

  1. Right to Freedom of Speech
  2. Right to Education
  3. Right to Property
  4. Right to Equality

Answer: Right to Property

Explanation: The Right to Property was removed from the list of fundamental rights and made a legal right under the 44th Amendment.

4. The Indian Constitution was adopted on which date?

  1. 26 January 1950
  2. 15 August 1947
  3. 26 November 1949
  4. 2 October 1949

Answer: 26 November 1949

Explanation: The Indian Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into effect on 26 January 1950.

5. Which of the following is a feature of the Indian Constitution?

  1. Unitary System
  2. Federal System
  3. Monarchy
  4. Theocracy

Answer: Federal System

Explanation: The Indian Constitution establishes a federal system of government with a strong central authority.

6. Which article of the Indian Constitution guarantees the Right to Equality?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32

Answer: Article 14

Explanation: Article 14 of the Indian Constitution ensures the Right to Equality to all citizens.

7. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution begins with the words:

  1. We, the People of India
  2. In the Name of God
  3. Justice, Liberty, Equality
  4. Sovereign, Socialist, Secular

Answer: We, the People of India

Explanation: The Preamble of the Indian Constitution starts with 'We, the People of India'.

8. Which of the following is NOT a fundamental duty under the Indian Constitution?

  1. To uphold and protect the sovereignty of India
  2. To safeguard public property
  3. To pay taxes
  4. To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood

Answer: To pay taxes

Explanation: Paying taxes is a legal obligation but not listed as a fundamental duty under Article 51A of the Indian Constitution.

9. Which of the following is a primary duty of a citizen according to the Indian Constitution?

  1. Avoiding all political activity
  2. Respecting the National Flag and National Anthem
  3. Paying taxes only when requested
  4. Ignoring local government laws

Answer: Respecting the National Flag and National Anthem

Explanation: Respecting national symbols is a fundamental duty of every Indian citizen.

10. What is the minimum age for a citizen to vote in the General Elections in India?

  1. 16 years
  2. 18 years
  3. 21 years
  4. 25 years

Answer: 18 years

Explanation: The voting age was lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment Act.

11. Which document provides the legal framework for the acquisition and termination of citizenship in India?

  1. The Indian Penal Code
  2. The Citizenship Act 1955
  3. The Right to Education Act
  4. The Consumer Protection Act

Answer: The Citizenship Act 1955

Explanation: The Citizenship Act of 1955 governs the rules for citizenship in India.

12. Which of the following is a characteristic of a 'Global Citizen'?

  1. Loyalty to only one's own nation
  2. Awareness of global issues and shared responsibility
  3. Refusal to follow international laws
  4. Avoiding interaction with other cultures

Answer: Awareness of global issues and shared responsibility

Explanation: Global citizenship involves understanding the interconnectedness of the world.

13. Consider the following statements: 1. Citizenship gives a person the right to vote. 2. All residents of a country are automatically citizens. 3. Citizenship provides protection by the state. Which of the statements above are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: Residents are not automatically citizens; citizenship is a legal status.

14. Consider the following statements regarding the acquisition of citizenship: 1. Citizenship by birth is one way to acquire citizenship. 2. Citizenship by naturalization is only for those born in India. 3. Registration is a method for people from certain countries to become citizens. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 1 and 3 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. All of the above

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: Naturalization is specifically for foreigners who meet certain criteria, not those born in India.

15. Consider these statements about Fundamental Duties: 1. They were added by the 42nd Amendment Act. 2. They are legally enforceable in a court of law. 3. They encourage a sense of patriotism. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. All of the above

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: Fundamental duties are moral obligations and are generally not legally enforceable.

16. Consider the following statements about Dual Citizenship: 1. India allows dual citizenship for all its citizens. 2. Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI) is a special category. 3. OCI holders have the right to vote in Indian elections. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 3 only

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: India does not allow dual citizenship; OCI is a status, not full citizenship, and OCI holders cannot vote.

17. Consider these statements regarding the rights of citizens: 1. Right to freedom of speech is a fundamental right. 2. Right to vote is a constitutional right. 3. Right to property is currently a fundamental right. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. All of the above

Answer: 1 and 2 only

Explanation: Right to property is a legal right, not a fundamental right.

18. Consider the following statements about the loss of citizenship: 1. Renunciation is a voluntary act of giving up citizenship. 2. Termination occurs when a person acquires citizenship of another country. 3. Deprivation is a compulsory removal by the government for fraud. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. All of the above

Answer: All of the above

Explanation: All three are valid ways citizenship can be lost.

19. Consider these statements about the relationship between rights and duties: 1. Rights and duties are complementary to each other. 2. One person's right is another person's duty. 3. Rights can exist without any corresponding duties. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. All of the above

Answer: 1 and 2 only

Explanation: Rights and duties are interdependent; rights cannot exist without duties.

20. Consider the following statements on the role of a citizen in a democracy: 1. Voting is the only way to participate in democracy. 2. Paying taxes is a duty of a citizen. 3. Participating in community service is a civic responsibility. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. All of the above

Answer: 2 and 3 only

Explanation: Voting is one way, but not the only way (e.g., protests, petitions, community work).

21. Assertion: A citizen has a higher legal status than a resident. Reason: Citizenship grants the right to hold public office and vote in elections.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Explanation: Citizenship provides political rights that residents do not possess.

22. Assertion: Global citizenship is essential in the modern era. Reason: Problems like climate change and pandemics require international cooperation.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

Explanation: Global challenges necessitate a mindset of global citizenship.

23. Citizenship is a legal relationship between an individual and a state.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: True

Explanation: This is the basic definition of citizenship.

24. The Right to Information (RTI) is a duty of the citizen.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: RTI is a right, not a duty.

25. Naturalization is the process of acquiring citizenship for a person born in the country.

  1. True
  2. False

Answer: False

Explanation: Naturalization is for foreigners; birth is for those born in the country.
