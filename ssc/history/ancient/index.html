Ancient MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Ancient questions to revise the SSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Who was the first ruler of the Maurya Empire?

  1. Ashoka
  2. Chandragupta Maurya
  3. Bindusara
  4. Bimbisara

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire around 321 BCE with the help of Chanakya (Kautilya).

2. The ancient city of Mohenjo-daro is located in present-day:

  1. India
  2. Pakistan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. Nepal

Answer: Pakistan

Explanation: Mohenjo-daro is located in Sindh province of present-day Pakistan. It was one of the major centres of the Indus Valley Civilization.

3. The Indus Valley Civilization is also known as:

  1. Vedic Civilization
  2. Harappan Civilization
  3. Aryan Civilization
  4. Dravidian Civilization

Answer: Harappan Civilization

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilization is also called Harappan Civilization, named after Harappa — one of its major sites (in present-day Pakistan), excavated in 1921.

4. Who wrote 'Arthashastra', a treatise on statecraft and economic policy?

  1. Kalidasa
  2. Chanakya (Kautilya)
  3. Patanjali
  4. Vatsyayana

Answer: Chanakya (Kautilya)

Explanation: Arthashastra was written by Chanakya (Kautilya), the chief minister of Chandragupta Maurya. It is an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and military strategy.

5. The Indus Valley Civilisation is also called:

  1. Vedic Civilisation
  2. Harappan Civilisation
  3. Dravidian Civilisation
  4. Gangetic Civilisation

Answer: Harappan Civilisation

Explanation: The Indus Valley Civilisation is also called the Harappan Civilisation, named after Harappa — the first site to be excavated (1921). It was one of the four ancient river valley civilisations.

6. Harappa was excavated for the first time by:

  1. John Marshall
  2. R.D. Banerji
  3. Daya Ram Sahni
  4. Mortimer Wheeler

Answer: Daya Ram Sahni

Explanation: Harappa was first excavated by Daya Ram Sahni in 1921. Mohenjo-daro was excavated by R.D. Banerji in 1922. Sir John Marshall was the Director General of ASI who coordinated the excavations.

7. Mohenjo-daro is located in present-day:

  1. India (Gujarat)
  2. India (Rajasthan)
  3. Pakistan (Sindh)
  4. Pakistan (Punjab)

Answer: Pakistan (Sindh)

Explanation: Mohenjo-daro (meaning 'Mound of the Dead') is located in Sindh province, Pakistan, on the right bank of the Indus River. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

8. Which Indus Valley site is famous for a dockyard?

  1. Harappa
  2. Mohenjo-daro
  3. Lothal
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Lothal

Explanation: Lothal (Gujarat) had a unique dockyard (214m × 36m) — the earliest known in the world. It connected to the Sabarmati river, suggesting advanced maritime trade. Also famous for bead-making factories.

9. The Indus Valley people worshipped:

  1. Only male gods
  2. Mother goddess, Pashupati (proto-Shiva), and animals like bull and unicorn
  3. The sun and moon exclusively
  4. No deities were found

Answer: Mother goddess, Pashupati (proto-Shiva), and animals like bull and unicorn

Explanation: IVC people worshipped a Mother Goddess (fertility), Pashupati (a seated figure surrounded by animals — proto-Shiva), trees (peepal), and animals. Figurines and seals show these religious elements.

10. The script of the Indus Valley Civilisation is:

  1. Brahmi
  2. Kharosthi
  3. Undeciphered pictographic script
  4. Devanagari

Answer: Undeciphered pictographic script

Explanation: The Indus script is a pictographic/logo-syllabic script that has not been deciphered. Over 400 signs have been identified. Without a bilingual inscription like the Rosetta Stone, scholars cannot read it.

11. Kalibangan, an Indus Valley site, is located in:

  1. Gujarat
  2. Punjab
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Haryana

Answer: Rajasthan

Explanation: Kalibangan is located in Hanumangarh district, Rajasthan, on the Ghaggar (ancient Saraswati) river. Famous for fire altars, ploughed fields (earliest evidence of ploughing), and pre-Harappan settlement.

12. The Indus Valley people traded with:

  1. China and Japan
  2. Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) — seals found in Ur and other Mesopotamian sites
  3. Egypt and Greece
  4. No external trade evidence

Answer: Mesopotamia (present-day Iraq) — seals found in Ur and other Mesopotamian sites

Explanation: IVC traded with Mesopotamia (Sumer, Akkad). Harappan seals, weights, and carnelian beads found at Ur. Mesopotamian texts mention 'Meluhha' — believed to refer to the Indus region.

13. Which crop was NOT cultivated by the Indus Valley people?

  1. Wheat
  2. Barley
  3. Rice (at some sites)
  4. Maize

Answer: Maize

Explanation: Maize is a New World crop that reached India only after Columbus (post-1492). Indus people cultivated wheat, barley, cotton (first in the world!), rice (Lothal), dates, sesame, and mustard.

14. Cotton was first cultivated in the world by:

  1. Egyptians
  2. Mesopotamians
  3. Indus Valley people
  4. Chinese

Answer: Indus Valley people

Explanation: Cotton was first cultivated by the Indus Valley people around 3000 BCE. They also produced cotton textiles. Mesopotamians called it 'sindon' (from Sindh). India remained a major cotton producer throughout history.

15. The Later Vedic period saw the shift of Aryan culture from Punjab to:

  1. Deccan plateau
  2. Gangetic plain (Doab region)
  3. Bengal
  4. South India

Answer: Gangetic plain (Doab region)

Explanation: In the Later Vedic period (c. 1000–600 BCE), the centre of Aryan culture shifted from the Punjab to the Gangetic plain (between Ganga and Yamuna), known as the Doab or 'Madhyadesha'.

16. The Varna system in early Vedic society was based on:

  1. Birth
  2. Occupation (karma) — it was not hereditary initially
  3. Colour of skin
  4. Wealth

Answer: Occupation (karma) — it was not hereditary initially

Explanation: In the Rigvedic period, the varna system was based on occupation, not birth. The four varnas were Brahmin (priest), Kshatriya (warrior), Vaishya (trader), Shudra (servant). Hereditary rigidity came in the Later Vedic period.

17. The founder of Jainism is considered to be:

  1. Mahavira
  2. Parshvanatha
  3. Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)
  4. Neminatha

Answer: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha)

Explanation: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha) is considered the first Tirthankara and founder of Jainism. Mahavira (599–527 BCE) was the 24th and last Tirthankara who revived and systematised Jainism.

18. The five vows (Pancha Mahavrata) of Jainism include all EXCEPT:

  1. Ahimsa (non-violence)
  2. Satya (truth)
  3. Asteya (non-stealing)
  4. Ahara (food consumption)

Answer: Ahara (food consumption)

Explanation: The five Mahavrata of Jainism: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya (non-theft), Brahmacharya (celibacy), and Aparigraha (non-possession). 'Ahara' (food) is not one of them. Brahmacharya was added by Mahavira to Parshvanatha's four vows.

19. Jainism is divided into two sects:

  1. Hinayana and Mahayana
  2. Digambara and Shvetambara
  3. Theravada and Vajrayana
  4. Shaiva and Vaishnava

Answer: Digambara and Shvetambara

Explanation: After Mahavira's death, Jainism split into: Digambara ('sky-clad' — monks go naked) and Shvetambara ('white-clad' — monks wear white). The split occurred at the Pataliputra council (c. 300 BCE) due to a famine.

20. Gautama Buddha was born at:

  1. Bodh Gaya
  2. Sarnath
  3. Lumbini (in present-day Nepal)
  4. Kushinagar

Answer: Lumbini (in present-day Nepal)

Explanation: Siddhartha Gautama was born c. 563 BCE at Lumbini (present-day Nepal) in the Shakya clan. His birth, enlightenment, first sermon, and Mahaparinirvana are the four most sacred events in Buddhism.

21. Buddha attained enlightenment (Nirvana/Bodhi) at:

  1. Lumbini
  2. Sarnath
  3. Bodh Gaya (under the Bodhi tree)
  4. Kushinagar

Answer: Bodh Gaya (under the Bodhi tree)

Explanation: Siddhartha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya (formerly Uruvela/Gaya), Bihar, under the Peepal tree (Bodhi tree) on the bank of the Niranjana river at the age of 35.

22. The first sermon of the Buddha is called:

  1. Mahaparinibbana
  2. Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Turning of the Wheel of Dharma)
  3. Tripitaka
  4. Jataka

Answer: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Turning of the Wheel of Dharma)

Explanation: The Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Setting the Wheel of Dharma in Motion) was the first sermon delivered at Sarnath (Deer Park/Isipatana) to the five ascetics. This event is called 'Dharma-chakra-pravartana'.

23. The Buddhist councils were held to:

  1. Elect new kings
  2. Compile, recite, and standardise Buddhist scriptures
  3. Discuss political matters
  4. Spread Buddhism outside India

Answer: Compile, recite, and standardise Buddhist scriptures

Explanation: Four major Buddhist Councils: 1st at Rajgriha (483 BCE), 2nd at Vaishali (383 BCE), 3rd at Pataliputra (250 BCE, Ashoka's reign), 4th at Kundalvana, Kashmir (1st century CE, Kanishka's reign).

24. Which Buddhist council was held during the reign of Ashoka?

  1. 1st Council (Rajgriha)
  2. 2nd Council (Vaishali)
  3. 3rd Council (Pataliputra)
  4. 4th Council (Kashmir)

Answer: 3rd Council (Pataliputra)

Explanation: The 3rd Buddhist Council was held at Pataliputra (c. 250 BCE) under the patronage of Emperor Ashoka, presided over by Moggaliputta Tissa. The Pali Canon (Tipitaka) was finalised here.

25. The Buddhist scriptures are collectively called:

  1. Vedas
  2. Tipitaka (Three Baskets) — Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma Pitakas
  3. Jataka Tales
  4. Dhammapada

Answer: Tipitaka (Three Baskets) — Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma Pitakas

Explanation: Tipitaka (Pali) or Tripitaka (Sanskrit) = Three Baskets: Vinaya Pitaka (monastic rules), Sutta Pitaka (discourses of Buddha), Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical analysis). Written in Pali.
