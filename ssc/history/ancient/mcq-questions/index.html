Ancient MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Ancient questions to revise the SSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. Chandragupta Maurya established the Mauryan Empire in:

  1. 321 BCE
  2. 305 BCE
  3. 273 BCE
  4. 232 BCE

Answer: 321 BCE

Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire in 321 BCE after defeating the last Nanda king (Dhana Nanda) with the help of Chanakya. He made Pataliputra (modern Patna) his capital.

27. Chanakya (Kautilya) authored:

  1. Arthashastra
  2. Indica
  3. Mudrarakshasa
  4. Rajatarangini

Answer: Arthashastra

Explanation: Chanakya (also Kautilya or Vishnugupta) wrote the Arthashastra — a comprehensive treatise on statecraft, economics, military strategy, and diplomacy. It is sometimes called 'the world's first political science text'.

28. The Greek ambassador Megasthenes visited the court of:

  1. Ashoka
  2. Bindusara
  3. Chandragupta Maurya
  4. Chandragupta II

Answer: Chandragupta Maurya

Explanation: Megasthenes (c. 350–290 BCE) was the Greek ambassador sent by Seleucus I Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya. His account 'Indica' (mostly lost, known through fragments) described Mauryan India.

29. Ashoka's policy of Dhamma was inspired by:

  1. Jainism only
  2. Buddhism (after the Kalinga War in 261 BCE)
  3. Vedic religion
  4. Ajivika sect

Answer: Buddhism (after the Kalinga War in 261 BCE)

Explanation: The Kalinga War (261 BCE, 100,000 killed, 150,000 displaced) deeply affected Ashoka. He embraced Buddhism, renounced war, and adopted a policy of Dhamma — moral governance, non-violence, tolerance, and welfare.

30. The Mauryan Empire had its capital at:

  1. Taxila
  2. Ujjain
  3. Pataliputra (modern Patna)
  4. Vaishali

Answer: Pataliputra (modern Patna)

Explanation: Pataliputra (modern Patna, Bihar) was the capital of the Mauryan Empire. Megasthenes described it as a magnificent city surrounded by a wooden wall with 64 gates and 570 towers.

31. The last Mauryan emperor was:

  1. Ashoka
  2. Dasharatha
  3. Brihadratha
  4. Samprati

Answer: Brihadratha

Explanation: Brihadratha was the last Mauryan emperor. He was assassinated by his commander-in-chief Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BCE, ending the Mauryan dynasty and starting the Shunga dynasty.

32. The Shunga dynasty was founded by:

  1. Vasudeva Shunga
  2. Pushyamitra Shunga
  3. Agnimitra
  4. Devabhuti

Answer: Pushyamitra Shunga

Explanation: Pushyamitra Shunga (185–149 BCE) founded the Shunga dynasty after assassinating Brihadratha, the last Mauryan emperor. He was a Brahmin and reversed some of Ashoka's Buddhist policies.

33. The Gandhara art style was a blend of:

  1. Indian and Chinese art
  2. Greek/Hellenistic and Indian (Buddhist) artistic traditions
  3. Persian and Indian art
  4. Roman and Indian art exclusively

Answer: Greek/Hellenistic and Indian (Buddhist) artistic traditions

Explanation: Gandhara art (1st–5th century CE) blended Hellenistic/Greek realism with Buddhist iconography. It emerged in the Gandhara region (Afghanistan/Pakistan). First human images of the Buddha were made here.

34. The Harappan city known for its large dockyard is:

  1. Lothal
  2. Mohenjo-daro
  3. Harappa
  4. Kalibangan

Answer: Lothal

Explanation: Lothal in Gujarat had a well-planned dockyard (170m×37m), indicating maritime trade with Mesopotamia. It is the only Harappan site with a dockyard.

35. Great Bath was found at which Harappan site?

  1. Mohenjo-daro
  2. Harappa
  3. Lothal
  4. Dholavira

Answer: Mohenjo-daro

Explanation: The Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro (11.88m×7.01m) is the most striking feature of the Indus Valley Civilization, used for ritualistic bathing.

36. The Indus script is:

  1. Undeciphered
  2. Brahmi-based
  3. Sanskrit-derived
  4. Dravidian

Answer: Undeciphered

Explanation: The Indus script (Harappan script) remains undeciphered despite many attempts. It contains over 400 signs and was written right-to-left.

37. Which Harappan site is located in Gujarat and is known for its water conservation systems?

  1. Dholavira
  2. Lothal
  3. Surkotada
  4. Rangpur

Answer: Dholavira

Explanation: Dholavira in Gujarat is known for its elaborate water conservation system with large reservoirs, stepwells, and dams — unique among all Harappan sites.

38. The concept of 'Ahimsa' was first emphasized in:

  1. Jainism
  2. Buddhism
  3. Vedic texts
  4. Bhagavad Gita

Answer: Jainism

Explanation: Ahimsa (non-violence) was most strongly and systematically emphasized in Jainism, especially by Mahavira. It became central to Jain philosophy.

39. Sabha and Samiti in Vedic period were:

  1. Tribal assemblies
  2. Religious centers
  3. Military units
  4. Tax collection bodies

Answer: Tribal assemblies

Explanation: Sabha and Samiti were tribal assemblies in the Vedic period. Sabha was a smaller, exclusive council of elders; Samiti was a larger, more democratic gathering.

40. Iron was first used in India during:

  1. Later Vedic period
  2. Mauryan period
  3. Gupta period
  4. Early Vedic period

Answer: Later Vedic period

Explanation: Iron came into widespread use in India during the Later Vedic period (1000-600 BCE), transforming agriculture, warfare, and craftsmanship.

41. The first Tirthankara of Jainism is:

  1. Rishabhadeva
  2. Mahavira
  3. Parsvanatha
  4. Neminatha

Answer: Rishabhadeva

Explanation: Rishabhadeva (Adinatha) is the first Tirthankara (ford-maker) of Jainism. There are 24 Tirthankaras in total.

42. The principle of 'Anekantavada' is associated with:

  1. Jainism
  2. Buddhism
  3. Vedanta
  4. Lokayata

Answer: Jainism

Explanation: Anekantavada (many-sidedness of truth) is a Jain doctrine stating that truth and reality are complex, and no single perspective is complete.

43. Triratna of Jainism refers to:

  1. Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct
  2. Three Tirthankaras
  3. Three sacred texts
  4. Three monasteries

Answer: Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct

Explanation: Jain Triratna (Three Jewels): Right Faith (Samyak Darshan), Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana), Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra) — the path to liberation.

44. The First Buddhist Council was held at:

  1. Rajgriha
  2. Vaishali
  3. Pataliputra
  4. Kundalvana

Answer: Rajgriha

Explanation: First Buddhist Council (483 BCE) at Rajgriha (Sattapanni Cave), immediately after Buddha's death, presided over by Mahakassapa, compiled Vinaya Pitaka and Sutta Pitaka.

45. 'Dhammachakkapavattana Sutta' refers to:

  1. Buddha's first sermon
  2. Buddha's last words
  3. Buddhist monastery rules
  4. Jataka tales

Answer: Buddha's first sermon

Explanation: Dhammachakkapavattana Sutta is the record of Buddha's first sermon at Sarnath (Deer Park) to his five former companions — the 'Setting in Motion of the Wheel of Dharma'.

46. Bodhgaya is associated with:

  1. Buddha's enlightenment
  2. Buddha's birth
  3. Buddha's first sermon
  4. Buddha's death

Answer: Buddha's enlightenment

Explanation: Bodhgaya (Bihar) is where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree and became the Buddha. It is the holiest Buddhist pilgrimage site.

47. Nalanda University was established during:

  1. Gupta period
  2. Mauryan period
  3. Kushan period
  4. Pala period

Answer: Gupta period

Explanation: Nalanda University was established during the Gupta period (5th century CE, under Kumaragupta I) and flourished until it was destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khilji in 1193 CE.

48. Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nanda dynasty with the help of:

  1. Chanakya
  2. Seleucus
  3. Bindusara
  4. Ashoka

Answer: Chanakya

Explanation: Chanakya (Kautilya), the brilliant political strategist and author of Arthashastra, helped Chandragupta Maurya overthrow the last Nanda king (Dhana Nanda) around 321 BCE.

49. Kalinga was captured by Ashoka in:

  1. 261 BCE
  2. 273 BCE
  3. 232 BCE
  4. 269 BCE

Answer: 261 BCE

Explanation: Ashoka conquered Kalinga (modern Odisha) in 261 BCE (8th year of his reign), 13 years after becoming king. The massive bloodshed transformed him.

50. The term 'Dhamma' used by Ashoka was derived from:

  1. Pali
  2. Sanskrit
  3. Prakrit
  4. Tamil

Answer: Pali

Explanation: Dhamma is the Pali equivalent of Sanskrit 'Dharma'. Ashoka used Pali and Prakrit languages in his edicts to make them accessible to common people.
