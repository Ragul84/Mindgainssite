Modern MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Modern questions to revise the SSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. Which of the following acts established a system of 'dyarchy' in the provinces of British India?

  1. Indian Councils Act of 1892
  2. Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909
  3. Government of India Act of 1919
  4. Government of India Act of 1935

Answer: Government of India Act of 1919

Explanation: The Government of India Act of 1919 introduced the system of 'dyarchy' in the provinces, dividing subjects into 'reserved' and 'transferred' lists. Reserved subjects were administered by the Governor and his Executive Council, while transferred subjects were administered by the Governor acting with ministers responsible to the provincial legislative council.

2. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory, which critiqued British economic policies in India, was primarily propounded by:

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dadabhai Naoroji
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: Dadabhai Naoroji

Explanation: Dadabhai Naoroji, often referred to as the 'Grand Old Man of India', was the foremost proponent of the 'Drain of Wealth' theory. He meticulously documented how British rule led to the economic exploitation of India, with wealth being siphoned off to Britain without adequate returns.

3. Which of the following socio-religious reform movements focused on the upliftment of the depressed classes and advocated for their rights, particularly in Western India?

  1. Brahmo Samaj
  2. Arya Samaj
  3. Satya Shodhak Samaj
  4. Ramakrishna Mission

Answer: Satya Shodhak Samaj

Explanation: The Satya Shodhak Samaj, founded by Jyotirao Phule in Maharashtra, was a pioneering movement dedicated to the upliftment of the depressed classes (Dalits and other backward castes) and challenging the caste system and Brahmanical dominance.

4. The 'August Offer' of 1940, proposed by Lord Linlithgow, primarily aimed to:

  1. Grant complete independence to India immediately after World War II
  2. Expand the Viceroy's Executive Council to include more Indian members and establish a War Advisory Council
  3. Create a separate Muslim state in India
  4. Introduce universal adult franchise in India

Answer: Expand the Viceroy's Executive Council to include more Indian members and establish a War Advisory Council

Explanation: The August Offer of 1940 proposed to expand the Viceroy's Executive Council to include more Indians, establish a War Advisory Council, and promised a constituent assembly after the war to frame a new constitution. It did not offer immediate independence or a separate Muslim state.

5. Who among the following was NOT associated with the formation of the Indian National Congress in 1885?

  1. A.O. Hume
  2. W.C. Bonnerjee
  3. Surendranath Banerjee
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Explanation: While A.O. Hume (a retired British civil servant) was instrumental in its formation and W.C. Bonnerjee was its first president, Surendranath Banerjee was also a prominent early leader. Bal Gangadhar Tilak emerged as a prominent leader later, associated with the extremist faction, and was not directly involved in the initial formation in 1885.

6. The 'Doctrine of Lapse' was a policy primarily associated with which Governor-General of India?

  1. Lord William Bentinck
  2. Lord Dalhousie
  3. Lord Curzon
  4. Lord Hastings

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: Lord Dalhousie, who served as Governor-General from 1848 to 1856, is famously associated with the 'Doctrine of Lapse'. This policy allowed the British East India Company to annex any princely state whose ruler died without a natural heir, leading to the annexation of states like Satara, Sambalpur, Jhansi, and Nagpur.

7. Which of the following events is considered a direct precursor to the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)?

  1. Partition of Bengal (1905)
  2. Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)
  3. Swadeshi Movement (1905-08)
  4. Formation of the Muslim League (1906)

Answer: Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)

Explanation: The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre in April 1919, where hundreds of unarmed civilians were killed by British troops, deeply shocked the nation and was a major catalyst that led Mahatma Gandhi to launch the Non-Cooperation Movement as a mass protest against British rule.

8. The 'Poona Pact' of 1932 was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar regarding:

  1. The demand for complete independence from British rule
  2. Separate electorates for Muslims
  3. Joint electorates with reserved seats for depressed classes
  4. The formation of an interim government

Answer: Joint electorates with reserved seats for depressed classes

Explanation: The Poona Pact was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar in 1932. It abandoned the idea of separate electorates for the depressed classes (as proposed by the Communal Award) in favor of joint electorates with a larger number of reserved seats for them in the provincial and central legislatures.

9. Which of the following acts introduced separate electorates for Muslims in British India, significantly impacting communal politics?

  1. Indian Councils Act of 1861
  2. Indian Councils Act of 1892
  3. Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909
  4. Government of India Act of 1919

Answer: Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909

Explanation: The Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909 (Indian Councils Act of 1909) introduced separate electorates for Muslims, a move that was criticized for fostering communal divisions and laying the groundwork for future partition.

10. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory, which critiqued British economic exploitation of India, was primarily propounded by:

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dadabhai Naoroji
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: Dadabhai Naoroji

Explanation: Dadabhai Naoroji, known as the 'Grand Old Man of India', was the foremost proponent of the 'Drain of Wealth' theory, arguing that British rule was systematically impoverishing India by siphoning off its resources.

11. Which socio-religious reform movement focused on the upliftment of the depressed classes and advocated for their right to education and temple entry?

  1. Brahmo Samaj
  2. Arya Samaj
  3. Satyashodhak Samaj
  4. Ramakrishna Mission

Answer: Satyashodhak Samaj

Explanation: The Satyashodhak Samaj, founded by Jyotirao Phule in Maharashtra, specifically aimed at the emancipation of the lower castes and untouchables, advocating for their social and educational rights.

12. The 'August Offer' of 1940, proposed by Lord Linlithgow, primarily aimed at:

  1. Granting complete independence to India immediately
  2. Promising dominion status after World War II
  3. Establishing a constituent assembly for India
  4. Partitioning India into two separate nations

Answer: Promising dominion status after World War II

Explanation: The August Offer of 1940 promised dominion status for India after World War II, an expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council to include more Indians, and the establishment of a War Advisory Council.

13. Which of the following events immediately preceded the 'Quit India Movement' of 1942?

  1. The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
  2. The Cripps Mission's failure
  3. The Non-Cooperation Movement
  4. The formation of the Muslim League

Answer: The Cripps Mission's failure

Explanation: The failure of the Cripps Mission in March 1942, which offered limited self-government, led to widespread disillusionment and paved the way for Mahatma Gandhi to launch the Quit India Movement in August 1942.

14. The 'Doctrine of Lapse' was a policy introduced by which Governor-General of India, leading to the annexation of several Indian states?

  1. Lord William Bentinck
  2. Lord Dalhousie
  3. Lord Curzon
  4. Lord Hastings

Answer: Lord Dalhousie

Explanation: Lord Dalhousie, Governor-General from 1848 to 1856, introduced the 'Doctrine of Lapse', a controversial annexation policy that allowed the British East India Company to annex any princely state whose ruler died without a natural heir.

15. The 'August Offer' of 1940, proposed by Viceroy Linlithgow, primarily aimed at:

  1. Granting complete independence to India immediately after World War II
  2. Expanding the Viceroy's Executive Council with more Indian members and establishing a War Advisory Council
  3. Creating a separate Muslim state in India
  4. Holding direct elections for a constituent assembly to draft India's constitution

Answer: Expanding the Viceroy's Executive Council with more Indian members and establishing a War Advisory Council

Explanation: The August Offer of 1940 proposed to expand the Viceroy's Executive Council to include more Indians, establish a War Advisory Council, and promised a constituent assembly after the war, but it fell short of the Congress's demand for immediate self-government.

16. Which of the following events immediately preceded the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922)?

  1. Champaran Satyagraha
  2. Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
  3. Khilafat Movement
  4. Bardoli Satyagraha

Answer: Khilafat Movement

Explanation: The Khilafat Movement (1919-1924), launched by Indian Muslims to protest against the dismemberment of the Ottoman Caliphate, provided a significant backdrop and a common platform for Hindu-Muslim unity, which Mahatma Gandhi leveraged to launch the Non-Cooperation Movement.

17. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory, which critiqued British economic exploitation of India, was primarily propounded by which prominent nationalist leader?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dadabhai Naoroji
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: Dadabhai Naoroji

Explanation: Dadabhai Naoroji, known as the 'Grand Old Man of India', was the foremost proponent of the 'Drain of Wealth' theory, arguing that British rule was systematically impoverishing India by siphoning off its resources.

18. Which socio-religious reform movement focused on the upliftment of the depressed classes and was founded by Jyotirao Phule?

  1. Brahmo Samaj
  2. Arya Samaj
  3. Satyashodhak Samaj
  4. Ramakrishna Mission

Answer: Satyashodhak Samaj

Explanation: The Satyashodhak Samaj (Truth-Seekers' Society) was founded by Jyotirao Phule in 1873 in Maharashtra, with the primary objective of securing human rights and social justice for the lower castes and women.

19. The 'Poona Pact' of 1932 was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar regarding which of the following issues?

  1. Demand for complete independence
  2. Separate electorates for depressed classes
  3. Boycott of foreign goods
  4. Formation of a provisional government

Answer: Separate electorates for depressed classes

Explanation: The Poona Pact was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar in 1932, which abandoned separate electorates for the depressed classes in favor of reserved seats within the general electorate.

20. During the Revolt of 1857, who was the Mughal Emperor proclaimed as the Emperor of India by the rebels in Delhi?

  1. Akbar II
  2. Bahadur Shah Zafar
  3. Shah Alam II
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Bahadur Shah Zafar

Explanation: Bahadur Shah Zafar, the last Mughal Emperor, was proclaimed as the Emperor of India by the sepoys and rebels in Delhi during the 1857 Revolt, symbolizing a unified resistance against British rule.

21. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885 by A.O. Hume. Which Viceroy was in power at that time?

  1. Lord Lytton
  2. Lord Ripon
  3. Lord Dufferin
  4. Lord Curzon

Answer: Lord Dufferin

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was founded in December 1885 during the tenure of Lord Dufferin as the Viceroy of India (1884-1888).

22. Which of the following events is considered a direct consequence of the 'Partition of Bengal' in 1905?

  1. Formation of the Muslim League
  2. Launch of the Non-Cooperation Movement
  3. Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
  4. Champaran Satyagraha

Answer: Formation of the Muslim League

Explanation: The Partition of Bengal in 1905, which was seen as a 'divide and rule' policy, intensified communal feelings and is often cited as a significant factor contributing to the formation of the All-India Muslim League in 1906.

23. The 'Cabinet Mission Plan' of 1946 proposed which of the following for India?

  1. Immediate and complete independence with partition
  2. A federal union with a weak center and provincial autonomy
  3. Dominion status within the British Commonwealth
  4. A unitary state with strong central control

Answer: A federal union with a weak center and provincial autonomy

Explanation: The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 proposed a three-tier federal structure for India, with a weak center controlling defense, foreign affairs, and communications, and provinces having significant autonomy, along with grouping of provinces.

24. Which of the following was NOT a direct consequence of the Revolt of 1857?

  1. Transfer of power from the East India Company to the British Crown.
  2. Reorganization of the Indian Army on a more racial basis.
  3. Increased British investment in Indian infrastructure like railways.
  4. Introduction of the 'Doctrine of Lapse' by Lord Dalhousie.

Answer: Introduction of the 'Doctrine of Lapse' by Lord Dalhousie.

Explanation: The 'Doctrine of Lapse' was introduced by Lord Dalhousie before the Revolt of 1857 and was one of its contributing factors, not a consequence. The other options were direct outcomes of the revolt.

25. The 'Poona Sarvajanik Sabha' was a significant organization in the pre-Congress era. Who among the following was NOT a prominent figure associated with its early activities?

  1. Mahadev Govind Ranade
  2. Gopal Krishna Gokhale
  3. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  4. Ganesh Vasudev Joshi

Answer: Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Explanation: While Bal Gangadhar Tilak later became a prominent nationalist leader, he was not among the founding or early prominent figures of the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha. Mahadev Govind Ranade, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, and Ganesh Vasudev Joshi were key figures in its establishment and early functioning.
