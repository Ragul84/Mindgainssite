26. Which of the following acts formally recognized the principle of communal representation in British India?

  1. Indian Councils Act of 1861
  2. Indian Councils Act of 1892
  3. Minto-Morley Reforms (Indian Councils Act of 1909)
  4. Government of India Act of 1919

Answer: Minto-Morley Reforms (Indian Councils Act of 1909)

Explanation: The Minto-Morley Reforms (Indian Councils Act of 1909) formally introduced and legitimized the principle of separate electorates for Muslims, thus recognizing communal representation.

27. Consider the following statements regarding the 'Drain of Wealth' theory: 1. It was primarily propounded by Dadabhai Naoroji. 2. It argued that British rule was enriching India at the expense of Britain. 3. It highlighted the transfer of resources from India to Britain without adequate economic return. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 1 and 2 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: 1 and 3 only

Explanation: Statement 2 is incorrect. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory argued that British rule was impoverishing India by transferring its wealth to Britain, not enriching India. Statements 1 and 3 are correct.

28. The 'August Offer' of 1940 proposed which of the following?

  1. Complete independence for India after World War II.
  2. Dominion status for India and the establishment of a War Advisory Council.
  3. Immediate formation of an interim government with Indian members.
  4. Partition of India into two independent states.

Answer: Dominion status for India and the establishment of a War Advisory Council.

Explanation: The August Offer of 1940, made by Viceroy Linlithgow, proposed dominion status for India after World War II and the establishment of a War Advisory Council, among other things. It did not offer complete independence or immediate interim government.

29. Who among the following was NOT associated with the socio-religious reform movement of the 'Young Bengal' group?

  1. Henry Louis Vivian Derozio
  2. Ramtanu Lahiri
  3. Krishna Mohan Banerjee
  4. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar

Answer: Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar

Explanation: Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar was a prominent social reformer but was not directly associated with the 'Young Bengal' movement led by Henry Louis Vivian Derozio. Ramtanu Lahiri and Krishna Mohan Banerjee were key figures in the Derozian group.

30. The 'Lahore Session' of the Indian National Congress in 1929 is significant for which of the following declarations?

  1. Demand for 'Poorna Swaraj' (complete independence).
  2. Endorsement of the Non-Cooperation Movement.
  3. Acceptance of the Cabinet Mission Plan.
  4. Resolution for the partition of Bengal.

Answer: Demand for 'Poorna Swaraj' (complete independence).

Explanation: The Lahore Session of 1929, presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, famously declared 'Poorna Swaraj' (complete independence) as the ultimate goal of the Indian National Congress.

31. Which of the following treaties marked the end of the First Anglo-Maratha War?

  1. Treaty of Salbai
  2. Treaty of Bassein
  3. Treaty of Purandar
  4. Treaty of Seringapatam

Answer: Treaty of Salbai

Explanation: The First Anglo-Maratha War concluded with the Treaty of Salbai in 1782, which established a period of peace between the British and the Marathas for twenty years.

32. Which of the following was NOT a direct consequence or feature of the socio-religious reform movements in 19th century India?

  1. Emphasis on rationalism and humanism.
  2. Advocacy for women's education and widow remarriage.
  3. Promotion of caste system rigidity and untouchability.
  4. Critique of idolatry and polytheism by some reformers.

Answer: Promotion of caste system rigidity and untouchability.

Explanation: Socio-religious reform movements largely aimed to eradicate social evils like the caste system and untouchability, not promote them. They advocated for social equality and challenged traditional hierarchies.

33. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory, which highlighted the economic exploitation of India by the British, was primarily propounded by which of the following nationalist leaders?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dadabhai Naoroji
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: Dadabhai Naoroji

Explanation: Dadabhai Naoroji, through his book 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India', meticulously detailed how British policies led to the economic drain of India's resources, contributing significantly to nationalist thought.

34. The Lucknow Pact (1916) was a significant agreement between the Indian National Congress and the All-India Muslim League. What was its primary outcome?

  1. It led to the immediate independence of India.
  2. It established separate electorates for Muslims, agreed upon by both parties.
  3. It resulted in the permanent split of the Congress into moderates and extremists.
  4. It called for a complete boycott of British goods and institutions.

Answer: It established separate electorates for Muslims, agreed upon by both parties.

Explanation: The Lucknow Pact saw the Congress and the Muslim League agree to a joint scheme of constitutional reforms, and crucially, the Congress accepted the principle of separate electorates for Muslims, a controversial decision in hindsight.

35. Which of the following acts introduced the system of 'Dyarchy' at the provincial level in British India?

  1. Indian Councils Act of 1892
  2. Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909
  3. Government of India Act of 1919
  4. Government of India Act of 1935

Answer: Government of India Act of 1919

Explanation: The Government of India Act of 1919, also known as the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms, introduced the system of Dyarchy in the provinces, dividing subjects into 'reserved' and 'transferred' lists.

36. Who was the first Governor-General of Bengal?

  1. Lord William Bentinck
  2. Lord Dalhousie
  3. Warren Hastings
  4. Robert Clive

Answer: Warren Hastings

Explanation: Warren Hastings was the first Governor-General of Bengal from 1772 to 1785. This was a crucial period in the consolidation of British power in India.

37. The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in which year?

  1. 1919
  2. 1920
  3. 1922
  4. 1930

Answer: 1920

Explanation: Mahatma Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920. It aimed to resist British rule in India through nonviolent means.

38. Which of the following acts established the Supreme Court in Calcutta for the first time?

  1. Regulating Act of 1773
  2. Pitts India Act of 1784
  3. Charter Act of 1813
  4. Government of India Act of 1858

Answer: Regulating Act of 1773

Explanation: The Regulating Act of 1773 made provisions for the establishment of a Supreme Court in Calcutta. Sir Elijah Impey was appointed as the first Chief Justice.

39. During which Governor-General's tenure was the Permanent Settlement of Bengal introduced?

  1. Lord Cornwallis
  2. Lord William Bentinck
  3. Lord Dalhousie
  4. Lord Warren Hastings

Answer: Lord Cornwallis

Explanation: The Permanent Settlement of Bengal was introduced by Lord Cornwallis in 1793. This system fixed the land revenue demand permanently.

40. The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched in India in the year:

  1. 1920
  2. 1919
  3. 1922
  4. 1930

Answer: 1920

Explanation: The Non-Cooperation Movement, led by Mahatma Gandhi, was launched in 1920 to protest against British rule and demand Swaraj. It followed the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and the Rowlatt Act.

41. Which of the following events is NOT directly associated with Lord Curzon's viceroyalty in India?

  1. Partition of Bengal
  2. Indian Universities Act of 1904
  3. Appointment of the Famine Commission
  4. Formation of the Indian National Congress

Answer: Formation of the Indian National Congress

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was formed in 1885, long before Lord Curzon's tenure (1899-1905). The other events listed occurred during his viceroyalty.

42. Consider the following statements regarding the socio-religious reform movements in 19th-century India: 1. The Young Bengal movement, led by Henry Vivian Derozio, advocated for the abolition of Sati and child marriage, drawing inspiration from French revolutionary ideals. 2. The Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang, primarily focused on challenging the caste system and promoting inter-caste marriages, while rejecting idol worship. 3. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar's efforts were instrumental in the passing of the Widow Remarriage Act of 1856, a legislative success he achieved without direct support from the British government. Which of the statements given above are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. None of the above

Answer: 2 only

Explanation: Statement 1 is incorrect as while Derozio advocated for social reforms, the Young Bengal movement's primary focus was intellectual awakening among youth, and it was not directly instrumental in legislating against Sati and child marriage. Statement 3 is incorrect because although Vidyasagar was crucial, the Widow Remarriage Act of 1856 was indeed passed by the British government. Statement 2 is correct as Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang, focused on challenging the caste system, promoting inter-caste marriages, and rejecting idol worship.

43. During the Quit India Movement, several parallel governments were established in different parts of India. Which of the following pairs correctly matches a parallel government with its prominent leader/region and a characteristic feature? A. Satara (Maharashtra) – Y.B. Chavan; Focused on establishing a people's court (Nyayadan Mandals). B. Tamluk (Bengal) – Jatiya Sarkar; Implemented a 'Bhagini Sena' (women's brigade) for law and order. C. Ballia (Uttar Pradesh) – Chittu Pandey; Maintained law and order through a volunteer force and collected land revenue. D. Talcher (Odisha) – Laxman Nayak; Successfully established a completely independent administration for several months, completely replacing British authority.

  1. A and B only
  2. B and C only
  3. A, B and C only
  4. All of the above

Answer: B and C only

Explanation: Option A is incorrect as while Satara did establish a parallel government, Y.B. Chavan was not its prominent leader (it was Nana Patil). Option D is incorrect as while Talcher saw significant resistance, it did not establish a completely independent administration for several months, and Laxman Nayak's role was different. Options B and C are correct as both Tamluk's Jatiya Sarkar and Ballia's government under Chittu Pandey exhibited the mentioned features.

44. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22) saw a diverse range of participation and impact across different social groups and regions. Which of the following statements best describes a unique or nuanced aspect of its influence? A. The movement received unanimous support from the industrial working class, leading to widespread strikes and the formation of numerous trade unions across major industrial centers. B. In the Gudem Hills of Andhra Pradesh, the movement intertwined with local tribal grievances against forest laws, leading to a violent rebellion under Alluri Sitarama Raju, despite Gandhi's emphasis on non-violence. C. The Khilafat issue, while a catalyst for Hindu-Muslim unity, primarily resonated with the educated elite and had limited impact on the rural Muslim population in Bengal and Punjab. D. The withdrawal of the movement after the Chauri Chaura incident was met with universal disapproval from all Congress leaders, who believed it was a premature and ill-advised decision that stifled the momentum.

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: Statement B accurately describes a unique aspect where the Non-Cooperation movement's spirit merged with local tribal issues, leading to armed resistance under Alluri Sitarama Raju, showcasing regional variations despite the overall non-violent ethos. The other options contain inaccuracies about the extent of support, impact, or unanimous disapproval.

45. Which European country was the first to establish a trading post in India?

  1. Portugal
  2. England
  3. France
  4. Netherlands

Answer: Portugal

Explanation: Vasco da Gama, a Portuguese explorer, was the first European to reach India by sea in 1498, leading to the establishment of Portuguese trading posts.

46. Who was the last Mughal Emperor of India?

  1. Aurangzeb
  2. Akbar
  3. Bahadur Shah Zafar
  4. Shah Jahan

Answer: Bahadur Shah Zafar

Explanation: Bahadur Shah Zafar was the last Mughal Emperor, who was exiled by the British after the Revolt of 1857.

47. Which of the following acts established a Board of Control in Britain, effectively creating a dual system of governance for British India?

  1. Regulating Act of 1773
  2. Pitt's India Act of 1784
  3. Charter Act of 1813
  4. Government of India Act of 1858

Answer: Pitt's India Act of 1784

Explanation: Pitt's India Act of 1784 established the Board of Control to supervise the East India Company's civil, military, and revenue affairs, thus creating a dual system of control.

48. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory, which critiqued British economic policies in India, was primarily propounded by which of the following nationalist leaders?

  1. Mahatma Gandhi
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Dadabhai Naoroji
  4. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: Dadabhai Naoroji

Explanation: Dadabhai Naoroji, through his book 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India', meticulously detailed the theory of 'Drain of Wealth'.

49. Which significant event immediately preceded the formation of the Indian National Congress in 1885?

  1. The Ilbert Bill Controversy
  2. The Partition of Bengal
  3. The Sepoy Mutiny
  4. The Morley-Minto Reforms

Answer: The Ilbert Bill Controversy

Explanation: The Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883-84) exposed racial discrimination in the judicial system and fueled nationalist sentiments, contributing to the formation of the INC.

50. During which Viceroy's tenure was the Vernacular Press Act (1878) enacted, allowing the government to confiscate newspapers that published 'seditious' material?

  1. Lord Ripon
  2. Lord Lytton
  3. Lord Curzon
  4. Lord Dufferin

Answer: Lord Lytton

Explanation: Lord Lytton enacted the Vernacular Press Act in 1878 to curb the growing criticism by the Indian press against British policies.
