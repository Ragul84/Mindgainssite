Medieval MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Medieval questions to revise the SSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

26. The invasion of Timur (Tamerlane) devastated Delhi in:

  1. 1398 CE
  2. 1336 CE
  3. 1451 CE
  4. 1526 CE

Answer: 1398 CE

Explanation: Timur (Tamerlane) of Samarkand invaded India and sacked Delhi in 1398 CE, massacring 100,000+ prisoners and looting enormous wealth. This destroyed the Tughlaq dynasty and led to the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties.

27. The last Sultan of Delhi was:

  1. Sikandar Lodi
  2. Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Bahlul Lodi
  4. Daulat Khan Lodi

Answer: Ibrahim Lodi

Explanation: Ibrahim Lodi (1517–1526) was the last Sultan of Delhi. He was defeated and killed at the First Battle of Panipat (1526) by Babur, ending the Delhi Sultanate and beginning the Mughal Empire.

28. The Vijayanagara Empire was founded by:

  1. Deva Raya I
  2. Harihara I and Bukka Raya I (with guidance of sage Vidyaranya)
  3. Krishna Deva Raya
  4. Achyuta Deva Raya

Answer: Harihara I and Bukka Raya I (with guidance of sage Vidyaranya)

Explanation: The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 CE by brothers Harihara I and Bukka Raya I (formerly commanders of the Kampili kingdom) with the spiritual guidance of sage Vidyaranya (Madhava). Capital: Hampi (Vijayanagara).

29. The greatest ruler of the Vijayanagara Empire was:

  1. Harihara I
  2. Deva Raya II
  3. Krishna Deva Raya
  4. Rama Raya

Answer: Krishna Deva Raya

Explanation: Krishna Deva Raya (1509–1529 CE) was the greatest Vijayanagara ruler. He defeated Bijapur, Bidar, Golconda, and the Zamorin of Calicut. He was a poet (wrote Amuktamalyada in Telugu) and patron of Ashtadiggajas (eight court poets).

30. The Battle of Talikota (1565) resulted in:

  1. Vijayanagara victory over the Deccan Sultanates
  2. Destruction of the Vijayanagara Empire — the capital Hampi was sacked and ruined
  3. Mughal invasion of South India
  4. Alliance between Vijayanagara and Bijapur

Answer: Destruction of the Vijayanagara Empire — the capital Hampi was sacked and ruined

Explanation: The Battle of Talikota (Rakshasatangadi, 1565) was fought between Vijayanagara (Aliya Rama Raya) and the allied Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Bidar, Golconda). Rama Raya was killed, Hampi was devastated and never recovered.

31. Babur founded the Mughal Empire after winning:

  1. Battle of Khanwa
  2. Battle of Ghaghra
  3. First Battle of Panipat (1526)
  4. Battle of Chausa

Answer: First Battle of Panipat (1526)

Explanation: Babur (Zahir-ud-din Muhammad) defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526) using gunpowder and artillery (tulugma tactics). This established the Mughal Empire. He then defeated Rana Sanga at Khanwa (1527).

32. The founder of the Slave Dynasty was:

  1. Qutb-ud-din Aibak
  2. Iltutmish
  3. Balban
  4. Razia Sultana

Answer: Qutb-ud-din Aibak

Explanation: Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206-1210 CE) founded the Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty — the first Muslim dynasty in India. He was a slave of Muhammad Ghori who rose to become a sultan.

33. The first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate was:

  1. Razia Sultana
  2. Chand Bibi
  3. Nur Jahan
  4. Jahanara

Answer: Razia Sultana

Explanation: Razia Sultana (1236-1240 CE), daughter of Iltutmish, was the first woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate. She discarded purdah and held court openly.

34. Muhammad bin Tughluq's transfer of capital from Delhi to Daulatabad was:

  1. A failed administrative experiment
  2. Highly successful
  3. Done to promote trade
  4. Done for military reasons only

Answer: A failed administrative experiment

Explanation: Muhammad bin Tughluq (1327 CE) transferred the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) in the Deccan for better control of the empire, but it failed — the northern areas became unmanageable.

35. Ibn Battuta visited India during the reign of:

  1. Muhammad bin Tughluq
  2. Alauddin Khilji
  3. Firuz Shah Tughluq
  4. Balban

Answer: Muhammad bin Tughluq

Explanation: Ibn Battuta (Moroccan traveler) visited India 1333-1347 CE during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughluq, who appointed him as Qazi of Delhi. His book Rihla describes India in detail.

36. The 'iqta' system in Delhi Sultanate was similar to:

  1. Feudal land grants for military service
  2. Tax farming
  3. Permanent settlement
  4. Zamindari system

Answer: Feudal land grants for military service

Explanation: Iqta was a grant of land revenue (not land itself) to military officers (iqtadars) in lieu of salary, in return for maintaining troops and providing military service.

37. The greatest ruler of Vijayanagara Empire was:

  1. Krishnadevaraya
  2. Devaraya II
  3. Harihara II
  4. Saluva Narasimha

Answer: Krishnadevaraya

Explanation: Krishnadevaraya (1509-1529 CE) was the greatest ruler — a brilliant military commander, poet (wrote Amuktamalyada in Telugu), and generous patron of arts. Known as Kannada Rajya Rama Ramana.

38. Abdur Razzaq, the Persian ambassador, visited which Vijayanagara king's court?

  1. Devaraya II
  2. Krishnadevaraya
  3. Harihara I
  4. Bukka I

Answer: Devaraya II

Explanation: Abdur Razzaq (Persian envoy, 1443) visited the court of Devaraya II and gave a detailed description of Vijayanagara city and the empire's grandeur.

39. Battle of Talikota (1565) ended:

  1. Vijayanagara Empire
  2. Mughal dominance
  3. Delhi Sultanate
  4. Bahmani Kingdom

Answer: Vijayanagara Empire

Explanation: Battle of Talikota (Jan 1565): Vijayanagara defeated by the combined forces of Bijapur, Bidar, Ahmadnagar, and Golconda. The city of Vijayanagara was sacked and destroyed.

40. Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat in:

  1. 1526
  2. 1556
  3. 1761
  4. 1527

Answer: 1526

Explanation: First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526): Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi using the Tulughma strategy and artillery, establishing the Mughal Empire in India.

41. The Mughal emperor famous for building Taj Mahal:

  1. Shah Jahan
  2. Akbar
  3. Jahangir
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Shah Jahan

Explanation: Shah Jahan (1628-1658) built the Taj Mahal at Agra (1632-1643) as a mausoleum for his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

42. Aurangzeb reimposed the 'Jizya' tax which had been abolished by:

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Babur
  4. Shah Jahan

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar abolished the discriminatory Jizya (poll tax on non-Muslims) in 1564 CE as part of his policy of religious tolerance. Aurangzeb reimposed it in 1679 CE.

43. Todar Mal was the Finance Minister of:

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Jahangir

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Raja Todar Mal was Akbar's revenue minister who introduced the Zabti (measurement) system of land revenue — measuring land and fixing revenue based on soil type and cultivation.

44. The 'Mansabdari system' was a Mughal system of:

  1. Military ranking and revenue
  2. Religious administration
  3. Land grants
  4. Educational hierarchy

Answer: Military ranking and revenue

Explanation: Mansabdari system (introduced by Akbar) graded military and civil officials (mansabdars) by numerical ranks (mansabs) determining salary and cavalry obligation.

45. The Battle of Haldighati (1576) was fought between:

  1. Maharana Pratap and Akbar's army
  2. Prithviraj and Ghori
  3. Shivaji and Aurangzeb
  4. Rana Sanga and Babur

Answer: Maharana Pratap and Akbar's army

Explanation: Battle of Haldighati (18 June 1576) was fought between Maharana Pratap of Mewar and the Mughal forces led by Man Singh I on Akbar's behalf. Pratap was defeated but never surrendered.

46. The Battle of Buxar (1764) was significant because:

  1. British defeated combined armies of three powers
  2. First British victory in India
  3. British acquired Bengal
  4. First use of firearms in India

Answer: British defeated combined armies of three powers

Explanation: Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764): British under Hector Munro defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula (Awadh), and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II — establishing British dominance.

47. The 'Ain-i-Akbari' was written by:

  1. Abul Fazl
  2. Birbal
  3. Todar Mal
  4. Tansen

Answer: Abul Fazl

Explanation: Ain-i-Akbari (Institutes of Akbar) was written by Abul Fazl, Akbar's court historian. It is the third volume of Akbarnama, providing detailed accounts of Akbar's administration, policies, and the Mughal court.

48. Akbar's 'Ain-i-Akbari' and 'Akbarnama' were written by:

  1. Birbal
  2. Abul Fazl
  3. Faizi
  4. Tansen

Answer: Abul Fazl

Explanation: Abul Fazl (1551–1602), a minister and historian, wrote Akbarnama (in 3 volumes, including the Ain-i-Akbari). The Ain-i-Akbari is a detailed account of Akbar's administration, court, culture, and statistics of the empire.

49. The Mughal land revenue system during Akbar's reign was called:

  1. Iqta
  2. Zamindari
  3. Dahsala or Zabti system (introduced by Todar Mal)
  4. Mahalwari

Answer: Dahsala or Zabti system (introduced by Todar Mal)

Explanation: Raja Todar Mal introduced the Dahsala (ten-year average) system: land was measured (zabt), soil quality classified into 4 types, and revenue fixed for 10 years based on average produce. It replaced earlier arbitrary systems.

50. The partition of Bengal was annulled in:

  1. 1911
  2. 1905
  3. 1919
  4. 1916

Answer: 1911

Explanation: Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905. Due to massive protests (Swadeshi Movement), the partition was annulled in 1911 when the capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.
