Medieval MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Medieval questions to revise the SSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The Taj Mahal was built by which Mughal emperor?

  1. Akbar
  2. Humayun
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Aurangzeb

Answer: Shah Jahan

Explanation: Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra (1632–1653) in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

2. Which Mughal emperor is known as 'Akbar the Great'?

  1. Humayun
  2. Babur
  3. Akbar
  4. Jahangir

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar (1556–1605) is regarded as one of the greatest Mughal emperors known for religious tolerance, administrative reforms, and expanding the empire.

3. Which was the first capital of the Mughal Empire in India?

  1. Agra
  2. Delhi
  3. Lahore
  4. Kabul

Answer: Agra

Explanation: Agra was the first capital of the Mughal Empire in India. Babur established it after the First Battle of Panipat (1526).

4. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between:

  1. Akbar and Hemu
  2. Babur and Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Humayun and Sher Shah Suri
  4. Baber and Rana Sanga

Answer: Babur and Ibrahim Lodi

Explanation: The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur (founder of Mughal Empire) and Ibrahim Lodi (last ruler of Delhi Sultanate). Babur's victory established Mughal rule in India.

5. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was annulled in:

  1. 1907
  2. 1910
  3. 1911
  4. 1919

Answer: 1911

Explanation: The Partition of Bengal (1905) by Lord Curzon was annulled in 1911 under Lord Hardinge following massive protests. The capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

6. Tipu Sultan was the ruler of which kingdom?

  1. Hyderabad
  2. Mysore
  3. Maratha Empire
  4. Vijayanagara Empire

Answer: Mysore

Explanation: Tipu Sultan (1750–1799) was the Sultan of Mysore. He was called the 'Tiger of Mysore' for his fierce resistance against British expansion. He died in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War.

7. The Pandya dynasty's capital was:

  1. Madurai
  2. Uraiyur
  3. Kaveripattanam
  4. Kanchipuram

Answer: Madurai

Explanation: Madurai was the capital of the Pandya dynasty throughout history — from Sangam Age through medieval period. Ancient Madurai is described in detail in Sangam literature (Maturaikkanci).

8. Hampi was the capital of:

  1. Vijayanagara Empire
  2. Chalukyas
  3. Rashtrakutas
  4. Kakatiya Kingdom

Answer: Vijayanagara Empire

Explanation: Hampi (Karnataka) was the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire. It is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site with magnificent ruins including Virupaksha Temple, Vittala Temple, and palace structures.

9. The Indian National Army trials (INA trials, 1945) were held at:

  1. Red Fort, Delhi
  2. Cellular Jail, Andaman
  3. Allahabad High Court
  4. Bombay

Answer: Red Fort, Delhi

Explanation: The INA trials were held at Red Fort, Delhi (November 1945), charging INA officers with treason against the British. The trials created massive public outrage that unified Indians.

10. Sher Shah Suri introduced the currency called:

  1. Rupee
  2. Tanka
  3. Dam
  4. Mohur

Answer: Rupee

Explanation: Sher Shah Suri (1540-1545) introduced the silver rupee (rupiya) weighing 178 grains. He also introduced copper paisa and gold mohur — currency reforms that continued under Mughals.

11. Who was the author of 'Baburnama'?

  1. Babur
  2. Akbar
  3. Abul Fazl
  4. Gulbadan Begum

Answer: Babur

Explanation: Baburnama (Tuzuk-i-Baburi) was written by Babur himself in Chagatai Turkish — it is the first autobiography in Islamic literary tradition, describing his life, campaigns, and observations of India.

12. Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) was established by:

  1. Akbar
  2. Babur
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar established Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri in 1575 CE for religious debates among scholars of different faiths — precursor to his Din-i-Ilahi.

13. The Battle of Khanwa (1527) was between:

  1. Babur and Rana Sanga
  2. Babur and Ibrahim Lodi
  3. Humayun and Sher Shah
  4. Akbar and Hemu

Answer: Babur and Rana Sanga

Explanation: Battle of Khanwa (17 March 1527): Babur defeated Rana Sanga (Rana Sangram Singh) of Mewar, who had formed a coalition of Rajput chiefs to challenge Babur.

14. Akbar defeated Hemu in:

  1. Second Battle of Panipat (1556)
  2. First Battle of Panipat (1526)
  3. Battle of Khanwa (1527)
  4. Battle of Haldighati (1576)

Answer: Second Battle of Panipat (1556)

Explanation: Second Battle of Panipat (5 November 1556): Akbar's general Bairam Khan defeated and killed Hemu (the last Hindu emperor to sit on Delhi's throne briefly), securing Mughal rule.

15. The treaty that ended the Third Anglo-Mysore War was:

  1. Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
  2. Treaty of Mangalore (1784)
  3. Treaty of Srirangapatna (1799)
  4. Treaty of Mysore (1799)

Answer: Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)

Explanation: Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) ended the Third Anglo-Mysore War — Tipu Sultan surrendered half his territory and paid large indemnity. The Fourth War (1799) ended with Tipu's death.

16. Tipu Sultan died defending:

  1. Srirangapatna Fort
  2. Mysore Palace
  3. Bangalore Fort
  4. Chitradurga

Answer: Srirangapatna Fort

Explanation: Tipu Sultan (Sultan of Mysore) died on 4 May 1799 defending his capital Srirangapatna against British forces. His death ended the kingdom of Mysore.

17. The Mughal emperor who was also called 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint):

  1. Aurangzeb
  2. Akbar
  3. Shah Jahan
  4. Humayun

Answer: Aurangzeb

Explanation: Aurangzeb was called 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint) for his piety — he fasted regularly, never drank alcohol, lived simply, and supplemented his income by copying Qurans and making prayer caps.

18. In Indian history, 'Second Battle of Tarain (1192)' established:

  1. Permanent Muslim rule in North India
  2. Maratha dominance
  3. Rajput supremacy
  4. British East India Company dominance

Answer: Permanent Muslim rule in North India

Explanation: Second Battle of Tarain (1192) — Muhammad Ghori's defeat of Prithviraj Chauhan — established permanent Muslim rule in North India, beginning the Delhi Sultanate period.

19. The Bahmani Kingdom was founded by:

  1. Alauddin Bahman Shah
  2. Mahmud Gawan
  3. Firuz Shah Bahmani
  4. Ibrahim Adil Shah

Answer: Alauddin Bahman Shah

Explanation: Alauddin Bahman Shah (Hassan Gangu, 1347-1358) founded the Bahmani Kingdom after revolting against Muhammad bin Tughluq of Delhi. It split into 5 Deccan Sultanates after 1518.

20. Akbar's 'Sulh-i-Kul' policy means:

  1. Universal peace and tolerance
  2. Military alliance
  3. Royal decree
  4. Religious conversion

Answer: Universal peace and tolerance

Explanation: Sulh-i-Kul (Peace with all/Absolute peace) was Akbar's policy of universal tolerance — treating all religions equally, respecting all faiths without discrimination.

21. Which Mughal emperor translated the Ramayana and Mahabharata into Persian?

  1. Akbar
  2. Shah Jahan
  3. Humayun
  4. Babur

Answer: Akbar

Explanation: Akbar had the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and other Sanskrit texts translated into Persian, making them accessible to Persian-speaking scholars and promoting cultural synthesis.

22. The first Sultan of Delhi was:

  1. Iltutmish
  2. Muhammad Ghori
  3. Qutb-ud-din Aibak
  4. Balban

Answer: Qutb-ud-din Aibak

Explanation: Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206–1210 CE) was the first Sultan of Delhi and founder of the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty. He was a slave of Muhammad Ghori. He started the Qutb Minar and the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque.

23. The five dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate in order were:

  1. Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi
  2. Khalji, Slave, Tughlaq, Lodi, Sayyid
  3. Lodi, Tughlaq, Slave, Khalji, Sayyid
  4. Slave, Lodi, Khalji, Sayyid, Tughlaq

Answer: Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi

Explanation: Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526): 1) Mamluk/Slave dynasty (1206–1290), 2) Khalji dynasty (1290–1320), 3) Tughlaq dynasty (1320–1414), 4) Sayyid dynasty (1414–1451), 5) Lodi dynasty (1451–1526).

24. Iltutmish is considered the true founder of the Delhi Sultanate because:

  1. He was the first Sultan
  2. He consolidated the Sultanate, got recognition from Caliph of Baghdad, completed Qutb Minar, and introduced iqta system
  3. He defeated Prithviraj Chauhan
  4. He invaded South India

Answer: He consolidated the Sultanate, got recognition from Caliph of Baghdad, completed Qutb Minar, and introduced iqta system

Explanation: Iltutmish (1210–1236) got recognition from the Abbasid Caliph (Islamic legitimacy), introduced the iqta system (land revenue assignments for military service), completed the Qutb Minar (started by Aibak), and introduced silver tanka and copper jital coins.

25. Muhammad bin Tughlaq's controversial decision to shift the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) was:

  1. Successful and permanent
  2. A disaster — he forced the entire population of Delhi to relocate, causing mass deaths; later reversed
  3. Done for religious reasons
  4. Done at the request of Sufi saints

Answer: A disaster — he forced the entire population of Delhi to relocate, causing mass deaths; later reversed

Explanation: Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325–1351) shifted the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (1327) to better control the Deccan. The forced migration caused suffering and deaths. The plan failed — he had to shift back. Ibn Battuta documented this.
