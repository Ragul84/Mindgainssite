26. The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) delineated spheres of influence for Spain and Portugal in the newly discovered lands. Which of the following geopolitical implications, stemming from this treaty, had the most profound and lasting impact on the demographic and cultural landscape of a major South American nation?

  1. The establishment of a predominantly Portuguese-speaking Brazil, distinct from its Spanish-speaking neighbors.
  2. The rapid decline of indigenous populations across the entire continent due to European diseases and conflict.
  3. The eventual rise of the British Empire as the dominant naval power in the Atlantic.
  4. The formation of the Viceroyalty of Peru, encompassing vast territories in western South America.

Answer: The establishment of a predominantly Portuguese-speaking Brazil, distinct from its Spanish-speaking neighbors.

Explanation: The Treaty of Tordesillas directly led to Portugal's claim over Brazil, fundamentally shaping its language and culture. This separated Brazil demographically and culturally from the Spanish-speaking nations.

27. During the 'Scramble for Africa' in the late 19th century, European powers often justified their colonial expansion through the concept of the 'civilizing mission' and the economic imperative of accessing raw materials and new markets. Which of the following historical events, preceding this period, most significantly contributed to the technological and logistical capabilities that facilitated such rapid and extensive colonization?

  1. The Industrial Revolution, with its advancements in steam power, weaponry, and communication.
  2. The Napoleonic Wars, which reshaped European political boundaries and military strategies.
  3. The American Revolution, demonstrating the feasibility of challenging established colonial powers.
  4. The Age of Exploration, establishing long-distance maritime trade routes and mapping techniques.

Answer: The Industrial Revolution, with its advancements in steam power, weaponry, and communication.

Explanation: The Industrial Revolution provided the advanced weaponry, transportation (steamships, railways), and communication (telegraph) necessary for European powers to effectively project their dominance and administer vast colonial territories in Africa. These technological advancements were crucial for the scale and speed of the Scramble.

28. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) is often cited as a foundational moment for the modern international system, establishing the principle of state sovereignty. However, its long-term impact on the balance of power in Central Europe was also significantly influenced by the preceding Thirty Years' War. Which of the following statements best describes the combined effect of the war's devastation and the treaty's provisions on the Holy Roman Empire?

  1. It considerably weakened the authority of the Holy Roman Emperor, leading to greater autonomy for individual German states and contributing to Germany's delayed unification.
  2. It solidified the Catholic Church's dominance over Protestantism throughout the Empire, preventing further religious conflicts.
  3. It ushered in a period of unprecedented economic prosperity and cultural revival across all German territories.
  4. It led to the immediate dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire, paving the way for a unified German nation-state centuries earlier.

Answer: It considerably weakened the authority of the Holy Roman Emperor, leading to greater autonomy for individual German states and contributing to Germany's delayed unification.

Explanation: The Thirty Years' War devastated the Holy Roman Empire, and the Peace of Westphalia formalized the autonomy of its constituent states. This fragmentation significantly weakened imperial authority and contributed to Germany's delayed unification.
