SSC History practice

World MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked World questions to revise the SSC History syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The famous astronomer who stated Earth rotates on its axis was:

  1. Aryabhata
  2. Brahmagupta
  3. Varahamihira
  4. Bhaskara

Answer: Aryabhata

Explanation: Aryabhata (476-550 CE) stated that the Earth rotates on its own axis, causing apparent movement of stars — a revolutionary concept 1000 years before Copernicus.

2. Who was the leader of Chinese Revolution (1949)?

  1. Mao Zedong
  2. Sun Yat-sen
  3. Chiang Kai-shek
  4. Zhou Enlai

Answer: Mao Zedong

Explanation: Mao Zedong led the Communist Party of China to victory in the Chinese Civil War, proclaiming the People's Republic of China on 1 October 1949.

3. The First World War was also known as:

  1. The Great War
  2. War of Nations
  3. War of Empires
  4. The European War

Answer: The Great War

Explanation: World War I (1914-1918) was contemporaneously called 'The Great War' or 'The War to End All Wars'. The name 'World War I' became common only after the Second World War began.

4. The 'Green Revolution' in India is associated with:

  1. M.S. Swaminathan
  2. Amartya Sen
  3. Verghese Kurien
  4. C. Subramaniam

Answer: M.S. Swaminathan

Explanation: Dr. M.S. Swaminathan is called 'Father of Green Revolution in India' for introducing high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice in the 1960s, transforming India's food production.

5. The French Revolution began in:

  1. 1789
  2. 1799
  3. 1776
  4. 1804

Answer: 1789

Explanation: The French Revolution began in 1789 with the storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789). It overthrew the monarchy and established principles of Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.

6. World War I began in:

  1. 1914
  2. 1939
  3. 1918
  4. 1904

Answer: 1914

Explanation: World War I began in 1914 following the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria (June 28, 1914). It ended in 1918 with the Armistice of November 11.

7. The Russian Revolution (Bolshevik) occurred in:

  1. 1917
  2. 1905
  3. 1918
  4. 1921

Answer: 1917

Explanation: The October Revolution (Bolshevik Revolution) of November 7, 1917 (October 25 in old Julian calendar) led by Lenin overthrew the Provisional Government and established Soviet Russia.

8. World War I was triggered by:

  1. Germany's invasion of Poland
  2. Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary at Sarajevo (28 June 1914)
  3. The sinking of the Lusitania
  4. Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor

Answer: Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary at Sarajevo (28 June 1914)

Explanation: WWI (1914–1918) was triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (heir to Austria-Hungary) by Gavrilo Princip (Serbian nationalist) in Sarajevo, Bosnia. This set off the alliance system and led to a global conflict.

9. Which ancient civilization is famous for building the Pyramids?

  1. Roman Empire
  2. Ancient Greece
  3. Ancient Egypt
  4. Indus Valley Civilization

Answer: Ancient Egypt

Explanation: The Pyramids are iconic structures built by the ancient Egyptians, primarily as tombs for pharaohs.

10. Who is widely considered to be the 'Father of History'?

  1. Thucydides
  2. Herodotus
  3. Plato
  4. Aristotle

Answer: Herodotus

Explanation: Herodotus was an ancient Greek historian who is often called the 'Father of History' for his work 'The Histories'.

11. Which ancient civilization is credited with the invention of cuneiform script, one of the earliest systems of writing?

  1. Sumerian
  2. Egyptian
  3. Indus Valley
  4. Roman

Answer: Sumerian

Explanation: Cuneiform script originated with the Sumerians in Mesopotamia. It was developed to record administrative and economic transactions.

12. The 'Edict of Milan' in 313 CE, issued by Emperor Constantine the Great, had a profound impact on the religious landscape of the Roman Empire. What was its primary effect?

  1. It established Christianity as the official state religion.
  2. It outlawed all pagan religions within the empire.
  3. It granted religious tolerance to Christians throughout the empire.
  4. It initiated the construction of the Hagia Sophia.

Answer: It granted religious tolerance to Christians throughout the empire.

Explanation: The Edict of Milan granted religious toleration to Christians, effectively ending their persecution. It did not make Christianity the official state religion, which happened later.

13. Which of the following was a key factor in the decline of the Western Roman Empire in the 5th century CE?

  1. The rise of the Sassanid Empire in Persia.
  2. Increased trade with Eastern civilizations.
  3. Invasions by Germanic tribes and internal political instability.
  4. The conversion of emperors to Christianity.

Answer: Invasions by Germanic tribes and internal political instability.

Explanation: Invasions by Germanic tribes, coupled with internal political and economic instability, significantly contributed to the Western Roman Empire's collapse. The Sassanid Empire and trade were external factors but not primary causes of decline.

14. The Renaissance, a period of renewed interest in classical art, literature, and philosophy, originated in which region?

  1. England
  2. France
  3. Italy
  4. Germany

Answer: Italy

Explanation: The Renaissance began in Italy, particularly in cities like Florence, before spreading across Europe. It was fueled by wealthy patrons and a rediscovery of classical knowledge.

15. Which Enlightenment philosopher is best known for advocating the separation of powers within government to prevent tyranny?

  1. Jean-Jacques Rousseau
  2. Voltaire
  3. John Locke
  4. Baron de Montesquieu

Answer: Baron de Montesquieu

Explanation: Baron de Montesquieu, in his work 'The Spirit of the Laws', famously proposed the separation of powers into legislative, executive, and judicial branches. While other philosophers discussed government, Montesquieu is directly associated with this concept.

16. The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) formally divided the newly discovered lands outside Europe between Spain and Portugal. Which of the following subsequent historical developments, primarily driven by this treaty's concessions, most significantly altered the geopolitical landscape of the Americas and Africa?

  1. The establishment of the Dutch East India Company's extensive trade network in Asia.
  2. The widespread adoption of mercantilist economic policies by European powers.
  3. The unchallenged dominance of the Portuguese in the transatlantic slave trade and Brazilian colonization.
  4. The rise of the British Empire as the preeminent global naval power in the 18th century.

Answer: The unchallenged dominance of the Portuguese in the transatlantic slave trade and Brazilian colonization.

Explanation: The Treaty of Tordesillas directly facilitated Portugal's unchallenged claim over Brazil and its initial dominance in the transatlantic slave trade, profoundly shaping the demographics and economies of both continents.

17. The Opium Wars (1839-1842 and 1856-1860) demonstrated the military superiority of Western powers over Qing China. Beyond the immediate territorial concessions and trade agreements, which of the following long-term consequences most profoundly contributed to the eventual collapse of the Qing Dynasty and the subsequent rise of Chinese nationalism?

  1. The forced opening of treaty ports, leading to a surge in foreign investment and industrialization.
  2. The internal rebellions, such as the Taiping Rebellion, which were exacerbated by the weakened central government.
  3. The introduction of Western democratic ideals that directly challenged the imperial system.
  4. The widespread adoption of opium, leading to a demographic crisis across China.

Answer: The internal rebellions, such as the Taiping Rebellion, which were exacerbated by the weakened central government.

Explanation: The Opium Wars exposed the Qing's weakness, fueling internal unrest like the Taiping Rebellion, which severely destabilized the dynasty and paved the way for later nationalist movements.

18. The Congress of Vienna (1815) aimed to restore the balance of power in Europe after the Napoleonic Wars. Despite its immediate success in preventing large-scale conflicts for several decades, which of the following underlying principles established at Vienna ultimately proved most problematic and unsustainable in the face of burgeoning 19th-century political ideologies?

  1. The principle of legitimacy, restoring hereditary monarchies to their thrones.
  2. The establishment of the Concert of Europe, promoting cooperation among great powers.
  3. The territorial adjustments that significantly expanded the Austrian Empire's influence.
  4. The suppression of liberal and nationalist movements across the continent.

Answer: The suppression of liberal and nationalist movements across the continent.

Explanation: The Congress of Vienna's attempts to suppress burgeoning liberal and nationalist sentiments, in favor of conservative monarchical rule, directly clashed with the rising tide of 19th-century political ideologies, leading to future revolutions.

19. Which ancient civilization built the pyramids?

  1. Roman
  2. Greek
  3. Egyptian
  4. Mesopotamian

Answer: Egyptian

Explanation: The ancient Egyptians are well-known for constructing the majestic pyramids as tombs for their pharaohs.

20. Who was the first Roman Emperor?

  1. Julius Caesar
  2. Augustus
  3. Nero
  4. Constantine

Answer: Augustus

Explanation: Augustus, born Octavian, was the first Roman Emperor, reigning from 27 BC until his death in AD 14.

21. Which ancient civilization is credited with the invention of cuneiform script, one of the earliest known systems of writing?

  1. Sumerian
  2. Egyptian
  3. Indus Valley
  4. Mesopotamian

Answer: Sumerian

Explanation: Cuneiform was developed by the Sumerians in Mesopotamia. While Mesopotamia is the region, Sumerians were the specific civilization responsible for its invention.

22. The Peloponnesian War, a protracted conflict between Athens and Sparta, significantly weakened which of the following ancient Greek city-states, paving the way for Macedonian dominance?

  1. Corinth
  2. Thebes
  3. Athens
  4. Syracuse

Answer: Athens

Explanation: The Peloponnesian War primarily involved Athens and Sparta. Athens, though initially powerful, was ultimately defeated and severely weakened.

23. Which of the following events is considered a pivotal moment in the decline of the Roman Republic and the rise of the Roman Empire?

  1. The Punic Wars
  2. The assassination of Julius Caesar
  3. The Battle of Adrianople
  4. The Edict of Milan

Answer: The assassination of Julius Caesar

Explanation: The assassination of Julius Caesar in 44 BCE led to a power vacuum and subsequent civil wars, ultimately culminating in the establishment of the Roman Empire under Augustus.

24. The concept of 'Mandate of Heaven,' used to legitimize the rule of emperors, was a prominent feature of which ancient civilization?

  1. Ancient Egypt
  2. Imperial China
  3. Persian Empire
  4. Maurya Empire

Answer: Imperial China

Explanation: The Mandate of Heaven was a philosophical concept that originated in ancient China, used to justify the rule of emperors and dynasties.

25. The Black Death, a devastating pandemic in the 14th century, had its most significant immediate impact on which of the following regions, leading to widespread social and economic changes?

  1. East Asia
  2. Sub-Saharan Africa
  3. Europe
  4. The Americas

Answer: Europe

Explanation: The Black Death primarily affected Europe, causing a significant population decline and profound social and economic disruption across the continent.
