Constitution MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Constitution questions to revise the SSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 701 questions.

26. Which schedule of the Constitution deals with anti-defection law?

  1. Eighth Schedule
  2. Ninth Schedule
  3. Tenth Schedule
  4. Eleventh Schedule

Answer: Tenth Schedule

Explanation: The Tenth Schedule (added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985) contains provisions for disqualification on grounds of defection.

27. Article 21 of the Indian Constitution guarantees:

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right against Exploitation
  3. Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion

Answer: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty

Explanation: Article 21 states: 'No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.'

28. The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by which amendment?

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 46th Amendment
  4. 52nd Amendment

Answer: 44th Amendment

Explanation: The 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) removed the Right to Property from Fundamental Rights and made it a legal right under Article 300A.

29. How many members can the President nominate to the Rajya Sabha?

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 10
  4. 12

Answer: 12

Explanation: The President nominates 12 members to Rajya Sabha who have distinguished themselves in literature, science, art, or social service.

30. Which constitutional body is known as the 'watchdog of public finance'?

  1. Reserve Bank of India
  2. Comptroller and Auditor General
  3. Finance Commission
  4. SEBI

Answer: Comptroller and Auditor General

Explanation: The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG), under Article 148, audits all government accounts and is called the watchdog of public finance.

31. Under which article can the President impose President's Rule in a State?

  1. Article 352
  2. Article 356
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 370

Answer: Article 356

Explanation: Article 356 (President's Rule) is invoked when the constitutional machinery of a State fails. It is also called State Emergency.

32. How many Fundamental Duties are listed in the Indian Constitution?

  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 11
  4. 12

Answer: 11

Explanation: There are 11 Fundamental Duties. Initially 10 were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976); the 11th (related to education for children) was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

33. The concept of 'Judicial Review' in India means:

  1. The judiciary reviews the budget
  2. The judiciary can declare laws unconstitutional
  3. The President reviews judicial decisions
  4. Parliament reviews court judgements

Answer: The judiciary can declare laws unconstitutional

Explanation: Judicial Review gives the Supreme Court and High Courts the power to examine legislation and executive actions and declare them void if they violate the Constitution.

34. Which article of the Indian Constitution deals with the formation of new States?

  1. Article 1
  2. Article 2
  3. Article 3
  4. Article 5

Answer: Article 3

Explanation: Article 3 empowers Parliament to admit new States, form new States from existing ones, alter boundaries or change names.

35. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) is called:

  1. The Democratic Amendment
  2. The Mini Constitution
  3. The Social Amendment
  4. The Economic Amendment

Answer: The Mini Constitution

Explanation: The 42nd Amendment is called the 'Mini Constitution' because it made extensive changes, including adding 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' to the Preamble.

36. Which article guarantees freedom of speech and expression in India?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 17
  3. Article 19(1)(a)
  4. Article 21

Answer: Article 19(1)(a)

Explanation: Article 19(1)(a) guarantees all citizens the right to freedom of speech and expression.

37. The Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with:

  1. Languages
  2. Anti-defection law
  3. Distribution of powers between Centre and States
  4. Tribal areas

Answer: Distribution of powers between Centre and States

Explanation: The Seventh Schedule contains three lists — Union List, State List, and Concurrent List — which distribute legislative powers between the Centre and States.

38. What is the minimum age for a person to become a member of Rajya Sabha?

  1. 21 years
  2. 25 years
  3. 30 years
  4. 35 years

Answer: 30 years

Explanation: A person must be at least 30 years old to be elected to the Rajya Sabha.

39. How is the Speaker of Lok Sabha elected?

  1. Appointed by the President
  2. Elected by all Lok Sabha members
  3. Appointed by the Prime Minister
  4. Elected by senior members only

Answer: Elected by all Lok Sabha members

Explanation: The Speaker of Lok Sabha is elected from among the members of the Lok Sabha by its members.

40. Which amendment reduced the voting age from 21 to 18 years?

  1. 42nd Amendment
  2. 44th Amendment
  3. 61st Amendment
  4. 73rd Amendment

Answer: 61st Amendment

Explanation: The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act (1988) reduced the voting age from 21 to 18 years.

41. The Indian Constitution was adopted on:

  1. 15 August 1947
  2. 26 November 1949
  3. 26 January 1950
  4. 1 January 1949

Answer: 26 November 1949

Explanation: The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day/Samvidhan Divas). It came into force on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months, 18 days.

42. The Constituent Assembly was chaired by:

  1. Jawaharlal Nehru
  2. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  3. Dr. Rajendra Prasad
  4. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Explanation: Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President (permanent chairman) of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the chairman of the Drafting Committee and is called the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'.

43. The Constitution of India originally had:

  1. 395 articles, 8 schedules
  2. 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules (1950)
  3. 448 articles, 12 schedules
  4. 300 articles, 7 schedules

Answer: 395 articles, 22 parts, 8 schedules (1950)

Explanation: The original Constitution (1950) had 395 articles, 22 parts, and 8 schedules. After amendments, it now has 448 articles, 25 parts, and 12 schedules. It is the world's lengthiest written constitution.

44. India borrowed the concept of 'Fundamental Rights' from:

  1. UK
  2. USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)
  3. USSR
  4. Australia

Answer: USA (Bill of Rights, 1791)

Explanation: India borrowed the concept of Fundamental Rights from the US Constitution (First 10 Amendments — Bill of Rights). The idea of judicial review was also borrowed from the USA.

45. The concept of 'Directive Principles of State Policy' was borrowed from:

  1. USA
  2. UK
  3. Ireland (Irish Constitution of 1937)
  4. USSR

Answer: Ireland (Irish Constitution of 1937)

Explanation: DPSP (Part IV, Articles 36–51) was borrowed from the Irish Constitution (Éire) of 1937. DPSPs are non-justiciable (not enforceable by courts) but fundamental to governance.

46. The Preamble to the Indian Constitution begins with:

  1. 'We, the Parliament of India'
  2. 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'
  3. 'India is a democratic country'
  4. 'In the name of God'

Answer: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved...'

Explanation: The Preamble begins: 'We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic and to secure to all its citizens JUSTICE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY, and FRATERNITY...'

47. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the:

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
  2. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  3. 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
  4. 73rd Amendment Act, 1992

Answer: 42nd Amendment Act, 1976

Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) during Emergency (PM Indira Gandhi) added 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble, making India a 'Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic'.

48. The Preamble is considered to be:

  1. A part of the Constitution (Supreme Court's Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)
  2. Not a part of the Constitution
  3. A directive principle
  4. An ordinary statute

Answer: A part of the Constitution (Supreme Court's Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973)

Explanation: Initially in Berubari Union Case (1960), the SC said Preamble is not part of the Constitution. But in Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973), the SC held that the Preamble IS a part of the Constitution and can be amended (but not the basic structure).

49. Fundamental Rights are enshrined in:

  1. Part II (Articles 5–11)
  2. Part III (Articles 12–35)
  3. Part IV (Articles 36–51)
  4. Part I (Articles 1–4)

Answer: Part III (Articles 12–35)

Explanation: Fundamental Rights are in Part III (Articles 12–35) of the Indian Constitution. They are justiciable (enforceable by courts). Originally 7, now 6 (Right to Property removed by 44th Amendment 1978, made a legal right under Article 300A).

50. The six Fundamental Rights are:

  1. Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies
  2. Right to Property, Right to Education, Right to Equality, Right to Vote, Right to Work, Right to Freedom
  3. Right to Life, Right to Liberty, Right to Property, Right to Religion, Right to Speech, Right to Assembly
  4. Right to Equality, Right to Work, Right to Education, Right to Food, Right to Health, Right to Housing

Answer: Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural & Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies

Explanation: The 6 Fundamental Rights: 1) Right to Equality (Art 14-18), 2) Right to Freedom (Art 19-22), 3) Right against Exploitation (Art 23-24), 4) Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25-28), 5) Cultural and Educational Rights (Art 29-30), 6) Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art 32).
