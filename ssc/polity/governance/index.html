Governance MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Governance questions to revise the SSC Polity syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 424 questions.

1. Directive Principles are:

  1. Non-justiciable but fundamental to governance
  2. Justiciable and enforceable by courts
  3. Neither justiciable nor fundamental
  4. Justiciable only for SCs/STs

Answer: Non-justiciable but fundamental to governance

Explanation: DPSPs are non-justiciable (courts cannot enforce them) but are fundamental in governance — the state 'shall endeavor' to implement them. Courts use them to interpret laws.

2. Election to Rajya Sabha is conducted through:

  1. Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote
  2. Direct election by people
  3. Appointment by President
  4. Nomination by state legislatures

Answer: Proportional representation by Single Transferable Vote

Explanation: Rajya Sabha members are elected by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies through Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV).

3. The Lokpal Act was passed in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2010
  3. 2015
  4. 2016

Answer: 2013

Explanation: The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act was passed in December 2013. India's first Lokpal (Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose) was appointed in March 2019 — nearly 6 years after the Act was passed.

4. Right to Information Act (RTI) came into force in:

  1. October 2005
  2. January 2005
  3. August 2004
  4. January 2006

Answer: October 2005

Explanation: The Right to Information Act was passed in June 2005 and came into force on 12 October 2005. It replaced the Freedom of Information Act, 2002. Citizens can request information from any public authority.

5. The First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) electoral system used in India means:

  1. Top 2 candidates compete in a runoff
  2. The candidate with the most votes wins, regardless of whether they have an absolute majority
  3. Votes are proportionally distributed to parties
  4. Candidates need 50%+1 votes to win

Answer: The candidate with the most votes wins, regardless of whether they have an absolute majority

Explanation: FPTP (or plurality/simple majority system): the candidate who gets more votes than any other in the constituency wins, even without an absolute majority. India uses FPTP for Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections. Rajya Sabha and State Council elections use proportional representation.

6. Writ of 'Certiorari' is issued to:

  1. Quash/review orders of lower courts or tribunals
  2. Produce a person before court
  3. Prohibit an authority from acting
  4. Command a person to perform duty

Answer: Quash/review orders of lower courts or tribunals

Explanation: Certiorari (meaning 'to be certified'): issued by higher court to lower court/tribunal to quash its order on grounds of lack of jurisdiction, error of law, or violation of natural justice.

7. Tamil Nadu has how many seats in Lok Sabha?

  1. 39
  2. 38
  3. 40
  4. 41

Answer: 39

Explanation: Tamil Nadu has 39 Lok Sabha seats — one of the highest in India. It also has 18 Rajya Sabha seats (one of the highest proportional representations).

8. India's first Lokpal was appointed in:

  1. 2019
  2. 2018
  3. 2020
  4. 2017

Answer: 2019

Explanation: Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose became India's first Lokpal in March 2019, more than 48 years after the first Lokpal Bill was introduced in 1968.

9. The Directive Principles of State Policy are:

  1. Enforceable by courts
  2. Non-enforceable but fundamental in governance
  3. Enforceable only in emergencies
  4. Applicable only to the Central Government

Answer: Non-enforceable but fundamental in governance

Explanation: DPSPs (Part IV) are non-justiciable — courts cannot enforce them — but they are 'fundamental in the governance of the country' and guide State policy.

10. The Right to Information Act was passed in:

  1. 2005
  2. 2000
  3. 2003
  4. 2007

Answer: 2005

Explanation: The Right to Information (RTI) Act 2005 was passed to provide citizens access to information held by public authorities, making governance more transparent and accountable.

11. The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act was passed in:

  1. 2013
  2. 2011
  3. 2015
  4. 2010

Answer: 2013

Explanation: The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013 established the Lokpal at the Union level and Lokayuktas at the state level for investigation of corruption charges against public servants.

12. Which writ is used to transfer a case from lower court to High Court?

  1. Certiorari
  2. Mandamus
  3. Habeas Corpus
  4. Prohibition

Answer: Certiorari

Explanation: Certiorari can be used to transfer a case from a lower court to a superior court and to quash the order of the lower court if it has exceeded its jurisdiction or violated natural justice.

13. Which of the following is responsible for making laws in India?

  1. Executive
  2. Judiciary
  3. Legislature
  4. Press

Answer: Legislature

Explanation: The Legislature, comprising Parliament at the Union level and State Legislatures, is primarily responsible for law-making.

14. What is the main purpose of good governance?

  1. To benefit a specific political party
  2. To ensure efficient and effective public service delivery
  3. To increase military power
  4. To control private businesses

Answer: To ensure efficient and effective public service delivery

Explanation: Good governance focuses on transparent, accountable, and participatory processes that deliver public services effectively to all citizens.

15. Which of the following scenarios best exemplifies a successful application of 'adaptive governance' principles in India, considering both institutional flexibility and stakeholder inclusivity?

  1. The rapid amendment of environmental laws to accommodate new industrial projects without prior public consultation.
  2. The establishment of a multi-stakeholder platform for managing a critical river basin, allowing for recalibration of water usage policies based on evolving ecological data and local community input.
  3. The centralization of all disaster management responses under a single national agency, streamlining decision-making during emergencies.
  4. The creation of a permanent, unelected expert committee to oversee all policy formulation in a specific sector, bypassing parliamentary debate.

Answer: The establishment of a multi-stakeholder platform for managing a critical river basin, allowing for recalibration of water usage policies based on evolving ecological data and local community input.

Explanation: Adaptive governance emphasizes flexible institutions and stakeholder participation to respond to complex, changing environments. The river basin example demonstrates both these principles effectively.

16. Consider the impact of the 'Right to Information Act' (RTI) on governance in India. Which of the following statements most accurately reflects its contribution to both 'transparency' and 'accountability' while simultaneously posing a challenge to the traditional 'bureaucratic discretion' model?

  1. RTI has primarily led to an increase in litigation against government officials, thereby reducing the efficiency of public services.
  2. By mandating proactive disclosure and enabling citizens to seek information, RTI directly enhances transparency and holds public authorities accountable, thereby limiting arbitrary decision-making.
  3. The Act has inadvertently created a parallel system of information dissemination, often contradicting official government reports and causing public confusion.
  4. RTI's main effect has been to shift the burden of proof from the government to the citizen, making it harder to challenge official decisions.

Answer: By mandating proactive disclosure and enabling citizens to seek information, RTI directly enhances transparency and holds public authorities accountable, thereby limiting arbitrary decision-making.

Explanation: RTI directly fosters transparency by enabling access to information and promotes accountability by allowing scrutiny of government actions, which inherently curtails unchecked bureaucratic discretion.

17. A state government implements a new 'single window clearance system' for industrial investments, aiming to reduce bureaucratic hurdles and improve the 'ease of doing business'. This initiative, while promoting economic growth, raises potential concerns regarding 'environmental governance' if which of the following conditions is met?

  1. The system integrates robust environmental impact assessments and public hearings as mandatory steps within the single window process.
  2. The new system significantly reduces the time taken for all approvals, including environmental clearances, without proportionate strengthening of regulatory oversight capacity.
  3. It establishes a dedicated grievance redressal mechanism for environmental violations identified post-clearance.
  4. The system is designed to prioritize investments in renewable energy sectors exclusively.

Answer: The new system significantly reduces the time taken for all approvals, including environmental clearances, without proportionate strengthening of regulatory oversight capacity.

Explanation: Reducing clearance times without strengthening regulatory oversight can lead to superficial environmental assessments, compromising environmental governance despite economic benefits. The focus is on the potential negative impact.

18. Which of the following is considered a key characteristic of good governance?

  1. Transparency
  2. Bureaucracy
  3. Centralization
  4. Secrecy

Answer: Transparency

Explanation: Transparency ensures open access to information and accountability. It is a fundamental principle of good governance.

19. In a democratic governance system, who ultimately holds the power?

  1. The people
  2. The military
  3. The king
  4. The judiciary

Answer: The people

Explanation: In a democracy, power is derived from the consent of the governed, meaning the people ultimately hold the power.

20. Which of the following principles is NOT a core component of good governance as advocated by international organizations like the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)?

  1. Transparency and Accountability
  2. Rule of Law and Equity
  3. Efficiency and Effectiveness
  4. Centralized Decision-Making

Answer: Centralized Decision-Making

Explanation: Good governance emphasizes participatory and decentralized decision-making, not centralized. Centralization can hinder accountability and responsiveness.

21. The concept of 'e-governance' primarily aims to achieve which of the following in public administration?

  1. Increase physical presence of citizens at government offices
  2. Reduce government expenditure on infrastructure projects
  3. Enhance transparency, efficiency, and citizen participation through technology
  4. Promote manual record-keeping for better security

Answer: Enhance transparency, efficiency, and citizen participation through technology

Explanation: E-governance leverages information and communication technologies to improve public service delivery, transparency, and citizen engagement. It aims to streamline processes and reduce manual interventions.

22. Which of the following bodies is primarily responsible for ensuring accountability of the executive to the legislature in the Indian parliamentary system?

  1. The Supreme Court of India
  2. The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)
  3. Parliamentary Committees and Question Hour
  4. The Election Commission of India

Answer: Parliamentary Committees and Question Hour

Explanation: Parliamentary committees and the Question Hour are direct mechanisms for the legislature to scrutinize and hold the executive accountable. CAG audits financial accounts, and the Supreme Court interprets laws.

23. The Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005, is a significant tool for promoting good governance in India because it:

  1. Allows citizens to directly participate in policy formulation
  2. Empowers citizens to demand information from public authorities
  3. Provides financial assistance to marginalized sections
  4. Establishes a centralized authority for all government services

Answer: Empowers citizens to demand information from public authorities

Explanation: The RTI Act grants citizens the legal right to access information held by public authorities, enhancing transparency and accountability. It is not about direct policy formulation or financial aid.

24. Which of the following initiatives is NOT directly aimed at strengthening 'local governance' in India?

  1. 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts
  2. Panchayati Raj Institutions
  3. Smart Cities Mission
  4. National Security Council

Answer: National Security Council

Explanation: The National Security Council deals with national security matters, not local governance. The 73rd and 74th Amendments, along with Panchayati Raj and Smart Cities, focus on strengthening local bodies.

25. What is the term for the system of governance where power is divided between a central authority and constituent units?

  1. Federalism
  2. Confederalism
  3. Unitary
  4. Autocracy

Answer: Federalism

Explanation: Federalism is a system of governance where power is divided between a central authority and constituent units, such as states or provinces.
