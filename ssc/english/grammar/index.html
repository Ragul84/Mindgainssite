1. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Neither of the two candidates (A)/ have been able to (B)/ secure the minimum qualifying marks (C)/ for the interview. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). 'Neither of' is followed by a plural noun but takes a singular verb. Therefore, 'have' should be replaced with 'has'.

2. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. If I ______ a bird, I would fly across the ocean.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. am
  4. had been

Answer: were

Explanation: This is a hypothetical conditional sentence (Type 2). In such sentences, 'were' is used for all subjects (including 'I', 'he', 'she', 'it') in the 'if' clause to express an unreal or improbable condition.

3. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. The manager, along with his team members, (A)/ are working diligently (B)/ to meet the project deadline (C)/ by next week. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). When two subjects are joined by 'along with', 'as well as', 'together with', etc., the verb agrees with the first subject. Here, 'The manager' is singular, so the verb should be 'is' instead of 'are'.

4. Choose the most appropriate option to complete the sentence. Hardly had he entered the room ______ the phone rang.

  1. than
  2. when
  3. then
  4. as soon as

Answer: when

Explanation: The correlative conjunction for 'Hardly' (or 'Scarcely') is 'when' (or 'before'). 'Than' is used with 'No sooner'.

5. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. She is one of the most brilliant students (A)/ that has ever (B)/ attended this university (C)/ in its history. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). When 'one of the' is followed by a plural noun and a relative pronoun (like 'that', 'who', 'which'), the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun (students), not 'one'. So, 'has' should be 'have'.

6. Choose the most appropriate option to complete the sentence. The old man was accused ______ stealing the valuable antique.

  1. for
  2. of
  3. with
  4. on

Answer: of

Explanation: The correct preposition to use with 'accused' when referring to a crime is 'of'. (Accused of something).

7. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Many a student (A)/ have failed (B)/ in the examination (C)/ this year. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). The phrase 'Many a' is always followed by a singular noun and a singular verb. Therefore, 'have' should be replaced with 'has'.

8. Choose the most appropriate option to complete the sentence. By the time we reach the station, the train ______.

  1. will leave
  2. will have left
  3. would leave
  4. is leaving

Answer: will have left

Explanation: This sentence expresses an action that will be completed before another future action. This requires the future perfect tense ('will have left').

9. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Neither of the two candidates (A)/ have been able to (B)/ secure the minimum qualifying marks. (C)/ No error (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). 'Neither of' is followed by a plural noun/pronoun but takes a singular verb. Therefore, 'have' should be replaced with 'has'.

10. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. am
  4. have been

Answer: were

Explanation: This is a hypothetical conditional sentence (Type 2). In such sentences, 'were' is used for all subjects (I, he, she, it, we, they, you) in the 'if' clause, regardless of number or person, to express an unreal or improbable situation.

11. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. She is one of the most intelligent (A)/ students that has ever (B)/ attended this university. (C)/ No error (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). When 'one of the' is followed by a plural noun and then a relative pronoun (who, which, that), the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun (students), not 'one'. So, 'has' should be 'have'.

12. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. Hardly had I reached the station ______ the train departed.

  1. than
  2. when
  3. then
  4. as

Answer: when

Explanation: The correlative conjunction 'Hardly...when' (or 'Scarcely...when') is used to indicate that one event happened immediately after another. 'Than' is used with 'No sooner'.

13. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. The committee has decided (A)/ to submit its report (B)/ by the end of next month. (C)/ No error (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: D

Explanation: The sentence is grammatically correct. 'Committee' is a collective noun and can take a singular verb ('has decided') when acting as a single unit, and the possessive pronoun 'its' is also singular, agreeing with the singular verb.

14. Choose the correct form of the verb to complete the sentence. Many a student ______ failed in the examination.

  1. have
  2. has
  3. are
  4. were

Answer: has

Explanation: The phrase 'many a' is always followed by a singular noun and takes a singular verb. Therefore, 'has' is the correct choice.

15. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. If he would have studied (A)/ harder, he would have (B)/ passed the exam. (C)/ No error (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: A

Explanation: The error is in part (A). This is a Type 3 conditional sentence (unreal past). The 'if' clause should use the past perfect tense (had + past participle), not 'would have'. So, it should be 'If he had studied'.

16. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ present at the meeting.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. is
  4. has been

Answer: were

Explanation: When 'neither...nor' connects two subjects, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. In this case, 'employees' is plural, so the verb should be 'were'.

17. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. She is one of the most (A)/ brilliant students that (B)/ has ever attended (C)/ this university. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: The error is in part (C). When 'one of the' is followed by a plural noun and then a relative pronoun (like 'that', 'who', 'which'), the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun (students), not 'one'. Therefore, 'has' should be 'have'.

18. Choose the correct option to complete the sentence. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the new policy.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. is
  4. has been

Answer: were

Explanation: When a collective noun (like 'committee') refers to the individual members acting separately, it takes a plural verb. Here, the members are 'divided', indicating individual opinions, hence 'were' is appropriate.

19. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Hardly had I (A)/ reached the station (B)/ when the train (C)/ already left. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: D

Explanation: The error is in part (D). When 'Hardly...when' or 'Scarcely...when' is used, the first action (reaching the station) is in the past perfect tense, and the second action (the train leaving) should be in the simple past tense. So, 'already left' should be 'left'.

20. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. Neither the manager nor his employees ______ present at the meeting.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. is
  4. has been

Answer: were

Explanation: When 'neither...nor' connects two subjects, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. Here, 'employees' is plural, so the verb should be 'were'.

21. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. He asked me (A)/ that why I (B)/ was late (C)/ for the class. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). In indirect questions, conjunctions like 'that' are not used before question words like 'why', 'what', 'where', etc. So, 'that why' should simply be 'why'.

22. Choose the correct option to complete the sentence. By the time we reach the theatre, the movie ______.

  1. will start
  2. will have started
  3. starts
  4. started

Answer: will have started

Explanation: This sentence expresses an action that will be completed before another future action. The future perfect tense ('will have started') is used for the action that will be completed first, and the simple present tense ('reach') for the second action.

23. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. The committee (A)/ is divided (B)/ on this issue (C)/ and their opinions vary. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). When a collective noun like 'committee' is divided, it refers to the individual members acting separately, and thus takes a plural verb. So, 'is divided' should be 'are divided'.

24. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. Hardly had he reached the station ______ the train departed.

  1. when
  2. than
  3. then
  4. as

Answer: when

Explanation: The correlative conjunction for 'Hardly' (or 'Scarcely') is 'when'. 'No sooner' is followed by 'than'.

25. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. She is one of the most (A)/ brilliant students (B)/ that has ever (C)/ attended this university. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: The error is in part (C). When 'one of the' is followed by a plural noun and a relative pronoun (who, which, that), the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun, not 'one'. So, 'has' should be 'have' to agree with 'students'.
