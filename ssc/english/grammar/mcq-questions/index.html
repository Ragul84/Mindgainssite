26. Choose the sentence that is grammatically correct.

  1. The news are alarming.
  2. Each of the students have submitted their assignments.
  3. Neither he nor I am responsible for the mistake.
  4. More than one person were injured in the accident.

Answer: Neither he nor I am responsible for the mistake.

Explanation: Option (C) is correct. With 'neither...nor', the verb agrees with the noun/pronoun closest to it ('I' takes 'am'). (A) 'News' is singular, so 'is' should be used. (B) 'Each of' takes a singular verb, so 'has' should be used. (D) 'More than one' takes a singular verb, so 'was' should be used.

27. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. By the time we reach the theatre, the play ______.

  1. will start
  2. will have started
  3. starts
  4. started

Answer: will have started

Explanation: This sentence describes an action that will be completed before another future action. This requires the future perfect tense, 'will have started'.

28. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. He asked me (A)/ that why I was (B)/ late for the meeting (C)/ the previous day. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). In indirect speech, when a question word (like 'why') is used, 'that' is not used before it. The correct form would be 'He asked me why I was...'

29. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Neither of the two candidates (A) have been considered (B) for the position (C) due to their lack of experience (D).

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The subject 'Neither of the two candidates' is singular, so the verb should also be singular. 'have been' should be replaced with 'has been'.

30. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. The committee (A) is divided (B) on this issue, and (C) their opinions vary widely (D).

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: C

Explanation: When a collective noun like 'committee' is divided, it is treated as plural, and the pronoun referring to it should also be plural. Therefore, 'their' is correct, but the verb 'is' should be 'are' if the committee members are acting individually. However, the error is more prominent in the pronoun agreement if we consider 'committee' as a single entity. But if it's divided, it means individual members are acting, making 'their' correct, and 'is divided' should be 'are divided'. The most direct error is the inconsistency. If 'is divided' is used, it implies the committee as a single entity is divided, but then 'their opinions' suggests individual opinions. To maintain consistency, if 'is divided' is used, it should be 'its opinions'. If 'their opinions' is used, it should be 'are divided'. Given the options, 'their opinions' is the segment that creates the inconsistency with 'is divided'. A better phrasing would be 'The committee members are divided on this issue, and their opinions vary widely.' Or 'The committee is divided on this issue, and its opinion varies widely.' The error lies in using 'is divided' with 'their opinions'.

31. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate option. She insisted ______ going to the party alone.

  1. on
  2. at
  3. for
  4. with

Answer: on

Explanation: The verb 'insist' is typically followed by the preposition 'on' when it refers to demanding something or persisting in an action.

32. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. Neither of the two candidates (A) / have been considered (B) / for the position (C) / in the company. (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in part (B). 'Neither of' is followed by a plural noun but takes a singular verb. Therefore, 'have been considered' should be 'has been considered'.

33. Choose the option that best transforms the given sentence into passive voice. Someone has stolen my bicycle.

  1. My bicycle has been stolen by someone.
  2. My bicycle was stolen by someone.
  3. My bicycle is stolen by someone.
  4. My bicycle had been stolen by someone.

Answer: My bicycle has been stolen by someone.

Explanation: The original sentence is in the present perfect tense (has stolen). When converting to passive voice, the structure for present perfect is 'has/have + been + past participle'. Therefore, 'My bicycle has been stolen' is the correct passive form. 'By someone' is optional but grammatically correct.

34. Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. If there is no error, select 'No error'. Neither of the two candidates (A) / have been able to (B) / secure the required votes. (C) / No error (D)

  1. A
  2. B
  3. C
  4. D

Answer: B

Explanation: The error is in segment (B). When 'neither of' is used, the verb should be singular. Therefore, 'have' should be replaced with 'has'. The correct sentence is: 'Neither of the two candidates has been able to secure the required votes.'

35. Choose the correct sentence:

  1. She go to the park every day.
  2. She goes to the park every day.
  3. She going to the park every day.
  4. She gone to the park every day.

Answer: She goes to the park every day.

Explanation: For third-person singular subjects (she, he, it), the verb in the simple present tense ends with -s or -es.

36. Identify the type of sentence: 'The sun shines brightly.'

  1. Interrogative
  2. Imperative
  3. Declarative
  4. Exclamatory

Answer: Declarative

Explanation: A declarative sentence makes a statement or expresses an opinion. This sentence states a fact.

37. Identify the sentence with the correct use of a pronoun.

  1. Between you and I, this secret must be kept.
  2. She blamed himself for the mistake.
  3. Everyone submitted their assignments on time.
  4. The dog wagged it's tail excitedly.

Answer: Everyone submitted their assignments on time.

Explanation: 'Everyone' is a singular indefinite pronoun, but in modern English, 'their' is often used for gender neutrality. 'Between you and I' should be 'between you and me'.

38. Which of the following sentences uses the passive voice correctly?

  1. The manager was congratulated by the team.
  2. They were congratulated the manager.
  3. The team congratulated the manager.
  4. Congratulating the manager was done by the team.

Answer: The manager was congratulated by the team.

Explanation: In passive voice, the subject receives the action. 'The manager' is the recipient of 'congratulated'.

39. Select the sentence with the appropriate use of articles.

  1. He is a honest man.
  2. The sun rises in east.
  3. I saw an unique bird.
  4. She bought a new car.

Answer: She bought a new car.

Explanation: 'Honest' starts with a vowel sound, so 'an' is needed. 'East' needs 'the'. 'Unique' starts with a consonant sound, so 'a' is needed.

40. Identify the sentence with a dangling participle.

  1. Running quickly, the bus was missed by John.
  2. Having finished the book, I put it on the shelf.
  3. Walking down the street, I saw an old friend.
  4. Polishing the car, he whistled a tune.

Answer: Running quickly, the bus was missed by John.

Explanation: The participle 'running quickly' illogically modifies 'the bus' instead of 'John'. The subject performing the action of the participle is unclear.

41. Which of the following sentences is grammatically correct and stylistically coherent?

  1. Despite his fervent appeals, the council remained impervious to the plight of the marginalized communities, nor did they offer any amelioration.
  2. Despite his fervent appeals, the council remained impervious to the plight of the marginalized communities; neither did they offer any amelioration.
  3. Despite his fervent appeals, the council remained impervious to the plight of the marginalized communities, and they did not offer any amelioration.
  4. Despite his fervent appeals, the council remained impervious to the plight of the marginalized communities, so they did not offer any amelioration.

Answer: Despite his fervent appeals, the council remained impervious to the plight of the marginalized communities; neither did they offer any amelioration.

Explanation: 'Neither did they' correctly follows the semi-colon to maintain parallelism and express a negative consequence. 'Nor did they' would typically follow a negative statement within the same clause.

42. Identify the sentence that correctly employs both subjunctive mood and proper pronoun agreement.

  1. If I were him, I would insist that the committee reconsider their decision, even if it meant a public outcry.
  2. If I was him, I would insist that the committee reconsiders its decision, even if it meant a public outcry.
  3. If I were he, I would insist that the committee reconsider its decision, even if it meant a public outcry.
  4. If I was he, I would insist that the committee reconsider their decision, even if it meant a public outcry.

Answer: If I were he, I would insist that the committee reconsider its decision, even if it meant a public outcry.

Explanation: The subjunctive mood requires 'were' for hypothetical situations. The pronoun after 'were' should be in the nominative case ('he'), and 'committee' takes the singular possessive pronoun 'its'.

43. Choose the sentence that demonstrates correct parallelism and appropriate use of correlative conjunctions.

  1. Not only did the team demonstrate exceptional skill in the preliminary rounds, but also they displayed remarkable sportsmanship throughout the entire tournament.
  2. Not only the team demonstrated exceptional skill in the preliminary rounds, but they also displayed remarkable sportsmanship throughout the entire tournament.
  3. The team not only demonstrated exceptional skill in the preliminary rounds, but also they displayed remarkable sportsmanship throughout the entire tournament.
  4. Not only did the team demonstrate exceptional skill in the preliminary rounds, but they also displayed remarkable sportsmanship throughout the entire tournament.

Answer: Not only did the team demonstrate exceptional skill in the preliminary rounds, but they also displayed remarkable sportsmanship throughout the entire tournament.

Explanation: When 'not only' introduces an inverted sentence structure (did the team demonstrate), 'but also' must maintain a parallel structure. Option D correctly places 'they also displayed' to align with the initial inversion.

44. Identify the common noun in the following sentence: 'The boy played with his new toy.'

  1. boy
  2. played
  3. his
  4. new

Answer: boy

Explanation: A common noun refers to a general person, place, or thing. 'Boy' is a general term for a male child.

45. The manager, along with his team members, _______ responsible for the project's success.

  1. is
  2. are
  3. were
  4. have been

Answer: is

Explanation: The subject of the sentence is 'The manager' (singular), and 'along with his team members' is a parenthetical phrase that does not affect the verb's agreement. Therefore, a singular verb is required.

46. Neither of the two candidates _______ suitable for the position.

  1. is
  2. are
  3. were
  4. have been

Answer: is

Explanation: When 'neither of' is used with a plural noun, the verb should still be singular. 'Neither' acts as a singular pronoun.

47. If I _______ a bird, I would fly to you.

  1. was
  2. were
  3. am
  4. had been

Answer: were

Explanation: This is a hypothetical conditional sentence (Type 2) expressing an unreal situation. In such cases, 'were' is used for all persons, including 'I'.

48. She insisted _______ going to the party, despite feeling unwell.

  1. on
  2. at
  3. in
  4. for

Answer: on

Explanation: The verb 'insist' is typically followed by the preposition 'on' when referring to a course of action.

49. Having finished her homework, _______ to the park.

  1. she went
  2. went she
  3. her going
  4. she going

Answer: she went

Explanation: The participial phrase 'Having finished her homework' modifies the subject of the main clause. The main clause needs a subject and a finite verb.

50. Which of the following sentences correctly employs both a subjunctive mood and a gerund phrase?

  1. It is imperative that he attend the meeting, despite his disliking public speaking.
  2. If he were to attend the meeting, his disliking public speaking would be evident.
  3. He insists on attending the meeting, although he dislikes public speaking.
  4. The committee recommended him attending the meeting, should he overcome his dislike for public speaking.

Answer: It is imperative that he attend the meeting, despite his disliking public speaking.

Explanation: Option A uses 'imperative that he attend' (subjunctive) and 'disliking public speaking' (gerund phrase functioning as a noun). The other options either lack one of these elements or use them incorrectly.
