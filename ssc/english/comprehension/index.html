Comprehension MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Comprehension questions to revise the SSC English syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 400 questions.

1. The recent surge in global food prices, exacerbated by supply chain disruptions and geopolitical tensions, has raised concerns about food security in vulnerable nations. Experts warn that without concerted international efforts to stabilize markets and boost agricultural productivity, millions could face severe hunger. This situation underscores the urgent need for resilient food systems and diversified sourcing strategies.

  1. The passage primarily discusses the immediate causes and potential consequences of rising food prices.
  2. The passage highlights the success of current international efforts in addressing food security.
  3. The passage focuses on the agricultural productivity of developed nations.
  4. The passage suggests that geopolitical tensions are the sole cause of food price inflation.

Answer: The passage primarily discusses the immediate causes and potential consequences of rising food prices.

Explanation: The passage details the reasons for the price surge and its negative impact, which aligns with the first option. It does not focus on success, specific nations, or a single cause.

2. Despite significant advancements in artificial intelligence, the ethical implications of its widespread deployment remain a subject of intense debate. Concerns range from job displacement and algorithmic bias to issues of privacy and accountability. Striking a balance between innovation and responsible development is crucial for harnessing AI's potential without compromising societal values.

  1. What is the central theme of the provided passage?
  2. The passage suggests that AI's ethical implications are easily resolvable.
  3. The passage primarily focuses on the technical challenges of AI development.
  4. The passage advocates for unrestricted AI development to accelerate innovation.

Answer: What is the central theme of the provided passage?

Explanation: The passage revolves around the ethical challenges of AI and the need for balance. This directly corresponds to the central theme of the passage.

3. The recent policy shifts concerning renewable energy tariffs have ignited a fervent debate among stakeholders. While proponents laud the move as a necessary step towards fiscal prudence and market rationalization, critics argue it could stifle nascent industries and deter foreign investment. The long-term implications for India's ambitious climate targets remain uncertain, contingent on how these adjustments are absorbed by the energy sector and the subsequent investment climate they foster.

  1. The primary purpose of the passage is to explain the technical aspects of renewable energy tariffs.
  2. The passage suggests that the new policy will unequivocally boost foreign investment in renewable energy.
  3. The author presents a balanced view on the recent policy changes, highlighting both potential benefits and drawbacks.
  4. The passage implies that the government's main goal is to reduce India's climate targets.

Answer: The author presents a balanced view on the recent policy changes, highlighting both potential benefits and drawbacks.

Explanation: The passage discusses both positive and negative perspectives on the policy shifts, indicating a balanced viewpoint. It does not lean definitively towards one outcome.

4. Despite impressive economic growth figures, a closer examination reveals a persistent disparity in wealth distribution across various demographics. This chasm, often exacerbated by technological advancements and automation, poses a significant challenge to long-term social cohesion and sustainable development. Addressing this requires not just economic policy adjustments but also a renewed focus on inclusive education and skill development programs to ensure a broader participation in the economic mainstream.

  1. The passage primarily discusses the benefits of technological advancements on wealth distribution.
  2. The author believes that economic growth alone is sufficient to resolve wealth disparity.
  3. The passage suggests that wealth disparity is a complex issue requiring multi-faceted solutions beyond just economic policies.
  4. Technological advancements are presented as the sole cause of wealth disparity.

Answer: The passage suggests that wealth disparity is a complex issue requiring multi-faceted solutions beyond just economic policies.

Explanation: The passage highlights that wealth disparity is a complex issue requiring economic policy, inclusive education, and skill development. It does not attribute disparity solely to technology or suggest economic growth is a standalone solution.

5. The burgeoning gig economy, while offering unprecedented flexibility to workers and businesses, also presents a quagmire of regulatory challenges. Traditional labor laws, designed for a more conventional employer-employee relationship, often struggle to encompass the fluid nature of gig work, leaving many workers vulnerable without adequate social security or benefits. Policymakers are grappling with how to foster innovation without compromising worker protections in this evolving landscape.

  1. The passage primarily praises the gig economy for its flexibility and benefits to workers.
  2. The term 'quagmire' in this context most closely means a simple, easily solvable problem.
  3. The author suggests that existing labor laws are perfectly adequate for the gig economy.
  4. The passage highlights the regulatory difficulties in balancing innovation with worker protection in the gig economy.

Answer: The passage highlights the regulatory difficulties in balancing innovation with worker protection in the gig economy.

Explanation: The passage explicitly states that policymakers are grappling with balancing innovation and worker protection due to the regulatory challenges. 'Quagmire' implies a difficult and complex situation.

6. The recent archaeological discovery in the Indus Valley has shed new light on the sophisticated urban planning and drainage systems of ancient civilizations. Far from being rudimentary, the findings indicate a meticulous approach to public sanitation and civic infrastructure that predates many contemporary assumptions about early human settlements. This reevaluation challenges the Eurocentric narrative often prevalent in historical discourse, suggesting a more diverse and advanced trajectory for ancient societal development.

  1. The discovery confirms that ancient civilizations in the Indus Valley had rudimentary urban planning.
  2. The passage argues that ancient civilizations lacked sophisticated public sanitation.
  3. The findings suggest that ancient Indus Valley civilizations had advanced urban planning, challenging previous historical assumptions.
  4. The primary focus of the passage is on contemporary assumptions about modern human settlements.

Answer: The findings suggest that ancient Indus Valley civilizations had advanced urban planning, challenging previous historical assumptions.

Explanation: The passage states that the discovery sheds new light on sophisticated urban planning, challenging rudimentary assumptions. It highlights the advanced nature of the Indus Valley civilization.

7. The pervasive influence of social media on political discourse has become a double-edged sword. While it facilitates rapid dissemination of information and mobilizes public opinion, it also fosters echo chambers and amplifies misinformation, often leading to increased polarization. Navigating this complex digital landscape requires media literacy and critical thinking skills more than ever, as citizens grapple with discerning credible sources from propaganda.

  1. Social media's primary role is to unite diverse political opinions.
  2. The passage suggests that social media universally improves political discourse.
  3. The author implies that social media has both beneficial and detrimental effects on political discourse.
  4. The passage emphasizes that misinformation is easily identifiable on social media without critical thinking.

Answer: The author implies that social media has both beneficial and detrimental effects on political discourse.

Explanation: The phrase 'double-edged sword' indicates both positive (dissemination, mobilization) and negative (echo chambers, misinformation) effects. Critical thinking is presented as necessary for navigating this.

8. The recent policy pivot towards indigenous manufacturing, while ostensibly aimed at fostering self-reliance, risks alienating crucial foreign direct investment (FDI) at a juncture when global capital flows are already constricted. Critics argue that an overzealous protectionist stance could inadvertently stifle innovation by limiting exposure to international best practices and technologies. Furthermore, the long-term viability of domestic industries without robust export markets remains a pertinent concern, suggesting a potential trade-off between immediate nationalistic gains and sustainable economic growth. Which of the following inferences can be most logically drawn from the passage?

  1. The passage suggests that prioritizing indigenous manufacturing might have unintended negative consequences for economic growth.
  2. The author believes that foreign direct investment is inherently detrimental to national self-reliance.
  3. The primary goal of the new policy is to completely eliminate the need for international trade.
  4. The passage implies that domestic industries are incapable of robust growth without government subsidies.

Answer: The passage suggests that prioritizing indigenous manufacturing might have unintended negative consequences for economic growth.

Explanation: The passage highlights risks and concerns associated with the policy, indicating potential negative consequences. It discusses trade-offs and unintended effects.

9. Despite the prevailing narrative of technological ubiquity democratizing access to information, a subtle but significant 'digital divide' persists, not merely in terms of internet access, but in the nuanced ability to critically evaluate and synthesize information from diverse online sources. This disparity, often correlated with socioeconomic status and educational attainment, creates an epistemic chasm, where a segment of the population remains vulnerable to misinformation and algorithmic biases, further exacerbating existing inequalities in civic engagement and economic opportunity. What does the term 'epistemic chasm' signify in the context of the passage?

  1. A physical gap in internet infrastructure between different regions.
  2. A fundamental difference in the quality and quantity of available online content.
  3. A significant divergence in the ability to understand and process information, leading to unequal knowledge.
  4. A political divide caused by conflicting opinions found on social media platforms.

Answer: A significant divergence in the ability to understand and process information, leading to unequal knowledge.

Explanation: 'Epistemic' relates to knowledge, and 'chasm' implies a deep divide. The passage links this to unequal abilities in evaluating information and vulnerability to misinformation.

10. The ongoing debate surrounding artificial intelligence ethics often fixates on existential threats, overlooking the more immediate and insidious challenge of algorithmic bias embedded in systems that influence everything from loan approvals to criminal justice. These biases, frequently a reflection of historical societal prejudices encoded in training data, perpetuate and amplify inequalities, rather than merely mirroring them. Addressing this systemic issue requires not just technical fixes, but a fundamental re-evaluation of data collection practices and a proactive commitment to diverse representation in AI development teams. What is the central theme of the passage?

  1. The philosophical implications of artificial intelligence surpassing human intelligence.
  2. The critical need to address inherent biases in AI systems that exacerbate societal inequalities.
  3. The technical difficulties in developing unbiased algorithms for complex applications.
  4. The dangers of AI becoming too powerful and controlling human decision-making processes.

Answer: The critical need to address inherent biases in AI systems that exacerbate societal inequalities.

Explanation: The passage emphasizes that algorithmic bias, stemming from societal prejudices in data, perpetuates inequalities and needs systemic re-evaluation. It highlights this immediate challenge over existential threats.

11. The recent surge in e-commerce has led to a significant increase in packaging waste, with many companies relying on single-use plastics to protect their products during shipping. This trend has sparked concerns among environmentalists, who argue that the resulting pollution harms marine life and contaminates the food chain. In response, some firms have begun exploring sustainable packaging alternatives, such as biodegradable materials and reusable containers. As consumers become more environmentally conscious, companies that adopt eco-friendly practices are likely to gain a competitive edge. What is the primary concern driving the shift towards sustainable packaging?

  1. Climate Change
  2. Marine Life
  3. Food Chain
  4. Pollution

Answer: Pollution

Explanation: The primary concern driving the shift is pollution, which harms marine life and contaminates the food chain.

12. India's economic growth has been characterized by a paradox, where high rates of GDP expansion coexist with significant disparities in income and access to basic services. This dichotomy is particularly evident in the rural-urban divide, where urban areas tend to have better infrastructure and more opportunities for economic mobility. To address this issue, policymakers have implemented initiatives aimed at promoting rural development and reducing inequality. What is the primary characteristic of India's economic growth?

  1. Stable
  2. Unbalanced
  3. Sustainable
  4. Volatile

Answer: Unbalanced

Explanation: The primary characteristic is unbalanced growth, marked by disparities in income and access to services.

13. The concept of universal basic income (UBI) has gained traction in recent years, with proponents arguing that it could help alleviate poverty and promote economic security. UBI involves providing every citizen with a regular, unconditional sum of money to cover their basic needs. While some experts see UBI as a potential solution to income inequality, others have raised concerns about its feasibility and effectiveness. What is the primary goal of universal basic income?

  1. Reduce Inequality
  2. Promote Employment
  3. Alleviate Poverty
  4. Increase Productivity

Answer: Alleviate Poverty

Explanation: The primary goal of UBI is to alleviate poverty and promote economic security.

14. The impact of social media on mental health has become a topic of increasing concern, with studies suggesting that excessive use can lead to anxiety, depression, and loneliness. To mitigate these effects, some experts recommend establishing boundaries around social media use, such as limiting screen time and engaging in offline activities. What is the primary concern regarding social media use?

  1. Addiction
  2. Cyberbullying
  3. Mental Health
  4. Privacy

Answer: Mental Health

Explanation: The primary concern is the negative impact on mental health, including anxiety and depression.

15. The Indian government has launched several initiatives aimed at promoting digital literacy and bridging the gap between urban and rural areas. One such initiative is the Digital India program, which seeks to provide universal access to digital infrastructure and services. What is the primary goal of the Digital India program?

  1. Promote E-commerce
  2. Improve Governance
  3. Bridge Urban-Rural Gap
  4. Enhance Cybersecurity

Answer: Bridge Urban-Rural Gap

Explanation: The primary goal is to bridge the gap between urban and rural areas by providing universal access to digital infrastructure.

16. Climate change has become a pressing global issue, with rising temperatures and extreme weather events affecting ecosystems and human societies. To address this challenge, many countries have committed to reducing their carbon emissions and transitioning to renewable energy sources. What is the primary cause of climate change?

  1. Deforestation
  2. Pollution
  3. Greenhouse Gases
  4. Overpopulation

Answer: Greenhouse Gases

Explanation: The primary cause is greenhouse gases, which trap heat and contribute to global warming.

17. The concept of corporate social responsibility (CSR) has gained significant attention in recent years, with companies recognizing the importance of giving back to society. CSR involves voluntary efforts by businesses to improve social and environmental conditions, often through philanthropy or community development projects. What is the primary motivation behind CSR initiatives?

  1. Financial Gain
  2. Regulatory Compliance
  3. Social Welfare
  4. Reputation Management

Answer: Social Welfare

Explanation: The primary motivation is social welfare, as companies seek to improve social and environmental conditions.

18. The Indian economy has experienced significant growth in recent years, driven by a combination of factors including demographic changes, urbanization, and government policies. However, this growth has also been accompanied by concerns about inequality, job creation, and environmental sustainability. What is the primary driver of India's economic growth?

  1. Demographic Dividend
  2. Government Policies
  3. Urbanization
  4. Globalization

Answer: Demographic Dividend

Explanation: The primary driver is the demographic dividend, which refers to the benefits of a large and young workforce.

19. The role of education in promoting social mobility and economic development has been widely recognized, with many experts arguing that it is a key factor in reducing poverty and inequality. To improve educational outcomes, policymakers have implemented initiatives aimed at increasing access to quality education, particularly for disadvantaged groups. What is the primary benefit of education?

  1. Economic Growth
  2. Social Mobility
  3. Cultural Enrichment
  4. Personal Fulfillment

Answer: Social Mobility

Explanation: The primary benefit is social mobility, as education helps individuals acquire skills and knowledge to improve their socio-economic status.

20. The concept of sustainable development has become increasingly important, as countries recognize the need to balance economic growth with environmental protection and social welfare. Sustainable development involves meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. What is the primary principle of sustainable development?

  1. Economic Growth
  2. Environmental Protection
  3. Social Justice
  4. Inter-generational Equity

Answer: Inter-generational Equity

Explanation: The primary principle is inter-generational equity, which involves considering the needs of both present and future generations.

21. The recent surge in e-commerce has led to a significant increase in packaging waste, prompting concerns about the environmental impact of online shopping. As consumers become more environmentally conscious, companies are being forced to rethink their packaging strategies. Sustainable packaging options, such as biodegradable materials and minimal packaging, are gaining popularity. However, implementing these changes can be costly and may affect profit margins. What is the primary concern driving changes in packaging strategies?

  1. Profit margins
  2. Environmental impact
  3. Consumer convenience
  4. Production costs

Answer: Environmental impact

Explanation: The primary concern driving changes in packaging strategies is the environmental impact of online shopping, as consumers become more environmentally conscious.

22. The government's decision to invest in renewable energy sources has been hailed as a major step towards reducing the country's carbon footprint. However, critics argue that the transition to renewable energy will be costly and may lead to job losses in the traditional energy sector. Despite these challenges, the government remains committed to its goal of increasing the share of renewable energy in the national energy mix. What is the government's primary goal?

  1. Reducing energy costs
  2. Increasing job opportunities
  3. Reducing carbon footprint
  4. Improving energy efficiency

Answer: Reducing carbon footprint

Explanation: The government's primary goal is to reduce the country's carbon footprint by investing in renewable energy sources.

23. The rise of social media has transformed the way people consume news, with many opting for online sources over traditional print newspapers. However, this shift has also raised concerns about the spread of misinformation and the erosion of trust in traditional media outlets. As a result, fact-checking initiatives have become increasingly important. What is the primary concern related to the rise of social media?

  1. Loss of traditional jobs
  2. Spread of misinformation
  3. Increased accessibility
  4. Improved journalism

Answer: Spread of misinformation

Explanation: The primary concern related to the rise of social media is the spread of misinformation and the erosion of trust in traditional media outlets.

24. The concept of a universal basic income has gained significant attention in recent years, with proponents arguing that it could be an effective solution to poverty and income inequality. However, critics argue that implementing such a system would be costly and may not be feasible in the current economic climate. What is the primary argument in favor of a universal basic income?

  1. Reducing administrative costs
  2. Simplifying welfare systems
  3. Addressing poverty and inequality
  4. Promoting economic growth

Answer: Addressing poverty and inequality

Explanation: The primary argument in favor of a universal basic income is that it could be an effective solution to poverty and income inequality.

25. The growth of urban populations has led to an increase in demand for housing, resulting in a surge in property prices and rents. As a result, many low-income households are being priced out of their neighborhoods, leading to concerns about gentrification and social inequality. What is the primary consequence of urban population growth?

  1. Increased economic growth
  2. Improved public services
  3. Reduced social inequality
  4. Housing unaffordability

Answer: Housing unaffordability

Explanation: The primary consequence of urban population growth is housing unaffordability, as property prices and rents surge.
