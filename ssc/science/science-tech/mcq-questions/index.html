Science Tech MCQs with answers

26. The SI unit of temperature is:

  1. Kelvin (K)
  2. Celsius (°C)
  3. Fahrenheit (°F)
  4. Rankine (R)

Answer: Kelvin (K)

Explanation: The SI unit of temperature is Kelvin (K). 0 K = absolute zero (−273.15°C), the lowest possible temperature. Conversion: K = °C + 273. Water freezes at 273 K (0°C) and boils at 373 K (100°C).

27. Heat transfer by direct contact of particles is called:

  1. Conduction
  2. Convection
  3. Radiation
  4. Advection

Answer: Conduction

Explanation: Conduction is heat transfer through direct contact — heat flows from hot to cold through a material without the material itself moving. Metals are good conductors; wood and air are poor conductors (insulators).

28. Heat transfer in fluids (liquids and gases) by bulk movement is called:

  1. Convection
  2. Conduction
  3. Radiation
  4. Diffusion

Answer: Convection

Explanation: Convection is heat transfer in fluids (liquids/gases) through the bulk movement of the fluid itself. Hot fluid (less dense) rises, cool fluid (denser) sinks, creating convection currents. Ocean currents and atmospheric circulation are examples.

29. Heat transfer through electromagnetic waves (without a medium) is:

  1. Radiation
  2. Conduction
  3. Convection
  4. Induction

Answer: Radiation

Explanation: Radiation is heat transfer through electromagnetic waves (infrared radiation). It does not need a medium — heat from the sun reaches Earth through vacuum. All objects above 0 K emit thermal radiation.

30. The First Law of Thermodynamics states:

  1. Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted (Conservation of Energy)
  2. Entropy of a system always increases
  3. Heat flows from cold to hot
  4. Energy is quantized

Answer: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted (Conservation of Energy)

Explanation: First Law of Thermodynamics is the law of conservation of energy: the total energy of an isolated system is constant. Energy can change forms (kinetic to potential, heat to work) but cannot be created or destroyed.

31. The Second Law of Thermodynamics states:

  1. Entropy of an isolated system always increases (natural processes are irreversible)
  2. Energy is conserved
  3. Heat flows from hot to cold
  4. All processes are reversible

Answer: Entropy of an isolated system always increases (natural processes are irreversible)

Explanation: Second Law: Entropy (disorder) of the universe always increases in natural processes. Heat spontaneously flows from hot to cold (not vice versa). No heat engine can be 100% efficient — some energy always becomes unusable.

32. The specific heat capacity of water is:

  1. 4,186 J/kg/K (4.186 kJ/kg/K) — highest among common substances
  2. 1,000 J/kg/K
  3. 400 J/kg/K
  4. 100 J/kg/K

Answer: 4,186 J/kg/K (4.186 kJ/kg/K) — highest among common substances

Explanation: Water has specific heat capacity of 4,186 J/kg/K — one of the highest among common substances. This means water heats up and cools down slowly, moderating Earth's climate and making it ideal as a coolant.

33. Radioactivity was discovered by:

  1. Henri Becquerel (1896)
  2. Marie Curie
  3. Ernest Rutherford
  4. Albert Einstein

Answer: Henri Becquerel (1896)

Explanation: Radioactivity was discovered by Henri Becquerel in 1896 when he found that uranium salts emitted penetrating radiation. Marie Curie and Pierre Curie subsequently discovered polonium and radium (1898).

34. Alpha particles are:

  1. Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2)
  2. Electrons
  3. Electromagnetic radiation
  4. Neutrons

Answer: Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2)

Explanation: Alpha particles (α) are helium-4 nuclei with 2 protons and 2 neutrons (charge +2). They have the greatest ionizing power but least penetrating power — stopped by a sheet of paper or a few cm of air.

35. Beta particles are:

  1. High-energy electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺) emitted from nuclei
  2. Helium nuclei
  3. Electromagnetic radiation
  4. Neutrons

Answer: High-energy electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺) emitted from nuclei

Explanation: Beta particles (β) are high-speed electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺) emitted during radioactive decay. They have moderate ionizing and penetrating power — stopped by a few mm of aluminum.

36. Gamma rays are:

  1. High-energy electromagnetic radiation — most penetrating type of nuclear radiation
  2. Alpha particles
  3. Beta particles
  4. X-rays from electrons

Answer: High-energy electromagnetic radiation — most penetrating type of nuclear radiation

Explanation: Gamma rays (γ) are high-energy electromagnetic radiation emitted from the nucleus during radioactive decay. They have the least ionizing power but greatest penetrating power — need several cm of lead or concrete to stop.

37. A transformer is used to:

  1. Change (increase or decrease) alternating current (AC) voltage
  2. Convert AC to DC
  3. Convert DC to AC
  4. Store electrical energy

Answer: Change (increase or decrease) alternating current (AC) voltage

Explanation: A transformer changes AC voltage using electromagnetic induction. Step-up transformers increase voltage (reduce current); step-down transformers decrease voltage (increase current). V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ (turns ratio).

38. The simplest hydrocarbon (alkane) is:

  1. Methane (CH₄)
  2. Ethane (C₂H₆)
  3. Propane (C₃H₈)
  4. Butane (C₄H₁₀)

Answer: Methane (CH₄)

Explanation: Methane (CH₄) is the simplest alkane (saturated hydrocarbon). It is the main component of natural gas. Propane and butane are used in LPG. Methane is also a potent greenhouse gas (cattle produce it).

39. LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas) mainly consists of:

  1. Propane and Butane
  2. Methane
  3. Ethane
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Propane and Butane

Explanation: LPG mainly consists of propane (C₃H₈) and butane (C₄H₁₀). LPG is stored in cylinders under pressure as liquid. It is used for cooking and heating. CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) is mainly methane.

40. The main component of natural gas is:

  1. Methane (CH₄)
  2. Propane
  3. Butane
  4. Ethane

Answer: Methane (CH₄)

Explanation: Natural gas is primarily methane (CH₄) — about 70-90%. It also contains smaller amounts of ethane, propane, butane, and other hydrocarbons. Used as a cleaner fuel than coal or oil. CNG (for vehicles) is compressed natural gas.

41. Ethanol (ethyl alcohol, C₂H₅OH) is produced by:

  1. Fermentation of sugars by yeast
  2. Distillation of petroleum
  3. Electrolysis of water
  4. Synthesis from methane

Answer: Fermentation of sugars by yeast

Explanation: Ethanol is produced by fermentation — yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) converts sugars (glucose) to ethanol and CO₂: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂. Used in alcoholic beverages, antiseptics, and biofuels.

42. Stainless steel is an alloy of:

  1. Iron, Chromium (10-20%), and Nickel
  2. Iron and Carbon only
  3. Iron and Aluminum
  4. Iron and Zinc

Answer: Iron, Chromium (10-20%), and Nickel

Explanation: Stainless steel = Iron + Chromium (10-20%) + Nickel (0-35%) + small amounts of other metals. Chromium forms a thin oxide layer that prevents rusting. Used in cutlery, medical equipment, building materials.

43. Brass is an alloy of:

  1. Copper and Zinc
  2. Copper and Tin
  3. Iron and Carbon
  4. Aluminum and Copper

Answer: Copper and Zinc

Explanation: Brass = Copper (60-90%) + Zinc (10-40%). It is a gold-colored alloy used in musical instruments, locks, plumbing fittings, and decorative items. Bronze = Copper + Tin (used in coins, statues, bells).

44. Which gas causes suffocation in coal mines (Blackdamp)?

  1. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) and Nitrogen mixture
  2. Carbon monoxide
  3. Methane
  4. Hydrogen sulfide

Answer: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) and Nitrogen mixture

Explanation: Blackdamp (choke damp) in coal mines is primarily CO₂ mixed with Nitrogen, which displaces oxygen and causes suffocation. Firedamp is methane (CH₄) which is explosive. Whitedamp is CO (toxic).

45. Osmosis is the movement of water:

  1. From high water concentration to low water concentration through a semi-permeable membrane
  2. From low to high concentration (active transport)
  3. Of solute particles only
  4. Through open pores in any direction

Answer: From high water concentration to low water concentration through a semi-permeable membrane

Explanation: Osmosis: movement of water molecules from a region of higher water concentration (dilute solution/pure water) to lower water concentration (concentrated solution) through a selectively permeable membrane.

46. The study of insects is called:

  1. Entomology
  2. Ornithology
  3. Ichthyology
  4. Herpetology

Answer: Entomology

Explanation: Entomology is the study of insects. Ornithology = birds. Ichthyology = fish. Herpetology = reptiles and amphibians. Mammology = mammals. Malacology = mollusks.

47. The largest animal on Earth is:

  1. Blue Whale (Balaenoptera musculus)
  2. African Elephant
  3. Sperm Whale
  4. Whale Shark

Answer: Blue Whale (Balaenoptera musculus)

Explanation: Blue Whale is the largest animal on Earth — up to 33 m long and 180 tonnes. Its heart is the size of a small car. Despite its size, it feeds on tiny krill (shrimp-like crustaceans).

48. The fastest land animal on Earth is:

  1. Cheetah (Acinonyx jubatus) — up to 120 km/h
  2. Lion
  3. Leopard
  4. Greyhound

Answer: Cheetah (Acinonyx jubatus) — up to 120 km/h

Explanation: Cheetah is the fastest land animal, capable of running up to 120 km/h in short bursts. They accelerate from 0 to 100 km/h in about 3 seconds. Found in Africa and small population in Iran.

49. Which is the national animal of India?

  1. Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris tigris)
  2. Indian Lion
  3. Indian Elephant
  4. Snow Leopard

Answer: Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris tigris)

Explanation: Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris tigris) is India's national animal (adopted 1973 with Project Tiger). National Bird: Indian Peacock. National Flower: Lotus. National Tree: Banyan. National Fruit: Mango.

50. The main site of absorption of nutrients is:

  1. Small intestine (especially the jejunum)
  2. Stomach
  3. Large intestine
  4. Liver

Answer: Small intestine (especially the jejunum)

Explanation: The small intestine (particularly the jejunum) is the main site of nutrient absorption. Villi and microvilli (brush border) greatly increase surface area. Large intestine absorbs water and forms feces.
