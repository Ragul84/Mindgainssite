SSC Science practice

Physics MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Physics questions to revise the SSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 1,118 questions.

1. Which gas do plants take in to make food during photosynthesis?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Carbon Dioxide
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Carbon Dioxide

Explanation: Plants use carbon dioxide (CO2) from air, water from soil, and sunlight to make food (glucose) through photosynthesis.

2. What is the speed of light?

  1. 3 × 10^6 m/s
  2. 3 × 10^8 m/s
  3. 3 × 10^10 m/s
  4. 3 × 10^4 m/s

Answer: 3 × 10^8 m/s

Explanation: The speed of light in vacuum is approximately 3 × 10^8 m/s (300,000 km/s).

3. What force keeps us on the ground?

  1. Magnetic force
  2. Friction
  3. Gravitational force
  4. Electrical force

Answer: Gravitational force

Explanation: Gravity (gravitational force) is the force of attraction between the Earth and all objects on it. It keeps us grounded.

4. Which part of the plant makes food using sunlight?

  1. Roots
  2. Stem
  3. Leaf
  4. Flower

Answer: Leaf

Explanation: Leaves contain chlorophyll (green pigment) and perform photosynthesis to make food using sunlight, CO2, and water.

5. What type of energy does the Sun give to Earth?

  1. Mechanical energy
  2. Chemical energy
  3. Nuclear energy
  4. Solar (radiant) energy

Answer: Solar (radiant) energy

Explanation: The Sun releases nuclear energy through fusion reactions, which reaches Earth as solar/radiant energy (light and heat).

6. Which vitamin is produced in the body by exposure to sunlight?

  1. Vitamin A
  2. Vitamin B12
  3. Vitamin C
  4. Vitamin D

Answer: Vitamin D

Explanation: Vitamin D is synthesized in the skin when exposed to ultraviolet B (UVB) radiation from sunlight. It is essential for bone health.

7. Sound cannot travel through:

  1. Water
  2. Air
  3. Steel
  4. Vacuum

Answer: Vacuum

Explanation: Sound requires a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel. It cannot travel through a vacuum because there are no particles to transmit the vibrations.

8. Newton's Second Law of Motion states that Force = :

  1. Mass × Velocity
  2. Mass × Acceleration
  3. Velocity / Time
  4. Mass × Distance

Answer: Mass × Acceleration

Explanation: Newton's Second Law: F = ma (Force = Mass × Acceleration). Greater force = greater acceleration for the same mass.

9. The phenomenon of light bending when it passes from one medium to another is:

  1. Reflection
  2. Diffraction
  3. Refraction
  4. Dispersion

Answer: Refraction

Explanation: Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another with different optical density (e.g., from air to glass).

10. Which gas is produced during photosynthesis and released into the atmosphere?

  1. Carbon Dioxide
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Oxygen
  4. Methane

Answer: Oxygen

Explanation: Oxygen is released as a byproduct of photosynthesis when water molecules are split (photolysis) to provide electrons for the light reactions.

11. The 'Law of Gravitation' was proposed by:

  1. Albert Einstein
  2. Galileo Galilei
  3. Isaac Newton
  4. Nicolaus Copernicus

Answer: Isaac Newton

Explanation: Sir Isaac Newton formulated the Universal Law of Gravitation (1687): F = Gm1m2/r², stating that every object attracts every other object with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

12. The 'Photoelectric Effect' was explained by:

  1. Isaac Newton
  2. Max Planck
  3. Albert Einstein
  4. Niels Bohr

Answer: Albert Einstein

Explanation: Albert Einstein explained the photoelectric effect in 1905 using the concept of light as photons (quanta of energy). This work won him the Nobel Prize in Physics (1921).

13. Which is the lightest element in the periodic table?

  1. Helium
  2. Hydrogen
  3. Lithium
  4. Carbon

Answer: Hydrogen

Explanation: Hydrogen (H) is the lightest element with atomic number 1 and atomic mass ~1. It is also the most abundant element in the universe.

14. Newton's First Law of Motion states that:

  1. A body at rest or uniform motion continues unless acted upon by an external force
  2. Force equals mass times acceleration
  3. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction
  4. Momentum is always conserved

Answer: A body at rest or uniform motion continues unless acted upon by an external force

Explanation: Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia): A body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force. This is why we feel a jerk when a bus suddenly brakes.

15. Newton's Second Law of Motion (F = ma) means:

  1. Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration
  2. Force is proportional to velocity
  3. Force is inversely proportional to mass
  4. All bodies accelerate at the same rate

Answer: Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration

Explanation: F = ma — the net force acting on a body equals the product of its mass and acceleration. A larger force produces greater acceleration; a larger mass requires more force to achieve the same acceleration.

16. Newton's Third Law of Motion states:

  1. For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction
  2. Objects fall at 9.8 m/s²
  3. F = ma
  4. Momentum is conserved

Answer: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction

Explanation: Newton's Third Law: For every action (force), there is an equal and opposite reaction. Example: rocket engines push exhaust gas backward (action), gas pushes rocket forward (reaction).

17. The SI unit of force is:

  1. Newton (N)
  2. Dyne
  3. Joule
  4. Pascal

Answer: Newton (N)

Explanation: The SI unit of force is Newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². 1 Newton is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². Named after Sir Isaac Newton.

18. The acceleration due to gravity on Earth is approximately:

  1. 9.8 m/s²
  2. 9.8 m/s
  3. 10 m/s²
  4. 9 m/s²

Answer: 9.8 m/s²

Explanation: The acceleration due to gravity (g) on Earth's surface is approximately 9.8 m/s² (often approximated as 10 m/s²). It varies slightly with latitude (less at equator, more at poles) and altitude (decreases with height).

19. Which law states that the total momentum of a closed system remains constant?

  1. Law of Conservation of Momentum
  2. Newton's Second Law
  3. Law of Conservation of Energy
  4. Archimedes' Principle

Answer: Law of Conservation of Momentum

Explanation: Law of Conservation of Momentum: The total linear momentum of a closed system (no external forces) remains constant. Total momentum before collision = Total momentum after collision.

20. Archimedes' Principle states that:

  1. A body immersed in fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of displaced fluid
  2. Pressure increases with depth
  3. Fluids flow from high to low pressure
  4. All bodies sink in water

Answer: A body immersed in fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of displaced fluid

Explanation: Archimedes' Principle: When a body is fully or partially submerged in a fluid, it experiences a buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced. This explains why ships float and hot air balloons rise.

21. Work done is measured in:

  1. Joules (J)
  2. Watts (W)
  3. Newton (N)
  4. Pascal (Pa)

Answer: Joules (J)

Explanation: Work = Force × Distance. The SI unit of work is Joule (J). 1 J = 1 N × 1 m. Work is done only when a force displaces an object in the direction of the force.

22. The speed of light in vacuum is:

  1. 3 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately 300,000 km/s)
  2. 3 × 10⁶ m/s
  3. 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s
  4. 3 × 10⁵ m/s

Answer: 3 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately 300,000 km/s)

Explanation: The speed of light in vacuum (c) is exactly 299,792,458 m/s ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s ≈ 300,000 km/s. Nothing can travel faster than the speed of light (Einstein's special relativity).

23. Which phenomenon causes the rainbow to form?

  1. Dispersion and total internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets
  2. Reflection of sunlight only
  3. Refraction of light only
  4. Interference of light

Answer: Dispersion and total internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets

Explanation: Rainbow forms because sunlight entering a water droplet is refracted, internally reflected, and dispersed (separated into colours). The 7 colours (VIBGYOR — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) are separated.

24. Total Internal Reflection occurs when:

  1. Light travels from denser to rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle
  2. Light hits a smooth surface
  3. Light passes through a prism
  4. Light bends around obstacles

Answer: Light travels from denser to rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle

Explanation: TIR occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle. TIR is the principle behind optical fibers and diamond's brilliance.

25. A convex lens is used in:

  1. Cameras, magnifying glasses, and correcting farsightedness (hypermetropia)
  2. Correcting nearsightedness
  3. Rearview mirrors
  4. Periscopes

Answer: Cameras, magnifying glasses, and correcting farsightedness (hypermetropia)

Explanation: Convex (converging) lenses are thicker in the middle. They converge parallel light rays to a focal point. Used in cameras, magnifying glasses, telescopes (objective lens), and to correct farsightedness.
