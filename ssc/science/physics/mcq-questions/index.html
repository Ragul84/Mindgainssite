26. A concave lens is used to correct:

  1. Myopia (nearsightedness/shortsightedness)
  2. Hypermetropia (farsightedness)
  3. Astigmatism
  4. Presbyopia

Answer: Myopia (nearsightedness/shortsightedness)

Explanation: Concave (diverging) lenses are thinner in the middle and diverge light rays. They are used to correct myopia (nearsightedness) — where distant objects appear blurry.

27. Which colour of light has the shortest wavelength?

  1. Violet
  2. Red
  3. Yellow
  4. Blue

Answer: Violet

Explanation: Violet light has the shortest wavelength (~380-450 nm) and highest frequency in the visible spectrum. Red has the longest wavelength (~620-750 nm) and lowest frequency. The visible spectrum is VIBGYOR.

28. The primary colours of light (additive colour mixing) are:

  1. Red, Green, Blue (RGB)
  2. Red, Yellow, Blue
  3. Red, Green, Yellow
  4. Cyan, Magenta, Yellow

Answer: Red, Green, Blue (RGB)

Explanation: Primary colours of light: Red, Green, Blue (RGB — additive mixing). Combining all three gives white light. Computer/TV screens use RGB. Pigment primary colours (subtractive) are Cyan, Magenta, Yellow (CMY).

29. Sound travels fastest in:

  1. Solids (e.g., steel ~5,960 m/s)
  2. Liquids
  3. Gases
  4. Vacuum

Answer: Solids (e.g., steel ~5,960 m/s)

Explanation: Sound is a mechanical wave (needs a medium). It travels fastest in solids (5,960 m/s in steel), slower in liquids (1,497 m/s in water), and slowest in gases (343 m/s in air at 20°C). It cannot travel in vacuum.

30. The speed of sound in air at 20°C is approximately:

  1. 343 m/s
  2. 300 m/s
  3. 1,497 m/s
  4. 1,000 m/s

Answer: 343 m/s

Explanation: Speed of sound in air at 20°C is about 343 m/s (approximately 1,235 km/h). Mach 1 = speed of sound. Aircraft flying faster than Mach 1 are supersonic.

31. The human ear can hear sounds in the frequency range:

  1. 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)
  2. 2 Hz to 200 Hz
  3. 200 Hz to 200,000 Hz
  4. 0-20 Hz

Answer: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)

Explanation: The human audible range is 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Sounds below 20 Hz are infrasound (heard by elephants and whales). Sounds above 20,000 Hz are ultrasound (heard by bats and dogs). Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz).

32. SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging) works on the principle of:

  1. Reflection of ultrasound waves
  2. Interference of sound waves
  3. Diffraction of sound
  4. Resonance of sound

Answer: Reflection of ultrasound waves

Explanation: SONAR uses ultrasound waves emitted into water; they reflect off objects (submarines, fish, seabed) and return to the source. By measuring the time taken, distance is calculated. Used in submarines and fish finding.

33. The 'kitchen of the cell' (food factory) is:

  1. Chloroplast
  2. Mitochondria
  3. Ribosome
  4. Golgi apparatus

Answer: Chloroplast

Explanation: Chloroplasts are called the 'kitchen of the cell' because they carry out photosynthesis — using sunlight, CO₂, and water to produce glucose (food) and oxygen. Found only in plant cells. Contain chlorophyll.

34. Nuclear fusion is the process of:

  1. Combining light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy
  2. Splitting heavy nuclei
  3. Beta decay
  4. Alpha emission

Answer: Combining light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy

Explanation: Nuclear fusion: light nuclei (H-2 deuterium + H-3 tritium) fuse at extremely high temperatures to form heavier nuclei (He-4), releasing enormous energy. Occurs in stars (the sun). Future clean energy source (ITER project).

35. Einstein's famous equation E = mc² means:

  1. Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared — mass and energy are equivalent
  2. Energy = mass × charge
  3. E = momentum × velocity
  4. E = force × time

Answer: Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared — mass and energy are equivalent

Explanation: E = mc² (Einstein, 1905): energy (E) = mass (m) × speed of light squared (c²). This shows mass and energy are equivalent. Small mass converts to enormous energy (nuclear reactions). Basis for nuclear technology.

36. Lasers work on the principle of:

  1. Stimulated emission of radiation — coherent, monochromatic, collimated light
  2. Spontaneous emission
  3. Fluorescence
  4. Bioluminescence

Answer: Stimulated emission of radiation — coherent, monochromatic, collimated light

Explanation: LASER (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation): photons stimulate excited atoms to emit identical photons — creating coherent (same phase), monochromatic (single wavelength), collimated (parallel) light. Used in medicine, cutting, communications.

37. The green pigment in plants responsible for photosynthesis is:

  1. Chlorophyll
  2. Carotene
  3. Xanthophyll
  4. Anthocyanin

Answer: Chlorophyll

Explanation: Chlorophyll (mainly chlorophyll-a and chlorophyll-b) absorbs red and blue light for photosynthesis. Carotene (orange) and xanthophyll (yellow) are accessory pigments. Anthocyanin gives red/purple color to flowers/fruits.

38. Birds belong to the class:

  1. Aves
  2. Mammalia
  3. Reptilia
  4. Amphibia

Answer: Aves

Explanation: Birds belong to Class Aves. They are warm-blooded vertebrates with feathers, beaks, hollow bones (for flight), and lay eggs with hard shells. About 10,000 species exist. Adapted for flight (wings, streamlined body).

39. Adrenaline (epinephrine) is produced by:

  1. Adrenal medulla (inner part of adrenal gland)
  2. Pituitary gland
  3. Thyroid gland
  4. Pancreas

Answer: Adrenal medulla (inner part of adrenal gland)

Explanation: Adrenaline (epinephrine) is produced by the adrenal medulla (inner part of adrenal glands, above kidneys). It is the 'fight or flight' hormone — increases heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose during stress.

40. The hormone responsible for 'fight or flight' response is:

  1. Adrenaline (epinephrine)
  2. Insulin
  3. Thyroxine
  4. Oxytocin

Answer: Adrenaline (epinephrine)

Explanation: Adrenaline (epinephrine) triggers the fight-or-flight response — dilates pupils, increases heart rate, raises blood pressure, diverts blood to muscles, releases glucose from glycogen stores.

41. Which phenomenon explains why the sky is blue?

  1. Rayleigh scattering of sunlight by air molecules (shorter wavelengths scatter more)
  2. Reflection of ocean blue color
  3. Absorption by ozone
  4. Refraction of light

Answer: Rayleigh scattering of sunlight by air molecules (shorter wavelengths scatter more)

Explanation: Rayleigh scattering: air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) much more than longer wavelengths (red). The sky appears blue because scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions. At sunset, light travels farther, and blue is scattered away, leaving red/orange.

42. A mirage is caused by:

  1. Total internal reflection of light in hot air layers near the ground
  2. Reflection of sky off water
  3. Refraction by clouds
  4. Dispersion of light

Answer: Total internal reflection of light in hot air layers near the ground

Explanation: Mirage is caused by TIR — hot air near the ground is less dense (lower refractive index) than cooler air above. Light from the sky bends back upward as it tries to enter the low-density layer, creating an illusion of water (the sky reflection).

43. The phenomenon of splitting of white light into its component colours is called:

  1. Dispersion
  2. Refraction
  3. Reflection
  4. Diffraction

Answer: Dispersion

Explanation: Dispersion: white light splits into its component colours (VIBGYOR) when it passes through a prism because different colours have different refractive indices. Violet refracts most; red least.

44. Scattering of light by particles larger than wavelength of light is:

  1. Tyndall effect
  2. Rayleigh scattering
  3. Compton scattering
  4. Mie scattering

Answer: Tyndall effect

Explanation: Tyndall Effect: scattering of light by colloidal particles (particle size ~ wavelength of light). Makes a beam of light visible in a colloid (like dust in a sunbeam through a window). Named after John Tyndall.

45. The principle of optical fiber is:

  1. Total Internal Reflection (TIR) of light through a glass/plastic fiber
  2. Refraction of light
  3. Absorption of light
  4. Interference of light

Answer: Total Internal Reflection (TIR) of light through a glass/plastic fiber

Explanation: Optical fibers work by TIR — light traveling through a dense glass core hits the glass-cladding interface at angles beyond the critical angle and is totally reflected, staying in the fiber over long distances. Used in communications.

46. Which mirror is used in the rear-view mirror of vehicles?

  1. Convex mirror (gives a wider field of view)
  2. Concave mirror
  3. Plane mirror
  4. Parabolic mirror

Answer: Convex mirror (gives a wider field of view)

Explanation: Convex mirrors are used as rear-view/wing mirrors because they produce an erect, diminished virtual image with a wide field of view. Concave mirrors are used as shaving/makeup mirrors and in searchlights.

47. Which mirror is used as a reflector in headlights and searchlights?

  1. Concave mirror (converges light into a parallel beam)
  2. Convex mirror
  3. Plane mirror
  4. Both concave and convex

Answer: Concave mirror (converges light into a parallel beam)

Explanation: Concave (converging) mirrors are used in headlights, searchlights, solar concentrators, and telescopes. When a light source is placed at the focal point, the reflected rays form a parallel (collimated) beam.

48. The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of:

  1. Speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in that medium (n = c/v)
  2. Wavelength in vacuum to wavelength in medium
  3. Frequency in medium to frequency in vacuum
  4. Density of medium to density of vacuum

Answer: Speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in that medium (n = c/v)

Explanation: Refractive index (n) = c/v (speed of light in vacuum / speed in medium). Higher n = slower light, more bending. Glass n ≈ 1.5, Water n ≈ 1.33, Diamond n ≈ 2.42 (hence diamond's high reflectance).

49. The household electricity supply in India is:

  1. 230 V AC at 50 Hz
  2. 110 V AC at 60 Hz
  3. 220 V DC
  4. 415 V AC at 50 Hz

Answer: 230 V AC at 50 Hz

Explanation: India uses 230 V AC at 50 Hz for household supply. USA uses 110-120 V at 60 Hz. Three-phase supply for industries is 415 V. The frequency (50 Hz) means current changes direction 50 times per second.

50. The unit of electrical energy consumed is:

  1. Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — also called a 'unit'
  2. Joule only
  3. Watt
  4. Volt

Answer: Kilowatt-hour (kWh) — also called a 'unit'

Explanation: Electrical energy consumed = Power (kW) × Time (hours). 1 kWh = 1 'unit' of electricity on the electricity bill. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules. A 1000 W (1 kW) appliance running for 1 hour uses 1 unit.
