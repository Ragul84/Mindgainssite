26. Consider the elements X, Y, and Z. Element X forms a stable ionic compound with chlorine, where X has a +3 charge. Element Y is a non-metal that exists as a diatomic gas at room temperature and is highly unreactive. Element Z is a metalloid that can act as both an acid and a base. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding their positions in the periodic table and their chemical properties?

  1. X is likely a Group 13 metal, Y is likely nitrogen, and Z is likely in Group 14 or 15.
  2. X is likely an alkaline earth metal, Y is likely oxygen, and Z is likely boron.
  3. X is likely an alkali metal, Y is likely a halogen, and Z is likely silicon.
  4. X is likely a transition metal, Y is likely hydrogen, and Z is likely germanium.

Answer: X is likely a Group 13 metal, Y is likely nitrogen, and Z is likely in Group 14 or 15.

Explanation: X with a +3 charge suggests Group 13. Y as an unreactive diatomic non-metal is nitrogen. Z as an amphoteric metalloid points to elements like Si, Ge, As, Sb (Groups 14, 15).

27. A student has three unlabeled bottles, each containing a colorless liquid: dilute sulfuric acid, dilute potassium hydroxide, and distilled water. The student has access to red litmus paper, blue litmus paper, and phenolphthalein indicator. Which is the most efficient sequence of tests to correctly identify all three liquids?

  1. Test with phenolphthalein, then use red litmus paper on the remaining liquids.
  2. Test with blue litmus paper, then use red litmus paper on the remaining liquids.
  3. Test with red litmus paper, then use blue litmus paper on the remaining liquids.
  4. Test with phenolphthalein, then use blue litmus paper on the remaining liquids.

Answer: Test with phenolphthalein, then use red litmus paper on the remaining liquids.

Explanation: Phenolphthalein identifies the base. Then, red litmus differentiates acid from water.
