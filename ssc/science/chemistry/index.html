Chemistry MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Chemistry questions to revise the SSC Science syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 1,339 questions.

1. Which of the following elements is a non-metal and exists as a diatomic molecule at room temperature?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Sodium
  3. Iron
  4. Helium

Answer: Oxygen

Explanation: Oxygen is a non-metal and exists as O₂ (a diatomic molecule) at room temperature. The other options are either metals or noble gases.

2. A solution turns red litmus paper blue. This solution is likely to be:

  1. Acidic
  2. Basic
  3. Neutral
  4. Amphoteric

Answer: Basic

Explanation: Red litmus paper turning blue indicates a basic solution. Acids turn blue litmus red, and neutral solutions do not change the color of litmus.

3. Which of the following elements has the highest electronegativity?

  1. Fluorine
  2. Chlorine
  3. Oxygen
  4. Nitrogen

Answer: Fluorine

Explanation: Electronegativity generally increases across a period and decreases down a group. Fluorine is in the top right of the periodic table (excluding noble gases).

4. An aqueous solution turns red litmus paper blue. What is the probable pH range of this solution?

  1. pH < 7
  2. pH = 7
  3. pH > 7
  4. pH = 0

Answer: pH > 7

Explanation: Red litmus paper turning blue indicates a basic solution. Basic solutions have a pH greater than 7.

5. Which type of chemical reaction is represented by the equation: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)?

  1. Combination reaction
  2. Decomposition reaction
  3. Displacement reaction
  4. Double displacement reaction

Answer: Decomposition reaction

Explanation: In a decomposition reaction, a single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances. Here, calcium carbonate breaks into calcium oxide and carbon dioxide.

6. Which of the following metals is most reactive?

  1. Copper
  2. Iron
  3. Sodium
  4. Silver

Answer: Sodium

Explanation: Reactivity of metals generally increases as you go down a group and decreases across a period. Sodium is an alkali metal and highly reactive.

7. What is the chemical formula for washing soda?

  1. NaOH
  2. Na₂CO₃
  3. NaHCO₃
  4. CaCO₃

Answer: Na₂CO₃

Explanation: Washing soda is chemically known as sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃). Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) is baking soda.

8. A student performs three experiments. In Experiment 1, a solution turns red litmus blue. In Experiment 2, a different solution reacts with zinc metal to produce hydrogen gas. In Experiment 3, a third solution reacts with sodium carbonate to release carbon dioxide gas. If the solution from Experiment 1 is then mixed with the solution from Experiment 3, what is the most likely observation and its chemical nature?

  1. No visible change, as both are neutral solutions.
  2. A vigorous exothermic reaction, indicating neutralization.
  3. Formation of a precipitate, suggesting a double displacement reaction.
  4. Release of a pungent gas, characteristic of an oxidation-reduction reaction.

Answer: A vigorous exothermic reaction, indicating neutralization.

Explanation: Experiment 1 indicates a base, and Experiment 3 indicates an acid. Mixing an acid and a base results in a neutralization reaction, which is typically exothermic.

9. Consider the elements X, Y, and Z. Element X forms a stable ionic compound with chlorine, where X has a +2 charge. Element Y is a non-metal that exists as a diatomic gas at room temperature and is highly reactive. Element Z is a metalloid that can act as both an acid and a base. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding their positions in the periodic table and their chemical properties?

  1. X is likely in Group 1, Y in Group 17, and Z in Group 13; X is more reactive than Z.
  2. X is likely in Group 2, Y in Group 17, and Z in Group 13; X has a lower ionization energy than Z.
  3. X is likely in Group 13, Y in Group 16, and Z in Group 14; Z exhibits metallic character.
  4. X is likely in Group 2, Y in Group 16, and Z in Group 15; Y forms acidic oxides.

Answer: X is likely in Group 2, Y in Group 17, and Z in Group 13; X has a lower ionization energy than Z.

Explanation: X (+2 charge) suggests Group 2. Y (diatomic, highly reactive non-metal) suggests Group 17 (halogens). Z (metalloid, amphoteric) suggests Group 13 (e.g., Boron, Aluminum). Group 2 metals have lower ionization energies than metalloids in Group 13.

10. A student has three unlabeled bottles, each containing a colorless liquid: dilute hydrochloric acid, dilute sodium hydroxide, and distilled water. The student has access to red litmus paper, blue litmus paper, and phenolphthalein indicator. Which is the most efficient sequence of tests to correctly identify all three liquids?

  1. Test each liquid with red litmus paper, then with blue litmus paper.
  2. Add phenolphthalein to each liquid; then use litmus paper if no color change occurs.
  3. Test each liquid with blue litmus paper. For those that show no change, add red litmus paper. If still no change, use phenolphthalein.
  4. Test each liquid with red litmus paper. For those that show no change, add phenolphthalein. For the remaining, use blue litmus paper.

Answer: Test each liquid with red litmus paper. For those that show no change, add phenolphthalein. For the remaining, use blue litmus paper.

Explanation: Red litmus identifies acid (turns blue). Phenolphthalein identifies base (turns pink). The remaining colorless liquid is distilled water.

11. A solution turns blue litmus paper red. This solution is likely to be:

  1. Basic
  2. Acidic
  3. Neutral
  4. Ionic

Answer: Acidic

Explanation: Blue litmus paper turns red in the presence of an acid. Therefore, the solution is acidic.

12. A solution causes no change to either red or blue litmus paper. This solution is likely to be:

  1. Basic
  2. Acidic
  3. Neutral
  4. Strongly acidic

Answer: Neutral

Explanation: A neutral solution does not change the color of either red or blue litmus paper. Therefore, the solution is neutral.

13. A solution has a pH of 10. This solution is likely to:

  1. Turn blue litmus red
  2. Turn red litmus blue
  3. Have no effect on litmus paper
  4. Be highly concentrated

Answer: Turn red litmus blue

Explanation: A pH of 10 indicates a basic solution. Basic solutions turn red litmus paper blue.

14. An aqueous solution turns blue litmus paper red. What is the probable pH range of this solution?

  1. pH > 7
  2. pH = 7
  3. pH < 7
  4. pH = 14

Answer: pH < 7

Explanation: Blue litmus paper turns red in acidic solutions. Acidic solutions have a pH less than 7.

15. An aqueous solution shows no change with either red or blue litmus paper. What is the probable pH range of this solution?

  1. pH < 6
  2. pH = 7
  3. pH > 8
  4. pH < 4

Answer: pH = 7

Explanation: A solution that doesn't change litmus paper is neutral. Neutral solutions have a pH of 7.

16. An aqueous solution has a pH of 12. What would be the expected color change with red litmus paper?

  1. No change
  2. Turns blue
  3. Turns red
  4. Turns green

Answer: Turns blue

Explanation: A pH of 12 indicates a strong base. Basic solutions turn red litmus paper blue.

17. Which type of chemical reaction is represented by the equation: 2KClO₃(s) → 2KCl(s) + 3O₂(g)?

  1. Decomposition reaction
  2. Synthesis reaction
  3. Single displacement reaction
  4. Double displacement reaction

Answer: Decomposition reaction

Explanation: A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances. This is the definition of a decomposition reaction.

18. Which type of chemical reaction is represented by the equation: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)?

  1. Combustion reaction
  2. Decomposition reaction
  3. Synthesis reaction
  4. Single displacement reaction

Answer: Synthesis reaction

Explanation: Two or more reactants combine to form a single, more complex product. This is the definition of a synthesis reaction.

19. Which type of chemical reaction is represented by the equation: Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)?

  1. Decomposition reaction
  2. Synthesis reaction
  3. Single displacement reaction
  4. Double displacement reaction

Answer: Single displacement reaction

Explanation: One element replaces another element in a compound. This describes a single displacement reaction.

20. What is the chemical formula for baking soda?

  1. Na2CO3
  2. NaHCO3
  3. KOH
  4. CaCO3

Answer: NaHCO3

Explanation: Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, which has the chemical formula NaHCO3.

21. What is the chemical formula for caustic soda?

  1. Na2CO3
  2. NaHCO3
  3. NaOH
  4. MgCl2

Answer: NaOH

Explanation: Caustic soda is sodium hydroxide, which has the chemical formula NaOH.

22. A student performs two experiments. In Experiment 1, a solution turns red litmus blue. In Experiment 2, a different solution reacts with magnesium metal to produce hydrogen gas. If the solution from Experiment 1 is then mixed with the solution from Experiment 2, what is the most likely observation and its chemical nature?

  1. The solution becomes warmer, indicating an exothermic neutralization reaction.
  2. The solution changes color, indicating a redox reaction.
  3. Gas bubbles are produced, indicating a decomposition reaction.
  4. A precipitate forms, indicating a double displacement reaction.

Answer: The solution becomes warmer, indicating an exothermic neutralization reaction.

Explanation: Experiment 1 indicates a base, Experiment 2 indicates an acid. Mixing an acid and a base results in a neutralization reaction, which is typically exothermic.

23. A student performs four experiments. In Experiment A, a solution turns blue litmus red. In Experiment B, a different solution reacts with calcium metal to produce hydrogen gas. In Experiment C, a third solution reacts with sodium bicarbonate to release carbon dioxide gas. In Experiment D, a fourth solution turns red litmus blue. If the solution from Experiment A is then mixed with the solution from Experiment D, what is the most likely observation and its chemical nature?

  1. The solution becomes warmer, indicating an exothermic neutralization reaction.
  2. The solution changes color, indicating a complexation reaction.
  3. Gas bubbles are produced, indicating a gas evolution reaction.
  4. A precipitate forms, indicating a precipitation reaction.

Answer: The solution becomes warmer, indicating an exothermic neutralization reaction.

Explanation: Experiment A indicates an acid, Experiment D indicates a base. Mixing an acid and a base results in a neutralization reaction, which is typically exothermic.

24. A student performs five experiments. In Experiment I, a solution turns red litmus blue. In Experiment II, a different solution reacts with potassium metal to produce hydrogen gas. In Experiment III, a third solution reacts with potassium carbonate to release carbon dioxide gas. In Experiment IV, a fourth solution turns blue litmus red. In Experiment V, a fifth solution is neutral. If the solution from Experiment I is then mixed with the solution from Experiment IV, what is the most likely observation and its chemical nature?

  1. The solution becomes warmer, indicating an exothermic neutralization reaction.
  2. The solution remains unchanged, indicating no reaction.
  3. A solid forms, indicating a precipitation reaction.
  4. The solution cools down, indicating an endothermic reaction.

Answer: The solution becomes warmer, indicating an exothermic neutralization reaction.

Explanation: Experiment I indicates a base, Experiment IV indicates an acid. Mixing an acid and a base results in a neutralization reaction, which is typically exothermic.

25. Consider the elements X, Y, and Z. Element X forms a stable ionic compound with chlorine, where X has a +1 charge. Element Y is a non-metal that exists as a diatomic gas at room temperature and is highly reactive. Element Z is a metalloid that can act as both an acid and a base. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding their positions in the periodic table and their chemical properties?

  1. X is likely an alkali metal, Y is likely a halogen, and Z is likely in Group 14 or 15.
  2. X is likely an alkaline earth metal, Y is likely oxygen, and Z is likely boron.
  3. X is likely a transition metal, Y is likely nitrogen, and Z is likely silicon.
  4. X is likely a noble gas, Y is likely hydrogen, and Z is likely germanium.

Answer: X is likely an alkali metal, Y is likely a halogen, and Z is likely in Group 14 or 15.

Explanation: X with a +1 charge suggests Group 1. Y as a reactive diatomic non-metal suggests halogens (Group 17). Z as an amphoteric metalloid points to elements like Si, Ge, As, Sb (Groups 14, 15).
