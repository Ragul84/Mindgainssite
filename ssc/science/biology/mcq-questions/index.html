26. Vitamin B12 deficiency causes:

  1. Pernicious anaemia (megaloblastic anaemia) and nerve damage
  2. Beriberi
  3. Scurvy
  4. Rickets

Answer: Pernicious anaemia (megaloblastic anaemia) and nerve damage

Explanation: Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) deficiency causes pernicious anaemia (megaloblastic anaemia — large, abnormal red blood cells) and peripheral neuropathy. Found only in animal products — vegans are at risk.

27. Mitosis results in:

  1. Two identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as parent (2n → 2n)
  2. Four cells with half chromosomes
  3. Gametes
  4. Genetically varied cells

Answer: Two identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as parent (2n → 2n)

Explanation: Mitosis: cell division producing 2 identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number (2n). Used for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. PMAT phases: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.

28. Meiosis results in:

  1. Four genetically diverse haploid cells (2n → 4 cells of n)
  2. Two identical diploid cells
  3. Two haploid cells
  4. Four identical cells

Answer: Four genetically diverse haploid cells (2n → 4 cells of n)

Explanation: Meiosis: two successive cell divisions producing 4 haploid (n) cells (gametes) from one diploid (2n) parent cell. Genetic variation through crossing-over (prophase I) and independent assortment. Meiosis I and Meiosis II.

29. Cloning is the process of producing:

  1. Genetically identical copies of an organism from a single cell (asexual reproduction)
  2. Hybrid organisms
  3. Genetically modified organisms
  4. Embryo splitting only

Answer: Genetically identical copies of an organism from a single cell (asexual reproduction)

Explanation: Cloning produces genetically identical organisms. Dolly the sheep (1996) was the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell (via nuclear transfer into an enucleated egg). Therapeutic cloning for stem cells is research topic.

30. CT scan (Computed Tomography) uses:

  1. X-rays from multiple angles to create 3D images of internal body structures
  2. Magnetic fields
  3. Ultrasound
  4. Radioactive isotopes

Answer: X-rays from multiple angles to create 3D images of internal body structures

Explanation: CT (Computed Tomography / CAT scan): X-ray beams rotate around the body from multiple angles. Computer compiles cross-sectional 2D images to create detailed 3D images of organs, tissues, and bones.

31. PET scan (Positron Emission Tomography) uses:

  1. Radioactive tracers (usually FDG — fluorodeoxyglucose) to image metabolic activity
  2. X-rays
  3. Ultrasound
  4. Magnetic fields only

Answer: Radioactive tracers (usually FDG — fluorodeoxyglucose) to image metabolic activity

Explanation: PET scan uses radioactive tracers (e.g., FDG — a radioactive glucose). Active cells (especially cancer cells) take up more glucose. Positrons emitted by the tracer produce gamma rays detected to create functional images.

32. A food chain always starts with:

  1. Producers (autotrophs — plants that photosynthesize)
  2. Herbivores
  3. Carnivores
  4. Decomposers

Answer: Producers (autotrophs — plants that photosynthesize)

Explanation: Food chain: Producers (plants) → Primary consumers (herbivores) → Secondary consumers (carnivores) → Tertiary consumers → Decomposers. Energy flows from producers upward; each level has only ~10% efficiency.

33. Decomposers in an ecosystem are:

  1. Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Secondary consumers
  4. Producers

Answer: Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients

Explanation: Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead organisms and waste products into simpler inorganic substances (minerals, CO₂, water), recycling nutrients back into the soil/ecosystem. Essential for nutrient cycling.

34. A symbiotic relationship where both organisms benefit is called:

  1. Mutualism (e.g., bees and flowers, Rhizobium and legumes)
  2. Parasitism
  3. Commensalism
  4. Competition

Answer: Mutualism (e.g., bees and flowers, Rhizobium and legumes)

Explanation: Mutualism: both organisms benefit. Examples: Bee-flower (bee gets nectar, flower gets pollinated); Rhizobium-legume (bacteria get nutrients, plant gets fixed nitrogen); Mycorrhizal fungi-plant roots.

35. A relationship where one organism benefits and the other is harmed is:

  1. Parasitism (e.g., tapeworm in humans, mistletoe on trees)
  2. Mutualism
  3. Commensalism
  4. Predation

Answer: Parasitism (e.g., tapeworm in humans, mistletoe on trees)

Explanation: Parasitism: parasite benefits (nutrition, shelter), host is harmed (reduced fitness). Examples: tapeworm (host: human), Plasmodium (host: human + mosquito), mistletoe on trees, head lice.

36. Antibodies are produced by:

  1. B-lymphocytes (B-cells) in response to antigens
  2. T-lymphocytes
  3. Red blood cells
  4. Platelets

Answer: B-lymphocytes (B-cells) in response to antigens

Explanation: B-lymphocytes (B-cells) produce antibodies (immunoglobulins) when stimulated by antigens. Antibodies are Y-shaped proteins that specifically bind to antigens and neutralize pathogens. T-cells coordinate the immune response.

37. Vaccines work by:

  1. Stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies without causing full disease (immunological memory)
  2. Killing bacteria directly
  3. Providing ready-made antibodies
  4. Reducing body temperature

Answer: Stimulating the immune system to produce antibodies without causing full disease (immunological memory)

Explanation: Vaccines contain weakened/killed pathogens, toxoids, or specific antigens. They stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies and memory cells. On future exposure to the real pathogen, the immune system responds rapidly.

38. HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks which cells?

  1. CD4+ T-helper cells (T-lymphocytes) — destroying the immune system's coordination
  2. Red blood cells
  3. Platelets
  4. Liver cells

Answer: CD4+ T-helper cells (T-lymphocytes) — destroying the immune system's coordination

Explanation: HIV specifically targets CD4+ T-helper lymphocytes (T-cells), which coordinate immune responses. As T-cell count drops, the immune system weakens, leading to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) — susceptibility to opportunistic infections.

39. The basic functional unit of the nervous system is:

  1. Neuron (nerve cell) — with dendrites, cell body, and axon
  2. Glial cell
  3. Synapse
  4. Myelin sheath

Answer: Neuron (nerve cell) — with dendrites, cell body, and axon

Explanation: A neuron is the basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system. It has: dendrites (receive signals), cell body/soma (processes signals), and axon (transmits signals). Signals travel as electrical impulses.

40. The gap between two neurons where nerve impulses are transmitted is called:

  1. Synapse (chemicals called neurotransmitters cross this gap)
  2. Axon
  3. Myelin
  4. Node of Ranvier

Answer: Synapse (chemicals called neurotransmitters cross this gap)

Explanation: A synapse is the junction between two neurons (or neuron and muscle). Nerve impulses trigger release of neurotransmitters (e.g., acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin) from the presynaptic terminal, which bind to receptors on the postsynaptic cell.

41. The hormone insulin is produced by the pancreas and its function is:

  1. Lower blood glucose levels by facilitating glucose uptake into cells (deficiency → Type 1 diabetes)
  2. Increase blood glucose levels
  3. Regulate calcium levels
  4. Control growth

Answer: Lower blood glucose levels by facilitating glucose uptake into cells (deficiency → Type 1 diabetes)

Explanation: Insulin (produced by beta cells of islets of Langerhans in pancreas) lowers blood glucose by helping cells absorb glucose. Deficiency of insulin or insulin resistance leads to diabetes mellitus. Glucagon (alpha cells) raises blood glucose.

42. Testosterone is produced by the:

  1. Testes (in males) and small amounts by adrenal cortex — responsible for male secondary sexual characteristics
  2. Ovaries
  3. Thyroid gland
  4. Adrenal medulla

Answer: Testes (in males) and small amounts by adrenal cortex — responsible for male secondary sexual characteristics

Explanation: Testosterone is the primary male sex hormone, produced by Leydig cells in the testes. It is responsible for: male secondary sexual characteristics (beard, deep voice, muscle mass), sperm production, and bone density. Women also produce small amounts in ovaries/adrenal glands.

43. The human heart has how many chambers?

  1. Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle
  2. Two chambers
  3. Three chambers
  4. Five chambers

Answer: Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle

Explanation: The human heart has 4 chambers: right atrium and right ventricle (receive deoxygenated blood from body, pump to lungs) and left atrium and left ventricle (receive oxygenated blood from lungs, pump to body). Left ventricle has the thickest wall.

44. The blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart is:

  1. Pulmonary vein (the only vein that carries oxygenated blood)
  2. Pulmonary artery
  3. Aorta
  4. Vena cava

Answer: Pulmonary vein (the only vein that carries oxygenated blood)

Explanation: Pulmonary veins (4 of them, 2 from each lung) carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. Note: arteries generally carry oxygenated blood EXCEPT pulmonary artery. Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs.

45. Platelets (thrombocytes) are responsible for:

  1. Blood clotting (stopping bleeding at wound sites by forming a clot)
  2. Carrying oxygen
  3. Fighting infection
  4. Producing antibodies

Answer: Blood clotting (stopping bleeding at wound sites by forming a clot)

Explanation: Platelets (thrombocytes) are small cell fragments that clump at wound sites and release clotting factors, leading to formation of a fibrin clot. Normal platelet count: 1.5-4 lakh per microliter. Low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) occurs in dengue.

46. The site of fertilization in the human female reproductive system is:

  1. Fallopian tube (oviduct) — where sperm meets the egg after ovulation
  2. Uterus
  3. Ovary
  4. Cervix

Answer: Fallopian tube (oviduct) — where sperm meets the egg after ovulation

Explanation: Fertilization normally occurs in the fallopian tube (usually the ampulla region). The fertilized egg (zygote) travels to the uterus and implants in the endometrium. Fertilization outside the fallopian tube is called ectopic pregnancy.

47. Xylem and phloem are plant tissues that transport:

  1. Xylem: water and minerals (upward from roots); Phloem: sugars/food (bidirectionally)
  2. Xylem: food; Phloem: water
  3. Only water
  4. Only food

Answer: Xylem: water and minerals (upward from roots); Phloem: sugars/food (bidirectionally)

Explanation: Xylem (dead tissue) transports water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves (upward). Phloem (living tissue) transports photosynthetic products (mainly sucrose) from leaves to other parts — up or down (bidirectional transport).

48. The male reproductive organ in flowers is the:

  1. Stamen (consists of anther and filament — anther produces pollen grains)
  2. Pistil
  3. Sepal
  4. Petal

Answer: Stamen (consists of anther and filament — anther produces pollen grains)

Explanation: Stamen is the male reproductive organ. It has: anther (produces and releases pollen/male gametes) and filament (supports the anther). The pistil (carpel) is the female organ: stigma + style + ovary.

49. Biomagnification refers to:

  1. Increasing concentration of toxic substances (DDT, mercury) at each higher trophic level in a food chain
  2. Reduction in biodiversity
  3. Increase in plant growth
  4. Decomposition process

Answer: Increasing concentration of toxic substances (DDT, mercury) at each higher trophic level in a food chain

Explanation: Biomagnification (bioaccumulation): non-biodegradable toxins (DDT, PCBs, mercury, lead) accumulate in fatty tissues and become more concentrated at each trophic level. Top predators (eagles, polar bears, humans) have the highest concentrations.

50. Solar cells (photovoltaic cells) convert:

  1. Solar (light) energy directly into electrical energy using the photoelectric effect in semiconductor (silicon) material
  2. Heat energy to electricity
  3. Chemical energy to electricity
  4. Kinetic energy to electricity

Answer: Solar (light) energy directly into electrical energy using the photoelectric effect in semiconductor (silicon) material

Explanation: Solar cells (PV cells) use silicon p-n junctions. When photons hit the cell, they excite electrons across the band gap, generating a potential difference (voltage). Multiple cells in series form solar panels. Efficiency of commercial cells: ~15-22%.
