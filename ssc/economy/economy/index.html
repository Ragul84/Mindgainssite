Economy MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Economy questions to revise the SSC Economy syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 410 questions.

1. GDP stands for:

  1. Gross Domestic Product
  2. General Domestic Price
  3. Gross Development Plan
  4. Government Domestic Policy

Answer: Gross Domestic Product

Explanation: GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is the total monetary value of all goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific period.

2. Which body formulates India's monetary policy?

  1. SEBI
  2. Ministry of Finance
  3. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
  4. NITI Aayog

Answer: Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

Explanation: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is India's central bank and formulates monetary policy to control inflation, money supply, and credit.

3. GST stands for:

  1. General Sales Tax
  2. Goods and Services Tax
  3. Government Service Tax
  4. Gross Service Tax

Answer: Goods and Services Tax

Explanation: GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a comprehensive indirect tax levied on supply of goods and services across India. It replaced multiple taxes like VAT, service tax, excise duty.

4. The Planning Commission of India was replaced by:

  1. Finance Commission
  2. NITI Aayog
  3. Economic Advisory Council
  4. CAG

Answer: NITI Aayog

Explanation: NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission in January 2015. It serves as a policy think tank.

5. Inflation means:

  1. Fall in general price level
  2. Rise in general price level
  3. Stable prices
  4. Fall in interest rates

Answer: Rise in general price level

Explanation: Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, eroding purchasing power.

6. The Union Budget in India is presented by:

  1. Prime Minister
  2. President
  3. Finance Minister
  4. RBI Governor

Answer: Finance Minister

Explanation: The Union Budget is presented by the Finance Minister to Parliament (Lok Sabha) on 1st February every year.

7. FDI stands for:

  1. Foreign Direct Investment
  2. Federal Direct Income
  3. Financial Development Index
  4. Foreign Debt Instrument

Answer: Foreign Direct Investment

Explanation: FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) is investment made by a foreign entity in a business or production in another country, providing control or significant influence.

8. The 'Repo Rate' is the rate at which:

  1. Banks borrow from each other
  2. RBI lends money to commercial banks (short-term)
  3. Commercial banks lend to customers
  4. Government borrows from RBI

Answer: RBI lends money to commercial banks (short-term)

Explanation: Repo Rate is the rate at which the RBI lends money to commercial banks for short periods (usually overnight) against government securities. Increasing repo rate reduces money supply and controls inflation.

9. The 'Human Development Index' (HDI) is published by:

  1. World Bank
  2. IMF
  3. UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)
  4. WHO

Answer: UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)

Explanation: The HDI is published by UNDP in the Human Development Report. It measures countries on three dimensions: life expectancy, education, and per capita income.

10. The 'Green Revolution' in India was associated with:

  1. Increase in forest cover
  2. High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds for food grains (wheat and rice)
  3. Development of renewable energy
  4. Growth of vegetable production

Answer: High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds for food grains (wheat and rice)

Explanation: The Green Revolution (1960s-70s) introduced HYV seeds (especially wheat by Norman Borlaug and M.S. Swaminathan), modern irrigation, and fertilisers, leading to a dramatic increase in food grain production.

11. India's economic liberalisation was initiated in:

  1. 1947
  2. 1951
  3. 1991
  4. 2001

Answer: 1991

Explanation: India adopted LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation) reforms in 1991 under PM Narasimha Rao and FM Manmohan Singh, triggered by a balance of payments crisis.

12. The WTO (World Trade Organisation) replaced which organisation?

  1. IMF
  2. GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)
  3. World Bank
  4. UNCTAD

Answer: GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade)

Explanation: WTO replaced GATT on 1 January 1995. It administers multilateral trade agreements and provides a forum for trade negotiations and dispute resolution.

13. Poverty line in India is determined based on:

  1. Income only
  2. Calorie intake / consumption expenditure
  3. Asset ownership
  4. Employment status

Answer: Calorie intake / consumption expenditure

Explanation: India's poverty line is primarily based on minimum required calorie intake (2,400 kcal/day rural; 2,100 kcal/day urban) and the expenditure needed to achieve it. The Tendulkar Committee revised this methodology.

14. Economics is defined as the study of:

  1. How individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants
  2. Only money and banking
  3. Only government finance
  4. Only international trade

Answer: How individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants

Explanation: Economics (from Greek 'oikonomia' = household management): study of how scarce resources (land, labor, capital, enterprise) are allocated to satisfy unlimited human wants. Alfred Marshall: 'Science of material welfare.' Lionel Robbins: 'Science of choice under scarcity.'

15. The law of demand states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)
  2. When price rises, demand rises
  3. Demand is always constant
  4. Supply determines demand

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity demanded falls (inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded)

Explanation: Law of Demand (Alfred Marshall): price and quantity demanded have an inverse relationship — as price rises, quantity demanded falls (consumers buy less; some switch to substitutes). Exceptions (Giffen goods, Veblen goods, necessities, wartime hoarding). Demand curve slopes downward.

16. The law of supply states that, all else equal:

  1. When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)
  2. When price rises, supply falls
  3. Supply is constant regardless of price
  4. Demand determines supply

Answer: When price of a good rises, quantity supplied increases (direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied)

Explanation: Law of Supply: price and quantity supplied have a direct relationship — as price rises, producers are willing to supply more (higher profit incentive). Supply curve slopes upward. Exceptions: agricultural products (time lag), antiques/art (fixed supply). Market equilibrium: demand = supply.

17. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) measures:

  1. Total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period (usually a year/quarter)
  2. Total income of all citizens including abroad
  3. Only industrial production
  4. Only exports

Answer: Total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period (usually a year/quarter)

Explanation: GDP = C + I + G + (X-M) [Expenditure method]. C = Private Consumption, I = Investment, G = Government Spending, X = Exports, M = Imports. GDP measures economic output within borders regardless of who produces it. India's GDP at current prices: ~$3.7 trillion (2024). Nominal GDP (current prices) vs Real GDP (constant prices, adjusts for inflation).

18. The difference between GDP and GNP is:

  1. GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) — GNP includes income of nationals abroad; GDP includes income of foreigners in India
  2. GDP is larger than GNP always
  3. They are the same thing
  4. GNP excludes government spending

Answer: GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) — GNP includes income of nationals abroad; GDP includes income of foreigners in India

Explanation: GDP: value of production within the territory. GNP: value of production by nationals (regardless of where). GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). For India: NFIA is slightly positive (remittances from Indian workers abroad exceed MNC profits repatriated out). GNP ≈ GNI (Gross National Income).

19. Net National Product (NNP) at factor cost is also known as:

  1. National Income — NNP at factor cost = GNP - Depreciation (capital consumption) — measures actual income generated by an economy
  2. Per capita income
  3. GDP
  4. Fiscal deficit

Answer: National Income — NNP at factor cost = GNP - Depreciation (capital consumption) — measures actual income generated by an economy

Explanation: National Income = NNP at factor cost = GNP - Depreciation. Depreciation/capital consumption allowance: value of fixed assets used up in production. At factor cost = at market price minus indirect taxes plus subsidies. National Income per capita = National Income ÷ Population = measure of average living standard.

20. Inflation refers to:

  1. Sustained increase in the general price level over time — reduces purchasing power of money
  2. Decrease in prices (deflation)
  3. Increase in production
  4. Increase in employment

Answer: Sustained increase in the general price level over time — reduces purchasing power of money

Explanation: Inflation: persistent rise in the general price level → purchasing power of money falls. Measured by: CPI (Consumer Price Index, India's headline inflation measure), WPI (Wholesale Price Index). RBI's inflation target: 4% ± 2% (2-6% band). Causes: demand-pull (excess demand), cost-push (higher costs), monetary (excess money supply).

21. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures:

  1. Change in retail prices of a basket of goods and services purchased by typical households — India's main inflation indicator since 2014
  2. Change in wholesale prices (WPI)
  3. Change in stock market prices
  4. Change in property prices

Answer: Change in retail prices of a basket of goods and services purchased by typical households — India's main inflation indicator since 2014

Explanation: CPI: measures change in average retail prices paid by consumers for a fixed basket of goods/services. Base year: 2012. Categories: food & beverages (weight ~46%), housing, fuel, clothing, misc. RBI uses CPI-Combined (Urban + Rural) as the headline inflation measure. Annual CPI target: 4% (with ±2% tolerance band).

22. The Repo Rate is:

  1. Rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks for the short term (against government securities as collateral)
  2. Rate at which banks lend to customers
  3. Rate at which banks accept deposits
  4. Rate of interest on government bonds

Answer: Rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks for the short term (against government securities as collateral)

Explanation: Repo Rate (Repurchase Rate): rate at which RBI provides short-term funds to commercial banks by purchasing their government securities (with agreement to repurchase). Increasing repo rate → commercial banks raise lending rates → reduces borrowing → controls inflation. Reverse Repo Rate: rate at which RBI borrows from banks (currently = Repo - 25 bps).

23. The Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is:

  1. Percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (deposits) that commercial banks must maintain as cash with RBI (earns no interest)
  2. Interest rate on loans
  3. Tax on bank profits
  4. Minimum capital requirement

Answer: Percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (deposits) that commercial banks must maintain as cash with RBI (earns no interest)

Explanation: CRR: RBI requires commercial banks to hold a certain % of their total deposits (NDTL) as cash with RBI. Current CRR: 4% (as of 2024). High CRR → banks have less money to lend → reduces money supply → controls inflation. RBI uses CRR as a quantitative monetary policy tool.

24. India's Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in:

  1. January 2015 — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) chaired by the Prime Minister, unlike Planning Commission
  2. 2010
  3. 2000
  4. 2020

Answer: January 2015 — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) chaired by the Prime Minister, unlike Planning Commission

Explanation: NITI Aayog: established January 1, 2015, replacing the Planning Commission (est. 1950). NITI Aayog is a policy think tank (not a fund-allocating body like Planning Commission). Chaired by PM. CEO runs daily operations. Prepares national strategies and provides technical advice. Planning Commission's 5-year plans replaced by 3-year, 7-year strategies.

25. India's Five Year Plans were a feature of which economic model?

  1. Socialist/Mixed economy planning — Soviet-influenced centralized planning to guide economic development (1951-2017)
  2. Pure capitalist economy
  3. Free market economy
  4. Laissez-faire economy

Answer: Socialist/Mixed economy planning — Soviet-influenced centralized planning to guide economic development (1951-2017)

Explanation: India's Five-Year Plans (1951-2017, 12 plans total) were inspired by Soviet Union's central planning model. First Plan (1951-56): agriculture focus (Bhakra dam, community development). Second Plan (Nehru-Mahalanobis): heavy industry. 12th Plan ended 2017. NITI Aayog replaced with strategy documents.
