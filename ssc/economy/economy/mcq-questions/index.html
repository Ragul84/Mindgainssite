26. The White Revolution in India refers to:

  1. Operation Flood (1970-1996) — massive increase in milk production; led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India') through AMUL cooperative model
  2. Green Revolution
  3. Blue Revolution (fisheries)
  4. Pink Revolution (meat/poultry)

Answer: Operation Flood (1970-1996) — massive increase in milk production; led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ('Milkman of India') through AMUL cooperative model

Explanation: White Revolution/Operation Flood: Dr. Verghese Kurien (NDDB — National Dairy Development Board) set up milk cooperatives (AMUL model) to eliminate middlemen, give fair price to farmers, and collect/process milk efficiently. India became world's largest milk producer. AMUL started in Anand, Gujarat, 1946.

27. India adopted economic liberalization in:

  1. 1991 — LPG (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) reforms under PM Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh during a severe balance of payments crisis
  2. 1947
  3. 1969 (bank nationalization)
  4. 2000

Answer: 1991 — LPG (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) reforms under PM Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh during a severe balance of payments crisis

Explanation: 1991 Economic Reforms: India faced severe forex crisis (only 3 weeks' reserves), high inflation, and fiscal deficit. PM P.V. Narasimha Rao and FM Dr. Manmohan Singh introduced: dismantling License Raj, reducing import tariffs, opening FDI, devaluing rupee, privatization. Called LPG reforms. India's GDP growth accelerated post-reform.

28. The term 'fiscal deficit' refers to:

  1. Excess of total government expenditure over total revenue receipts (tax + non-tax revenue) — financed by borrowing
  2. Excess of exports over imports (trade surplus)
  3. Excess of imports over exports (trade deficit)
  4. Excess of bank deposits over loans

Answer: Excess of total government expenditure over total revenue receipts (tax + non-tax revenue) — financed by borrowing

Explanation: Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure - (Revenue Receipts + Capital Receipts excluding borrowings). It shows government's borrowing requirement. India's fiscal deficit target: 3% of GDP (FRBM Act, 2003 target). Funded by borrowing from RBI (monetization, inflationary) or market borrowings (public debt). High fiscal deficit → high government debt.

29. Public Distribution System (PDS) in India is meant to:

  1. Distribute subsidized food grains (rice, wheat, sugar, kerosene) to Below Poverty Line (BPL) families through ration shops (Fair Price Shops)
  2. Distribute fertilizers
  3. Manage mid-day meals in schools
  4. Export food grains

Answer: Distribute subsidized food grains (rice, wheat, sugar, kerosene) to Below Poverty Line (BPL) families through ration shops (Fair Price Shops)

Explanation: PDS: network of about 5.3 lakh Fair Price Shops (ration shops) distributing subsidized food. Managed jointly by central (Food Corporation of India procures and allocates) and state governments (distributes). NFSA 2013 (National Food Security Act) extended PDS to ~81 crore people (75% rural, 50% urban population).

30. The Minimum Support Price (MSP) is:

  1. Government-guaranteed price for agricultural produce — ensures farmers get fair price and are not exploited by market forces; recommended by CACP
  2. Market price of commodities
  3. Minimum wage for agricultural workers
  4. Tax on agricultural income

Answer: Government-guaranteed price for agricultural produce — ensures farmers get fair price and are not exploited by market forces; recommended by CACP

Explanation: MSP: announced by Central Government (based on CACP — Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices recommendations) before sowing season. Government agencies (FCI, NAFED) purchase crops at MSP if market price falls below it. MSP for 23 major crops including paddy, wheat, pulses, oilseeds, and cotton. Not legally binding on private buyers.

31. The National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) guarantees:

  1. 100 days of unskilled manual work per year to any rural household that demands it — minimum wage paid, unemployed allowance if work not provided within 15 days
  2. 200 days of work
  3. Employment to urban workers
  4. Skilled employment only

Answer: 100 days of unskilled manual work per year to any rural household that demands it — minimum wage paid, unemployed allowance if work not provided within 15 days

Explanation: MGNREGA (2005, implemented 2006): provides legal right to 100 days of unskilled work per household per year at minimum wage. If work not provided within 15 days, unemployment allowance paid. Works include road construction, water conservation, land development. Among world's largest social protection programs. Renamed to include Gandhi: MGNREGS.

32. India's Union Budget is presented by the:

  1. Finance Minister of India in Parliament (Lok Sabha) — normally on February 1 since 2017; first Indian Union Budget: 1860 (James Wilson)
  2. Prime Minister
  3. RBI Governor
  4. President of India

Answer: Finance Minister of India in Parliament (Lok Sabha) — normally on February 1 since 2017; first Indian Union Budget: 1860 (James Wilson)

Explanation: Union Budget: presented by Finance Minister in Parliament (Lok Sabha) on February 1 (changed from last day of February in 2017, by Finance Minister Arun Jaitley). Consists of Revenue Budget (tax/non-tax income + current expenditure) and Capital Budget (assets/liabilities). First Budget: James Wilson (1860) under British. P. Chidambaram presented most Budgets (9 times).

33. Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented in India on:

  1. July 1, 2017 — replaced multiple central and state taxes; 101st Constitutional Amendment; 'One Nation One Tax'
  2. July 1, 2015
  3. January 1, 2017
  4. April 1, 2018

Answer: July 1, 2017 — replaced multiple central and state taxes; 101st Constitutional Amendment; 'One Nation One Tax'

Explanation: GST launched on 1 July 2017 under the 101st Constitutional Amendment (2016), replacing central excise, service tax, VAT, CST, octroi and other state taxes. Since 22 September 2025 its main rates are 5% and 18%, with 40% for luxury and sin goods (earlier 5, 12, 18 and 28%).

34. The Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) aims at:

  1. Financial inclusion — opening zero-balance bank accounts for unbanked sections with RuPay debit card, overdraft facility, and insurance
  2. Free LPG connections
  3. Healthcare for poor
  4. Rural electrification

Answer: Financial inclusion — opening zero-balance bank accounts for unbanked sections with RuPay debit card, overdraft facility, and insurance

Explanation: PMJDY (August 28, 2014): world's largest financial inclusion initiative. Features: zero-balance savings account (Jan Dhan account), RuPay debit card with Rs 2 lakh accident insurance, Rs 30,000 life insurance, Rs 10,000 overdraft facility. By 2024: 52+ crore accounts opened, mostly in rural areas.

35. The World Trade Organization (WTO) was established in:

  1. 1995 — replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1947); HQ in Geneva, Switzerland; 164 members; deals with international trade rules
  2. 1947
  3. 2000
  4. 1990

Answer: 1995 — replaced GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1947); HQ in Geneva, Switzerland; 164 members; deals with international trade rules

Explanation: WTO (January 1, 1995): replaced GATT (1947) after Uruguay Round negotiations (1986-1994). HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. 164 member countries. Functions: sets and enforces global trade rules, resolves trade disputes, negotiates new trade agreements. India is a founding member. Doha Development Round (2001): stalled over agricultural subsidies.

36. The Human Development Index (HDI) was developed by:

  1. UNDP (United Nations Development Programme) — Mahbub ul Haq (Pakistan) and Amartya Sen (India); measures health, education, and income (not just GDP)
  2. World Bank
  3. IMF
  4. WHO

Answer: UNDP (United Nations Development Programme) — Mahbub ul Haq (Pakistan) and Amartya Sen (India); measures health, education, and income (not just GDP)

Explanation: HDI (1990): developed by economist Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen for UNDP. Composite index measuring: Life Expectancy (health), Mean/Expected Years of Schooling (education), and GNI per capita (income). HDI ranges 0-1. India's HDI rank: 134 out of 193 (UNDP 2023 report). Norway consistently tops HDI.

37. The Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY) aims to provide:

  1. Housing for All — affordable housing to urban and rural poor by 2022 (extended to 2024); includes PMAY-Urban and PMAY-Gramin
  2. Free food for all
  3. Employment guarantee
  4. Healthcare for all

Answer: Housing for All — affordable housing to urban and rural poor by 2022 (extended to 2024); includes PMAY-Urban and PMAY-Gramin

Explanation: PMAY (2015): targets housing for all urban slum dwellers and rural homeless. PMAY-Urban: credit-linked subsidy scheme for home loans (for EWS/LIG/MIG). PMAY-Gramin: provides Rs 1.2 lakh (plain areas) or Rs 1.3 lakh (NE, hilly) grant for rural house construction. Completed 3 crore+ houses.

38. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) is:

  1. Investment by a foreign entity in a business in another country with significant control (typically 10%+ ownership) — in plant, machinery, or management control
  2. Portfolio investment (FPI/FII — just buying shares)
  3. Loans from foreign banks
  4. Government-to-government aid

Answer: Investment by a foreign entity in a business in another country with significant control (typically 10%+ ownership) — in plant, machinery, or management control

Explanation: FDI: foreign entity invests in a domestic company (typically 10%+ equity = significant control). Brings: capital, technology, management expertise, global market access. FPI/FII: foreign portfolio investment (buying shares without control). India attracted $70 billion+ FDI in 2021-22. Top FDI sectors: services, computer, telecom, construction.

39. SENSEX is the benchmark stock market index of:

  1. Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) — comprises 30 largest, most actively traded companies; launched January 1, 1986 (base year 1978-79 = 100)
  2. National Stock Exchange (NSE — that's NIFTY 50)
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. Finance Ministry

Answer: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) — comprises 30 largest, most actively traded companies; launched January 1, 1986 (base year 1978-79 = 100)

Explanation: SENSEX (Sensitive Index): BSE's 30-stock index. NSE's benchmark: NIFTY 50 (50 stocks). BSE (1875): world's oldest stock exchange in Asia, at Dalal Street, Mumbai. NSE (1992): India's largest exchange by trading volume. SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India, 1992): regulator of securities market.

40. India's first stock exchange was:

  1. Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, 1875) — established as 'The Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association' under a banyan tree on Dalal Street, Mumbai
  2. NSE (1992)
  3. Calcutta Stock Exchange (1908)
  4. Madras Stock Exchange (1937)

Answer: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, 1875) — established as 'The Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association' under a banyan tree on Dalal Street, Mumbai

Explanation: Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, 1875): Asia's oldest stock exchange. Started informally in 1855 under a banyan tree, formalized as 'The Native Share and Stock Brokers' Association.' Moved to Dalal Street, Mumbai. India's second oldest exchange: Ahmedabad Stock Exchange (1894). Calcutta Stock Exchange: 1908. Madras Stock Exchange: 1937.

41. SIDBI (Small Industries Development Bank of India) was established to:

  1. Finance and develop Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) — provides refinancing and direct loans; established 1990, HQ: Lucknow
  2. Finance agriculture
  3. Fund large industries
  4. Manage stock markets

Answer: Finance and develop Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) — provides refinancing and direct loans; established 1990, HQ: Lucknow

Explanation: SIDBI (1990, HQ Lucknow): principal financial institution for MSME promotion, financing, and development. Functions: refinancing commercial banks/SFCs/NBFCs for MSME loans, direct lending, equity assistance, microfinance support. MSMEs contribute ~30% of India's GDP and ~50% of exports.

42. India's economic growth rate (GDP growth) is measured by:

  1. Central Statistics Office (CSO) — now called NSO (National Statistical Office) under MoSPI; base year currently 2011-12
  2. RBI
  3. Finance Ministry directly
  4. World Bank

Answer: Central Statistics Office (CSO) — now called NSO (National Statistical Office) under MoSPI; base year currently 2011-12

Explanation: National Statistical Office (NSO, formerly CSO) under Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) compiles India's national accounts (GDP data). Current base year: 2011-12. GDP growth rate: ~7% in recent years (one of fastest growing major economies). IMF, World Bank provide their own GDP estimates.

43. The Economic Survey of India is published by:

  1. Ministry of Finance (Economic Division) — usually released the day before the Union Budget; provides comprehensive analysis of Indian economy
  2. RBI
  3. NITI Aayog
  4. Finance Commission

Answer: Ministry of Finance (Economic Division) — usually released the day before the Union Budget; provides comprehensive analysis of Indian economy

Explanation: Economic Survey: prepared by the Economic Division of India's Ministry of Finance (typically under the Chief Economic Adviser to the Government). Released one day before the Union Budget. Provides: macroeconomic overview, sector-wise analysis, fiscal data, and policy recommendations. Since 2017, it has been published in two volumes.

44. India is classified as a:

  1. Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)
  2. Pure socialist economy
  3. Pure capitalist economy
  4. Closed economy

Answer: Mixed economy — combines elements of market economy (private sector, price mechanism) and planned economy (government intervention, public sector)

Explanation: India's mixed economy (as in the Constitution's Directive Principles): public sector controls strategic industries (defense, rail, atomic energy), while private sector operates in most areas. Since 1991 reforms, India has moved significantly toward market economy with reduced government control.

45. The National Food Security Act (NFSA) 2013 provides:

  1. Subsidized food grains to ~81 crore beneficiaries (75% rural, 50% urban population) — rice at Rs 3/kg, wheat at Rs 2/kg, coarse grains at Rs 1/kg
  2. Free food to all citizens
  3. Food to BPL families only
  4. Export of food grains

Answer: Subsidized food grains to ~81 crore beneficiaries (75% rural, 50% urban population) — rice at Rs 3/kg, wheat at Rs 2/kg, coarse grains at Rs 1/kg

Explanation: NFSA 2013: legal entitlement to subsidized food. Priority households: 5 kg/person/month. Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY — poorest of poor): 35 kg/household/month. Prices: rice Rs 3, wheat Rs 2, coarse cereals Rs 1 per kg. Implemented through PDS. About 81 crore people covered.

46. India's largest trading partner (exports + imports combined) in recent years is:

  1. USA (largest export destination), China (largest import source) — China tops as overall trading partner by value
  2. Pakistan
  3. Bangladesh
  4. UAE

Answer: USA (largest export destination), China (largest import source) — China tops as overall trading partner by value

Explanation: India's top trading partners: USA is the largest export destination (~18% of India's exports). China is the largest source of imports (~15% of India's imports). As a bilateral trading partner (exports + imports combined), China and USA alternately top the list. UAE and Saudi Arabia are also major partners.

47. The term 'Laissez-Faire' in economics means:

  1. Let it be/do — minimal government intervention; markets self-regulate through price mechanism (Adam Smith's 'invisible hand')
  2. Heavy government control
  3. Protectionist trade policy
  4. Mixed economy

Answer: Let it be/do — minimal government intervention; markets self-regulate through price mechanism (Adam Smith's 'invisible hand')

Explanation: Laissez-faire (French: 'let do/let go'): economic philosophy advocating minimal government intervention. Markets, through price mechanism ('invisible hand' — Adam Smith, 'Wealth of Nations,' 1776), efficiently allocate resources. Associated with classical economics. Opposite: interventionism/planned economy.

48. The Gini Coefficient measures:

  1. Income inequality within a country — 0 = perfect equality (everyone has same income); 1 = perfect inequality (one person has all income)
  2. Economic growth rate
  3. Poverty headcount
  4. Inflation rate

Answer: Income inequality within a country — 0 = perfect equality (everyone has same income); 1 = perfect inequality (one person has all income)

Explanation: Gini Coefficient (Corrado Gini, 1912): 0-1 scale measuring income distribution inequality. Derived from the Lorenz Curve. Values: 0-0.3 (low inequality), 0.3-0.5 (medium), >0.5 (high). India's Gini ≈ 0.33. High inequality countries: South Africa (0.63), Brazil (0.48). Low inequality: Scandinavian countries (0.25-0.27).

49. The National Rural Health Mission (NHM) was launched in:

  1. 2005 — to improve healthcare in rural India; later merged with National Urban Health Mission to form NHM in 2013; ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) are key workers
  2. 2000
  3. 2010
  4. 1995

Answer: 2005 — to improve healthcare in rural India; later merged with National Urban Health Mission to form NHM in 2013; ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) are key workers

Explanation: NRHM (National Rural Health Mission, 2005): flagship health scheme for rural areas. Key feature: ASHA workers — trained female community health activists (one per 1000 population) who link communities with health facilities. NRHM + National Urban Health Mission merged into NHM (National Health Mission) in 2013.

Key programs: Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) — maker spaces in schools (Grades 6-12) with 3D printers, robotics kits, etc. Atal Incubation Centres (AICs): support startups. Atal Community Innovation Centres (ACICs): tier-2/3 cities and rural areas.
