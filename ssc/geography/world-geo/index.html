SSC Geography practice

World Geo MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked World Geo questions to revise the SSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 149 questions.

1. Which ocean is to the south of India?

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Indian Ocean

Explanation: India is bounded by the Indian Ocean to the south, the Bay of Bengal to the southeast, and the Arabian Sea to the southwest.

2. Which continent is India a part of?

  1. Africa
  2. Europe
  3. Asia
  4. Australia

Answer: Asia

Explanation: India is located in South Asia, which is a subregion of Asia.

3. India shares its longest international border with which country?

  1. China
  2. Nepal
  3. Pakistan
  4. Bangladesh

Answer: Bangladesh

Explanation: India shares its longest international border with Bangladesh (4,096 km).

4. The Atacama Desert — the driest place on Earth — is in which continent?

  1. Africa
  2. Asia
  3. South America
  4. Australia

Answer: South America

Explanation: The Atacama Desert, the driest non-polar desert on Earth, is in northern Chile (South America).

5. India's total geographical area is approximately:

  1. 2.4 million km²
  2. 3.28 million km²
  3. 4.5 million km²
  4. 1.9 million km²

Answer: 3.28 million km²

Explanation: India's total area is 3.28 million km² (32.8 lakh sq km), making it the 7th largest country in the world. It accounts for about 2.4% of the world's total land area.

6. The world's largest ocean is:

  1. Pacific Ocean
  2. Atlantic Ocean
  3. Indian Ocean
  4. Arctic Ocean

Answer: Pacific Ocean

Explanation: The Pacific Ocean (165 million sq km) is the world's largest ocean. It contains the Mariana Trench — the deepest point on Earth (11,034 m).

7. The Panama Canal connects:

  1. Atlantic Ocean and Pacific Ocean
  2. Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea
  3. South Atlantic and North Atlantic
  4. Caribbean Sea and Amazon River

Answer: Atlantic Ocean and Pacific Ocean

Explanation: The Panama Canal (82 km, Panama) connects the Atlantic (via Caribbean Sea) and Pacific Ocean. Opened in 1914, ships avoid going around South America (Cape Horn), saving about 12,000 km.

8. According to the 2011 census, India's population was approximately:

  1. 1.21 billion (121 crore)
  2. 1.35 billion
  3. 1 billion
  4. 1.15 billion

Answer: 1.21 billion (121 crore)

Explanation: India's population was 1.21 billion (121 crore) as per the 2011 census. India is projected to overtake China as the world's most populous country — which happened around 2023.

9. India shares the longest border with which country?

  1. Bangladesh
  2. China
  3. Pakistan
  4. Nepal

Answer: Bangladesh

Explanation: India shares its longest land border with Bangladesh (4,156 km), followed by China (3,488 km), Pakistan (3,323 km), Nepal (1,751 km), Myanmar (1,643 km), Bhutan (699 km), and Afghanistan (106 km, disputed).

10. India's Silicon Valley is:

  1. Bengaluru (Bangalore)
  2. Hyderabad
  3. Chennai
  4. Mumbai

Answer: Bengaluru (Bangalore)

Explanation: Bengaluru (Bangalore) is called India's Silicon Valley due to concentration of IT companies — Infosys, Wipro, HAL, ISRO are headquartered here. It is also called the 'Pub Capital', 'Garden City', 'Startup Capital of India'.

11. White Revolution in India is associated with:

  1. Milk production (Operation Flood)
  2. Sugar production
  3. Cotton production
  4. Salt production

Answer: Milk production (Operation Flood)

Explanation: White Revolution (Operation Flood, 1970-1996) under Dr. Verghese Kurien (Father of White Revolution) transformed India from a milk-deficient country to the world's largest milk producer through cooperative dairy development (AMUL model).

12. 'Lagoons' are:

  1. Shallow water bodies separated from the sea by a narrow barrier (sandbar/coral reef)
  2. Deep sea trenches
  3. Mountain lakes
  4. River ox-bow lakes

Answer: Shallow water bodies separated from the sea by a narrow barrier (sandbar/coral reef)

Explanation: Lagoons are shallow water bodies (lakes) separated from the larger sea/ocean by a narrow barrier of land (sandbar, barrier island, or coral reef). Chilika (Odisha) and Pulicat (Tamil Nadu-AP) are famous Indian lagoons.

13. The largest country in Africa by area is:

  1. Algeria
  2. Sudan
  3. DR Congo
  4. Libya

Answer: Algeria

Explanation: Algeria (2.38 million sq km) is the largest country in Africa by area and the 10th largest in the world, after the 2011 partition of Sudan (which created South Sudan — Sudan is now smaller).

14. The Strait of Gibraltar separates:

  1. Spain (Europe) and Morocco (Africa)
  2. Italy and Tunisia
  3. Greece and Turkey
  4. UK and France

Answer: Spain (Europe) and Morocco (Africa)

Explanation: The Strait of Gibraltar (14 km wide at its narrowest) separates Spain (Europe) from Morocco (Africa). It connects the Atlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea. Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory.

15. The Strait of Malacca connects:

  1. Indian Ocean and South China Sea (between Malaysia and Indonesia)
  2. Pacific and Indian Ocean
  3. China Sea and Japan Sea
  4. Bay of Bengal and South China Sea

Answer: Indian Ocean and South China Sea (between Malaysia and Indonesia)

Explanation: The Strait of Malacca (between Malaysia/Singapore and Sumatra/Indonesia) is one of the world's most important shipping lanes, connecting the Indian Ocean with the South China Sea and Pacific Ocean.

16. The Bering Strait separates:

  1. Russia (Asia) and USA (Alaska)
  2. Alaska and Canada
  3. USA and Canada
  4. Russia and Japan

Answer: Russia (Asia) and USA (Alaska)

Explanation: The Bering Strait (85 km wide) separates Russia (Siberia, Asia) from the USA (Alaska, North America), connecting the Pacific Ocean (Bering Sea) with the Arctic Ocean. A proposed land bridge existed here during ice ages.

17. Which Indian city has the largest natural harbour?

  1. Mumbai
  2. Vizag (Visakhapatnam)
  3. Kochi
  4. Chennai

Answer: Mumbai

Explanation: Mumbai (Bombay) has one of the best natural harbours in the world. This is a key reason why it became India's commercial capital — it allowed large ships to dock safely. Vizag is India's deepest port.

18. The island country of Sri Lanka was formerly known as:

  1. Ceylon
  2. Serendip
  3. Lanka
  4. Taprobane

Answer: Ceylon

Explanation: Sri Lanka was called Ceylon (in English, from Portuguese 'Ceilão') until 1972 when it became a republic and adopted the name Sri Lanka. The ancient name was Serendip (Arabian/Persian) and Taprobane (Greek).

19. The capital of Australia is:

  1. Canberra
  2. Sydney
  3. Melbourne
  4. Brisbane

Answer: Canberra

Explanation: Canberra is the capital of Australia — it was specifically chosen as a compromise between rival cities Sydney and Melbourne. Sydney and Melbourne are much larger, but Canberra was built as a planned federal capital (1913).

20. The capital of Canada is:

  1. Ottawa
  2. Toronto
  3. Vancouver
  4. Montreal

Answer: Ottawa

Explanation: Ottawa (Ontario) is the capital of Canada. Toronto is the largest city and financial capital. Ottawa was chosen as capital by Queen Victoria in 1857 as a compromise between Toronto (Ontario) and Montreal (Quebec).

21. The capital of Brazil is:

  1. Brasília
  2. São Paulo
  3. Rio de Janeiro
  4. Salvador

Answer: Brasília

Explanation: Brasília is the capital of Brazil — a planned capital built in just 41 months and inaugurated in 1960. Before Brasília, Rio de Janeiro was the capital. São Paulo is Brazil's largest city and economic center.

22. The capital of Japan is:

  1. Tokyo
  2. Osaka
  3. Kyoto
  4. Hiroshima

Answer: Tokyo

Explanation: Tokyo is the capital and largest city of Japan (population ~14 million city, ~38 million metro area — world's largest metropolitan area). Before 1868, Kyoto was Japan's capital.

23. Which is the only state in India with two capitals (summer and winter)?

  1. Himachal Pradesh (Shimla in summer, Dharamsala or Shimla all year; J&K — Srinagar/Jammu)
  2. Uttarakhand
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Tamil Nadu

Answer: Himachal Pradesh (Shimla in summer, Dharamsala or Shimla all year; J&K — Srinagar/Jammu)

Explanation: J&K had two capitals — Srinagar (summer) and Jammu (winter) — before becoming a UT. After J&K reorganization (2019), both Srinagar and Jammu serve as capitals of the J&K UT. Himachal Pradesh's capital is only Shimla.

24. Chandigarh is the capital of:

  1. Punjab and Haryana both (and a Union Territory itself)
  2. Only Punjab
  3. Only Haryana
  4. Himachal Pradesh

Answer: Punjab and Haryana both (and a Union Territory itself)

Explanation: Chandigarh is a Union Territory that serves as the shared capital of both Punjab and Haryana. Designed by Le Corbusier, it was established after Partition (Lahore went to Pakistan) as Punjab's new capital.

25. Itanagar is the capital of:

  1. Arunachal Pradesh
  2. Meghalaya
  3. Nagaland
  4. Manipur

Answer: Arunachal Pradesh

Explanation: Itanagar is the capital of Arunachal Pradesh. Arunachal Pradesh is India's largest northeastern state by area and borders China, Bhutan, and Myanmar. Its capital Itanagar is named after Ita Fort.
