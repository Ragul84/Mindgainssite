India Geo MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked India Geo questions to revise the SSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 433 questions.

26. The Narmada river flows into which sea/ocean?

  1. Bay of Bengal
  2. Arabian Sea
  3. Indian Ocean directly
  4. Palk Bay

Answer: Arabian Sea

Explanation: The Narmada river flows westward and empties into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat/Cambay).

27. India's largest river island is:

  1. Majuli
  2. Wheeler Island
  3. Sriharikota
  4. Neil Island

Answer: Majuli

Explanation: Majuli in Assam is India's largest river island, located on the Brahmaputra river. It is also the world's largest inhabited river island.

28. The Rann of Kutch is a seasonal salt marsh located in:

  1. Rajasthan
  2. Gujarat
  3. Maharashtra
  4. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: Gujarat

Explanation: The Rann of Kutch is a vast seasonal salt marsh in Gujarat, India, and Pakistan. It is the world's largest salt desert.

29. The Shillong Plateau is in which region of India?

  1. Western India
  2. Central India
  3. Northern India
  4. Northeastern India

Answer: Northeastern India

Explanation: The Shillong Plateau (also called the Meghalaya Plateau) is in Northeastern India, in the state of Meghalaya.

30. Which Indian state is the leading producer of rubber?

  1. Tamil Nadu
  2. Andhra Pradesh
  3. Kerala
  4. Karnataka

Answer: Kerala

Explanation: Kerala is India's leading producer of natural rubber, accounting for around 77% of total production. India is the third-largest producer globally.

31. Which is the highest waterfall in India?

  1. Jog Falls
  2. Dudhsagar Falls
  3. Nohkalikai Falls
  4. Kunchikal Falls

Answer: Kunchikal Falls

Explanation: Kunchikal Falls in Karnataka (455 m) is the highest waterfall in India. Nohkalikai (340 m) in Meghalaya is often confused for this, but Kunchikal is higher.

32. The southernmost tip of mainland India is:

  1. Kanyakumari
  2. Indira Point
  3. Rameshwaram
  4. Thiruvananthapuram

Answer: Kanyakumari

Explanation: Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin), Tamil Nadu is the southernmost tip of peninsular (mainland) India. Indira Point in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands is the southernmost point of Indian territory overall.

33. The northernmost point of India is:

  1. Siachen Glacier
  2. Ladakh
  3. Indira Col (in Ladakh, J&K — administered)
  4. Leh

Answer: Indira Col (in Ladakh, J&K — administered)

Explanation: Indira Col (Indira Point) in the Karakoram Range, Ladakh (J&K) is the northernmost point of India at approximately 37°6'N latitude.

34. The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) passes through how many Indian states?

  1. 6
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 9

Answer: 8

Explanation: The Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram. It also passes through West Bengal and Tripura.

35. The Himalayas were formed by the collision of:

  1. African and Eurasian plates
  2. Indian (Indo-Australian) plate and Eurasian (Asian) plate — about 50 million years ago
  3. Pacific and Indian plates
  4. Arabian and Eurasian plates

Answer: Indian (Indo-Australian) plate and Eurasian (Asian) plate — about 50 million years ago

Explanation: The Himalayas are fold mountains formed c. 50 million years ago when the northward-moving Indo-Australian plate collided with the Eurasian plate. The Tethys Sea's sediments were folded up to form the Himalayas (still rising ~5mm/year).

36. The highest peak in India is:

  1. Mt. Everest
  2. Kanchenjunga
  3. K2 (Godwin-Austen)
  4. Nanga Parbat

Answer: K2 (Godwin-Austen)

Explanation: K2 (Godwin-Austen, 8611m) in the Karakoram Range is the highest peak in India (in the Pakistan-India disputed territory of Gilgit-Baltistan). Within undisputed India, Kanchenjunga (8586m) on the Sikkim-Nepal border is the highest. Mt. Everest is in Nepal.

37. The Himalayan mountain ranges from south to north are:

  1. Shiwaliks, Greater Himalayas, Lesser Himalayas
  2. Lesser Himalayas, Shiwaliks, Greater Himalayas
  3. Shiwaliks, Lesser Himalayas, Greater Himalayas
  4. Greater Himalayas, Lesser Himalayas, Shiwaliks

Answer: Shiwaliks, Lesser Himalayas, Greater Himalayas

Explanation: South to North: Shiwaliks (Outer/Sub-Himalayas, 600–1500m) → Lesser Himalayas/Himachal (Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, 1500–4500m) → Greater Himalayas/Himadri (Himalaya proper, 5000–8000m) → Trans-Himalayas (Karakoram, Ladakh).

38. The Ganga originates from:

  1. Yamunotri glacier
  2. Gangotri glacier (Bhagirathi river)
  3. Kedarnath
  4. Badrinath

Answer: Gangotri glacier (Bhagirathi river)

Explanation: The Ganga originates as the Bhagirathi river from Gangotri glacier at Gomukh (3892m), Uttarakhand. The Bhagirathi and Alaknanda rivers meet at Devprayag to form the Ganga. The river flows ~2525 km before meeting the Bay of Bengal.

39. The Brahmaputra river is called 'Tsangpo' in:

  1. India (Assam)
  2. Bangladesh
  3. Tibet (China)
  4. Bhutan

Answer: Tibet (China)

Explanation: The Brahmaputra originates as the Tsangpo river in Tibet (China). It enters India through Arunachal Pradesh (called Dihang/Siang), flows through Assam as Brahmaputra, and enters Bangladesh as Jamuna before joining the Ganga-Padma.

40. The Western Ghats (Sahyadri) separate:

  1. Tamil Nadu and Karnataka
  2. The narrow coastal plains (Malabar/Konkan coast) from the Deccan Plateau
  3. Gujarat from Maharashtra
  4. The Gangetic plain from Deccan

Answer: The narrow coastal plains (Malabar/Konkan coast) from the Deccan Plateau

Explanation: The Western Ghats run ~1600 km parallel to the west coast of India (Gujarat to Kanyakumari). They separate the narrow Konkan/Malabar coastal plains from the broad Deccan Plateau. They receive heavy rainfall due to the southwest monsoon.

41. The highest peak of the Western Ghats is:

  1. Anaimudi (2695m) in Kerala/Tamil Nadu border
  2. Doddabetta (2637m) in Nilgiris
  3. Kodaikanal
  4. Palani Hills

Answer: Anaimudi (2695m) in Kerala/Tamil Nadu border

Explanation: Anaimudi (2695m), located in Eravikulam National Park, Kerala, is the highest peak of the Western Ghats and South India. It is also called the 'Everest of South India'.

42. The Eastern Ghats are interrupted by which rivers?

  1. Ganga and Yamuna
  2. Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri — these rivers cut through the Eastern Ghats
  3. Brahmaputra and Barak
  4. Tapti and Narmada

Answer: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri — these rivers cut through the Eastern Ghats

Explanation: Unlike the continuous Western Ghats, the Eastern Ghats are discontinuous (broken/dissected) because major Deccan rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Pennar, Kaveri) cut through them as they flow east to the Bay of Bengal.

43. The Palk Strait separates:

  1. India and Pakistan
  2. India and Sri Lanka (between Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka)
  3. India and Bangladesh
  4. Andaman and Nicobar Islands

Answer: India and Sri Lanka (between Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka)

Explanation: The Palk Strait separates India (Tamil Nadu) from Sri Lanka. It is about 64–137 km wide. The Gulf of Mannar lies to the south and Palk Bay to the north of the strait.

44. India's climate is classified as:

  1. Tropical monsoon climate
  2. Mediterranean climate
  3. Continental climate
  4. Desert climate

Answer: Tropical monsoon climate

Explanation: India has a tropical monsoon climate (Koppen: Am/Aw/Bsh). The word 'monsoon' comes from Arabic 'mausim' (season). The four seasons: Cold weather (Dec-Feb), Hot weather (Mar-May), Southwest monsoon (Jun-Sep), Retreating monsoon (Oct-Nov).

45. The southwest monsoon enters India first through:

  1. Mumbai
  2. Kolkata
  3. Thiruvananthapuram (Kerala coast) around 1 June
  4. Chennai

Answer: Thiruvananthapuram (Kerala coast) around 1 June

Explanation: The Southwest Monsoon first arrives at the Kerala coast (Thiruvananthapuram area) around 1 June each year. It then advances in two branches: Arabian Sea branch (west coast) and Bay of Bengal branch (northeast). Reaches Delhi by ~29 June, rest of India by 15 July.

46. The wettest place in India (and one of the wettest in the world) is:

  1. Assam
  2. Cherrapunji/Sohra (and nearby Mawsynram), Meghalaya
  3. Mumbai
  4. Thiruvananthapuram

Answer: Cherrapunji/Sohra (and nearby Mawsynram), Meghalaya

Explanation: Mawsynram (11,873mm/year average) and Cherrapunji/Sohra (11,777mm/year) in Meghalaya receive the highest rainfall in India and among the highest in the world. This is due to orographic (relief) rainfall as moist Bay of Bengal winds hit the Khasi Hills.

47. The driest region of India is:

  1. Rajasthan Thar Desert (but Tamil Nadu's Ramanathapuram gets ~350mm)
  2. Leh, Ladakh — gets <100mm annual rainfall
  3. Rajasthan (Barmer area)
  4. Gujarat (Kutch area)

Answer: Leh, Ladakh — gets <100mm annual rainfall

Explanation: Leh (Ladakh) receives less than 100mm of precipitation annually, making it the driest inhabited region of India. The Thar Desert in Rajasthan gets 100–250mm. The Himalayan rain shadow effect keeps Ladakh extremely dry.

48. The rain shadow area (leeward side) of the Western Ghats includes:

  1. Konkan Coast
  2. Karnataka coast
  3. Deccan Plateau (parts of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh)
  4. Kerala coast

Answer: Deccan Plateau (parts of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh)

Explanation: The Western Ghats block the southwest monsoon. The windward (western) side gets heavy rainfall (Konkan, Malabar coasts). The leeward (eastern) side is a rain shadow — the Deccan Plateau of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh gets relatively less rainfall (300–700mm).

49. El Niño affects Indian monsoon by:

  1. Strengthening the monsoon
  2. Weakening the southwest monsoon, often leading to deficient rainfall/drought in India
  3. Having no effect
  4. Causing floods in Tamil Nadu

Answer: Weakening the southwest monsoon, often leading to deficient rainfall/drought in India

Explanation: El Niño (warming of central/eastern Pacific Ocean) weakens the Walker Circulation, suppresses Indian monsoon convection, and typically leads to below-normal monsoon rainfall in India — causing droughts. La Niña has the opposite effect (stronger monsoon).

50. The 'Loo' winds in North India are:

  1. Cool mountain winds
  2. Hot, dry, dusty winds blowing from the west/northwest in May-June in the northern plains (temperatures up to 45-48°C)
  3. Coastal sea breezes
  4. Monsoon winds

Answer: Hot, dry, dusty winds blowing from the west/northwest in May-June in the northern plains (temperatures up to 45-48°C)

Explanation: 'Loo' winds are hot, dry, dust-laden local winds that blow in the afternoon over the North Indian Plains (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan) in May-June. They can cause heatstroke and death. They result from intense heating of the Thar Desert.
