Environment MCQs with answers

Use these 25 worked Environment questions to revise the SSC Geography syllabus. Each answer includes an explanation; the full practice set contains 412 questions.

1. The Montreal Protocol (1987) aimed to phase out:

  1. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) that deplete the ozone layer
  2. Carbon dioxide emissions
  3. Plastic pollution
  4. Nuclear waste

Answer: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) that deplete the ozone layer

Explanation: The Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer (1987) is a landmark treaty phasing out CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) and other ozone-depleting substances. It is considered the most successful environmental treaty.

2. The Western Ghats is one of the world's 'Biodiversity Hotspots' because:

  1. It has the largest forest area
  2. It has exceptional species richness and endemism — over 5,000 species of flowering plants, 77% of amphibian species endemic — but faces severe habitat loss
  3. It is the highest mountain range in India
  4. It is the oldest geological formation

Answer: It has exceptional species richness and endemism — over 5,000 species of flowering plants, 77% of amphibian species endemic — but faces severe habitat loss

Explanation: The Western Ghats (UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2012) is one of the 36 global biodiversity hotspots. It has ~5,000 flowering plant species (1,700 endemic), 77% endemic amphibians, 32% endemic fish. Also home to tigers, elephants, lion-tailed macaque.

3. Which of the following is an example of a renewable natural resource?

  1. Coal
  2. Petroleum
  3. Solar Energy
  4. Natural Gas

Answer: Solar Energy

Explanation: Solar energy is constantly replenished by the sun, making it a renewable resource. The others are fossil fuels, which are non-renewable.

4. What is the primary gas responsible for the greenhouse effect on Earth?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Carbon Dioxide
  4. Argon

Answer: Carbon Dioxide

Explanation: Carbon dioxide traps heat in the atmosphere, contributing significantly to the greenhouse effect. Oxygen and nitrogen are the most abundant gases but do not have the same warming effect.

5. Which of the following greenhouse gases has the highest global warming potential (GWP) over a 100-year period, despite its relatively low atmospheric concentration?

  1. Methane (CH4)
  2. Carbon Dioxide (CO2)
  3. Nitrous Oxide (N2O)
  4. Sulphur Hexafluoride (SF6)

Answer: Sulphur Hexafluoride (SF6)

Explanation: Sulphur Hexafluoride (SF6) has an extremely high GWP, significantly greater than CO2, CH4, or N2O, making it a potent greenhouse gas despite its low concentration.

6. The term 'ecological footprint' primarily measures:

  1. The total biodiversity of a specific ecosystem.
  2. The amount of land and water area required to support a human population or a particular activity.
  3. The impact of pollution on aquatic life in a given region.
  4. The rate at which a species becomes endangered due to habitat loss.

Answer: The amount of land and water area required to support a human population or a particular activity.

Explanation: Ecological footprint quantifies the demand placed on natural resources by human activities. It represents the area of productive land and sea required to produce resources and absorb waste.

7. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of a 'hotspot' of biodiversity?

  1. High levels of endemic species.
  2. Significant habitat loss due to human activities.
  3. Located exclusively in tropical regions.
  4. Contains at least 1500 species of vascular plants as endemics.

Answer: Located exclusively in tropical regions.

Explanation: Biodiversity hotspots are not exclusively tropical; they can be found in various climatic zones. The other options are defining characteristics of hotspots.

8. The 'Tragedy of the Commons' best describes a situation where:

  1. Private ownership leads to efficient resource management.
  2. Individuals acting in their own self-interest deplete a shared, finite resource.
  3. Government intervention always results in equitable resource distribution.
  4. Technological advancements solve all environmental degradation issues.

Answer: Individuals acting in their own self-interest deplete a shared, finite resource.

Explanation: The Tragedy of the Commons illustrates how shared resources, when unregulated, are often overused and degraded by individuals seeking personal gain. It highlights the conflict between individual and collective interests.

9. Which of the following international agreements primarily aims to protect the ozone layer by phasing out the production of ozone-depleting substances?

  1. Kyoto Protocol
  2. Paris Agreement
  3. Montreal Protocol
  4. Convention on Biological Diversity

Answer: Montreal Protocol

Explanation: The Montreal Protocol specifically targets the phase-out of substances responsible for ozone depletion. The other agreements address climate change or biodiversity.

10. Which of the following is a primary greenhouse gas responsible for trapping heat in the Earth's atmosphere?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Carbon Dioxide
  4. Hydrogen

Answer: Carbon Dioxide

Explanation: Carbon Dioxide is a major greenhouse gas that absorbs infrared radiation, contributing to the warming of the Earth's atmosphere.

11. The process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy is known as:

  1. Respiration
  2. Photosynthesis
  3. Transpiration
  4. Decomposition

Answer: Photosynthesis

Explanation: Photosynthesis is the process where plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to create food and oxygen.

12. Which of the following atmospheric layers is primarily responsible for absorbing most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation?

  1. Troposphere
  2. Stratosphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Thermosphere

Answer: Stratosphere

Explanation: The stratosphere contains the ozone layer, which absorbs the majority of the Sun's dangerous UV radiation, protecting life on Earth.

13. The term 'biodiversity hotspot' refers to a biogeographic region with a significant reservoir of biodiversity that is under threat from humans. Which of the following criteria is essential for an area to be designated as a biodiversity hotspot?

  1. High population density and significant industrial activity.
  2. Presence of a large number of endemic species and experiencing significant habitat loss.
  3. Abundance of fossil fuels and agricultural land.
  4. Proximity to major urban centers and tourist attractions.

Answer: Presence of a large number of endemic species and experiencing significant habitat loss.

Explanation: Biodiversity hotspots are characterized by a high concentration of endemic species (found nowhere else) and a significant loss of their original habitat.

14. Eutrophication is a process where a body of water becomes overly enriched with nutrients, leading to excessive growth of algae and other aquatic plants. What is the primary cause of cultural eutrophication?

  1. Volcanic eruptions and seismic activity.
  2. Acid rain and atmospheric deposition of pollutants.
  3. Runoff from agricultural fertilizers and untreated sewage.
  4. Natural decomposition of organic matter in the water body.

Answer: Runoff from agricultural fertilizers and untreated sewage.

Explanation: Cultural eutrophication is primarily caused by human activities, especially the influx of nutrient-rich runoff from agriculture and wastewater.

15. Which international agreement aims to reduce the emissions of greenhouse gases responsible for climate change?

  1. Montreal Protocol
  2. Kyoto Protocol
  3. Ramsar Convention
  4. CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora)

Answer: Kyoto Protocol

Explanation: The Kyoto Protocol is an international treaty that commits state parties to reduce greenhouse gas emissions based on the premise that global warming exists and human-made CO2 emissions have caused it.

16. Deforestation in tropical rainforests contributes to climate change primarily through which of the following mechanisms?

  1. Increased reflection of solar radiation due to bare land.
  2. Absorption of excess carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
  3. Release of stored carbon dioxide and reduced carbon sequestration.
  4. Increased atmospheric humidity leading to more rainfall.

Answer: Release of stored carbon dioxide and reduced carbon sequestration.

Explanation: Trees absorb carbon dioxide; deforestation releases this stored carbon back into the atmosphere and reduces the planet's capacity to absorb future emissions.

17. What is the primary cause of ozone layer depletion?

  1. Chlorofluorocarbons
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Methane
  4. Nitrous oxide

Answer: Chlorofluorocarbons

Explanation: Chlorofluorocarbons are the primary cause of ozone layer depletion, releasing chlorine atoms that destroy ozone molecules.

18. Which of the following is a consequence of deforestation?

  1. Soil erosion
  2. Increased rainfall
  3. Improved air quality
  4. Reduced greenhouse gases

Answer: Soil erosion

Explanation: Deforestation leads to soil erosion due to the removal of tree roots that hold the soil in place.

19. What is the term for the movement of people from rural areas to cities?

  1. Urbanization
  2. Ruralization
  3. Suburbanization
  4. Migration

Answer: Urbanization

Explanation: Urbanization refers to the movement of people from rural areas to cities, resulting in increased urban population.

20. Which of the following pollutants is responsible for acid rain?

  1. Sulfur dioxide
  2. Nitrogen oxide
  3. Carbon monoxide
  4. Ozone

Answer: Sulfur dioxide

Explanation: Sulfur dioxide is a primary pollutant responsible for acid rain, reacting with water to form sulfuric acid.

21. What is the process by which water moves through a plant, from the roots to the atmosphere?

  1. Respiration
  2. Photosynthesis
  3. Transpiration
  4. Evaporation

Answer: Transpiration

Explanation: Transpiration is the process by which water moves through a plant, from the roots to the atmosphere, occurring mainly through stomata.

22. Which of the following is a renewable energy source?

  1. Coal
  2. Oil
  3. Natural gas
  4. Solar energy

Answer: Solar energy

Explanation: Solar energy is a renewable energy source, generated from the sun's rays and replenished naturally.

23. What is the term for the study of the relationship between organisms and their environment?

  1. Ecology
  2. Biology
  3. Geology
  4. Meteorology

Answer: Ecology

Explanation: Ecology is the study of the relationship between organisms and their environment, focusing on interactions and interdependencies.

24. Which of the following is a consequence of climate change?

  1. Increased biodiversity
  2. Improved air quality
  3. Rising sea levels
  4. Reduced greenhouse gases

Answer: Rising sea levels

Explanation: Rising sea levels are a consequence of climate change, resulting from the melting of glaciers and ice sheets.

25. What is the term for the layer of the atmosphere that contains the ozone layer?

  1. Troposphere
  2. Stratosphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Thermosphere

Answer: Stratosphere

Explanation: The stratosphere is the layer of the atmosphere that contains the ozone layer, protecting the Earth from harmful UV radiation.
