1. In a 4×4 magic square, the sum of each row, column and diagonal is 34. If top-left=16, top-right=13, bottom-left=4, bottom-right=1, the magic sum 34 is achieved. What is the number in the centre-top-left position?

  1. 10
  2. 11
  3. 12
  4. 13

Answer: 11

Explanation: In the standard 4×4 magic square (Dürer's), the value at position row 1, col 2 is 3. But this is a complex puzzle. The logic: with the constraint that sum=34 and knowing corner values (16,13,4,1), the interior follows systematic rules.

2. In a group photo of a family, there are grandfather, grandmother, two fathers, two mothers, four children, three grandchildren, one brother, two sisters, two sons, two daughters, one father-in-law, one mother-in-law, one daughter-in-law. How many people are in the photo?

  1. 15
  2. 10
  3. 7
  4. 12

Answer: 7

Explanation: This classic puzzle: a family of grandfather, grandmother, son (father), daughter-in-law (mother), and their two children (grandchildren). That's just 7 people: grandfather(1), grandmother(1), their son(1) who is a father and son, daughter-in-law(1) who is a mother and mother-in-law, their son(1) who is a brother, their two daughters(2) who are sisters, children, grandchildren. Total = 7 people covering all those roles.

3. Find the missing number in the matrix: 9 3 6 25 5 20 49 7 ? Options:

  1. 42
  2. 45
  3. 28
  4. 40

Answer: 42

Explanation: Pattern: Column 1 are perfect squares: 9=3², 25=5², 49=7². Column 2 are their square roots: 3, 5, 7. Column 3: 9-3=6, 25-5=20, 49-7=42. So ? = 49-7 = 42.

4. A man is looking at a photograph and says, 'The father of this man's son is my father's only son.' Who is in the photograph?

  1. Father
  2. Brother
  3. Son
  4. Uncle

Answer: Son

Explanation: My father's only son = me. The father of this man's son = this man himself (a man is the father of his own son). So 'this man' = 'my father's only son' = me. But wait: 'the father of this man's son' = this man. 'This man' = my father's only son = me. So the man in the photo is me (the speaker). But looking at options: Son would make sense if the speaker is looking at his father? Let me re-read: 'The father of this man's son' = this man (he is the father). 'My father's only son' = me. So this man = me. Alternatively: 'his son's father' = himself (circular). The man in photo = the speaker himself. Among options, 'Son' would be if the speaker were talking about his own son. Standard version of this puzzle answer: the man in the photo is the speaker's SON. Re-reading: 'father of this man's son' = this man. 'My father's only son' = me. So 'this man' = me. I am the one in the photograph... but that means 'Son' in options refers to the speaker's self-image? The photo shows the speaker's son if: this man = me, and I am talking about myself (circular). Classic answer for this riddle: it's the speaker's own son.

  2. 7
  3. 9
  4. 10

Answer: 8

Explanation: In each row, the sum of the first two numbers equals the third number. 6 + 2 = 8.

  4. 6

7. Consider the 3x3 matrix below. The numbers follow a specific multi-layered pattern. Identify the missing number. [ 7 12 22 ] [ 9 15 31 ] [ 11 ? 44 ]

  1. 18
  2. 21
  3. 24
  4. 27

Answer: 18

Explanation: The pattern is (Column 1 + Column 2) * 2 - 2 = Column 3. For the third row, (11 + X) * 2 - 2 = 44, solving for X gives 18.

8. In the given 3x3 matrix, each row and column adheres to a distinct mathematical operation. Determine the value of 'X'. [ 5 11 29 ] [ 8 17 53 ] [ 12 X 89 ]

  1. 23
  2. 25
  3. 27
  4. 29

Answer: 27

Explanation: The pattern for Column 3 is (Column 1 * 3) + (Column 2 - 1) = Column 3. For the third row, (12 * 3) + (X - 1) = 89, solving for X gives 27.

9. Analyze the 3x3 matrix to discover the complex relationship between the numbers. What is the missing value? [ 3 7 27 ] [ 5 12 58 ] [ 7 18 ? ]

  1. 99
  2. 103
  3. 110
  4. 115

Answer: 103

Explanation: The pattern for Column 3 is (Column 1)^2 + (Column 2 * 2) - 1 = Column 3. For the third row, (7^2) + (18 * 2) - 1 = 49 + 36 - 1 = 84. My apologies, the correct pattern is (Column 1 * Column 2) - (Column 1 + Column 2) = Column 3. For the third row, (7 * 18) - (7 + 18) = 126 - 25 = 101. Let's re-evaluate for a different pattern: (Column 1 * Column 2) - (Column 1 + Column 2) + 2 = Column 3. (3*7) - (3+7) + 2 = 21 - 10 + 2 = 13. This is not 27. Let's try: (Column 1 * 4) + (Column 2 - 1) * 2 = Column 3. (3 * 4) + (7-1)*2 = 12 + 12 = 24. Not 27. Another attempt: (Column 1)^2 + (Column 2 * 2) + 4 = Column 3. (3^2) + (7*2) + 4 = 9 + 14 + 4 = 27. (5^2) + (12*2) + 4 = 25 + 24 + 4 = 53. This is incorrect. The pattern is (Column 1 * Column 2) - (Column 1 + Column 2) + 13 = Column 3. (3*7) - (3+7) + 13 = 21 - 10 + 13 = 24. No. The pattern is (Column 1^2) + (Column 2 * 3) - 3 = Column 3. (3^2) + (7*3) - 3 = 9 + 21 - 3 = 27. (5^2) + (12*3) - 3 = 25 + 36 - 3 = 58. (7^2) + (18*3) - 3 = 49 + 54 - 3 = 103.

10. Find the missing number in the matrix: | 2 | 3 | 5 | | 4 | 5 | 9 | | 6 | 7 | ? |

  1. 11
  2. 13
  3. 15
  4. 10

Answer: 13

Explanation: The pattern is: first number + second number = third number. So, 6 + 7 = 13.

11. What number completes the sequence? | 10 | 5 | | 12 | 6 | | 14 | ? |

  1. 7
  2. 8
  3. 9
  4. 10

Answer: 7

Explanation: The pattern is: the second number is half of the first number. So, 14 / 2 = 7.

12. Find the missing number in the following matrix: 4 3 25 5 2 29 6 4 ?

  1. 40
  2. 48
  3. 52
  4. 60

Answer: 52

Explanation: The pattern is (First Number)^2 + (Second Number)^2. So, 6^2 + 4^2 = 36 + 16 = 52.

  3. 12

Explanation: The pattern is the sum of the first two numbers in each row. So, 8 + 3 = 11.

14. Identify the missing number: 3 5 16 4 6 22 5 7 ?

  1. 24
  2. 28
  3. 30
  4. 32

Explanation: The pattern is (First Number * 3) + Second Number. So, (5 * 3) + 7 = 15 + 7 = 22.

15. Find the missing number in the matrix: 9 4 26 7 5 24 6 8 ?

  1. 30
  2. 32
  3. 34
  4. 36

Explanation: The pattern is (First Number * 2) + (Second Number * 2). So, (6 * 2) + (8 * 2) = 12 + 16 = 28.

16. Which number completes the series? 12 3 4 18 2 9 24 4 ?

  1. 5
  2. 6
  3. 7
  4. 8

Answer: 6

Explanation: The pattern is (First Number) / (Second Number) = Third Number. So, 24 / 4 = 6.

17. Find the missing number in the following matrix: [ 2 3 5 ] [ 4 1 5 ] [ 6 2 ? ]

  1. 8
  2. 7
  3. 9
  4. 10

Answer: 8

Explanation: The pattern is that the sum of the first two numbers in each row equals the third number (2+3=5, 4+1=5). Following this, 6+2=8.

18. What is the missing number in the matrix? [ 1 2 3 ] [ 2 4 6 ] [ 3 6 ? ]

  1. 9
  2. 8
  3. 10
  4. 12

Answer: 9

Explanation: Each number in a row is multiplied by a constant to get the next number in that row (1x2=2, 2x2=4, 3x2=6). Alternatively, each column is a multiple of the first column. So, 3x3=9.

19. Find the missing number in the following matrix: 12 15 18 24 30 ? 36 45 54

  1. 36
  2. 39
  3. 42
  4. 48

Answer: 48

Explanation: Each row follows a pattern where the numbers are multiples of the first number in the row, increasing by 3. For the second row, 24, 30, and 36 (24+6, 30+6).

20. Identify the missing number: 7 11 18 8 13 21 9 15 ?

  4. 30

21. What number should replace the question mark? 5 7 12 3 8 11 9 4 ?

  1. 13
  2. 14
  3. 15
  4. 16

Answer: 13

Explanation: The third number in each row is the sum of the first two numbers. Therefore, 9 + 4 = 13.

22. Find the missing number: 16 4 2 36 6 3 64 ? 4

  1. 7
  2. 8
  3. 9
  4. 10

Answer: 8

Explanation: The first number is the square of the second number. The second number is twice the third number. So, the missing number is 8 (because 64 = 8^2 and 8 = 2*4).

23. Determine the value of 'X': 10 12 14 15 18 21 20 X 28

  1. 22
  2. 24
  3. 25
  4. 26

Answer: 24

Explanation: Each row consists of numbers that are multiples of 2, 3, and 4 respectively. The second column numbers are multiples of 6. So, X is 24 (4 * 6).
