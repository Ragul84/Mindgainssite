1. Which of the following Venn diagrams best represents the relationship between 'Males', 'Fathers', and 'Doctors'?

  1. Three concentric circles, with 'Males' as the outermost, 'Fathers' in the middle, and 'Doctors' as the innermost.
  2. Three overlapping circles, where 'Fathers' is completely inside 'Males', and 'Doctors' overlaps with both 'Males' and 'Fathers'.
  3. Three separate circles, indicating no relationship between them.
  4. Two overlapping circles, with a third circle completely inside one of the overlapping regions.

Answer: Three overlapping circles, where 'Fathers' is completely inside 'Males', and 'Doctors' overlaps with both 'Males' and 'Fathers'.

Explanation: All fathers are males, so the circle representing 'Fathers' must be completely inside the circle representing 'Males'. Doctors can be males or females, and some doctors can also be fathers. Therefore, the circle for 'Doctors' will overlap with both 'Males' and 'Fathers', but not be fully contained within either.

2. Which Venn diagram correctly depicts the relationship between 'Animals', 'Tigers', and 'Carnivores'?

  1. Three separate circles.
  2. 'Tigers' completely inside 'Carnivores', and 'Carnivores' completely inside 'Animals'.
  3. 'Tigers' completely inside 'Animals', and 'Carnivores' overlapping with 'Animals' but also containing 'Tigers'.
  4. 'Tigers' completely inside 'Carnivores', and 'Carnivores' overlapping with 'Animals'.

Answer: 'Tigers' completely inside 'Carnivores', and 'Carnivores' completely inside 'Animals'.

Explanation: All tigers are carnivores, and all carnivores are animals. Thus, the circle for 'Tigers' should be completely contained within 'Carnivores', and the circle for 'Carnivores' should be completely contained within 'Animals'. This forms a nested structure.

Answer: Three circles, where 'Mothers' is completely inside 'Women', and 'Engineers' overlaps with both 'Women' and 'Mothers'.

4. Which of the following diagrams best represents the relationship between 'Students', 'Players', and 'Singers'?

  1. Three separate circles.
  2. Three circles, each partially overlapping with the other two.
  3. Two concentric circles, with a third circle completely outside.
  4. One circle completely inside another, with a third circle overlapping the inner one.

Answer: Three circles, each partially overlapping with the other two.

Explanation: Some students can be players, some can be singers, and some can be both. Similarly, some players can be singers, and some can be students. There are also individuals who are only students, only players, or only singers. This indicates a partial overlap between all three categories.

5. In the given Venn diagram, the circle represents 'Students', the triangle represents 'Players', and the rectangle represents 'Singers'. The numbers in different segments indicate the number of persons in that category. How many students are players but not singers?

  1. 12
  2. 10
  3. 8
  4. 5

Answer: 8

Explanation: We need to find the region that is common to the 'Students' (Circle) and 'Players' (Triangle) but not included in 'Singers' (Rectangle). Looking at a typical Venn diagram for this setup, the number '8' would be in the intersection of the circle and triangle, but outside the rectangle.

14. Which of the following Venn diagrams best represents the relationship between 'Books', 'Novels', and 'Dictionaries'?

  1. Two separate circles, with a third circle completely inside one of them.
  2. 'Novels' completely inside 'Books', and 'Dictionaries' completely inside 'Books', but 'Novels' and 'Dictionaries' are separate from each other.
  3. Three overlapping circles.
  4. Three concentric circles.

Answer: 'Novels' completely inside 'Books', and 'Dictionaries' completely inside 'Books', but 'Novels' and 'Dictionaries' are separate from each other.

Explanation: All novels are books, and all dictionaries are books. However, novels and dictionaries are distinct categories of books and do not overlap with each other. Therefore, 'Novels' and 'Dictionaries' circles should both be inside the 'Books' circle, but separate from each other.
